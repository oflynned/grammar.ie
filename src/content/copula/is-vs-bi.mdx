## Overview

In Irish, two distinct verbs correspond to the English verb "to be":

*   **`is`**: Known as the **copula** (`an chopail`), its sole function is to link a subject with its predicate for classification or identification.
*   **`bí`**: Known as the **substantive verb** (`an briathar substainteach`), it is used to describe existence, states, and locations.

The core difference is that `bí` is predicative, meaning it can form a predicate on its own, while `is` simply acts as a link.

## The Substantive Verb `Bí`

The verb `bí` expresses that something exists in a particular state or location. Its simplest form, `táim`, means "I am" in the sense of "I exist". It is often used with prepositions of location, most fundamentally with `ann` ("there" or "in existence").

### Existence, State, and Location

`Bí` is used to describe where someone is, what state they are in, or what they are doing.

| Irish | English | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Tá sé ann.* | He is there. / He exists. | is he in-it |
| *Tá sé sa teach.* | He is in the house. | is he in the house |
| *Tá sé ar an mbord.* | He is on the table. | is he on the table |
| *Tá sé ina sheasamh.* | He is standing. | is he in his standing |
| *Tá sé ina chónaí.* | He lives/resides. | is he in his living |
| *Tá sé ina dhochtúir.* | He works as a doctor. | is he in his being-a-doctor |
| *Tá sé ina lá.* | It is daytime. | is it in its being-day |
| *Tá sé fuar.* | It is cold. | is it being-cold |

### Expressing Measure

Specifications of size, weight, age, and time are expressed using `bí` without a preposition.

*   *Tá sí seacht mbliana déag d’aois.* - She is 17 years old.
*   *Tá sí naoi gclocha meáchain.* - She weighs 9 stone.
*   *Tá sé ceathrú chun a trí.* - It is a quarter to three.

### Auxiliary Functions

The modern use of `bí` as an auxiliary verb, such as in the progressive aspect, still carries the sense of a temporary state.

*   *Tá sé ag ól.* - He is drinking (right now).

## Comparing `Bí` and `Is`

While both verbs can translate to "is", they convey different meanings. The copula, `is`, classifies or identifies, whereas `bí` describes a state. This is often summarised as a difference between permanent and temporary attribution.

| | Irish | English | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copula** | *Is dochtúir é.* | He is a doctor. | Classification: "he = doctor" |
| **`Bí`** | *Tá sé ina dhochtúir.* | He is a doctor. | State: "he is in the state of being a doctor" |

The sentence with `is` suggests a permanent classification. The sentence with `bí` suggests a state that could change; he works as a doctor now but might not always.

This distinction becomes clearer in the past tense:

*   *Ba mhúinteoir é.* - He was a teacher. (This implies he is no longer alive, as the permanent attribute could not have been lost otherwise).
*   *Bhí sé ina mhúinteoir.* - He was a teacher. (This implies he is now something else, like retired or in a new profession).

However, this rule can be flexible. Using a time-specific adverb like `anois` (now) can allow the copula for a new classification (*Is fear é anois* - He is a man now).

## Usage with Adjectives

The choice between `bí` and `is` with adjectives depends on the type of quality being described.

### Adjectives with `Bí`

Today, predicative adjectives are most commonly used with `bí`, implying a temporary or observed state.

*   *Tá sé te.* - It is hot.
*   *Tá an fear tinn.* - The man is sick.

For subjectively evaluative adjectives, the particle `go` is often added.

*   *Tá sé go maith.* - It is good.
*   *Tá sé go dona.* - It is bad.

Traditionally, adjectives describing permanent properties like size, length, or colour were not used with `bí`. Instead, an attributive construction with the copula was preferred.

*   *Is fear mór é.* - He is a big man. (Instead of "The man is big.")
*   *Is bóthar fada é.* - It is a long road. (Instead of "The road is long.")

This rule is relaxed when the adjective is modified by a prefix like `ró-` (too) or `an-` (very).

*   *Tá an bord ró-mhór.* - The table is too big.
*   *Tá an t-éan an-bheag.* - The bird is very small.

### Adjectives with the Copula

Using the copula with predicative adjectives is less common in modern Irish and is reserved for specific contexts. It describes a permanent, inherent quality and often has an exclamatory feel, emphasising the attribute itself.

| | Irish | English | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copula** | *Is maith é!* | He is good! | Emphatic statement of quality |
| **`Bí`** | *Tá sé go maith.* | He is good. | Description of his current state |

## Advanced Concepts and Variations

### The `Is ann do` Construction

Existence can also be expressed with the copula using the formula `is ann do` + subject. This is a more literary form.

*   *Is ann duit!* - There you are! / You exist!
*   *An ann dó?* - Does it exist?
*   *Ní hann don mhonarcha a thuilleadh.* - The factory no longer exists.

### Periphrasis for Colour

With colour adjectives, a common alternative construction is `bí dath` + adjective + `ar`.

*   *Bhí dath bán ar a haghaidh.* - Her face was white. (Literally: "Was colour white on her face.")

### Linguistic Parallels

Other languages, such as Spanish, also have two verbs for "to be" (`ser` and `estar`). While there are similarities (`is`/`ser` and `tá`/`estar`), their usage with adjectives differs significantly. Spanish typically uses `ser` with adjectives, whereas modern Irish overwhelmingly prefers `tá`.
