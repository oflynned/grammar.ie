The Irish copula, `is`, is a unique verb used for identification and classification, distinct from the substantive verb `bí`. It has only a few base forms, `is` (present) and `ba` (past/conditional), with most other forms arising from fusions with particles, conjunctions, or prepositions. In some cases, the copula is omitted entirely, and other words take on its function.

## Usage Before Vowels

The form of the copula can change depending on whether it precedes a consonant or a vowel.

### The form ba

Before a vowel or `fh-`, `ba` becomes `b’`. However, there are exceptions.

**`ba` is used before:**
*   The pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, `eisean`, `ise`, `iadsan`, and `éard`.
    *   *Ba é an fear é.* - He was the man.
    *   *Ba éard a dúirt sé…* - What he said was…
*   Prepositions.
    *   *Ba ar an mbord a chuir sé é.* - It was on the table that he put it.
*   Prepositional pronouns.
    *   *Ba air a chuir sé é.* - It was on it that he put it.
*   Adverbs.
    *   *Ba arú inné a tháinig sé.* - It was the day before yesterday that he came.

**`b’` is used before:**
*   Nouns.
    *   *B’fhear mór óil é.* - He was a great man for drinking.
*   Adjectives.
    *   *B’álainn an radharc é.* - It was a magnificent view.
*   Adverbs derived from adjectives.
    *   *B’annamh ólta é.* - Seldom was he drunk.
*   The adverb `amhlaidh`.
    *   *Agus b’amhlaidh an scéal i Ros Comáin.* - And the situation was the same in Roscommon.
*   The demonstrative pronouns `eo`, `in`, and `iúd`.
    *   *…agus b’eo ina ndiaidh é.* - …and off he went after them.

### Forms ending in -r

In the present tense, `gur` behaves differently from other forms ending in `-r`.

**`gur` (without -b) is used before:**
*   Most nouns: *gur amadán mé* - that I am a fool.
*   Prepositions: *gur i nGaillimh atá mé* - that I am in Galway.
*   Prepositional pronouns: *gur inti atá sé* - that it is in her.
*   Adverbs not derived from adjectives: *gur anseo atá mé* - that I am here.
*   The pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, etc., when part of an infinitive construction: *gur iad a chur air is fearr liom* - that I prefer to put them on it.

**`gurb` (with -b) is used before:**
*   The pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, `éard`, etc., in most other cases: *gurb é mo mhac é* - that he is my son.
*   Demonstrative pronouns: *Deir sí gurb in é an fear.* - She said that this is the man.
*   Adjectives: *gurb álainn an lá é* - that it is a beautiful day.
*   Adverbs derived from adjectives: *gurb annamh a rinne sé é* - that he did it seldom.
*   Certain nouns, such as `ionadh` or `eagal`: *gurb ionadh liom é* - that it surprises me; *gurb eol dom* - that I know.

Other present tense forms ending in `-r` (e.g., `arb`, `cérb`, `darb`) typically add a `-b` before vowels. In the preterite, all forms ending in `-r` add `-bh` before a vowel (e.g., `gurbh`, `arbh`, `níorbh`).

*   *An fear darb ainm Seán* - a man named Seán.
*   *Gurbh amadán mé* - that I was a fool.

### The form ní

The negative form `ní` prefixes `h-` to a following vowel only in specific cases.

**`ní h-` is used before:**
*   Pronouns: *Ní hé mo mhac é.* - He is not my son.
*   Some adjectives: *Ní hionann iad.* - They are not the same.
*   A few nouns: *Ní hionadh é sin.* - That is no wonder.

In other cases, no `h-` is added.
*   *Ní amadán mé.* - I am not a fool.

Present and subjunctive forms do not cause any initial mutation. In some dialects (Ulster and Connacht), adjectives beginning with `t`, `d`, or `s` are not lenited after `ba`.

## Omission of the Copula

In certain contexts, the copula form can be omitted for conciseness.

*   `is` can be omitted before emphatic pronouns and most nouns.
    *   *Is eisean an fear.* → *Eisean an fear.* - He is the man.
*   `is` cannot be omitted before adjectives in a standard copular clause.
    *   *Is maith liom í.* - I like her.
*   In cleft sentences (used for fronting or emphasis), `is` can be omitted before adjectives and must be omitted before indefinite nouns.
    *   *Is tuirseach atá sé.* → *Tuirseach atá sé.* - Tired is what he is.
    *   *Fear a rinne sin.* - A man did that.
*   In exclamatory sentences, `is` is always omitted.
    *   *Grá mo chroí thú!* - You are the love of my heart!
*   `ba` is rarely omitted, but can be in Munster after the direct relative particle.
    *   *Seomra na beatha a cheart a thabhairt air.* - The living room is what one should call it. (`a cheart` instead of `ba cheart`).

## Dialectal and Advanced Usage

*   **Pronunciation**: `is` is often pronounced [əs]. Before `é`, `í`, `iad`, etc., it combines to sound like [s′e:], [s′i:], etc. This is often written as `sé`, `sí`, `siad`.
    *   *Is é an fear é* → *’Sé an fear é* - He is the man.
*   **Munster**: The form `dob’` or `do` is sometimes used for `b’` or `ba`. The interrogative `an` can appear as `ar` or `ab`. The indirect relative forms often use `gur`, `gurb`, `gurbh` instead of `ar`, `arb`, `arbh`.
*   **Connacht**: Forms like `ní ba`, `an mba`, and `go mba` are common. The subjunctive forms `gura(b)` may appear as `go mba`. Variants of `níos` include `tíos` and `díos`.
*   **Ulster**: The negative particle `cha` or `chan` is used instead of `ní`. Generalised forms like `is é`, `ní hé` are used regardless of the gender or number of the subject.
*   **Conditional Forms**: In Connemara, forms like `go mba` are used for the conditional. In the Standard, these are often replaced by forms with `-r` (e.g., `gur`), which were originally only preterite forms. However, `go mb’fhéidir` (that perhaps) and `go mb’fiú` (that it's worth) are standard.
*   **Prepositional Combinations**: In indirect relative clauses, prepositions can combine with the relative pronoun and the copula, e.g., `lenar` (`le` + `a` + `ar`).
    *   *An fear lenar maith é* - The man who likes it.
*   **Historical Forms**: The future form of the copula, `budh`, is now obsolete. The older spelling `badh` for `ba` may also be encountered in older texts.
*   **Comparative `níos`**: Dialectal variants include `níosa` (Munster), `nas` (Ulster), and `tíos`/`díos` (Connemara).
