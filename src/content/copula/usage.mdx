## Overview

In Irish, a copula is a word that links the subject of a sentence with a predicate, which can be a noun, pronoun, or adjective. While English uses the verb "to be" for this function (e.g., "He *is* a teacher"), Irish distinguishes between two different words: `bí` and `is`.

The verb `bí` (present tense `tá`) is not used to link a subject with a noun. For example, you cannot say *Tá mé dochtúir* for "I am a doctor". Instead, Irish uses a distinct copula, `is`, for this purpose. The copula is not considered a true verb but rather a particle or a defective verb.

- **Correct (Copula):** *Is dochtúir mé.* (I am a doctor.)
- **Incorrect:** *Tá mé dochtúir.*
- **Correct (Adjective):** *Tá mé tinn.* (I am sick.)

It is possible to express the same idea using the verb `bí` by turning the noun into a prepositional phrase, such as *Tá mé i mo dhochtúir* (literally, "I am in my doctorhood").

## Core Properties

The copula follows a unique set of grammatical rules that distinguish it from regular verbs.

### Forms and Tenses

The copula has only two primary forms, which do not conjugate for person or number.

- **`is`**: Used for the present and future tenses. In practice, it is mostly used for present or timeless statements.
- **`ba`**: Used for the past tense (preterite) and the conditional.
- **`gura`**: A rare form used for the subjunctive.

Because the copula lacks an imperative form or a verbal noun, constructions with `bí` are used instead.

- **Imperative:** *Bí i d’fhear!* (Be a man!)
- **Verbal Noun:** *bheith ina fhear* (to be a man)

### Sentence Structure

Copular sentences follow a strict `Copula - Predicate - Subject` word order.

- *Is dochtúir é.* (He is a doctor.)
  - **Copula:** `Is`
  - **Predicate:** `dochtúir`
  - **Subject:** `é`

### Pronoun Usage

When a 3rd person personal pronoun (`sé`, `sí`, `siad`) is the subject of a copular sentence, its disjunctive form (`é`, `í`, `iad`) must be used.

- *Is múinteoir **í**.* (She is a teacher.)
- *Is feirmeoirí **iad**.* (They are farmers.)

### Definite Nouns

A definite noun cannot be placed directly after the copula. Instead, a pronoun (`é`, `í`, or `iad`) is inserted between the copula and the definite noun.

- **Indefinite Predicate:** *Is dochtúir é.* (He is a doctor.)
- **Definite Predicate:** *Is **é** an dochtúir é.* (He is the doctor.)

### Particles and Fusions

Verbal particles like `ní` (not), `an` (question), and `go` (that) fuse with the copula. Often, the copula `is` is implied and omitted after the particle, while `ba` is represented by a change in the particle (e.g., `níor`).

- `ní` = not-is (*Ní maith sin.* - That is not good.)
- `an` = whether-is? (*An maith sin?* - Is that good?)
- `cé` = who-is? (*Cé hé sin?* - Who is that?)

### Mutations

The copula's two forms have different effects on the following word.

- **`is` (Present/Future):** Causes no mutation.
- **`ba` (Past/Conditional):** Causes lenition.
  - *Ba mhaith liom.* (I would like.)
  - *Níor mhaith liom.* (I would not like.)

In the negative past/conditional, the lenition is triggered by the implied `ba` that follows the particle `níor`.

## Primary Functions

The copula is essential for several core grammatical structures in Irish.

### Classification and Identification

This is the most common use of the copula, defining what someone or something is.

- **Classification (indefinite noun):** *Is dochtúir é.* (He is a doctor.)
- **Identification (definite noun):** *Is é an dochtúir é.* (He is the doctor.)

### Emphasis (Cleft Sentences)

The copula is used to create cleft sentences, which emphasise a specific part of the sentence.

- *Is é an fear a dhéanann na bróga.* (It is the man who makes the shoes.)
- *Is i mBaile Átha Cliath a rugadh mé.* (It is in Dublin that I was born.)

### Questions

All WH-questions (questions using words like "who", "what", "where") are fundamentally copular constructions, as they are a type of cleft sentence.

- *Cé a rinne sin?* (Who did that? - literally, "Who is it that did that?")
- *Cá bhfuil tú?* (Where are you? - literally, "Where is it that you are?")

### Possession and Origin

The copula is used with prepositions like `le` (with), `as` (out of), and `de` (from/of) to show possession or origin.

- *Is le Pól an carr.* (The car belongs to Paul.)
- *Is as Corcaigh é.* (He is from Cork.)

### Adjectival Predicates

While `tá` is used with most adjectives describing a state, `is` is used for subjective assessments, comparisons, and certain modal-like phrases.

- **Subjective Assessment:** *Is maith sin!* (That is good!)
- **Equality:** *Is ionann iad.* (They are the same.)
- **Comparatives:** *an fear is mó* (the bigger/biggest man)
- **Modal Phrases:** *Ba mhaith le Pól an carr a cheannach.* (Paul would like to buy the car.)
