## Overview

In Irish, all copular clauses fall into two main types: identification clauses and classification clauses.

**Identification clauses** equate the subject and predicate, describing them as identical (X = Y). This is used when the predicate is definite, like saying "X is *the* Y".

**Classification clauses** assign the subject to a class or group (X is a member of Y). This is used when the predicate is indefinite, like saying "X is *a* Y".

The two clause types have different grammatical structures. The copula itself has very few forms, mainly changing for tense and in positive or negative statements.

## Identification Clauses

An identification clause, or `an abairt ionannais`, equates a subject with a definite predicate. The core structure is "X is *the* Y".

### With a Definite Noun Subject

The standard sentence structure places the predicate before the subject. Because a definite noun cannot immediately follow the copula, a "subpredicate" pronoun (`é`, `í`, or `iad`) is required.

| Form             | Structure                              | Example                          | Translation                   |
|:-----------------|:---------------------------------------|:---------------------------------|:------------------------------|
| Present Positive | `Is é/í/iad` + Predicate + Subject     | *Is é an sagart an fear.*        | The man is the priest.        |
| Present Negative | `Ní hé/hí/hiad` + Predicate + Subject  | *Ní hé an dochtúir an fear.*     | The man is not the doctor.    |
| Past Positive    | `Ba é/í/iad` + Predicate + Subject     | *Ba é an dochtúir an bhean.*     | The woman was the doctor.     |
| Past Negative    | `Níorbh é/í/iad` + Predicate + Subject | *Níorbh é an dochtúir an bhean.* | The woman was not the doctor. |

### With the Subject Before the Predicate

In some cases, the subject can appear before the main predicate. This structure is often used for emphasis or when the predicate is a subordinate clause. The subpredicate (`é/í/iad`) still appears before the subject, maintaining the basic word order.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                              | Example                | Translation             |
|:--------|:---------|:---------------------------------------|:-----------------------|:------------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + `é/í/iad` + Subject + Predicate | *Is é mo bharúil go …* | It is my opinion that…  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + `é/í/iad` + Subject + Predicate | *Ba é mo bharúil go …* | It was my opinion that… |

**The Subpredicate**

The subpredicate `é/í/iad` agrees with the predicate, not the subject, when the predicate is a clause or infinitive.

- *Is é mo bharúil go …* (My opinion is that...)
Here, `é` agrees with the `go`-clause, even though `barúil` (opinion) is feminine.

When the predicate is a noun, the subpredicate usually agrees with the natural gender of the person being discussed.

- *Is í Máire an múinteoir.* (Máire is the teacher.)
Here, `í` is used because Máire is female, even though `múinteoir` is a masculine noun.

**Usage**

This word order is used in several specific situations:

- **For emphasis or definitions**: This structure, known as a fronting sentence, highlights the predicate. In Munster, `ná` (than) or `ach` (but) often appears before the main predicate for extra emphasis.
  - *Is é an rud a chonaic mé ná capall.* (What I saw was a horse.)
  - *Is é ainm an fhearainn sin inniú Liath Mancháin.* (The name of that area is now Lemanaghan.)
- **With `rud` as a subject**: The noun `rud` (thing) is often used as a general pronoun subject. In Connacht, `is é rud` often contracts to `séard`.
  - *Séard a chonaic mé capall.* (What I saw was a horse.)
- **With a subordinate clause as predicate**: When the predicate is a `go/nach` clause or an infinitive, this structure is common.
  - *Is é m’aidhm Gaeilge a thuiscint.* (My goal is to understand Irish.)
  - *Ba é mo chomhairle dó an capall a dhíol.* (My advice to him was to sell the horse.)
- **When the subject is a proper name**: The proper name usually comes before the predicate noun.
  - *Is é Seán an dochtúir.* (Seán is the doctor.)
- **When the subject is a demonstrative pronoun**: The demonstrative (`seo`, `sin`, `siúd`) comes before the predicate.
  - *Is é sin an dochtúir.* (That is the doctor.)

### With a Personal Pronoun Subject

**1st/2nd Person (`mé`, `tú`, `muid`, `sibh`)**

When the subject is a 1st or 2nd person pronoun, it typically comes before the predicate, which is an exception to the usual word order.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                    | Example                | Translation           |
|:--------|:---------|:-----------------------------|:-----------------------|:----------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + pronoun + Predicate   | *Is mé an dochtúir.*   | I am the doctor.      |
|         | Negative | `Ní` + pronoun + Predicate   | *Ní mé an dochtúir.*   | I am not the doctor.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + pronoun + Predicate   | *Ba mé an dochtúir.*   | I was the doctor.     |
|         | Negative | `Níor` + pronoun + Predicate | *Níor mé an dochtúir.* | I was not the doctor. |

**3rd Person (`é`, `í`, `iad`)**

With a 3rd person pronoun as the subject, the pronoun appears twice. The first is the required subpredicate, and the second is the actual subject.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                                | Example               | Translation        |
|:--------|:---------|:-----------------------------------------|:----------------------|:-------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + `é/í/iad` + Predicate + `é/í/iad` | *Is é an dochtúir é.* | He is the doctor.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + `é/í/iad` + Predicate + `é/í/iad` | *Ba é an dochtúir é.* | He was the doctor. |

The subpredicate always agrees with the subject pronoun.

- *Is **í** an dochtúir **í**.* (She is the doctor.)
- *Is **iad** an fhoireann **iad**.* (They are the team.)

### With a Personal Pronoun as Predicate

When a personal pronoun is the predicate, the emphatic form is used (`mise`, `tusa`, `eisean`, etc.). This structure emphasizes the pronoun.

**1st/2nd Person**

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                | Example                | Translation         |
|:--------|:---------|:-------------------------|:-----------------------|:--------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + pronoun + Subject | *Is mise an dochtúir.* | *I* am the doctor.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + pronoun + Subject | *Ba mise an dochtúir.* | *I* was the doctor. |

**3rd Person**

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                | Example                  | Translation          |
|:--------|:---------|:-------------------------|:-------------------------|:---------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + pronoun + Subject | *Is eisean an dochtúir.* | *He* is the doctor.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + pronoun + Subject | *Ba eisean an dochtúir.* | *He* was the doctor. |

## Classification Clauses

A classification clause, or `an abairt aicme`, assigns a subject to a class using an indefinite predicate. The core structure is "X is *a* Y".

### With a Definite Subject

The pronoun `é/í/iad` acts as a "subsubject" (`fo-ainmní`) and can sometimes be omitted, especially in Ulster.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                              | Example                   | Translation           |
|:--------|:---------|:---------------------------------------|:--------------------------|:----------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + Predicate + `é/í/iad` + Subject | *Is dochtúir é an fear.*  | The man is a doctor.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + Predicate + `é/í/iad` + Subject | *Ba dhochtúir é an fear.* | The man was a doctor. |

For emphasis, or commonly in Munster, the predicate can be fronted using `is ea` or `ba ea`.

- *Dochtúir is ea an fear.* (The man is a doctor.)
- *Dochtúir ba ea an fear.* (The man was a doctor.)

### With a Personal Pronoun Subject

The structure is straightforward, with the pronoun following the indefinite predicate.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                  | Example            | Translation     |
|:--------|:---------|:---------------------------|:-------------------|:----------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + Predicate + pronoun | *Is dochtúir mé.*  | I am a doctor.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + Predicate + pronoun | *Ba dhochtúir mé.* | I was a doctor. |

In the past tense, it is more common to use `bí` with the preposition `i`: *Bhí mé i mo dhochtúir* (I was a doctor).

### With an Indefinite Subject

When both the subject and predicate are indefinite, the structure is simple. This is common in proverbs.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                  | Example           | Translation          |
|:--------|:---------|:---------------------------|:------------------|:---------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + Predicate + Subject | *Is iasc bradán.* | A salmon is a fish.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + Predicate + Subject | *B’iasc bradán.*  | A salmon was a fish. |

In many proverbs with this structure, the copula is omitted entirely.

- *Tír gan teanga tír gan anam.* (A country without a language is a country without a soul.)
- *Tosach maith leath na hoibre.* (A good start is half the work.)

## The Copula with Adjectives

### Predicative Adjectives

A copular clause with a predicative adjective is a type of classification clause. It describes a quality of the subject.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                          | Example               | Translation          |
|:--------|:---------|:-----------------------------------|:----------------------|:---------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + Adj + (`é/í/iad`) + Subject | *Is deas é an duine.* | The person is nice.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + Adj + (`é/í/iad`) + Subject | *Ba deas é an duine.* | The person was nice. |

Today, using the copula with adjectives is common in specific contexts:

- **Subjective evaluation**: For exclamatory statements about a quality.
  - *Is deas é sin!* (That is nice!)
  - *Nach dall é?* (Isn't it stupid?)
- **Equality or truth**: With adjectives like `ionann` (equal), `cosúil` (similar), and `fíor` (true).
  - *Is fíor é.* (It is true.)
- **Difficulty or ease**: With adjectives like `deacair` (difficult) and `furasta` (easy), often followed by an infinitive.
  - *Is deacair a rá go…* (It is difficult to say that…)
- **Indefinite quantity**: With adjectives like `iomaí` (many), `beag` (few), and `annamh` (rare).
  - *Is iomaí fear a bhí ann.* (Many a man was there.)
- **Comparatives**: The comparative form of an adjective always requires the copula.
  - *Is fearr mo charr ná do cheannsa.* (My car is better than yours.)
- **Emphasis**: An adverb can be moved to the front of a sentence for emphasis, becoming a predicative adjective with the copula.
  - *Rinne sé an obair go cúramach.* -> *Is cúramach a rinne sé an obair.* (He did the work carefully.)

### The 'Nice Person' Construction

A special structure exists to emphasize an adjective modifying an indefinite noun. It uses the article `an` even though the predicate is indefinite.

| Tense   | Form     | Structure                          | Example               | Translation             |
|:--------|:---------|:-----------------------------------|:----------------------|:------------------------|
| Present | Positive | `Is` + Adj + `an` + Noun + Subject | *Is deas an duine é.* | He is a *nice* person.  |
| Past    | Positive | `Ba` + Adj + `an` + Noun + Subject | *Ba deas an duine é.* | He was a *nice* person. |

This form highlights the adjective. It is common in exclamations and rhetorical questions.

- *Nach fuar an lá é?* (Isn't it a cold day?)
- *Maith an buachaill!* (Good boy!)

### Adjectives with Prepositions

The copula is frequently used with an adjective and a preposition to express feelings, opinions, or obligations, similar to modal verbs in English.

| Concept     | Formula          | Example                        | Translation (Literal)                                     |
|:------------|:-----------------|:-------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------|
| Liking      | `Is maith liom`  | *Is maith liom fuisce a ól.*   | I like to drink whiskey. (Is good with-me...)             |
| Preference  | `Ba mhaith liom` | *Ba mhaith liom fuisce a ól.*  | I would like to drink whiskey. (Would-be good with-me...) |
| Obligation  | `Ba cheart duit` | *Ba cheart duit imeacht.*      | You should leave. (Would-be right for-you...)             |
| Possibility | `Is féidir liom` | *Is féidir liom é a dhéanamh.* | I can do it. (Is possible with-me...)                     |

The choice of preposition is key:

- **`le`** (`liom`, `leat`, etc.) indicates a **subjective** assessment or feeling.
  - *Is fada liom an tsráid.* (I find the street to be long.)
- **`do`** (`dom`, `duit`, etc.) indicates an **objective** statement or something beneficial.
  - *Is maith bainne duit.* (Milk is good for you.)

Many nouns can also be used in this construction, such as `dóigh` (opinion), `cuimhin` (memory), `eagal` (fear), and `cuma` (indifferent).

- *Is dóigh liom go…* (I think that…)
- *Is cuimhin liom é.* (I remember it.)
- *Is cuma liom.* (I don't care.)

## Interrogative Forms

### Yes/No Questions

Yes/no questions are formed with `an` in the present and `ar` in the past.

- Present: *An tú Seán?* (Are you Seán?)
- Past: *Arbh é an dochtúir é?* (Was he the doctor?)

### Wh-Questions

**With `Cé`, `Cad`, `Céard`**

- **`Cé`** = Who is?
  - *Cé hé an múinteoir?* (Who is the teacher?)
- **`Cé` + article** = Which is?
  - *Cé an fear is fearr?* (Which is the better man?)
- **`Cad` + `é/í/iad`** = Which is? / What is? (identification)
  - *Cad é an scéal?* (What is the story?)
- **`Cad`** = What is? (classification)
  - *Cad is crann ann?* (What is a tree?)

**With `Cá`**

The interrogative `cá` can have several meanings with the copula.

- **`Cá`** = Where is?
  - *Cárb as duit?* (Where are you from?)
- **`Cá`** = How is?
  - *Cá haois thú?* (How old are you?)
- **`Cá`** = What is?
  - *Cá hainm atá ort?* (What is your name?)

## The Copula in Subordinate Clauses

### In `go/nach` Clauses

These clauses are used for reported speech and indirect statements.

| Tense   | Positive      | Negative | Example (Positive)                                          |
|:--------|:--------------|:---------|:------------------------------------------------------------|
| Present | `gur`/`gurb`  | `nach`   | *Dúirt sé gur dochtúir é.* (He said that he is a doctor.)   |
| Past    | `gur`/`gurbh` | `nár`    | *Dúirt sé gur dhochtúir é.* (He said that he was a doctor.) |

`Gurb` and `gurbh` are used before vowels with personal pronouns and adjectives.

### In Direct Relative Clauses

The direct relative clause modifies the subject or object of the main clause.

| Tense   | Positive  | Negative      | Example (Positive)                                   |
|:--------|:----------|:--------------|:-----------------------------------------------------|
| Present | `is`      | `nach`        | *an fear is múinteoir* (the man who is a teacher)    |
| Past    | `ba`/`ab` | `nár`/`nárbh` | *an rud ab fhéidir liom* (the thing that I could do) |

`Ab` and `nárbh` are used before words starting with a vowel or `f`.
