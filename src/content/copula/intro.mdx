## Overview

In Irish, there are two verbs for "to be": the copula `is` and the substantive verb `bí` (usually seen as `tá`). The copula is a special linking verb used for classification and identification. It defines what something or someone *is* in a permanent or essential sense.

It connects two nouns or a noun and a pronoun. This is different from `tá`, which describes a temporary state, condition, or location.

| Feature | `is` (The Copula) | `bí` (The Substantive Verb) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Function** | Classification, Identification, Definition | State, Location, Action |
| **Connects** | Noun ⇔ Noun/Pronoun | Noun ⇔ Adjective/Prepositional Phrase |
| **Example** | *Is múinteoir í.* (She is a teacher.) | *Tá sí go maith.* (She is well.) |
| **Example** | *Is é Seán an dochtúir.* (Seán is the doctor.) | *Tá an dochtúir anseo.* (The doctor is here.) |

## Sentence Structure

The copula follows a rigid sentence structure that is different from both English and the standard Irish VSO (Verb-Subject-Object) order. The structure is:

**Copula + Predicate + Subject**

The predicate is the noun that classifies or identifies the subject.

*   *Is fear é.* (Literally: Is a man he.)
*   *Is leabhar é.* (Literally: Is a book it.)
*   *Is mise an múinteoir.* (Literally: Is me the teacher.)

## Forms of the Copula

The copula has very few forms. Its tense is often indicated by particles that come before it. The most common forms are `is` for the present tense and `ba` for the past and conditional.

| Tense/Mood | Affirmative | Negative | Interrogative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `is` | `ní` | `an` |
| **Past** | `ba` | `níor` | `ar` |
| **Conditional** | `ba` | `níor` | `ar` |

### Present Tense Forms

In the present tense, the affirmative form `is` is often omitted, especially in speech. The negative and interrogative forms `ní` and `an` are used instead.

*   *An múinteoir thú?* (Are you a teacher?)
*   *Ní múinteoir mé.* (I am not a teacher.)
*   *(Is) múinteoir mé.* (I am a teacher.) - The `is` is optional here.

### Past and Conditional Forms

The form `ba` is used for both the past tense ("was/were") and the conditional mood ("would be").

*   *Ba dhochtúir maith é.* (He was a good doctor.)
*   *Ba mhaith liom tae.* (I would like tea.)

Before a vowel or `fh` followed by a vowel, `ba` is shortened to `b'`.

*   *B'fhearr liom sin.* (I would prefer that.)
*   *B'álainn an lá é.* (It was a beautiful day.)

The negative and interrogative forms `níor` and `ar` are used for both past and conditional.

*   *Arbh é do dheartháir é?* (Was that your brother?)
*   *Níorbh é mo dheartháir é.* (That was not my brother.)

Note the `bh` is added to `ar` and `níor` before a vowel.

## Core Functions

The copula is used in specific grammatical situations to express identity, classification, or emphasis.

### Classification

Use the copula to state what category something belongs to. This is its most common function.

*   *Is duine mé.* (I am a person.)
*   *Is uirlis é an casúr.* (The hammer is a tool.)
*   *Is glasraí iad na prátaí.* (Potatoes are vegetables.)

### Identification

Use the copula to identify a specific person or thing. In these cases, a pronoun must be inserted between the copula and the subject.

*   *Is é Seán mo chara.* (Seán is my friend.)
*   *Is í an Fhrainc an tír is fearr liom.* (France is the country I like best.)

### Emphasis (Cleft Sentences)

The copula is essential for creating "cleft sentences", which are used to add emphasis to a particular part of a sentence. This structure is extremely common in Irish.

The basic formula is: `Is` + [element to be emphasised] + [relative particle] + [rest of sentence].

*   *Is é Pól a bhris an fhuinneog.* (It was **Pól** who broke the window.)
*   *Is i mBaile Átha Cliath a chónaíonn sí.* (It is in **Dublin** that she lives.)
*   *Is amárach a thiocfaidh an bus.* (It is **tomorrow** that the bus will come.)
