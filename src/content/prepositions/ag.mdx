## Overview
The preposition **ag** primarily denotes location (at), but its most vital role for learners is expressing **possession** and **ongoing action**.

### Initial Mutations
With the article *an*, the noun changes:

- ag an <span class="highlight">m</span>bord *(at the table)*
- ag an <span class="highlight">n</span>doras *(at the door)*

### Semantic Uses
Based on the *Gramadach na Gaeilge* archive, here are the functional uses of ag:

<LogicSection title="Possession & Knowledge">
    - **Possession:** *Tá leabhar agam* (I have a book).
    - **Knowledge:** *Tá Gaeilge aige* (He knows Irish).
    - **Ability:** *Tá snámh aici* (She can swim).
</LogicSection>

<LogicSection title="Spatial & Temporal">
    - **Place:** *Ag an doras* (At the door).
    - **Time:** *Ag am dinnéir* (At dinner time).
    - **Events:** *Ag an rince* (At the dance).
</LogicSection>

<LogicSection title="Advanced Grammatical Functions">
    - **Verbal Noun:** *Ag obair* (Working).
    - **The Partitive:** *Duine acu* (One of them).
    - **Genitive Substitute:** *Mac ag an rí* (A son of the king).
</LogicSection>
