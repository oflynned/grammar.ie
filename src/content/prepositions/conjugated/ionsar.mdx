## Overview

The preposition `ionsar` is a compound form, originating from `d’ionsaí ar`, which literally means "to an approaching on". It is primarily used in Ulster Irish to indicate movement or direction towards a person or place.

Historically, it was also written as `ionns’ar`, with `inseair` being another variant. An older form, `d’ionsaí`, was used without `ar` and governed the genitive case.

### Case and Interrogatives

`Ionsar` governs the dative case.

The corresponding question form is `cé ionsair` or `cá hionsar`, meaning "to whom?" or "to what?".

## Prepositional Pronouns

When combined with pronouns, `ionsar` forms the following contractions:

| Person    | Standard Form | Emphatic Form  | Translation |
|:----------|:--------------|:---------------|:------------|
| I         | `ionsorm`     | `ionsormsa`    | to me       |
| You (sg.) | `ionsort`     | `ionsortsa`    | to you      |
| He        | `ionsair`     | `ionsairsean`  | to him      |
| She       | `ionsuirthi`  | `ionsuirthise` | to her      |
| We        | `ionsorainn`  | `ionsorainne`  | to us       |
| You (pl.) | `ionsoraibh`  | `ionsoraibhse` | to you      |
| They      | `ionsorthu`   | `ionsorthusan` | to them     |

## Usage and Function

`Ionsar` is used to express direction towards a target.

*   *Druid ionsar* - to turn towards
*   *Tar ionsar an teach* - to come to the house
*   *Ionsar theach Bhriain* - to Brian's house
