## Overview

The preposition `le` primarily means "with". It is a merger of the Old Irish prepositions *le* and *re*. In the Munster dialect, it is often pronounced and written as `lé`.

Like most simple prepositions, `le` governs the dative case. When combined with the definite article `an` or `na`, it becomes `leis an` and `leis na` respectively.

### Interrogatives

The question forms involving `le` are:

- **With whom?**: `cé leis?`
- **With what?**: `cad leis?`, `cé leis?`, or `cá leis?`
- **Whose is...?**: `cé leis?` (used in a copular sentence)

## Conjugated Forms

`le` combines with pronouns to form a single word.

### Personal Pronouns

| Person   | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | English   |
|:---------|:--------------|:--------------|:----------|
| I        | `liom`        | `liomsa`      | with me   |
| you (sg) | `leat`        | `leatsa`      | with you  |
| he       | `leis`        | `leis-sean`   | with him  |
| she      | `léi`         | `léise`       | with her  |
| we       | `linn`        | `linne`       | with us   |
| you (pl) | `libh`        | `libhse`      | with you  |
| they     | `leo`         | `leosan`      | with them |

### Possessive Pronouns

| Person    | Standard Form   | English    | Mutation |
|:----------|:----------------|:-----------|:---------|
| my        | `le mo` (`lem`) | with my    | Lenition |
| your (sg) | `le do` (`led`) | with your  | Lenition |
| his       | `lena`          | with his   | Lenition |
| her       | `lena`          | with her   | None     |
| our       | `lenár`         | with our   | Eclipsis |
| your (pl) | `le bhur`       | with your  | Eclipsis |
| their     | `lena`          | with their | Eclipsis |

The forms `lem` and `led` are common in Munster. Note that the three forms of `lena` are distinguished by the initial mutation they cause on the following noun.

### Relative Pronouns

| Tense   | Relative Pronoun Form | Copula Form         | English         |
|:--------|:----------------------|:--------------------|:----------------|
| Present | `lena` (+ Eclipsis)   | `lenar` / `lenarb`  | with which/whom |
| Past    | `lenar` (+ Lenition)  | `lenar` / `lenarbh` | with which/whom |

These forms are used when the preposition comes before the relative pronoun, as in *an bhean lena dtéim* (the woman with whom I go). It is more common, however, to leave the preposition at the end of the clause: *an bhean a dtéim léi*.

## Core Functions

### Accompaniment and Instrument

`le` is used to indicate who or what accompanies an action.

- **Accompaniment**: *le Máirtín* (with Martin)
- **Instrument**: *le spáid* (with a spade)
- **Motive Force**: *le gaoth* (with the wind)

### Possession and Agency

`le` is fundamental in expressing ownership and authorship, often with the copula.

- **Ownership**: *Is le Cáit an teach* (The house belongs to Cáit).
- **Partitive Possession**: *cara liom* (a friend of mine), *iníon leis* (a daughter of his).
- **Agency/Authorship**: *"Ulysses" le James Joyce* ("Ulysses" by James Joyce).
- **Paternity**: *triúr clainne leis* (three children by him).

### Subjective States and Opinions

Many common expressions for feelings, opinions, and preferences are formed with the copula, an adjective, and `le`.

| Concept         | Formula        | Example                             | Translation (Literal)  |
|:----------------|:---------------|:------------------------------------|:-----------------------|
| To like         | `Is maith le`  | *Is maith liom tae.*                | is good with me        |
| To prefer       | `Is fearr le`  | *Is fearr liom caife.*              | is better with me      |
| To hate         | `Is fuath le`  | *Is fuath liom an aimsir.*          | is hate with me        |
| To want         | `Ba mhaith le` | *Ba mhaith liom deoch.*             | would be good with me  |
| To think        | `Is dóigh le`  | *Is dóigh liom go bhfuil sé ceart.* | is a thought with me   |
| To be surprised | `Is ionadh le` | *Is ionadh liom an scéal.*          | is a surprise with me  |
| To not care     | `Is cuma le`   | *Is cuma liom faoi.*                | is indifferent with me |

It can also express subjective experience:
- *Is fada liom an lá.* (I find the day long.)
- *Is minic liom a bheith anseo.* (I am often here.)

## Spatiotemporal Usage

### Time

`le` can denote both a point in time and a duration.

- **Point in Time**: *leis an lá* (at daybreak), *leis an mí* (at the beginning of the month).
- **Duration**: *le seachtain* (for a week), *le fada* (for a long time).

### Location and Motion

`le` is used to describe proximity, direction, and movement along a surface.

- **Position**: *leis an mballa* (against the wall), *in aice le* (next to).
- **Motion Along**: *d'éirigh sé le balla* (he climbed up a wall).
- **Direction Towards**: *luí le grian* (to lie in the sun), *tá súil agam le...* (I hope for...).
- **Manner of Movement**: *bhí siúl géar leis* (he had a brisk pace).

## Abstract Concepts

### Cause and Purpose

`le` can explain the reason for a state or the purpose of an action.

- **Cause of State**: *leis an tuirse* (from tiredness), *le hól* (from drinking), *le greann* (for fun).
- **Purpose (with Verbal Noun)**: *le balla a phéinteáil* (to paint a wall).
- **Obligation**: *scéal le hinsint* (a story to tell), *neart le n-ól* (plenty to drink).

### Comparison

`le` is used in comparative structures.

- **As... as**: *Tá sé chomh mór leat* (He is as big as you).
- **In comparison with**: *i gcomparáid le mo dheartháir* (in comparison with my brother).

### Behaviour and Communication

`le` often appears in phrases describing interactions.

- **Behaviour towards**: *bí deas le do dheirfiúr* (be nice to your sister).
- **Communication**: *labhair le Seán* (speak with Seán), *éist leis an raidió* (listen to the radio).

## Common Phrases and Idioms

`le` is a component of many fixed phrases and verbal idioms.

- **Ability**: *Is féidir liom snámh* (I can swim).
- **Agreement**: *Aontaím leat* (I agree with you).
- **Continuation**: *Oibrigh leat!* (Work away!), *D'imigh sí léi* (She went off).
- **Turn-taking**: *Is leatsa anois é* (It's your turn now).
- **To take/bring**: *An bhfuil do mhála leat?* (Do you have your bag with you?).
- **Regarding**: *maidir leis an gcruinniú* (regarding the meeting).
- **Together with**: *in éineacht le mo chairde* (together with my friends).
- **Success**: *D'éirigh liom sa scrúdú* (I succeeded in the exam).

### Adverbial Use

In Munster, `leis` is often used as an adverb meaning "also" or "too", in place of `freisin` or `fosta`.
- *Bhí mé ann, leis.* (I was there, too.)
