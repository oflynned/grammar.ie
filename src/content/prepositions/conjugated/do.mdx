## Overview

The preposition `do` governs the dative case.

In most dialects, `do` and `de` are pronounced identically: as `[gə]` in Connacht and `[də]` elsewhere. The Connacht pronunciation is distinguished from the preposition `go` by the lenition that follows `do`.

Before a vowel or `fh`, `do` contracts to `d’`, as in *d’Úna* (to Úna) or *d’fhear* (to a man).

### Forms and Interrogatives

| Type                    | Form      | Translation       |
|-------------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| With singular article   | `don`     | to the            |
| With plural article     | `do na`   | to the            |
| Interrogative (person)  | `cé dó?`  | to whom?          |
| Interrogative (purpose) | `cad dó?` | for what purpose? |

### Conjugated Forms

| Person      | Standard | Emphatic    |
|-------------|----------|-------------|
| 1st sg.     | `dom`    | `domhsa`    |
| 2nd sg.     | `duit`   | `duitse`    |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `dó`     | `dósan`     |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `di`     | `dise`      |
| 1st pl.     | `dúinn`  | `dúinne`    |
| 2nd pl.     | `daoibh` | `daoibhse`  |
| 3rd pl.     | `dóibh`  | `dóibhsean` |

### Combinations with Pronouns

| Type                     | Combination        | Translation      |
|--------------------------|--------------------|------------------|
| Possessive (1st sg.)     | `do mo` (or `dom`) | to my            |
| Possessive (2nd sg.)     | `do do` (or `dod`) | to your          |
| Possessive (3rd sg./pl.) | `dá`               | to his/her/their |
| Possessive (1st pl.)     | `dár`              | to our           |
| Possessive (2nd pl.)     | `do bhur`          | to your (pl.)    |
| Indirect Relative        | `dá` / `dár`       | to which         |

Dialectal variations are common. In Ulster, one might hear `damh` instead of `dom`. In Connemara, short forms like `’om` (for `dom`) and `’uit` (for `duit`) are used.

## Core Functions

### Indirect Object

`Do` marks the indirect object of a verb, indicating the person or thing affected by an action. This is often translated as "to" or "for".

- *Scríobh mé litir duit.* (I wrote you a letter.)
- *Thug sé an leabhar dom.* (He gave the book to me.)
- *Inis an scéal dóibh.* (Tell them the story.)

When an action benefits someone, `do` is used. When it disadvantages them, `ar` is used instead.

- *Rinne sé obair an tí dom.* (He did the housework for me.)
- *Bhris sé an cupán orm.* (He broke the cup on me.)

### Spatial and Temporal Use

While less common than `chun` or `chuig` for spatial goals, `do` can indicate direction. It is also used when telling time.

- *Téigh don siopa.* (Go to the shop.)
- *Don diabhal leat!* (To hell with you!)
- *Tá sé ceathrú don a trí.* (It is a quarter to three.)

## Possession and Relationship

`Do` is used to express relationship, proximity, and a type of partitive possession ("a friend of mine").

- *Is cara dom é.* (He is a friend of mine.)
- *Sin mac di.* (That is a son of hers.)
- *Tá an teach cóngarach don scoil.* (The house is near to the school.)
- *Ba mhaith liom seomra dom féin.* (I would like a room for myself.)

## Copular and Modal Constructions

### Subject in Copular Sentences

`Do` often indicates the subject in certain common questions and statements with the copula.

- *Cad is ainm duit?* (What is your name?)
- *Séamas is ainm dom.* (My name is Séamas.)
- *Cá haois duit?* (How old are you?)
- *Cárb as duit?* (Where are you from?)

### Modal Expressions of Obligation

`Do` is a key component in phrases that express necessity or obligation, functioning like modal verbs such as "must" or "should".

| Concept           | Formula        | Example                                | Translation                 |
|-------------------|----------------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Must / Have to    | `is éigean do` | *Is éigean dom imeacht.*               | I have to go.               |
| Must / Have to    | `ní mór do`    | *Ní mór duit éisteacht.*               | You must listen.            |
| Should / Ought to | `is ceart do`  | *Is ceart dóibh an fhírinne a insint.* | They should tell the truth. |
| Should / Ought to | `ba chóir do`  | *Ba chóir daoibh a bheith anseo.*      | You (pl.) should be here.   |

### With Adjectives

`Do` can be used with adjectives in copular sentences to describe how something affects a person.

- *Is breá liom an aimsir.* (The weather is fine for me / I love the weather.)
- *Ní gá duit a bheith buartha.* (It is not necessary for you to be worried.)
- *Is fiú duit an leabhar sin a léamh.* (It is worthwhile for you to read that book.)

## Verbal Noun Constructions

### Agent Marking

`Do` is used to mark the agent (the person performing the action) in certain verbal noun phrases, particularly those involving time.

| Concept  | Formula                       | Example                     | Translation (Literal)   |
|----------|-------------------------------|-----------------------------|-------------------------|
| After X  | `tar éis` + VN + `do` + Agent | *tar éis é a dhéanamh dom*  | after its doing to-me   |
| While X  | `le linn` + VN + `do` + Agent | *le linn é a dhéanamh duit* | during its doing to-you |
| Before X | `roimh` + VN + `do` + Agent   | *roimh é a dhéanamh dó*     | before its doing to-him |

### Progressive Aspect

In the progressive (continuous) aspect, `do` + a possessive pronoun is used to represent the object of the verb.

- *Tá mé do do bhualadh.* (I am hitting you.)
- *Tá sí dá ghortú.* (She is hurting him.)
- *Tá mé do mo bhualadh aige.* (I am being hit by him.)

In Munster, `do` is sometimes used instead of the modern short form `a` in infinitive constructions, as in *chun Gaeilge d’fhoghlaim* (to learn Irish).
