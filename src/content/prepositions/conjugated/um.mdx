## Overview

The preposition `um` generally means 'around' or 'about'. While present in the standard language, its use is most common in Munster Irish. In other dialects, prepositions like `faoi` often take its place, stemming from a historical development of `um` to `fá`, which then became associated with `faoi`.

`Um` governs the dative case.

### Conjugated Forms

When combined with personal pronouns, `um` has the following forms:

| Person    | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | English       |
|:----------|:--------------|:--------------|:--------------|
| I         | `umam`        | `umamsa`      | around me     |
| You (sg.) | `umat`        | `umatsa`      | around you    |
| He/It     | `uime`        | `uimesean`    | around him/it |
| She       | `uimpi`       | `uimpise`     | around her    |
| We        | `umainn`      | `umainne`     | around us     |
| You (pl.) | `umaibh`      | `umaibhse`    | around you    |
| They      | `umpu`        | `umpusan`     | around them   |

### Interrogative Forms

- `cé uime` - around whom...?
- `cad uime` / `cá huime` - why...?

## Core Functions

### Temporal Usage

`Um` is used to specify a point in time, such as a holiday, a time of day, or a general period. It is not used for vague time references.

- *um Nollaig* - at Christmas
- *um thráthnóna* - in the afternoon
- *um an am sin* - around that time

### Spatial Usage

The primary meaning of `um` is to describe a location 'around', 'along', or 'against' something.

- *D’iaigh siad um an rí.* - They closed in around the king.
- *um Shionainn* - along the Shannon
- *um an gcoirthe* - against the stone

## Idiomatic and Formal Usage

### Accompaniment and Attire

`Um` can describe something being physically around a person, either as company or as clothing.

- *a bhéirt mhac uime* - his two sons accompanying him (literally, 'his two sons around him')
- *Chuir sí an cóta uimpi.* - She put on the coat.

### Cause and Reason

The conjugated form `uime` is central to expressing reason.

- `is uime` - therefore, for that reason
- `fáth um` (+ indirect relative clause) - the reason why
- *Cén fáth (um) a bhfuil tú anseo?* - Why are you here?

### Abstract Relations

In more abstract or formal contexts, `um` can mean 'for', 'at', or 'concerning'. This is common in the titles of official acts and organisations.

- `ag fonóid um` - to laugh at
- `grá um` - love for
- *An tAcht um Chosaint Sonraí* - The Data Protection Act
- *An tÚdarás um Ard-Oideachas* - The Higher Education Authority

### Common Substitutes

Note that `um` is not used to express 'in order to'. For this purpose, the prepositions `le` or `chun` are used instead.
