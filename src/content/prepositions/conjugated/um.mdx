## Overview

*Um* is primarily used in Munster (Muskerry) and Standard Irish. Northern dialects often use other prepositions in its place.

Due to historical shifts (*um > má > fá*), *faoi* is often used today for "about" (e.g., *labhairt fúm* - speaking about me).

### Forms

| Pronoun      | Form   | Contrast Form | Translation            |
|:-------------|:-------|:--------------|:-----------------------|
| **I**        | umam   | umamsa        | around me              |
| **You**      | umat   | umatsa        | around you             |
| **He/It**    | uime   | uimesean      | around him / therefore |
| **She**      | uimpi  | uimpise       | around her             |
| **We**       | umainn | umainne       | around us              |
| **You (pl)** | umaibh | umaibhse      | around you             |
| **They**     | umpu   | umpusan       | around them            |

* **General Rule (Limited Lenition)** — *um banríon* — around a queen — Without the article, *um* causes lenition, but **only** on *c, d, f, g, s, t*. It does **not** lenite *b, m, p*.
* **With the Article** — *um an mbaile* — around the town — *Um* generally causes **eclipsis**.
  * **Munster Dialect**: Eclipses *d* and *t* as well.
  * **Connacht Dialect**: Adds a **t-prefix** to feminine nouns starting with *s* (e.g., *um an tsúil*).
  * **Ulster Dialect**: Uses **lenition** instead of eclipsis (e.g., *um an fhear*).

## Lenition Exceptions
Unlike most prepositions that lenite, *um* is selective.

* **Lenites:** c, d, f, g, s, t.
* **Does NOT Lenite:** b, m, p.
* *Example:* *um banríon* (no change to 'b').

| **Cad uime** | About what / Why? |                    |         |

## Physical World

### Clothing & Space
* **Putting on Clothes** — *cuir um* — to put on / dress (lit. "put around").
  * *Chuir sí an cóta uimpi* — She put the coat on (around her).
* **Surrounding** — *d’iaigh siad um an rí* — they closed in around the king.
* **Accompaniment** — *a bhéirt mhac uime* — his two sons around him (accompanying him).
* **Location** — *um Shionainn* — along the Shannon.
  * *um an gcoirthe* — against the stone.

## Abstract Usage

### Time
* **Specific Time** — *um Nollaig* — at Christmas.
  * *um thráthnóna* — in the afternoon.
  * *um an am sin* — at that time.

> **Note:** *Um* is used for specific time points ("at"), not for vague approximations like "around Christmas time" in English/German.

### Official & Formal Use
* **Official Titles** — *um* is frequently used in legal and official titles for "regarding" or "for".
  * *An tÚdarás um Ard-Oideachas* — Higher Education Authority.
  * *An tAcht um Chosaint Sonraí* — Data Protection Act.

### Reason & Emotion
* **Reason** — *is uime* — therefore / that is why.
  * *Cén fáth (um) a bhfuil tú anseo?* — Why are you here?
* **Emotion/Action** — *ag fonóid um* — laughing at / mocking.
  * *grá um* — love for.
