`Roimh` is a preposition that generally means "before". It is usually pronounced [riv′].

## Forms and Case

`Roimh` requires the dative case. In some parts of Munster, it can unconventionally be followed by the genitive, as in *roimis na Nollag* (before Christmas).

Dialectal variants include `roimhe` in Ulster, `roim` in Munster, and `roimis` before the article.

### Conjugated Forms

Like many Irish prepositions, `roimh` combines with personal pronouns to form a single word.

| Person    | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | English     |
|:----------|:--------------|:--------------|:------------|
| I         | `romham`      | `romhamsa`    | before me   |
| You (sg.) | `romhat`      | `romhatsa`    | before you  |
| He        | `roimhe`      | `roimhesean`  | before him  |
| She       | `roimpi`      | `roimpise`    | before her  |
| We        | `romhainn`    | `romhainne`   | before us   |
| You (pl.) | `romhaibh`    | `romhaibhse`  | before you  |
| They      | `rompu`       | `rompusan`    | before them |

In Munster, `roimis` is sometimes used instead of `roimhe`.

### Question Forms

- **Cé roimhe...?**: Before whom...?
- **Cad roimhe / Cé roimhe / Cá roimhe...?**: Before what...?

These question forms require an indirect relative clause.

### Dialectal Rules with the Article

- **Munster**: Eclipsis applies to all consonants, including `d` and `t`. For example, *roimis an ndalta* (before the student).
- **Connacht**: T-prothesis occurs before feminine singular nouns beginning with `s`. For example, *roimh an tsúil* (before the eye).
- **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis. For example, *roimh an bhus* (before the bus).

## Usage and Functions

### Temporal and Spatial Meaning

The primary use of `roimh` is to indicate something happening earlier in time.

- *roimh mhaidin* - before the morning
- *Tá mé roimhe ann.* - I am there before him.

It can also mean "before" in a spatial sense, but this is less common. The phrase `os comhair` is more frequently used for spatial positioning.

### Idiomatic Expressions

`Roimh` is used in several common idioms, particularly to express fear and welcome.

| Concept | Formula                        | Example                           | Translation (Literal)                                   |
|:--------|:-------------------------------|:----------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------|
| Fear of | Tá faitíos agam roimh + [noun] | *Tá faitíos agam roimh an madra.* | I am afraid of the dog. (Is fear at-me before the dog.) |
| Welcome | Tá fáilte agam roimh + [noun]  | *Tá fáilte agam romhat.*          | I welcome you. (Is welcome at-me before-you.)           |

It also appears with certain verbs:

- **tar roimh / téigh roimh**: to intercept, stop, prevent, or interrupt.
