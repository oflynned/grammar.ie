## Overview

### Forms

| Pronoun      | Form     | Contrast Form | Translation |
|:-------------|:---------|:--------------|:------------|
| **I**        | tharam   | tharamsa      | past me     |
| **You**      | tharat   | tharatsa      | past you    |
| **He**       | thairis  | thairis-sean  | past him    |
| **She**      | thairti  | thairtise     | past her    |
| **We**       | tharainn | tharainne     | past us     |
| **You (pl)** | tharaibh | tharaibhse    | past you    |
| **They**     | thartu   | thartusan     | past them   |

`Thart` means "past" or "over" in a general sense (e.g., *tá an t-am thart* - the time is past).

### Mutations

* **General Rule** — *thar bhosca* — over a box — Without the article, *thar* causes **lenition**.
  * **Exception:** No lenition occurs with general abstract terms (e.g., *thar muir* - overseas).
* **With the Article** — *thar an mbosca* — over the box — *Thar* generally causes **eclipsis** (except on d, t) when used with the article.
  * **Munster Dialect**: Eclipses *d* and *t* as well.
  * **Connacht Dialect**: Adds a **t-prefix** to feminine nouns starting with *s* (e.g., *thar an tsúil*).
  * **Ulster Dialect**: Uses **lenition** instead of eclipsis (e.g., *thar an bhosca*).

## Thar vs. Thart
* **Thar** is the preposition used with an object (e.g., *thar an teach* - past the house).
* **Thart** is the adverbial form used without an object (e.g., *chuaigh sé thart* - he went past).

---

## Physical World

### Movement & Position
* **Horizontal** — *thar an dteach* — past the house.
* **Vertical** — *thar an mbosca* — over the box.
* **Overseas** — *thar muir* — overseas (lit. over sea).

---

## Abstract Usage

### Time & Limits
* **Time** — *bhí sé thar am agat* — it was high time for you (lit. "it was over time at you").
* **Quantity** — *thar bhliain* — over a year / more than a year.
* **Excess** — *d’ól sé braon thar an gceart* — he drank a drop too much (lit. "over the right amount").

### Comparison & Superlatives
* **Comparison** — *tharas aon duine eile* — more than anyone else.
  * *thar mar a shíl mé* — more than I thought.
* **Superlative State** — *thar a bheith go maith* — extraordinarily good (lit. "over being good").

### Figurative ("Despite")
* **Ignoring/Despite** — *thar chomhairle a athair* — despite/ignoring his father's advice (lit. "over the advice of his father").
