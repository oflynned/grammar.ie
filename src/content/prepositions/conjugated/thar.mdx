## Overview

The preposition `thar` governs the dative case. It is used to express concepts like "over", "past", and "beyond".

It combines with personal pronouns to form a single word, as shown in the table below.

| Person   | Standard Form | Emphatic Form  | Translation |
|:---------|:--------------|:---------------|:------------|
| I        | `tharam`      | `tharamsa`     | past me     |
| You (sg) | `tharat`      | `tharatsa`     | past you    |
| He       | `thairis`     | `thairis-sean` | past him    |
| She      | `thairti`     | `thairtise`    | past her    |
| We       | `tharainn`    | `tharainne`    | past us     |
| You (pl) | `tharaibh`    | `tharaibhse`   | past you    |
| They     | `thartu`      | `thartusan`    | past them   |
| It       | `thart`       | -              | past it     |

The form `thart` is also used adverbially to mean "past" or "over".

### Interrogatives

To ask questions using `thar`, the following forms are used, typically followed by an indirect relative clause.

*   **Cé thart?** - Past whom?
*   **Cad thart / Cé thart / Cá thart?** - Past what?

### Dialectal Variations

The mutation following `thar an` varies between the major dialects.

*   **Munster**: Eclipsis is applied to all eligible consonants, including `d` and `t`.
*   **Connacht**: T-prothesis occurs before feminine nouns beginning with `s-`. For example, `thar an tsúil` (past the eye).
*   **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis. For example, `thar an bhosca` (over the box).

## Functions and Usage

### Spatial and Temporal Movement

`Thar` is used to describe movement towards and beyond a point, both physically and in time.

*   **Spatial (over/past)**: It can describe horizontal or vertical movement.
    *   *Chuaigh sé thar an teach.* - He went past the house.
    *   *Léim an madra thar an mbosca.* - The dog jumped over the box.
*   **Temporal (beyond)**: It can indicate that a point in time has passed.
    *   *Bhí sé thar am agat.* - It was high time for you (lit. it was beyond the time at you).

### Abstract and Figurative Use

The preposition can also be used figuratively to mean "beyond", "against", or "more than".

*   **Figurative (despite/against)**: It can express that something happened in spite of an obstacle.
    *   *Rinne sé é thar chomhairle a athar.* - He did it despite his father's advice.
*   **Quantity (more than/over)**: It is used to specify a quantity that has been exceeded.
    *   *Tá sé anseo thar bhliain.* - He has been here for over a year.
    *   *D’ól sé braon thar an gceart.* - He drank a drop too much (lit. a drop beyond what is right).

### Comparative Expressions

`Thar` is a key component in forming certain types of comparisons, often meaning "more than".

*   *Tá an scannán thar a bheith go maith.* - The film is more than good.
*   *Bhí sé níos fearr thar mar a shíl mé.* - It was better than I thought.
*   *Is breá liom í tharas aon duine eile.* - I love her more than anyone else.
