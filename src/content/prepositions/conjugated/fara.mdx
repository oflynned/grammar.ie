## Overview

The preposition `fara` is a variant form of `ar` (from Old Irish *for*). Other historical and dialectal forms include `fré`, `fre`, `farae`, `fros`, and `frois`.

Today, `fara` is rare in common speech but can be heard in Munster, particularly in Kerry. It is more frequently encountered in formal writing, such as legal texts and the Irish Bible. A common alternative in modern Irish is `in éineacht le`. In Cois Fhairrge, the forms `fros` and `frois` are used.

### Conjugated Forms

Like many Irish prepositions, `fara` combines with personal pronouns to create a single word.

| Pronoun   | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Translation |
|:----------|:--------------|:--------------|:------------|
| I         | `faram`       | `faramsa`     | with me     |
| You (sg.) | `farat`       | `faratsa`     | with you    |
| He        | `fairis`      | `fairis-san`  | with him    |
| She       | `farae`       | `faraese`     | with her    |
| We        | `farainn`     | `farainne`    | with us     |
| You (pl.) | `faraibh`     | `faraibhse`   | with you    |
| They      | `faru`        | `farusan`     | with them   |

### Dialectal Forms

*   An alternative structure, `fara liom`, `fara leat`, etc., is also used.
*   In Cois Fhairrge, forms of `fros` are used: `frum`, `frat`, `frois`, `frae`, `fruinn`, `fraoib`, `frób`. The structure `fru liom`, `fru leat` is also found.
*   In Munster, `fara` can combine with possessive pronouns, e.g., `farana` (with his).

## Grammatical Rules

### Case

`Fara` governs the dative case.

### Mutations

`Fara` does not cause any initial mutation (lenition or eclipsis) on the following noun, regardless of whether the definite article is present.

### Forms with the Article

When followed by the definite article `an` or `na`, `fara` changes form:

*   `fara` + `an` becomes `fairis an` (or `farais an`).
*   `fara` + `na` becomes `fairis na` (or `farais na`).

## Core Functions

### Accompaniment

The primary meaning of `fara` is "with" or "in the company of".

*   *Bhí Mícheál faru.* - Michael was with them.
*   *Buachaill amháin atá fanta faram anso sa bhaile.* - One boy has stayed with me here in the locality.
*   *Ná suífeá farainn?* - Won't you sit with us?

> *Tháinig siad chun Eoin und dúirt siad leis: "A raibí, an té a bhí farat lastall den Iordáin, an té ar a ndearna tú fianaise, féach, tá seisean ag déanamh baiste und tá na daoine go léir ag dul chuige."*
>
> And they came unto John, and said unto him, Rabbi, he that was with thee beyond Jordan, to whom thou barest witness, behold, the same baptizeth, and all men come to him. (John 3:26)

### Addition and Comparison

`Fara` can also express the idea of "as well as", "besides", or "in comparison to".

*   *fara cabhair Dé* - so help me God.
*   *punt fara do cheart* - a pound more than you are due (literally, "a pound besides your right").
*   *is beag é fara a bhfuair tusa* - it is little compared with what you got.
*   The phrase `fara a bheith...` means "instead of being...".

## Etymology

### The Origin of Freisin

The common Irish word for "also" or "too", `freisin`, evolved from a form of `fara`. The development was:

`fairis sin` (besides that) > `freis sin` > `freisin`
