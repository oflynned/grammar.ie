## Overview

The preposition `chuig` and its variant `chun` both indicate "to" or "towards". Their usage depends on dialect and the grammatical case they govern.

Historically, `chun` derives from the Old Irish verbal noun `dochum`, meaning "a stepping towards", which explains its requirement for the genitive case. The form `chuig` is a back-formation from the 3rd person conjugated form, `chuige`.

### Case Requirements

The choice between `chun` and `chuig` affects the case of the following noun.

- **chun**: Requires the **genitive case**. This form is more common in Munster and the official standard.
- **chuig**: Requires the **dative case** (which is identical to the nominative for most nouns). This form is common in Connacht and Ulster. In the dialect of Clare, `chuig` historically required the genitive.

### Question Forms

The preposition is used to form several common interrogatives.

- **Cé chuige?**: To whom?
- **Cad chuige? / Cá chuige?**: Why?
- **Tuige?**: A common contraction of `cad chuige` in Connacht and Ulster, also meaning "Why?".

## Conjugation and Mutations

Like all prepositions, `chuig` combines with personal pronouns to form a single word. It also causes initial mutations on the noun that follows it, with rules that differ between `chuig` and `chun`.

### Conjugated Forms

| Person      | Standard Form | Contrastive Form | Translation |
|:------------|:--------------|:-----------------|:------------|
| 1st sg.     | `chugam`      | `chugamsa`       | to me       |
| 2nd sg.     | `chugat`      | `chugatsa`       | to you      |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `chuige`      | `chuigesean`     | to him      |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `chuici`      | `chuicise`       | to her      |
| 1st pl.     | `chugainn`    | `chugainne`      | to us       |
| 2nd pl.     | `chugaibh`    | `chugaibhse`     | to you      |
| 3rd pl.     | `chucu`       | `chucusan`       | to them     |

- **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis (e.g., *chuig an chailín*).

## Usage and Functions

`Chuig` and `chun` have a wide range of uses, from indicating physical motion to expressing abstract concepts like purpose and time.

### Motion and Direction

The primary function is to indicate movement towards a person, place, or state.

- *Scríobh chugam.* (Write to me.)
- *Dul chuig an dochtúir.* (To go to the doctor.)
- *Chun na Fraince.* (To France.)
- *Cuir chun báis.* (To put to death.)
- *Dul chun olcais.* (To go to the bad.)

### Purpose and Reason

It can be used to explain the reason for an action or the purpose of an object.

- *Rinne muid chuig glóire Dé é.* (We did it for the glory of God.)

When used with a verbal noun, it expresses intention or purpose, similar to "in order to". In this structure, `chun` does not require the genitive.

| Concept            | Formula            | Example                    | Translation (Literal) |
|:-------------------|:-------------------|:---------------------------|:----------------------|
| Expressing Purpose | chun + verbal noun | *Chun teanga a fhoghlaim.* | To learn a language.  |

### Temporal Expressions

`Chuig` and `chun` are used in various time-related phrases.

- **Until/Before**: *Chun gur ghlaoigh an coileach.* (Before the cock crowed.)
- **Telling Time**: *Ceathrú chun a dó.* (A quarter to two.)
- **Approaching Time**: *Tá sé chun a dó.* (It is almost 2 o'clock.)
- **Future Events**: *Dé Luan seo chugainn.* (Next Monday; "this Monday to-us".)

### State and Proximity

It can describe proximity or a state of readiness.

- **Proximity**:
    - *Chun na gaoithe.* (Close to the wind.)
    - *Chun mo laimhe.* (Close to my hand.)
- **Readiness/Suitability**:
    - *Tá an féar chun bainte.* (The grass is ready to be mown.)
    - *Fear chun cainte.* (A man for talking.)
    - *Lá chun taistil.* (A day for travelling.)

### Idiomatic Expressions

- **chuige**: Used as an adverb to mean "at all".
- **chugat leat**: An idiom meaning "be off with you!".

## Dialectal Variations

Significant differences exist in the pronunciation and usage of `chuig` and `chun` across the main dialects.

### Pronunciation of Chun

- **Munster**: Pronounced [xun] or [xun′], sometimes spelled `chuin`.
- **Connacht/Ulster**: Often reduced to [un], [ən], or just [ə]. This leads to the spelling `’un`, as in *’un an aonaigh* (to the fair). In Ulster, `chun an` can merge to `’na`, as in *’na bhaile* (home).

### Alternative Forms

- **Connacht/Ulster**: The preposition `ag` is often used as a substitute for `chuig`. The conjugated forms may appear with a prefixed 'h', such as `hagam` instead of `chugam`.
- **Munster**: The conjugated forms are often lenited, e.g., `chugham`, `chughat`, `chuichi`. The 3rd person masculine `chuige` is an exception and is not lenited.
