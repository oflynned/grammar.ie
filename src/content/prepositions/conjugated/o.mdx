## Overview

The preposition `ó` primarily means "from". It governs the dative case.

When combined with the definite article, `ó` merges to form new words:
*   `ó` + `an` becomes `ón`
*   `ó` + `na` remains `ó na`

### Conjugated Forms

Like most prepositions in Irish, `ó` combines with personal pronouns to form a single word.

| Pronoun   | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Translation    |
|:----------|:--------------|:--------------|:---------------|
| I         | `uaim`        | `uaimse`      | from me        |
| You       | `uait`        | `uaitse`      | from you       |
| He        | `uaidh`       | `uaidhsean`   | from him       |
| She       | `uaithi`      | `uaithise`    | from her       |
| We        | `uainn`       | `uainne`      | from us        |
| You (pl.) | `uaibh`       | `uaibhse`     | from you (pl.) |
| They      | `uathu`       | `uathusan`    | from them      |

### Question Forms

*   `cé uaidh`? - from whom?
*   `cad uaidh` / `cé uaidh` / `cá huaidh`? - from what?

### Dialectal Variations with `ón`

The mutation following `ón` can vary by dialect:

*   **Munster**: Eclipsis occurs as standard, but also applies to `d` and `t`.
*   **Connacht**: T-prothesis occurs before feminine nouns beginning with `s`. For example, *ón tsúil* (from the eye).
*   **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis. For example, *ón Ghearmáin* (from Germany).

## Core Functions

### Spatial and Temporal Origin

`Ó` indicates a starting point in space or time.

*   **Spatial**: *Tar ón bposta.* - Come from the post office.
*   **Distance**: *Míle uaidh.* - A mile away from it.
*   **Temporal**: *Ón Nollaig.* - Since Christmas.
*   **Temporal**: *Ó mhaidin.* - Since this morning.

It can also function as the conjunction "since":
*   *Ó bhí sí tinn.* - Since she was ill.

### Figurative Origin and Separation

The concept of "from" can also be abstract, indicating source, origin, or absence.

*   **Source**: *Litir uait.* - A letter from you.
*   **Source**: *Cabhair ó chairde.* - Help from friends.
*   **Origin**: *Fear ó Thrá Lí.* - A man from Tralee.
*   **Separation**: *Is fearr d’fhear bean chéile uaidh.* - A man is better off without a wife.
*   **Freedom from**: *Bí ó bhaol.* - To be free from danger.

## Advanced Functions and Idioms

### Cause and Reason

`Ó` can be used to explain the reason or cause for a state of being.

*   *Bodhar ó thorann.* - Deaf from the noise.
*   *Tuirseach ó shiúil.* - Tired from walking.

### Expressing Need and Want

A common structure using `ó` is to express need or want.

| Concept | Formula | Example | Translation (Literal) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| To want/need | `Tá`/`Teastaíonn` + [Object] + `ó` + [Subject] | *Teastaíonn bainne uait.* | Milk is needed from you. |

More examples:
*   *Tá spóirt uaim.* - I want fun.
*   *Teastaíonn uaim labhairt leat.* - I want to speak with you.

### Ranges and Mathematics

`Ó` is used to define ranges and in simple subtraction.

*   **Range (from...to)**: The structure `ó...go` means "from...to".
    *   *Ó thús go deireadh.* - From beginning to end.
    *   *Ó lá go lá.* - From day to day.
    *   *Ó am go ham.* - From time to time.
*   **Limitation**: *Ó cúig phunt go deich bpunt.* - From 5 to 10 pounds.
*   **Mathematics (minus)**: Note the reverse order compared to English.
    *   *A dó óna trí sin a haon.* - 3 - 2 = 1 (literally "2 from the 3 that-is 1").

### Idiomatic Use with Adjectives

`Ó` appears in common phrases with adjectives.

*   *Deas uait.* - Nice of you.
*   *Maith uainn.* - Good of us.
*   *Is breá uait.* - To be good at something.

## Grammatical Combinations

### With Possessive Pronouns

| Form     | Translation     |
|:---------|:----------------|
| `ó mo`   | from my         |
| `ó do`   | from your       |
| `óna`    | from his        |
| `óna`    | from her        |
| `ónár`   | from our        |
| `ó bhur` | from your (pl.) |
| `óna`    | from their      |

### With Relative Pronouns and the Copula

`Ó` combines with the indirect relative pronoun `a`/`ar`.

| Tense     | Relative Pronoun (`ó` + `a`/`ar`) | Copula Form (`ó` + `ar`) | Copula Form (before vowel) | Translation     |
|:----------|:----------------------------------|:-------------------------|:---------------------------|:----------------|
| Present   | `óna`                             | `ónar`                   | `ónarb`                    | from which/whom |
| Preterite | `ónar`                            | `ónar`                   | `ónarbh`                   | from which/whom |

### With the Numeral Particle

`Ó` combines with the numeral particle `a` to form `óna`.

*   *Óna trí go dtí a hocht a chlog.* - From 3 to 8 o'clock.
