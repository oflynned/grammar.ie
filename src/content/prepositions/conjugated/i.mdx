## Overview

The preposition `i` is pronounced as a short schwa sound [ə], similar to the 'a' in 'about'. Its spelling is etymological, deriving from the older form `in`. While it primarily means "in", it does not distinguish between location and motion towards a location. It always governs the dative case.

### Conjugated Forms

The preposition `i` combines with personal and possessive pronouns to create a single word.

**Personal Pronouns**

| Person      | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | English |
|:------------|:--------------|:--------------|:--------|
| 1st Sg.     | `ionam`       | `ionamsa`     | in me   |
| 2nd Sg.     | `ionat`       | `ionatsa`     | in you  |
| 3rd Sg. (m) | `ann`         | `annsan`      | in him  |
| 3rd Sg. (f) | `inti`        | `intise`      | in her  |
| 1st Pl.     | `ionainn`     | `ionainne`    | in us   |
| 2nd Pl.     | `ionaibh`     | `ionaibhse`   | in you  |
| 3rd Pl.     | `iontu`       | `iontusan`    | in them |

**Possessive Pronouns**

| Person      | Standard Form | English  |
|:------------|:--------------|:---------|
| 1st Sg.     | `i mo` (`im`) | in my    |
| 2nd Sg.     | `i do` (`id`) | in your  |
| 3rd Sg. (m) | `ina`         | in his   |
| 3rd Sg. (f) | `ina`         | in her   |
| 1st Pl.     | `inár`        | in our   |
| 2nd Pl.     | `in bhur`     | in your  |
| 3rd Pl.     | `ina`         | in their |

The forms `im` and `id` are common in Munster, while `i mo` and `i do` are found in Ulster and Connacht. The forms `ina` and `inár` are often pronounced and written as `’na` and `’nár`.

### Interrogatives and Relatives

The question word for "in what?" or "in which?" is `cá hann`, `cé ann`, or `cad ann`, followed by an indirect relative clause.

| Tense   | Relative Pronoun | Copula Form | Copula Form (before vowel) | English  |
|:--------|:-----------------|:------------|:---------------------------|:---------|
| Present | `ina`            | `inar`      | `inarb`                    | in which |
| Past    | `inar`           | `inar`      | `inarbh`                   | in which |

## Forms and Mutations

The form of `i` and the mutation it causes depends on the word that follows it.

### The Form 'i'

When used alone, before a noun without an article, `i` causes eclipsis.

### Combination with the Article

When followed by the definite article, `i` combines with it.

- `i` + `an` becomes `sa` (before a consonant) or `san` (before a vowel).
- `i` + `na` becomes `sna`.

The mutation that follows depends on dialect and official standard. The older forms `ins an` and `ins na` are still common, especially in Ulster.

### The Form 'in'

The form `in` is used in specific contexts and causes no initial mutation.

- Before vowels: *in Éirinn* (in Ireland)
- Before the number `dhá`: *in dhá bhróig* (in two shoes)
- Before `bhur` (your, pl.): *in bhur dteach* (in your house)
- Before titles of books or works: *in "Cré na Cille"*

### The Forms 'ins' and 'is'

Before `gach` (each/every), and its variants `chuile` and `chaon`, the form `ins` or `is` is often used. This form does not cause the standard eclipsis of `gach`.

- *ins gach bliain* (in each year)
- *sa chuile áit* (everywhere)

The standard form is `i ngach`, which does cause eclipsis.

## Core Functions

### Spatial and Temporal Location

`I` is used to specify a location in space or a point in time.

- *Tá sé sa phota.* (He is in the pot.)
- *Tá siad ina gcónaí in íochtar na tíre.* (They live in the lower part of the country.)
- *Feicim é san oíche.* (I see him in the night.)

### Describing State and Quality

The preposition can describe a physical or emotional state, as well as an inherent quality.

- *Tá an bord briste sa lár.* (The table is broken in the middle.)
- *Tá sí i bpian.* (She is in pain.)
- *Tá éirim an cheoil sa ghasúr.* (The boy has a talent for music.)

## The 'In a State of' Construction

A common structure using `bí + i + possessive pronoun + noun` is used to describe a state of being, including roles, professions, and the progressive aspect of certain verbs.

### Progressive Aspect with Stative Verbs

This construction forms the continuous tense for verbs describing a state, such as sleeping, sitting, or living.

- *Tá mé i mo chodladh.* (I am sleeping.)
- *Tá sí ina cónaí anseo.* (She is living here.)

### Roles and Professions

It is used to state a temporary or newly acquired role or profession.

- *Tá mé i mo dhochtúir.* (I am a doctor.)
- *Tá tú i do fhear anois.* (You are a man now.)

When this structure is used, any adjective describing the role is lenited, e.g., *Tá mé i mo dhochtúir **mh**aith*.

### Existence and Becoming

The conjugated form `ann` (in him/it) is used to mean "there" or "in existence".

- *Tá teach ann.* (There is a house.)

To describe the process of becoming something, the verb `éirigh` is used with the same structure.

- *Éireoidh mé i mo dhochtúir.* (I will become a doctor.)

## Adverbial and Idiomatic Uses

### Adverbial Combinations

The form `ann` combines with `seo`, `sin`, and `siúd` to create adverbs of place.

- `ann` + `seo` -> `anseo` (here)
- `ann` + `sin` -> `ansin` (there, then)
- `ann` + `siúd` -> `ansiúd` (over there)

These are written as one word when used as adverbs. When used literally to mean "in this" or "in that", they are written as two words: `ann seo`, `ann sin`.

### Common Phrases

| Concept | Formula | Example | Translation (Literal) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ability | `in ann` + Verbal Noun | *Tá mé in ann snámh.* | I am in ability of swimming. |
| Membership | `i` + group | *Tá sé sna Gardaí.* | He is in the Guards (police). |
| Rate | X `sa` Y | *deich bpunt sa lá* | ten pounds in the day |
| Distribution | X `san` X | *fear san fhear* | man in the man |
| By Name | `glaoi in ainm` | *glaoigh in ainm duine* | to call in a person's name |
