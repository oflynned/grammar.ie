## Overview

The preposition `ar` is one of the most versatile in Irish. While its core meaning is "on", it has a vast range of other functions. It is usually pronounced as if written `oir` or `eir`.

Historically, `ar` is a merger of three distinct Old Irish prepositions: *ar* ("in front of"), *for* ("on"), and *iar* ("after"). This merger explains its wide variety of meanings today.

Like most simple prepositions, `ar` governs the dative case.

### Forms of Ar

When used with personal pronouns, `ar` combines to form a single word.

| Person    | Standard | Emphatic   |
|:----------|:---------|:-----------|
| I         | `orm`    | `ormsa`    |
| You       | `ort`    | `ortsa`    |
| He        | `air`    | `airsean`  |
| She       | `uirthi` | `uirthise` |
| We        | `orainn` | `orainne`  |
| You (pl.) | `oraibh` | `oraibhse` |
| They      | `orthu`  | `orthusan` |

When followed by a possessive pronoun, `ar` remains separate.

| Person     | Standard  | With Verbal Noun |
|:-----------|:----------|:-----------------|
| My         | `ar mo`   | -                |
| Your       | `ar do`   | -                |
| His        | `ar a`    | `arna`           |
| Her        | `ar a`    | `arna`           |
| Our        | `ar ár`   | `arnár`          |
| Your (pl.) | `ar bhur` | -                |
| Their      | `ar a`    | `arna`           |

The forms `arna` and `arnár` are primarily used with a verbal noun to mean "after", replacing the older *iar n-a* and *iar n-ár*. For example, *arna chríochnú dom* means "after I had finished it".

### Question Forms

- **On whom?**: `cé air...?` (+ indirect relative clause)
- **On what?**: `cad air...?` / `cé air...?` / `cá hair...?` (+ indirect relative clause)

### Dialectal Variations with the Article

- **Munster**: Eclipsis applies to all consonants, including `d` and `t`.
- **Connacht**: A t-prefix is added to feminine nouns starting with `s-`. For example, *ar an tsúil* (on the eye).
- **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis. For example, *ar an bhord* (on the table).

## Core Functions

### Spatial and Positional

`Ar` is used to describe physical location and direction.

- **On top of something**: *ar an mbord* (on the table)
- **In a town or place**: *ar an mbaile* (in the town)
- **Fastened to or held by**: *ar teaghrán* (by a rope), *ar srian* (by the reins)
- **Within range**: *ar amharc* (within sight), *ar éisteacht* (within earshot)
- **Direction**: *ar aghaidh* (forwards), *amach ar an doras* (out through the door)

### Temporal

`Ar` can specify points in time or sequence.

- **At or on a specific time**: *ar maidin* (in the morning), *ar a dó a chlog* (at 2 o'clock)
- **After an action (with verbal noun)**: *arna críochnú dom* (after I had finished it)

### Possession of States and Attributes

A key function of `ar` is to express having states, feelings, or attributes, often translated using "to have" or "to be" in English. The structure is typically `Tá [state] ar [person]`.

- **Physical or psychological states**: *Tá áthas orm* (I am happy, lit. "Joy is on me"). This applies to illnesses, sorrow, joy, thirst, fear, and regret.
- **External appearance**: Used to describe colours, looks, or special features.
- **Parts of an object**: *Tá doirse ar an teach* (The house has doors).
- **Having a name**: *Máirtín atá air* (He is called Martin, lit. "Martin is what is on him").

### Wearing and Acquiring

Related to possession, `ar` is used for clothing and the acquisition of states.

- **Wearing clothes**: The structure `Tá [clothing] ar [person]` means someone is wearing something.
- **Putting on clothes**: The structure `Cuir [clothing] ar [person]` means to put something on.
- **Acquiring a state**: The verb `cuir` with `ar` can mean to cause a state in someone. *Chuir sé fearg orm* (He made me angry).

## Abstract and Idiomatic Usage

### Obligation and Responsibility

`Ar` is used to express duty, necessity, and accountability.

- **To have to / must**: The structure `Tá ar [person]` indicates obligation. *Tá orm bainne a ól* (I have to drink milk).
- **Responsibility for**: *Tá a báis ort* (You are responsible for her death).
- **Authority over**: *Rí ar Sasana* (King of England).

### Indirect Objects and Common Verbs

`Ar` serves as the indirect object for many common verbs and expressions.

| Concept                 | Formula                     | Example                     | Translation (Literal)           |
|:------------------------|:----------------------------|:----------------------------|:--------------------------------|
| To love                 | `tá grá ag [X] ar [Y]`      | *Tá grá agam ort*           | I have love on you              |
| To know (a person)      | `tá aithne ag [X] ar [Y]`   | *Tá aithne agam ar Cháit*   | I have knowledge on Cáit        |
| To know (a place/topic) | `tá eolas ag [X] ar [Y]`    | *Tá eolas agat ar Éirinn*   | You have information on Ireland |
| To be thankful to       | `tá buíochas ag [X] ar [Y]` | *Tá buíochas agam ar Cháit* | I have thanks on Cáit           |
| To owe money to         | `tá airgead ag [X] ar [Y]`  | *Tá airgead agam ort*       | I have money on you             |

Common verbs that take `ar`:
- `iarr ar...`: to ask someone
- `féach ar...`: to look at something
- `tosaigh ar...`: to begin something
- `glaoigh ar...`: to call someone

### Advantage and Disadvantage

`Ar` can indicate that an action benefits or harms someone, a concept sometimes called the dative of advantage or disadvantage.

- **Disadvantage**: *Bhí sé ag briseadh fuinneogaí ort* (He was breaking your windows, to your detriment).
- **Advantage**: *Tá bliain agam air* (I am one year older than him, lit. "I have a year's advantage over him").

## Classification and Description

### Similarity and Sameness

`Ar` can be used to form descriptive phrases indicating similarity.

- **Likeness**: *ar dhath an róis* (rose-coloured), *ar dhéanamh báid* (boat-shaped)
- **Sameness**: *fear ar d’ainm* (a man with the same name as you)

### Measurement and Price

- **Dimensions**: *ar airde* (in height), *ar fad* (in length)
- **Price**: *ar phunt* (for a pound), *Tá punt ar an úll* (The apple costs a pound).

### Classification and Identification

`Ar` is used in non-copular sentences to identify something as part of a group or as a specific instance.

- **Part of a set**: *Tá sé ar na fir is mó* (He is one of the biggest men).
- **With a superlative**: *Tá sé ar an bhfear is mó acu* (He is the biggest man of them).
- **With ordinal numbers**: *Bhí sé ar an dara fear a tháinig* (He was the second man who came).

### Numbers and Arithmetic

- **Counting**: `ar` is used for numbers 21-29, 31-39, etc. *Duine ar fhichid* (21 people, lit. "one on twenty").
- **Arithmetic**: `ar` means "divided by". *A sé ar a trí sin a dó* (6 ÷ 3 = 2).

## Specialised Functions

### State and Activity

`Ar` combines with abstract nouns to describe a state of being or an ongoing activity.

- **State of being**: *ar meisce* (drunk), *ar buile* (mad, enraged), *ar fheabhas* (excellent)
- **Activity (with verbal noun)**: *ar an obair* (at work, working)

### Cause, Result, and Dependence

- **Cause**: *ar ghrá Dé* (for the love of God), *ar a thuarastal* (for his salary)
- **Result**: *ar a iarratas* (upon his application)
- **Dependence**: *ar arán* (to live on bread), *ar thoil Dé* (dependent on the will of God)

### About and Concerning

`Ar` can mean "about" or "concerning" in certain contexts.

- *scéal ar...*: a story about...
- *ainm ar...*: a name for...
- *cúis dlí ar...*: a legal case against...

### Other Uses

- **With adjectives**: *santach ar...* (greedy for...), *maith ar...* (good at...)
- **Probability**: *Tá báisteach air* (It looks like rain).
- **Emphasising the Imperative**: *Scríobh ort!* (Go on, write!).
