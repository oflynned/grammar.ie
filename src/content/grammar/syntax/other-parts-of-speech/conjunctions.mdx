A **conjunction** (Irish: `cónasc`) is a word that connects clauses or individual words. In addition to single-word conjunctions, Irish uses many multi-word phrases that function as compound conjunctions.

## Types of Conjunctions by Formation

### Simple Conjunctions (`cónaisc shimplí`)

These are single-word conjunctions. Examples include:
- `agus` (and), `ach` (but), `nó` (or)
- `go` (that), `ó` (since), `mar` (as, because)
- `má`, `dá`, `mura` (if, if not)
- Interrogative words used as conjunctions: `cé` (who), `céard` (what), `cá` (where), `conas` (how)
- Interrogative verbal particles: `an`, `nach` (whether, whether not)

### Compound Conjunctions (`cónaisc chomhshuite`)

Compound conjunctions are multi-word phrases, often consisting of:
- **A first element**, which can be a simple conjunction (`ach`), a preposition (`le`), a prepositional phrase (`tar éis`), a noun (`toisc`), or even a verb form (`bíodh`).
- **A second element**, which is typically a simple conjunction like `go`/`nach` (that/that not), a relative particle like `a`/`nach`, a conditional conjunction like `má`/`dá`, or an interrogative particle like `an`/`nach`.
- **An intervening element**, often `agus` (usually shortened to `is`), which can appear between the first and second elements (e.g., `i riocht is go` - so that).

Adverbs or even entire subordinate clauses can be inserted between the parts of a compound conjunction.

### Conjunctive Adverbs (`dobhriathra cónascacha`)

Some adverbs function like conjunctions by referring to a preceding clause, creating a logical link. Many are formed from a compound preposition, the possessive pronoun `a`, and a demonstrative like `sin`.
- *`ina dhiaidh sin`* - nevertheless (literally "after that")
- *`dá bhrí sin`* - therefore (literally "by reason of that")

These often have a corresponding compound conjunction that introduces the other clause. For example, `i ndiaidh go` (although) corresponds to the conjunctive adverb `ina dhiaidh sin` (nevertheless).

### Alternatives with Infinitive Constructions

Instead of a subordinate clause introduced by a conjunction, Irish often uses an infinitive construction (a phrase with a verbal noun).
- *`toisc go bhfuil sé anseo`* - because he is here (subordinate clause)
- *`toisc é a bheith anseo`* - because of him being here (infinitive construction)

These constructions are typically introduced by prepositions, some of which can also be part of a conjunction (like `toisc` above) while others cannot (e.g., `ag`).
- *`ag ól tae dom`* - while I was drinking tea (literally "at drinking of tea to-me")

## Coordinating vs. Subordinating Conjunctions

The distinction between coordinating conjunctions (joining two equal clauses) and subordinating conjunctions (joining a main clause to a dependent one) is less significant in Irish than in some other languages. This is because Irish word order does not change in a subordinate clause. Furthermore, conjunctions typically considered "coordinating" can also introduce subordinate clauses, blurring the lines.

## Coordinating Conjunctions (`cónaisc chomhordaitheacha`)

### Inclusive Conjunctions (`cónaisc charnacha`)

These conjunctions create a list where all items are included.

#### and

The word for "and" has two main forms: `agus` and the short form `is`. Colloquially, you may also see `'gus` or `'s`. In older script, the Tironian note **⁊** is common.

- *`Tá mé sásta inniu agus bhí mé sásta inné.`* - I am happy today and was happy yesterday.
- *`Pól agus Seán`* - Paul and John
- *`Pól is Seán`* - Paul and John

**Peculiarities**
- **In lists of nouns**, `agus` is often repeated between every item (polysyndeton).
  - *`Pól agus Seán agus Séamas a bhí ann.`* - Paul, Seán and James were there.
- **Between adjectives**, `agus` is usually omitted (asyndeton).
  - *`an cailín óg álainn`* - the young, beautiful girl.
  - *`Tá an aimsir te tirim.`* - The weather is hot and dry.
- **With the copula**, `is` must be repeated after `agus` when listing predicates.
  - *`Is ceart agus is cóir é.`* - It is right and proper.
- **With verbs**, any verbal particle must be repeated after `agus`.
  - *`Deir siad gur cheannaigh agus gur dhíol siad na tithe.`* - They say that they bought and sold the houses.
- **In negative lists with `gan` (without)**, `gan` or `ná` is used instead of `agus`.
  - *`Tháinig sé gan ór gan airgead.`* - He came without gold and silver.
  - *`gan bróg ná stoca`* - without shoe or stocking.

**Further uses of `agus`**
- **In positive answers**, `agus` can mean "also" or add an affirmation.
  - *`An bhfuil cead agam? - Tá agus fáilte.`* - May I? - Yes, and welcome.
  - *`Ólfaidh mé fuisce. - Ólfaidh agus mise.`* - I will drink whiskey. - Me too.
- **With `amhlaidh`**, the phrase `amhlaidh ... agus` means "namely" or "that is to say", introducing an explanation.
  - *`Is amhlaidh a tháinig sé abhaile agus a shrón briste.`* - He came home with his nose broken.
- **With imperatives**, `agus` can connect an address to a command, or link two commands where the second is a consequence of the first.
  - *`Dún an doras agus ná gabhadh an bhó amach.`* - Close the door so the cow doesn't get out.
- **In comparisons**, `agus` can mean "as" or "than".
  - *`chomh maith agus a rinne sé é`* - as well as he did it.
  - *`Ní hionann sin agus a bhí sé.`* - That is not as it was.
  - *`breis agus céad bliain`* - more than a hundred years.
- **With infinitive constructions**, `agus` can link a clause to a verbal noun phrase, especially to avoid repeating a similar verb form.
  - *`Chuamar ann go bhfeicfimis é agus éisteacht leis.`* - We went there to see him and to listen to him.
- **For circumstantial description**, `agus` followed by an infinitive construction or a "small clause" (a clause with the verb `bí` omitted) can describe the circumstances of the main clause. The meaning can be temporal ("while"), causal ("because"), concessive ("although"), or conditional ("if").
  - *`Agus é ag gáire, thit sé go talamh.`* - While he was laughing, he fell to the ground.
  - *`D'imigh tú agus fearg ort.`* - You left in anger.
  - *`D'fhill sé abhaile agus an t-airgead go léir ólta aige.`* - He returned home after he had drunk all the money.

#### both ... and ...

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `… chomh maith le …` | "… as good with …" |
| `idir … agus …` | "between … and …" |
| `ní hamháin … ach … freisin` | "not only … but … also" |

- *`Bhí Pól chomh maith le Seán sásta.`* - Both Paul and Seán were satisfied.
- *`Idir mhná agus fhir a bhí sásta.`* - Both women and men were satisfied.

#### nor

In negative contexts, `ná` is used instead of `agus` (and) or `nó` (or). This includes sentences with `ní` (not), `gan` (without), and other words with negative implications like `annamh` (rarely) or `deacair` (difficult).

- *`gan phost gan mhaoin ná cairde`* - without a job, property, or friends.
- *`Is annamh a dhéanann sé snámh ná dreapadóireacht.`* - He rarely goes swimming or climbing.

In negative answers, `ná` adds emphasis or another negative item.
- *`An raibh Pól ann? - Ní raibh, ná Seán.`* - Was Paul there? - No, nor was Seán.
- *`An bhfuil tú cinnte? - Níl ná cinnte.`* - Are you sure? - No, not at all.

> **Note:** There are four distinct words spelled `ná`:
> 1. The conjunction `ná` (nor).
> 2. The conjunction `ná` (than), used in comparisons.
> 3. The negative verbal particle `ná` (do not), used with imperatives and in some dialects instead of `nach`.
> 4. A conjunction `ná` used in cleft sentences in Munster.

#### neither ... nor ...

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ní … ná …` | "not … nor …" |
| `ní … agus ní …` | "not … and not …" |
| `ní … ná ní …` | "not … nor not …" |
| `ní … nó …` | "not … or …" |

- *`Ní raibh Pól ná Seán sásta.`* - Neither Paul nor Seán was satisfied.
- *`Ní íosfaidh mé agus ní ólfaidh mé.`* - I will not eat and I will not drink.

#### and also not / and even less

To add a second negated statement emphatically, you can use `agus ní mó` (and also not) or `agus is lú` (and even less).
- *`Ní raibh sé anseo agus ní mó a bhí sé ansin.`* - He was not here and he was not there either.
- *`Ní raibh sé sásta agus is lú a bhí mé sásta.`* - He was not satisfied, and I was even less satisfied.

#### on the one hand … on the other hand …

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ar láimh amháin … ar an láimh eile …` | "on one hand … on the other hand …" |
| `sa chéad chás … os a choinne sin …` | "in the first case … opposite that …" |

- *`Ar láimh amháin de bhí sé leisciúil, ar an láimh eile de rinne sé an obair go maith.`* - On the one hand he was lazy, on the other hand he did the work well.

#### furthermore, moreover

These are conjunctive adverbs that link ideas.
- `chomh maith leis sin`
- `ina theannta sin`
- `ar a bharr sin`
- `thairis sin`

#### namely, that is

- `is é sin (le rá)`
- `mar atá`
- `eadhon` (abbreviated `.i.`, literary)

### Exclusive Conjunctions (`cónaisc scaracha`)

These conjunctions present a choice or contrast, excluding one of the options.

#### or

The standard word is `nó`. In Connacht and Munster, it is often pronounced as if written `nú`.
- *`An raibh Pól nó Seán sásta?`* - Was Paul or Seán satisfied?
- *`dubh nó bán`* - black or white.

**Further uses of `nó`**
- **With negation**, `ní ... nó` can have a strong affirmative meaning, implying "I don't believe that it didn't happen."
  - *`Ní féidir nó fuair sé é.`* - He must have got it. (Literally: "It is not possible or he got it.")
- **With `cuma`**, `cuma nó` means "the same as" or "like".
  - *`Ba chuma nó máthair mhaith dhom í.`* - She was like a good mother to me.
- **As `nó go`**, it means "until".
- **As "for"**, it can be a causal conjunction.
  - *`nó is léir nach bhfuil sé furasta`* - for it is obvious that it is not easy.

#### either ... or ...

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `… (é) sin nó …` | "… that or …" |

- *`Bhí Pól sásta, sin nó bhí Seán sásta.`* - Either Paul or Seán was satisfied.

#### otherwise

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `nó` | "or" |
| `nó neachtar acu` | "or the other of them" |

After an imperative, `nó` means "otherwise".
- *`Imigh nó beidh tú déanach.`* - Go, otherwise you'll be late.
- *`Glaoigh air nó codlóidh sé go tráthnóna.`* - Call him, otherwise he'll sleep until evening.

### Contrastive Conjunctions (`cónaisc chodarsnacha`)

#### but, however, rather

The main word is `ach`. It can also mean "whereas" or "while". The adverbs `ámh` and `áfach` also mean "however".
- *`Tá mé sásta inniu ach ní raibh mé sásta inné.`* - I am happy today, but I was not happy yesterday.

**Further uses of `ach`**
- **In negative answers**, `ach` introduces a correction.
  - *`An dochtúir é? - Ní hea, ach múinteoir.`* - Is he a doctor? - No, a teacher.
- **In negative sentences**, `ach` can mean "except".
  - *`Ní raibh aon duine ann ach cailín óg.`* - There was no one there except a young girl.
- **Meaning "if"**, when a conditional main clause is followed by `ach` and a negated past tense clause.
  - *`D'imeoinn cheana ach ní raibh mé réidh.`* - I would have left already if I hadn't been ready.
- **With infinitive constructions**, `ach` can mean "provided that", "if", "as soon as", or "but rather".
  - *`Gheobhaidh tú é ach íoc as.`* - You'll get it, if you pay for it.
  - *`Níor labhair sé focal ach imeacht.`* - He spoke no word, but rather left.
- **As a reinforcing particle**, similar to "but" or "indeed" in English.
  - *`Céard eile a chonaic sí ansin ach capall?`* - What else did she see there but a horse?

#### only

In a semi-negative sentence (`ní ... ach`), `ach` means "only".
- *`Ní raibh mé sásta ach inné.`* - I was only satisfied yesterday.
- *`Ní raibh ann ach sin.`* - There was nothing there but that.

#### not only … but also …

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ní hamháin go … ach …` | "not only that … but …" |
| `ní hé amháin go … ach …` | "not it alone that … but …" |

- *`Ní hé amháin go bhfuil mé sásta inniu ach bhí mé sásta inné.`* - I am not only happy today but was also happy yesterday.

#### except

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ach amháin (go)` | "but only (that)" |
| `seachas` | "besides" |
| `cé is moite de` | (Connacht) |
| `diomaite de` | (Ulster) |

- *`Níor leagadh aon chúram eile ormsa ach amháin fanacht leis.`* - No other task was imposed on me, except to wait for him.
- *`daoine seachas eisean`* - people other than him.

## Subordinating Conjunctions (`cónaisc fho-ordaitheacha`)

### Complementary Conjunctions (`cónaisc ráiteasacha`)

These introduce clauses that function as the subject or object of the main verb.

#### that / that not

The conjunction `go` (that) and its negative counterpart `nach` (that not) are fundamental to forming subordinate clauses.

| | Present/Future | Mutation | Past | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive** | `go` | Eclipsis | `gur` | Lenition |
| **Negative** | `nach` | Eclipsis | `nár` | Lenition |

- *`Deir sé go bhfuil deifir air.`* - He says that he is in a hurry.
- *`Dúirt sí nár fhoghlaim sí a cuid.`* - She said that she had not learned her part.

### Circumstantial Conjunctions (`cónaisc imthoisceacha`)

These describe the circumstances of an action, often translated as "in view of the fact that", "considering that", or "after".

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `agus go` | `agus nach` | "and that" |
| `tar éis go` | `tar éis nach` | "after that" |
| `i ndiaidh go` | `i ndiaidh nach` | "after that" |
| `nuair a` | `nuair nach` | "when" |

- *`Ní haon ionadh sin agus gurb eisean do mhac.`* - That is no wonder, considering he is your son.

### Causal Conjunctions (`cónaisc chúise`)

These introduce a reason or cause.

#### because

Irish has a rich variety of ways to say "because".

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning | Dialect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `mar` | `mar ní/nach` | "as" | Universal |
| `ó` | `ó nach` | "since" | Universal |
| `de bhrí (is) go` | `de bhrí (is) nach` | "from strength that" | |
| `toisc go` | `toisc nach` | "purpose, that" | Munster |
| `faoi (is) go` | `faoi (is) nach` | "under that" | Connacht |
| `(ar/as) siocair go` | `(ar/as) siocair nach` | "from reason that" | Ulster |
| `mar gheall ar go` | `mar gheall ar nach` | "as pledge on that" | Connacht |
| `ós rud é go` | `ós rud é nach` | "since it is a thing that" | Munster |

- *`Bhí fearg air faoi go raibh siad ag déanamh gleo.`* - He was angry because they were making noise.
- *`Ní dheachaigh mé go hÉirinn de bharr go raibh mé tinn.`* - I did not go to Ireland because I was ill.

#### since / for

These conjunctions introduce a reason, often in a way that feels slightly less direct than "because". They are typically followed by a main clause structure, not a `go`-clause (unless in indirect speech).

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `mar` | Main clause |
| `óir` | Main clause |
| `arae` | Main clause |
| `ó` | Direct relative clause |

- *`Bhí faitíos roimpi mar bhí sí an-ard.`* - They were afraid of her, for she was very tall.
- *`Ós í an Ghaeilge an teanga náisiúnta...`* - Since Irish is the national language...

#### therefore / consequently

These are conjunctive adverbs that introduce a result.

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `mar sin` | "as that" |
| `dá bhrí sin` | "from that reason" |
| `dá bharr sin` | "from that result" |
| `ar an ábhar sin` | "on that cause" |

- *`Déanaim smaoineamh, dá bhrí sin táim ann.`* - I think, therefore I am.

### Concessive Conjunctions (`cónaisc fhaomhacha`)

These introduce a circumstance that hinders the main action but does not prevent it.

#### although / even if

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `cé go` | `cé nach` | "who, that" |
| `(d')ainneoin go` | `(d')ainneoin nach` | "despite that" |
| `i ndiaidh (is) go` | `i ndiaidh (is) nach` | "after that" |
| `bíodh (is) go` | `bíodh (is) nach` | "be it that" |
| `má (... féin)` | `mura (... féin)` | "if (... self)" |
| `dá (... féin)` | `mura (... féin)` | "if (... self)" |
| `fiú amháin má/dá` | `fiú amháin mura` | "even if" |

- *`Ní chreidim é cé go bhfeicim é.`* - I don't believe it, although I see it.
- *`Má tá sé bocht féin, tá sé fial.`* - Although he is poor, he is generous.

#### Nevertheless / All the same

These are conjunctive adverbs.

| Irish Form | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ina dhiaidh sin` | "after that" |
| `mar sin féin` | "like that itself" |
| `ar a shon sin` | "for its sake that" |
| `dá ainneoin sin` | "despite that" |

- *`Tá sé fuar ach ina dhiaidh sin tá sé breá tirim.`* - It is cold, but nevertheless it is quite dry.

### Final Conjunctions (`cónaisc aidhme`)

These introduce the purpose or goal of an action.

#### In order that / To

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `le go` | `le nach` | "with that" |
| `chun go` | `chun nach` | "to that" |
| `ionas go` | `ionas nach` | "manner, that" |
| `i gcaoi (is) go` | `i gcaoi (is) nach` | "in manner that" |
| `ar eagla go` | | "for fear that" (lest) |
| `le heagla go` | | "with fear that" (lest) |

The verb in the subordinate clause is often in the future or conditional. The subjunctive can also be used.
- *`Déan deifir le go mbeidh tú réidh in am.`* - Hurry up so that you will be ready in time.
- *`Scríobh sí síos é ar nós nach ndéanfadh sí dearmad air.`* - She wrote it down so that she would not forget it.
- *`Ná tugaigí breith le heagla go dtabharfaí breith oraibh.`* - Judge not, lest you be judged.

### Consecutive Conjunctions (`cónaisc iarmhartacha`)

These introduce the result or consequence of an action.

#### So that

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `go` | `nach` | "that" |
| `agus go` | `agus nach` | "and that" |
| `i riocht is go` | `i riocht is nach` | "in form and that" |

These are often used after expressions of degree like `chomh ...` (so...).
- *`Bhí mé chomh tuirseach sin gur thit mé i mo chodladh.`* - I was so tired that I fell asleep.
- *`Chodail mé rófhada i riocht is go raibh mé déanach.`* - I overslept, so that I was late.

### Restrictive Conjunctions (`cónaisc chuimsitheacha`)

These introduce a condition or limitation.

#### Provided that / On condition that

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ach go` | `ach nach` | "but, that" |
| `ar choinníoll go` | `ar choinníoll nach` | "on condition, that" |
| `ar acht go` | `ar acht nach` | "on condition, that" |

- *`Tig leat é a bhriseadh ar chuntar go n-íocfaidh tú as.`* - You may break it, on condition that you pay for it.

#### As far as / As long as

| Irish Form | Followed by | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `fad a` | direct relative clause | "extent, which" |
| `chomh fada is a` | direct relative clause | "so far, and which" |
| `sa mhéid (is) go` | `go`-clause | "in the extent that" |

- *`Níl aon eagla orm roimhe fad atá mo ghunna liom.`* - I am not afraid of him, as long as I have my gun with me.

#### Supposing that (assuming that)

| Positive | Negative | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `cuir i gcás go` | `cuir i gcás nach` | "put in case, that" |
| `abair (is) go` | `abair (is) nach` | "say, that" |

- *`Cuir i gcás go mbeadh sé ann.`* - Supposing he were there.

### Conditional Conjunctions (`cónaisc choinníollacha`)

These introduce conditions. Irish distinguishes between real (possible) and unreal (hypothetical) conditions.

#### If / In case

| | Positive | Mutation | Negative | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Real** | `má` | Lenition | `mura` | Eclipsis |
| **Unreal** | `dá` | Eclipsis | `mura` | Eclipsis |

- *`Má bhíonn sé sásta, tiocfaidh na fir.`* - If he is satisfied, the men will come. (Real)
- *`Dá mbeadh lá maith ann, dhéanfainn é.`* - If it were a nice day, I would do it. (Unreal)
- *`Mura dtiocfaidh sé, beidh fearg orm.`* - If he doesn't come, I will be angry. (Real)
- *`Mura dtiocfadh sé, bheadh fearg orm.`* - If he didn't come, I would be angry. (Unreal)

> **Note on `dá`**: The conditional `dá` (if) is one of several homophones. Others include `dá` (of his/her, to his/her), `dá` (of which, to which), and `dá` (two).

#### If it is not the case that / Unless

- `mura rud é go`
- `mura bhfuil i ndán is go`

- *`Cosnóidh sé é, mura rud é go dtiocfaidh caitheamh na gcloch ró-dhian ort.`* - He will protect it, unless the stone-throwing becomes too hard for you.

#### If it were not the case that

These introduce a counterfactual condition, something that is contrary to reality.
- `murach (go)`
- `murab é (go)`
- `ach (go)`

- *`Ní chreidfinn é murach go bhfaca mé féin é.`* - I would not believe it if I had not seen it myself.
- *`Murach an balla, thitfinn.`* - If it weren't for the wall, I would fall.

### Conjunctions in Indirect Questions (`cónaisc cheisteacha`)

#### Whether

| | Positive | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present/Future** | `an` | `nach` |
| **Past** | `ar` | `nár` |

- *`Níl mé cinnte an bhfuil mé sásta.`* - I am not sure whether I am satisfied.
- *`Ní mé ar chuala sé é.`* - I wonder whether he heard it.

#### Whether … or whether (not)

- `an … nó nach …`
- `cé acu a … nó nach …`

- *`Níl a fhios agam an bhfuil mé sásta nó nach bhfuil.`* - I don't know whether I am satisfied or not.
- *`Abair liom cé acu a bheidh tú ann nó nach mbeidh.`* - Tell me whether you will be there or not.

### Comparative Conjunctions (`cónaisc chomparáideacha`)

#### As if

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ar nós dá` | unreal conditional clause |
| `amhail (is) dá` | unreal conditional clause |
| `mar a` | direct relative clause (with conditional verb) |
| `faoi mar a` | direct relative clause (with conditional verb) |

- *`Chorraigh sí ar nós dá mbeadh sí ag iarraidh í féin a bhaint as poll.`* - She moved as if she were trying to get herself out of a hole.
- *`Lean sé den obair faoi mar nach gcloisfeadh sé mé.`* - He continued with the work as if he couldn't hear me.

#### (Just) as

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `mar a` | direct relative clause |
| `faoi mar a` | direct relative clause |
| `de réir mar a` | direct relative clause |

- *`D'fhág sí é sa chaoi a raibh sé.`* - She left it as it was.
- *`De réir mar a thuigim...`* - As I understand it...

#### (Greater) than

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ná` | Noun/Pronoun |
| `ná (mar) a` | direct relative clause |

- *`Tá sé níos airde ná an teach sin.`* - It is higher than that house.
- *`Tá sé níos fearr ná mar a shíl mé.`* - It is better than I thought.

### Temporal Conjunctions (`cónaisc ama`)

#### Until

| Positive | Negative |
| :--- | :--- |
| `go dtí go` | `go dtí nach` |
| `nó go` | - |
| `go` | - |

- *`Fan nó go dtiocfaidh sé.`* - Wait until he comes.
- *`Ní thiocfaidh sé go bhfaighidh sé cuireadh uait.`* - He will not come until he gets an invitation from you.

#### Since / Ever since

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ó` | direct relative clause |

- *`Ní fhaca mé iad ó chuala mé an scéal.`* - I have not seen them since I heard the news.

#### As soon as

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `ach a/ar` | indirect relative clause |
| `chomh luath is a` | direct relative clause |
| `a luaithe a` | direct relative clause |

- *`Glaofaidh sí chomh luath is a thiocfaidh sí abhaile.`* - She will call as soon as she gets home.

#### Before

| Present/Future | Mutation | Past | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `sula` | Eclipsis | `sular` | Lenition |

- *`Phéinteáil sé an doras sular tháinig Pádraig.`* - He painted the door before Pádraig came.

#### While, as (Simultaneity)

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `fad a` | direct relative clause |
| `agus` | Small Clause (`agus é ag caint`) |
| `ag` | Infinitive Construction (`ag léamh dó`) |

- *`Bhí sé ag caoineadh fad a bhí sé ag caint liom.`* - He was crying while he was talking to me.
- *`Agus é ag gáire, thit sé go talamh.`* - While he was laughing, he fell to the ground.

#### After

Irish typically uses an infinitive construction with a preposition instead of a conjunction.
- `tar éis` + verbal noun phrase
- `i ndiaidh` + verbal noun phrase

- *`Tiocfaidh an bhean tar éis imeacht don fhear.`* - The woman will come after the man has gone.

#### When (temporal) / As

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `nuair a` | direct relative clause |
| `an tráth a` | direct relative clause |

- *`Nuair a bhí mé óg, bhí mé i mo chónaí i nDún na nGall.`* - When I was young, I lived in Donegal.

### Local Conjunctions (`cónaisc áite`)

#### Where

| Irish Form | Followed by |
| :--- | :--- |
| `mar a/ar` | indirect relative clause |
| `an áit a/ar` | indirect relative clause |
| `cá/cár` | indirect relative clause (in indirect questions) |

- *`Fan san áit a bhfuil tú.`* - Stay where you are.
- *`Níl a fhios agam cá bhfuil tú.`* - I don't know where you are.
