Conditional sentences describe conditions and their consequences, following an "if... then..." structure. In Irish, this structure consists of two parts:

-   **Protasis** (`an céadbheart`): The subordinate clause containing the condition (the 'if-clause'). It is usually introduced by the conjunctions `má` (if), `dá` (if), or `mura(r)` (if... not).
-   **Apodosis** (`an t-iarbheart`): The main clause describing the consequence of the condition (the 'then-clause'). An introductory conjunction for 'then' is usually absent, though `agus` or `go` can occasionally appear.

The order of these two clauses is flexible. Irish grammar distinguishes between two main types of conditional sentences: real and unreal.

## Real Conditional Sentences

Real conditional sentences (`an clásal coinníollach oscailte`) are used when the condition is, was, or will be fulfilled, or when it is considered fulfillable or probable.

| Type     | Structure                               | Example                                       |
| :------- | :-------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| Positive | `má` + Verb + Subject + Object          | *Má fheiceann tú an fear.* (If you see the man) |
| Negative | `mura(r)` + Verb + Subject + Object | *Murar ól tú fuisce.* (If you did not drink whiskey) |

### Conjunction Rules

-   **`má`** (if) causes lenition and is followed by the independent verb form. This includes the `d'` prefix in the past tense (e.g., `má d'ól sé`).
-   **`mura`** (if... not) causes eclipsis and is followed by the dependent verb form.
-   **`murar`** (if... not, past tense) causes lenition and is followed by the independent verb form.

Dialectal variants include `mara` and `muna` for `mura`, and `marar` and `munar` for `murar`.

### Verb Forms in Real Conditions

#### The Condition Clause (Protasis)

The verb in the 'if' clause is typically in the indicative mood.

-   **Present and Past Tenses**: All indicative tenses can be used.
-   **Future Tense**: After `má`, the future tense is not used. Instead, the habitual present expresses a future meaning.
    -   *Má théann sé amárach...* (If he goes tomorrow...)
-   After `mura`, the future tense can be used. The present subjunctive can also be used to express future uncertainty.
    -   *mura rachaidh sé amárach* (if he will not go tomorrow)
    -   *mura dté sé amárach* (if he will not go tomorrow)
    -   *Cad a dhéanfaidh tú mura dtaga an traein?* (What will you do if the train does not come?)
-   **Conditional Mood**: The conditional is rarely used, but can appear if the mood is necessary but the condition is still considered 'real'. This is common with some modal verbs.
    -   *Agus má thiocfadh leis obair a dhéanamh, thiocfadh léise fanacht.* (And if he could work, then she could wait.)

#### The Consequence Clause (Apodosis)

The verb in the 'then' clause can be in several forms:

-   **Indicative**: The most common form, in whatever tense is required.
    -   *Má tá do chos briste, caithfidh tú fanacht anseo.* (If your leg is broken, you must stay here.)
-   **Indicative of Certainty**: To express a high degree of certainty, the present tense of `bí` or the present perfect (using `bí` + verbal adjective) is used instead of the future.
    -   *Má chloistear thú, tá tú gafa.* (If you are heard, you will certainly be caught.)
-   **Imperative**: To give a command as a consequence.
    -   *Má tá do chos briste, ná héirigh suas!* (If your leg is broken, do not get up!)
-   **Present Subjunctive**: To express a wish or prayer.
    -   *Má tá do chos briste, go bhfóire Dia ort.* (If your leg is broken, may God help you.)

In indirect speech, a real conditional sentence referring to the future may change to an unreal conditional sentence using `dá`.

### Implicative Conditions

For conditions that mean "whenever" or "if ever" (`an clásal coinníollach fíorasach`), the temporal conjunction `nuair` followed by a direct relative clause is often used instead of `má`.

## Unreal Conditional Sentences

Unreal conditional sentences (`an clásal coinníollach iata`) are used when the condition was not or will not be fulfilled, is impossible to fulfil, or when there are subjective doubts about its fulfillability.

| Type     | Structure                               | Example                                            |
| :------- | :-------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| Positive | `dá` + Verb + Subject + Object          | *Dá bhfeicteá an fear.* (If you were to see the man) |
| Negative | `mura` + Verb + Subject + Object | *Mura bhfeicteá an fear.* (If you were not to see the man) |

### Conjunction Rules

-   **`dá`** (if) causes eclipsis and is followed by the dependent verb form.
    -   Dialectal variants include `dhá`, `á`, or `ná` (Connacht). In Munster, `má` is sometimes used.
-   **`mura`** (if... not) causes eclipsis.
    -   Note that `mura` is used for both real and unreal negative conditions, but the verb form that follows is different (indicative for real, conditional/subjunctive for unreal).
    -   Dialectal variants include `mara` and `muna`.

### Verb Forms in Unreal Conditions

#### The Condition Clause (Protasis)

The verb in the 'if' clause can take several forms:

-   **Subjunctive Preterite**: The older standard tense, identical in form to the imperfect.
-   **Conditional**: The modern standard, which mostly replaces the subjunctive preterite. The two forms are synonymous.
    -   `dá bhfeicteá é` (subjunctive) = `dá bhfeicfeá é` (conditional) = if you were to see him.
-   **Imperative**: A dialectal form in Connacht, used without a conjunction like `dá` or `mura`.
    -   *Teagadh sé abhaile, chuirfeá fáilte roimhe.* (If he were to come home, you would welcome him.)
    -   If the clauses are reversed, `agus` or `is` is used: *Chuirfeá fáilte roimhe agus teagadh sé abhaile.*

Unreal conditional clauses are indifferent to time; the same form can refer to the past, present, or future.
- *Dá bhfeicfeá an fear inné / inniu / amárach.* (If you had seen / were to see the man yesterday / today / tomorrow.)

#### The Consequence Clause (Apodosis)

The verb in the 'then' clause is almost always in the **conditional mood**.

An exception is the **indicative of certainty**, used to vividly emphasise the certainty of the outcome if the condition were met.

-   **Preterite**:
    -   *Dá mbéadh sé sa teach nuair a dódh sé, bhí sé marbh.* (If he had been in thehouse when it burned, he would be dead.)
    -   *Dá bhfáiscfeá a thuilleadh mise, bhí mé marbh agat!* (If you had squeezed me any more, you would have killed me!)
-   **Pluperfect** (past tense of `bí` + verbal adjective):
    -   *Murach í, bhí sé imithe.* (If it weren't for her, he would certainly have gone.)

## Concessive Conditional Sentences

Conditional sentences can have a concessive meaning, like "even if" or "although". These are often marked by adverbs like `féin` or `fiú amháin`.

They still distinguish between real (`má`) and unreal (`dá`) conditions, but the link between the two clauses is looser. The consequence happens *despite* the condition, not because of it. This has grammatical effects:

-   In unreal concessive sentences, a `dá`-clause can be followed by a main clause in the indicative or imperative (not just the conditional).
-   In real concessive sentences, a `má`-clause can be followed by a main clause in the future tense (which is not allowed in standard real conditions).

## Exclamatory Conditional Sentences

The conjunctions `dá`, `má`, and `mura` can be used in exclamations, where their meaning changes.

### Exclamations with `dá`

These express unreal conditions where the consequence is unstated, often implying a wish.
- *Dá mbeadh a fhios agat!* (If you only knew!)

### Exclamations with `má`

Surprisingly, `má` in an exclamation can have a negative meaning, equivalent to `ní` (not).
- *Ar m’anam má d’aithin mé féin iad!* (By my soul, I did not recognise them myself!)
- *Agus má ba thaise duit!* (And you are no less! / You are just the same.)

### Exclamations with `mura`

A real conditional clause with `mura` can be used to make a sarcastic remark.
- *Mura glic atá tú!* (Well, if you're not clever! / You're a really clever one!)

## Multiple Conditional Sentences

When several conditions are listed in a sequence, only the first one is introduced by `má`, `dá`, or `mura`. Subsequent conditions are joined by `agus` and take one of the following forms:

-   **An infinitive construction**: Using the verbal noun, sometimes with `bheith` omitted.
    -   After `mura`, this construction is also negative.
    -   After `má` or `dá`, it is positive unless negated with `gan`.
-   **A `go`/`nach`-clause**: This form is not used after `mura`.
    -   In real conditions, the future tense is used here if a future meaning is intended.
    -   In unreal conditions, the conditional mood is always used.
-   **The imperative**: In Conamara, a further unreal condition can be added without a conjunction, using a verb in the imperative mood.
