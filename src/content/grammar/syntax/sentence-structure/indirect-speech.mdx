Indirect speech, also known as reported speech, is used to report what someone else has said without using a direct quotation. This involves transforming the original statement into a subordinate clause, which often requires changes to tenses, pronouns, and adverbs.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| _"Tá sé ag cur an cóta air," arsa mé._ <br/> "He's putting on his coat," I said. | _Dúirt mé gur chuir sé an cóta air._ <br/> I said that he put on his coat. |
| _"Cuir ort an cóta!" ar sise._ <br/> "Put on your coat!" she said. | _Dúirt sí leis an cóta a chur air._ <br/> She told him to put on his coat. |

When converting direct speech to indirect speech, several grammatical rules apply.

## The Verb 'to say'

In direct speech, the defective verb `ar` or `arsa` is often used. It is placed after the quotation and is not specific to any tense.

-   `ar` is used with 3rd person pronouns (`ar sé`, `ar sí`, `ar siad`).
-   `arsa` is used with all other pronouns and nouns (`arsa mise`, `arsa Seán`).

However, `ar`/`arsa` cannot be used in indirect speech. Instead, a form of the verb `abair` (to say) is required.

-   _Dúirt sé go..._ (He said that...)
-   _Deir sí go..._ (She says that...)

Other verbs like `fiafraigh` (to ask), `ordú` (to command), or `smaoinigh` (to think) can also introduce indirect speech.

## Sequence of Tenses (An Seicheamh Aimseartha)

The tense of the verb in the reported clause often changes if the main reporting verb (e.g., `dúirt`) is in a past tense. This is known as the sequence of tenses.

If the main verb is in a non-past tense (e.g., present `deir`), the tense in the reported clause does not change.

When the main verb is in a **past tense**, the tenses in the reported clause shift as follows:

| Tense in Direct Speech | Tense in Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| Present | Preterite (Past) |
| Habitual Present | Imperfect |
| Future | Conditional |
| Present Subjunctive | Preterite Subjunctive, Conditional, or Verbal Noun |

**Examples:**

-   **Present → Preterite**
    -   Direct: _"**Tá** Tomás istigh," a dúirt sé._ ("Tomás **is** inside," he said.)
    -   Indirect: _Dúirt sé go **raibh** Tomás istigh._ (He said that Tomás **was** inside.)
-   **Future → Conditional**
    -   Direct: _"**Beidh** mé ann," a dúirt sí._ ("I **will be** there," she said.)
    -   Indirect: _Dúirt sí go **mbeadh** sí ann._ (She said that she **would be** there.)

If a past tense (Preterite, Imperfect) is already used in the direct speech, no change is necessary.

### Exceptions and Notes

-   **Habitual Present**: If a habitual state described in the present tense is still true at the time of reporting, the tense may not shift.
    -   _Dúirt sé go **mbíonn** Tomás istigh ag meán lae._ (He said that Tomás is usually inside at midday. — This is still true.)
    -   _Dúirt sé go **mbíodh** Tomás istigh ag meán lae._ (He said that Tomás used to be inside at midday. — This is no longer true.)
-   **Perfect Tenses**: Perfect tenses are formed with `bí`. The tense of `bí` shifts according to the rules above. For example, a present perfect (`tá sé tar éis...`) becomes a pluperfect (`bhí sé tar éis...`).

## Conjunctions and Particles

Indirect statements are typically introduced by the conjunctions `go` (that) or `nach` (that not).

-   _Dúirt sé **go** mbeadh sé ann._ (He said that he would be there.)
-   _Dúirt sí **nach** raibh sé ann._ (She said that he was not there.)

In copula sentences, the forms `gur` (that is/was) and `nach` (that is/was not) are used.

-   _Dúirt sé **gur** mhaith leis tae._ (He said that he wanted tea.)
-   _Deir sí **nach** maith léi tae._ (She says that she doesn't like tea.)

## Indirect Questions

Indirect questions are introduced by the relevant interrogative word (`an`, `cé`, `cad`, `cá`, etc.).

-   _D’fhiafraigh sí de **ar** mhaith leis tae._ (She asked him **if** he wanted tea.)
-   _D’fhiafraigh sí de **cá** raibh an cailín óg._ (She asked him **where** the young girl was.)

Indirect "whether... or not" questions use `cé acu` or `an` in the first part and `nó nach` in the second.

-   _D’fhiafraigh sí de **cé acu** a bhí sé sásta **nó nach** raibh._ (She asked him whether he was satisfied or not.)

## Conditional Clauses

When reporting real conditional clauses (referring to the future) and the main verb is in the past, the structure changes.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| `má` + Habitual Present | `dá` + Conditional |
| `mura` + Future | `mura` + Conditional |
| `mura` + Present Subjunctive | `mura` + Conditional |

-   Direct: _"**Má bhíonn** am agam, déanfaidh mé é."_ ("**If I have** time, I will do it.")
-   Indirect: _Dúirt sé **dá mbeadh** am aige, go ndéanfadh sé é._ (He said that **if he had** time, he would do it.)

In Munster Irish, the change from `má` to `dá` is often omitted, so `má` is used with the conditional.

For conditional clauses about the present or past, `má` is retained in indirect speech, and only the tense shifts according to the general rules.

## Commands (Imperative)

An imperative in direct speech is **always** converted into a verbal noun construction in indirect speech, regardless of the tense of the main verb.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| Imperative | Verbal Noun |
| `ná` + Imperative | `gan` + Verbal Noun |

-   Direct: _"**Imigh**!" ar sé le Pól._ ("**Leave**!" he said to Paul.)
-   Indirect: _Dúirt sé le Pól **imeacht**._ (He told Paul **to leave**.)
-   Direct: _"**Ná caitear** tobac!"_ ("One must not smoke!")
-   Indirect: _Dúirt sé **gan** tobac **a chaitheamh**._ (He said not to smoke.)

## Adverbs of Time and Place

If the main reporting verb is in the past, adverbs of time and place often need to be adjusted to reflect the shift in perspective.

### Adverbs of Time

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `inniu` (today) | `an lá sin` | that day |
| `inné` (yesterday) | `an lá roimhe sin` | the day before that |
| `amárach` (tomorrow) | `an lá arna mhárach` | the following day |
| `anocht` (tonight) | `an oíche sin` | that night |
| `aréir` (last night) | `an oíche roimhe sin` | the night before that |
| `i mbliana` (this year) | `an bhliain sin` | that year |

### Adverbs of Place & Demonstratives

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| `anseo` (here) | `ansin`, `ansiúd`, `ann` (there) |
| `seo` (this) | `sin`, `siúd`, `úd` (that) |

-   Direct: _"Bhí sé **anseo**," arsa Seán._ ("He was **here**," said Seán.)
-   Indirect: _Dúirt Seán go raibh sé **ansin**._ (Seán said that he had been **there**.)

## Pronouns

Pronouns must be adjusted to match the context of the reported speech. For example, `mé` (I) might become `sé` (he) or `sí` (she).

A specific rule applies to emphatic and reflexive pronouns when the subject is in the 3rd person.

-   In indirect speech, `sé féin` / `sí féin` is **reflexive**, meaning it refers back to the subject of the reporting verb (the person who spoke).
-   To refer to a different person emphatically, `seisean` / `sise` is used.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| _"Rinne **mise** é," a dúirt Seán._ ("**I** did it," said Seán.) | _Dúirt Seán go ndearna **sé féin** é._ (Seán said that **he himself** did it.) | `sé féin` = Seán |
| _"Rinne **seisean** é," a dúirt Seán._ ("**He** did it," said Seán.) | _Dúirt Seán go ndearna **seisean** é._ (Seán said that **he** did it.) | `seisean` ≠ Seán |
| _"Rinne **sé féin** é," a dúirt Seán._ ("**He himself** did it," said Seán.) | _Dúirt Seán go ndearna **seisean** é._ (Seán said that **he** did it.) | `sé féin` in direct speech is just emphasis. In indirect speech, `seisean` is used because it does not refer to Seán. |
