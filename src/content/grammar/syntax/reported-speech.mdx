Indirect speech, also known as reported speech, is used to report what someone has said or thought without using a direct quotation. This involves changing the original statement into a subordinate clause, which often requires adjustments to verbs, pronouns, and adverbs.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| *“Tá sé ag cur an cóta air,” arsa mé.* <br/> "He's putting on his coat," I said. | *Dúirt mé gur chuir sé an cóta air.* <br/> I said that he put on his coat. |
| *“Cuir ort an cóta!” ar sise.* <br/> "Put on your coat!" she said. | *Dúirt sí leis an cóta a chur air.* <br/> She told him to put on his coat. |
| *Dúirt mé, “Cuireadh Pól an cóta air!”* <br/> I said, "Paul should put on his coat!" | *Dúirt mise Pól an cóta a chur air.* <br/> I said that Paul should put on his coat. |

## The Verb 'to say'

In direct speech, the defective verb `ar` (with 3rd person pronouns) or `arsa` (with other pronouns and nouns) is often used. These forms are not specific to any tense and can mean "says" or "said".

However, `ar` and `arsa` cannot be used in indirect speech. Instead, a form of the verb `abair` (to say) is always required.

Additionally, `ar`/`arsa` can only appear *after* the direct quotation. If the reporting verb comes before the quotation, a form of `abair` is used even in direct speech. If a verb other than `ar`/`arsa` follows the direct speech, it is connected with the direct relative particle `a`.

- *“...” a deir sé* - "..." he says/said.
- *“...” a dúirt sé* - "..." he said.

<Note>
The form `a deir` is often used in a timeless sense, even if the surrounding narrative is in the past tense.
</Note>

Of course, other reporting verbs can be used instead of "to say", such as *to command, to ask, to request, to wish, to think,* or *to mean*.

## Tense Changes: The Sequence of Tenses

If the main reporting clause is in a present or future tense, there is no change to the tense or mood of the verb in the reported clause. The only exception is the imperative, which always changes (see below).

However, if the main clause is in a **past tense**, the verb in the reported clause must also shift to a corresponding past tense. This is known as the sequence of tenses (*an seicheamh aimseartha*).

The primary tenses are transformed as follows:

| Direct Speech Tense | Indirect Speech Tense |
| :--- | :--- |
| Present | Past (Preterite) |
| Habitual Present | Imperfect |
| Habitual Present (for future) | Conditional |
| Future | Conditional |
| Present Subjunctive | Past Subjunctive, Conditional, or Verbal Noun |

### Exceptions and Nuances

**Habitual Present**
If a statement in the habitual present is still true at the time of reporting, the tense does not need to change.

- *Dúirt sé go mbíonn Tomás istigh ag meán lae.* - He said that Tomás is usually indoors at midday. (This is still true.)
- *Dúirt sé go mbíodh Tomás istigh ag meán lae.* - He said that Tomás used to be indoors at midday. (This is no longer true.)

The simple present, however, is always transformed.
- *Dúirt sé go raibh Tomás istigh.* - He said that Tomás was indoors. (Tomás may or may not still be indoors.)

**Past Tenses**
If the verb in the direct speech is already in a past tense (e.g., preterite, imperfect), no further tense change is necessary.

**Perfect Tenses**
Perfect tenses are formed with the auxiliary verb `bí`. The tense of `bí` will shift according to the rules above, e.g., from present to preterite, or from future to conditional.

## Conjunctions and Particles

### Using `go` and `nach`

Indirect statements are typically introduced by the conjunctions `go` (that) or `nach` (that not).

- *Dúirt sé go mbeadh sé ann.* - He said he would be there.
- *Dúirt sí nach raibh sé ann.* - She said that he was not there.

### Copula Sentences

In sentences using the copula, the forms `gur` (that...is/was) and `nach` (that...is/was not) are used.

- *Dúirt sé gur mhaith leis tae.* - He said that he wanted tea.
- *Deir sí nach maith léi tae.* - She says that she doesn't like tea.

If the original sentence uses the emphatic `is ea` structure, `gur` may appear twice.
- *Dúirt sé gur múinteoir gurb ea é.* - He says that he is a teacher. (Direct: *Múinteoir is ea mé.*)

If an introductory conjunction already precedes `is ea`, `gur` is only needed before `ea`.
- *Dúirt sé nuair a d’imigh sé gurbh ea a tháinig Pól.* - He said that when he left, Paul came. (Direct: *Nuair a d’imigh mé is ea a...*)

### Causal Conjunctions

Causal conjunctions like `mar` (because) are followed by `go`/`nach` in indirect speech, even though they are not in direct speech.

- *Dúirt sé gur fhan sé sa leaba mar go raibh sé tinn.* - He said that he stayed in bed because he was ill. (Direct: *D’fhan mé sa leaba mar bhí mé tinn.*)

## Indirect Questions

Indirect questions are introduced by the relevant interrogative word (`an`, `cé`, `cad`, `cá`, `conas`, etc.).

- *D’fhiafraigh sí de ar mhaith leis tae.* - She asked him if he wanted tea.
- *D’fhiafraigh sí de cá raibh an cailín óg.* - She asked him where the young girl was.

### Indirect 'Whether/Or' Questions

Questions involving "whether or not" are formed with `cé acu` or `an` in the first part and `nó nach` in the second.

- *D’fhiafraigh sí de cé acu a bhí sé sásta nó nach raibh.* - She asked him whether he was satisfied or not.

## Conditional Clauses

When reporting real conditional clauses that refer to the future, the conjunction and tense shift if the main verb is in the past. `má` (if) becomes `dá`, and the verb changes to the conditional mood.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech (after past tense verb) |
| :--- | :--- |
| `má` + Habitual Present | `dá` + Conditional |
| `mura` + Future | `mura` + Conditional |
| `mura` + Present Subjunctive | `mura` + Conditional |

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster Irish, the change from `má` to `dá` is often omitted, and `má` is used with the conditional.
</Dialect>

For real conditional clauses referring to the present or past, the conjunction `má` is retained in indirect speech. The verb tense changes according to the general sequence of tenses.

## The Imperative

The imperative is **always** transformed into a verbal noun construction in indirect speech, regardless of the tense of the main reporting verb.

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| Imperative | Verbal Noun |
| `ná` + Imperative | `gan` + Verbal Noun |

- *Dúirt sé le Pól imeacht.* - He told Paul to leave. (Direct: *Imigh!*)
- *Dúirt sé libh tae a ól.* - He told you to drink tea. (Direct: *Ólaigí tae!*)
- *Dúirt sé gan tobac a chaitheamh.* - He said that one should not smoke. (Direct: *Ná caitear tobac!*)

For a 1st or 3rd person imperative, the verbal noun construction includes a subject.
- *Dúirt sé leat Pól é a dhéanamh.* - He told you that Paul should do it. (Direct: *Déanadh Pól é!*)
- *Dúirt sí gan í sin a fheiceáil arís.* - She said that she should not see that again. (Direct: *Ná feicim sin arís!*)

## Changes to Adverbs and Pronouns

When the main reporting verb is in the past tense, adverbs and pronouns often need to be adjusted to reflect the change in perspective.

### Adverbs of Time

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `inniu` (today) | `an lá sin` | "that day" |
| `inné` (yesterday) | `an lá roimhe sin` | "the day before that" |
| `arú inné` (day before yesterday) | `dhá lá roimhe sin` | "two days before that" |
| `amárach` (tomorrow) | `an lá arna mhárach` | "the following day" |
| `arú amárach` (day after tomorrow) | `i gceann dhá lá` | "in two days' time" |
| `anocht` (tonight) | `an oíche sin` | "that night" |
| `aréir` (last night) | `an oíche roimhe sin` | "the night before that" |
| `i mbliana` (this year) | `an bhliain sin` | "that year" |
| `anuraidh` (last year) | `an bhliain roimhe sin` | "the year before that" |
| `an bhliain seo chugainn` (next year) | `an bhliain a bhí chugainn` | "the following year" |

Example:
- Direct: *“Ach beidh mé ann anocht,” arsa mé.* - "But I'll be there tonight," I said.
- Indirect: *Dúirt mé go mbeinn ann an oíche sin.* - I said that I would be there that night.

### Demonstratives

Demonstrative pronouns and adverbs shift from indicating proximity (`this`, `here`) to distance (`that`, `there`).

| Concept | Direct | Indirect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| here | `anseo` | `ansin`, `ansiúd`, `ann` |
| this | `seo` | `sin`, `siúd`, `úd` |

Example:
- Direct: *“Bhí sé anseo,” arsa Seán.* - "He was here," said Seán.
- Indirect: *Dúirt Seán go raibh sé ansin.* - Seán said that he had been there.

### Personal Pronouns

Pronouns are adjusted to match the perspective of the person reporting the speech. For example, `mé` (I) might become `sé` (he), and so on.

### Reflexive and Emphatic Pronouns

The pronoun `sé/sí féin` (himself/herself) is always reflexive in indirect speech, referring back to the subject of the reporting verb. This can cause confusion if it was used for simple emphasis in the direct speech.

<Warning>
Pay close attention to the difference between `sé féin` and the emphatic pronoun `seisean`. In indirect speech, `sé féin` refers to the speaker, while `seisean` refers to a different person.
</Warning>

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| *“Rinne seisean é,” a dúirt Seán.* <br/> "_He_ did it," said Seán. | *Dúirt Seán go ndearna seisean é.* <br/> Seán said that _he_ (someone else) did it. |
| *“Rinne sé féin é,” a dúirt Seán.* <br/> "_He himself_ did it," said Seán. | *Dúirt Seán go ndearna seisean é.* <br/> Seán said that _he_ (someone else) did it. |
| *“Rinne mise é,” a dúirt Seán.* <br/> "_I_ did it," said Seán. | *Dúirt Seán go ndearna sé féin é.* <br/> Seán said that _he_ (himself) did it. |
| *“Rinne mé féin é,” a dúirt Seán.* <br/> "_I myself_ did it," said Seán. | *Dúirt Seán go ndearna sé féin é.* <br/> Seán said that _he_ (himself) did it. |
