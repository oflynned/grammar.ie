The Irish copula, `is`, is used to link a subject to a predicate noun or adjective. Its sentences fall into two main types: identification sentences and classification sentences. These types have different grammatical structures.

## Identification Sentences (`An Abairt Ionannais`)

An identification sentence equates the subject and predicate, stating that they are one and the same (X = Y). It typically involves a **definite** predicative noun, answering the question "X is *the* Y?".

### With a Definite Noun as Subject

**Standard Word Order: Predicate before Subject**

When the predicate is a definite noun (e.g., one with an article like `an`), a pronoun `é`, `í`, or `iad` must be inserted between the copula and the predicate. This pronoun is called a **sub-predicate** (`fofhaisnéis`) and agrees in gender and number with the predicate.

- `í an bhean` (the woman)
- `é mo theach` (my house)
- `iad na fir` (the men)
- `iad an fear agus an bhean` (the man and the woman)

The basic structure is `Copula + Sub-predicate + Predicate + Subject`.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + é/í/iad + P + S` | *Is é an sagart an fear.* | The man is the priest. |
| | `Ní + hé/hí/hiad + P + S` | *Ní hé an dochtúir an fear.* | The man is not the doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + é/í/iad + P + S` | *Ba í an dochtúir an bhean.* | The woman was the doctor. |
| | `Níorbh + é/í/iad + P + S` | *Níorbh é an dochtúir an bhean.* | The woman was not the doctor. |

**Alternative Word Order: Subject before Predicate**

In certain cases, the subject can come before the main predicate. The sub-predicate `é/í/iad` still appears after the copula, so the structure becomes `Copula + Sub-predicate + Subject + Predicate`.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + é/í/iad + S + P` | *Is é mo bharúil go …* | It is my opinion that… |
| | `Ní + hé/hí/hiad + S + P` | *Ní hé mo bharúil go …* | It is not my opinion that… |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + é/í/iad + S + P` | *Ba é mo bharúil go …* | It was my opinion that… |
| | `Níorbh + é/í/iad + S + P` | *Níorbh é mo bharúil go …* | It was not my opinion that… |

**Notes on the Sub-predicate**

- If the predicate is a `go`-clause or a verbal noun phrase, the sub-predicate is always `é`, regardless of the subject's gender.
  - *Is é mo bharúil go…* (My opinion is that...) — `é` is used even though `barúil` is feminine.
- With a noun as the predicate, the sub-predicate agrees with the gender of the subject.
  - *Is é an chéad chrann a tharla dó saileach mhór.* (The first tree he encountered was a large willow.) — `é` agrees with the masculine subject `crann`, not the feminine predicate `saileach`.
- When referring to people, the sub-predicate agrees with the natural gender of the person.
  - *Is í Máire an múinteoir.* (Máire is the teacher.) — `í` agrees with Máire.

**When to Use the Subject-before-Predicate Order**

This structure is used in specific situations, often for emphasis, forming what is known as a cleft sentence.

- **To emphasise a noun predicate:** The subject is usually a definite noun, often followed by a relative clause. The word `rud` (thing) is frequently used as a general subject. In Munster, `ná` (or `ach`) often introduces the main predicate.
  - *Is é an rud a chonaic mé ná capall.* (What I saw was a horse.)
  - *Is é an t-ainm a bhí air ná Seán.* (The name he had was Seán.)
  - In Connacht, `is éard` can be contracted to `séard`: *Séard a chonaic mé capall.* (What I saw was a horse.)
- **For definitions:** Cleft sentences are common for defining terms.
  - *Is é rud is crann ann ná planda ard adhmadach.* (A tree is a tall woody plant. Lit: What a tree is, is...)
  - *Is é atá sa bhéaloideas ná scéalta...* (What folklore is, are stories...)
- **When the predicate is a subordinate clause** (e.g., a `go`-clause) or a verbal noun phrase.
  - *Is é mo bharúil go bhfuil ceart agat.* (It is my opinion that you are right.)
  - *Is é m’aidhm Gaeilge a thuiscint.* (My goal is to understand Irish.)
- **When the subject is a proper noun.**
  - *Is é Seán an dochtúir.* (Seán is the doctor.)
- **When the subject is a demonstrative pronoun** (`seo`, `sin`, `siúd`).
  - *Is é sin an dochtúir.* (That is the doctor.)
  - *Is é seo Seán Ó Néill.* (This is Seán Ó Néill.)

### With 1st/2nd Person Pronouns (`mé`, `tú`, etc.)

When the subject is a 1st or 2nd person pronoun, it usually comes before the predicate.

**Pronoun as Subject**

This form focuses on the predicate. It is common in questions but less so in statements.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + pron. + P` | *Is mé an dochtúir.* | I am the doctor. |
| | `Ní + pron. + P` | *Ní mé an dochtúir.* | I am not the doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + pron. + P` | *Ba mé an dochtúir.* | I was the doctor. |
| | `Níor + pron. + P` | *Níor mé an dochtúir.* | I was not the doctor. |

**Pronoun as Predicate**

This form, which uses emphatic pronouns (`mise`, `tusa`), is more common and focuses on the pronoun.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + pron. + S` | *Is mise an dochtúir.* | **I** am the doctor. |
| | `Ní + pron. + S` | *Ní mise an dochtúir.* | **I** am not the doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + pron. + S` | *Ba mise an dochtúir.* | **I** was the doctor. |
| | `Níor + pron. + S` | *Níor mise an dochtúir.* | **I** was not the doctor. |

### With 3rd Person Pronouns (`é`, `í`, `iad`)

**Pronoun as Subject**

When a 3rd person pronoun is the subject, it appears at the end of the sentence. The sentence also requires a sub-predicate after the copula, which agrees with the subject pronoun.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + é/í/iad + P + é/í/iad` | *Is é an dochtúir é.* | He is the doctor. |
| | `Ní + hé/hí/hiad + P + é/í/iad` | *Ní hé an dochtúir é.* | He is not the doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + é/í/iad + P + é/í/iad` | *Ba é an dochtúir é.* | He was the doctor. |
| | `Níorbh + é/í/iad + P + é/í/iad` | *Níorbh é an dochtúir é.* | He was not the doctor. |

In some Ulster dialects, the final subject pronoun is omitted: *Is é an fear.* (He is the man.)

**Pronoun as Predicate**

To use a 3rd person pronoun as the predicate for emphasis, the emphatic form (`eisean`, `ise`, `iadsan`) is required.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + pron. + S` | *Is eisean an dochtúir.* | **He** is the doctor. |
| | `Ní + pron. + S` | *Ní heisean an dochtúir.* | **He** is not the doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + pron. + S` | *Ba eisean an dochtúir.* | **He** was the doctor. |
| | `Níorbh + pron. + S` | *Níorbh eisean an dochtúir.* | **He** was not the doctor. |

## Classification Sentences (`An Abairt Aicme`)

A classification sentence assigns a subject to a class or group (X ∈ Y). It uses an **indefinite** predicative noun, answering the question "X is *a* Y?".

### With a Definite Subject

**Standard Form**

The structure is `Copula + Predicate + Sub-subject + Subject`. The pronoun `é/í/iad` here is a **sub-subject** (`fo-ainmní`) and can sometimes be omitted, especially in Ulster.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + P + é/í/iad + S` | *Is dochtúir é an fear.* | The man is a doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + P + é/í/iad + S` | *Ba dhochtúir é an fear.* | The man was a doctor. |

**Emphatic Form (Left-shift of Predicate)**

To emphasise the predicate, it can be moved to the front of the sentence. This form is standard in Munster.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `P + is + ea + S` | *Dochtúir is ea an fear.* | The man is a doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `P + ba + ea + S` | *Dochtúir ba ea an fear.* | The man was a doctor. |

### With a Personal Pronoun as Subject

**Standard Form**

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + P + pron.` | *Is dochtúir mé.* | I am a doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + P + pron.` | *Ba dhochtúir mé.* | I was a doctor. |

In Connacht and Ulster, the past tense is often formed with `bí` instead: *Bhí mé i mo dhochtúir.* (I was a doctor.)

**Emphatic Form (Left-shift of Predicate)**

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `P + is + ea + pron.` | *Dochtúir is ea mé.* | I am a doctor. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `P + ba + ea + pron.` | *Dochtúir ba ea mé.* | I was a doctor. |

### With an Indefinite Subject

When both subject and predicate are indefinite, the structure is simple.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + P + S` | *Is iasc bradán.* | A salmon is a fish. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + P + S` | *B’iasc bradán.* | A salmon was a fish. |

In proverbs, the copula is often omitted and the subject comes first: *Tír gan teanga tír gan anam.* (A country without a language is a country without a soul.)

## Usage with Adjectives

### Special Classification Form with an Adjective

To emphasise an adjective describing an indefinite predicate noun, a special structure is used. The adjective comes first, followed by the article `an` and the noun.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + Adj. + an + P + S` | *Is deas an duine é.* | He is a *nice* person. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + Adj. + an + P + S` | *Ba deas an duine é.* | He was a *nice* person. |

This structure is common in rhetorical questions: *Nach fuar an lá é?* (Isn't it a cold day?)

### With a Predicative Adjective

The copula can be used with a predicative adjective to describe a permanent or inherent quality.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + Adj. + (é/í/iad) + S` | *Is deas é an duine.* | The person is nice. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + Adj. + (é/í/iad) + S` | *Ba deas é an duine.* | The person was nice. |

The sub-subject `é/í/iad` adds nuance:
- *Is geal an solas.* (Light is bright.) — A general, inherent property.
- *Is geal é an solas.* (The light is bright!) — A specific observation.

This construction is common in specific contexts:
- **Subjective evaluation:** *Is deas é sin!* (That's nice!)
- **Equality:** *Is ionann iad.* (They are the same.) *Is cosúil go...* (It seems that...)
- **With verbal nouns:** *Is deacair a rá go...* (It is difficult to say that...)
- **Indefinite quantity:** *Is iomaí fear a bhí ann.* (Many a man was there.)
- **Comparatives and superlatives:** The comparative always requires the copula. *Is fearr mo charr.* (My car is better.)
- **Emphasis:** An adverb or adjective from a `bí` sentence can be moved to the front for emphasis. *Is cúramach a rinne sé an obair.* (He did the work carefully.)

### With Adjectives and Prepositions (Modal Expressions)

Many modal concepts like "like", "should", and "can" are expressed using the copula with an adjective and a prepositional pronoun.

| Tense | Form | Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `Is + Adj. + Prep. + ...` | *Is maith liom fuisce.* | I like whiskey. |
| **Past/Conditional** | `Ba + Adj. + Prep. + ...` | *Ba mhaith liom fuisce.* | I would like whiskey. |

The logical subject is part of the predicate (e.g., `liom` means "with me").
- `le` indicates a subjective assessment: *Is fada liom an tsráid.* (I find the street long.)
- `do` indicates an objective statement: *Is maith bainne duit.* (Milk is good for you.)

Nouns can also be used in this structure:
- *Is fiú duit é.* (It's worth it for you.)
- *Is dóigh liom.* (I think.)
- *Is fuath liom é.* (I hate it.)
- *Is féidir liom.* (I can.)

## The Copula in Different Clause Types

### In `go/nach` Clauses

| Tense | Positive | Negative | Example (Positive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `gur(b)` | `nach` | *...gur maith liom é.* (...that I like it.) |
| **Past/Conditional** | `gur(bh)` | `nár(bh)` | *...gur mhaith liom é.* (...that I would like it.) |

### In Relative Clauses

**Direct Relative Clause**

Used when the antecedent is the subject of the relative clause.

| Tense | Positive | Negative | Example (Positive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `is` | `nach` | *an rud is féidir liom* (the thing I can do) |
| **Past/Conditional** | `ba`/`ab` | `nár(bh)` | *an rud ab fhéidir liom* (the thing I could do) |

A special form `is ... ann` is used in questions and statements for definitions:
- *Cad is crann ann?* (What is a tree?)
- *Séard is crann ann ná planda...* (A tree is a plant...)

**Indirect Relative Clause**

Used when the antecedent has a different role (e.g., object) in the relative clause.

| Tense | Positive | Negative | Example (Positive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `ar(b)` | `nach` | *an bhean ar maith léi an bord* (the woman who likes the table) |
| **Past/Conditional** | `ar(bh)` | `nár(bh)` | *an bhean ar mhaith léi an bord* (the woman who liked the table) |

Classification and identification can be expressed this way:
- *an fear ar múinteoir é* (the man who is a teacher)
- *an fear arb é an múinteoir é* (the man who is the teacher)

### In Conditional and Optative Clauses

| Clause Type | Tense | Positive | Negative | Example (Positive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Real Conditional** | Present | `más` | `mura(b)` | *Más é an dochtúir é...* (If he is the doctor...) |
| | Past | `má ba` | `murar(bh)` | *Má ba é an dochtúir é...* (If he was the doctor...) |
| **Irreal Conditional** | Conditional | `dá mba` | `murar(bh)` | *Dá mba é an dochtúir é...* (If he were the doctor...) |
| **Optative (Wishes)** | Subjunctive | `gura(b)` | `nára(b)` | *Gurab amhlaidh duit!* (The same to you!) |

## Special Usages of the Copula

### Expressing Possession

The construction `is le` is used to state ownership of a definite noun.
- *Is le Pól an carr.* (The car belongs to Paul.)
- *Ba le Pól an carr.* (The car belonged to Paul.)
- Question: *Cé leis é?* (Whose is it?)

### Expressing Origin

The construction `is as` (or `is ó` in Munster) indicates where someone is from.
- *Is as an nGearmáin mé.* (I am from Germany.)
- *Is as an nGearmáin dom.* (I am from Germany. - lit. "is from Germany to-me")
- Question: *Cad as duit?* (Where are you from?)

### Naming

**Stating a Full Name**

This uses a cleft sentence structure where the name is fronted. The present tense `is` is often omitted.
- *(Is) Seán is ainm dom.* (My name is Seán.)
- *Deir sé gur Seán is ainm dó.* (He says his name is Seán.)
- Question: *Cad is ainm duit?* (What is your name?)

**Stating a Surname**

This uses the preposition `de`.
- *Is de Bhrianach mé.* (I am an O'Brien.)
- *Is de mhuintir Bhriain mé.* (I am of the O'Brien family.)
- Question: *Cér díobh thú?* (What is your surname?)

## The Copula in Questions

### Yes/No Questions

Special interrogative forms of the copula are used.

| Tense | Positive | Negative | Example (Positive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `an` | `nach` | *An eisean an dochtúir?* (Is he the doctor?) |
| **Past/Conditional** | `ar(bh)` | `nár(bh)` | *Ar dhochtúir é?* (Was he a doctor?) |

### Wh-Questions

**With `Cé` (Who), `Cad`/`Céard` (What)**

These interrogative pronouns often incorporate the copula (`cé` = "who is").
- *Cé thusa?* (Who are you?)
- *Cé hí an bhean sin?* (Who is that woman?)
- *Cad a rinne tú?* (What did you do?) - This is a cleft sentence.
- *Cad is fearr leat?* (What do you prefer?)

**With `Cá` (Where/How/What)**

- *Cá bhfuil tú?* (Where are you?) - `cá` + verb `bí`.
- *Cárbh fhéidir leat dul?* (Where could you go?) - `cá` + copula.
- *Cá hard é?* (How high is it?)
- *Cá haois thú?* (How old are you?)

### Answering Copula Questions

Irish has no single word for "yes" or "no". Instead, the answer repeats the predicate from the question with the appropriate copula form.

| Question Type | Answer "Yes" | Answer "No" | Example Question & Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Identification** (Is X *the* Y?) | `Is é/í/iad.` | `Ní hé/hí/hiad.` | *An é Pól an dochtúir? - Is é.* |
| **Classification** (Is X *a* Y?) | `Is ea.` (`Sea.`) | `Ní hea.` | *An dochtúir é Pól? - Sea.* |
| **With Adjective** | `Is` + Adj. | `Ní` + Adj. | *An deas an duine é? - Is deas.* |
| **With Adj. + Prep.** | `Is` + Adj. (+ pron.) | `Ní` + Adj. (+ pron.) | *An maith leat caife? - Is maith (liom).* |
| **Possession** (`le`) | `Is` + `liom`, etc. | `Ní` + `liom`, etc. | *An leatsa an leabhar? - Is liom.* |
| **Cleft Sentence** | `Is ea.` (`Sea.`) | `Ní hea.` | *An inné a rinne tú é? - Is ea.* |

## Usage with Demonstrative Pronouns

There are two main ways to say "This is..." or "That is...".

**Long Form**

This is a standard copula sentence where the demonstrative (`seo`, `sin`, `siúd`) follows a pronoun. It has a determining or identifying meaning.
- *Is é seo an múinteoir.* (This one is the teacher.)
- *Is múinteoir é seo.* (This is a teacher.)

**Short Form**

The demonstrative comes first and the copula `is` is omitted. This form has a more presentational meaning, like "Here is...".
- *Seo é an múinteoir.* (Here is the teacher.)
- *Sin fear.* (That is a man.)

In dialects (and now accepted in the standard), other copula forms can combine with the s-less forms of the demonstratives (`eo`, `in`, `iúd`).

| Form | Short Form Example | Long Form Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | *Seo é an fear.* | *Is é seo an fear.* | This is the man. |
| **Past** | *B’eo é an fear.* | *Ba é seo an fear.* | This was the man. |
| **Question** | *An eo é an fear?* | *An é seo an fear?* | Is this the man? |
| **"that..."** | *...gurb eo é an fear.* | *...gurb é seo an fear.* | ...that this is the man. |

## Emphasis in Copula Sentences

The copula is central to changing word order for emphasis.

### Cleft and Pseudo-Cleft Sentences

A copula sentence can itself be reordered for emphasis.
- **Cleft:** The emphasised element is fronted with `is`.
  - *Is maith liom í.* (I like her.) → *Is ise is maith liom.* (It is **her** I like.)
- **Pseudo-cleft:** Uses a structure like `Is é rud... ná...` to emphasise the final element.
  - *Is duine uasal tú.* (You are a gentleman.) → *Is é rud tú ná duine uasal.* (What you are is a gentleman.)

### Left-Dislocation

An element is moved to the very front of the sentence for topic-setting, followed by the original sentence with a pronoun in its place.

- **Predicate Dislocation (Classification):**
  - *Is dochtúir é an fear.* → *Dochtúir, is ea an fear.* (A doctor, that's what the man is.)
- **Subject Dislocation (Classification):**
  - *Is múinteoir é an fear.* → *An fear, is múinteoir é.* (The man, he is a teacher.)
- **Subject Dislocation (Identification):**
  - *Is é an múinteoir an fear.* → *An fear, is é an múinteoir é.* (The man, he is the teacher.)

## Alternatives to the Copula

The verb `bí` can be used with prepositions to form classification and identification sentences, often to express a temporary state or role.

### Classification with `bí` + `i`

This is a very common alternative to a copula classification sentence.
- **Structure 1: `Tá [Subject] i + [poss. pron.] + [Predicate]`**
  - *Tá sí ina dochtúir.* (She is a doctor. lit: She is in her doctor.)
  - *Bí i d'fhear!* (Be a man!)
- **Structure 2 (Emphatic): `[Predicate] atá i + [pron./noun]`**
  - *Dochtúir atá inti.* (She is a **doctor**.)
  - *Fear atá ionat.* (You are a **man**.)

This construction is used for temporary roles or when a copula tense is unavailable (e.g., future, imperative).

### Other Prepositions with `bí`

- **`ar` (on):** For "one of the..." or superlatives.
  - *Tá sí ar na cailíní is áille.* (She is one of the most beautiful girls.)
  - *Tá sé ar an duine is cliste.* (He is the cleverest person.)
- **`mar` (as/like):**
  - *Bhí sé mar athair agam.* (He was like a father to me.)
- **`de` (of):** In equative sentences.
  - *Tá an teach chomh mór de theach agus a chonaic mé riamh.* (The house is as big a house as I have ever seen.)

## Miscellaneous Constructions

### Subjectless Copula Sentences

In some constructions, the logical subject is introduced by the preposition `do` instead of being in the nominative case.
- *Is as Doire dom.* (I am from Derry.)
- *An ann do Dhia?* (Does God exist?)
- *Cá haois duit?* (How old are you?)

### Idiomatic Expressions

- **`Ní X go Y`**: "There is no X without Y" or "It isn't X until it's Y".
  - *Ní féasta go rósta.* (It's no feast without a roast.)
  - *Ní neart go cur le chéile.* (There is no strength without unity.)
- **`Ní X go dtí Y`**: "There is no X like Y".
  - *Níor bhean go dtí í.* (There was no woman like her.)
  - *Ní haoibhneas go dtí é.* (There is no joy like it.)
