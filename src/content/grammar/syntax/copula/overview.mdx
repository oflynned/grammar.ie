In Irish, there are two distinct words that translate to the English verb "to be": the substantive verb `bí` and the copula `is`. They serve different grammatical functions and are not interchangeable.

-   The **substantive verb `bí`** (present tense `tá`) describes existence, states, or locations. It acts as a true verb.
-   The **copula `is`** is a particle that links a subject to a predicate noun or pronoun. It is used for classification and identification.

Understanding the difference between `bí` and `is` is fundamental to forming correct sentences in Irish.

## The Substantive Verb `bí`

The verb `bí` expresses existence in a particular state or location. Its core meaning is "to be there" or "to exist". It is often used with prepositions to describe where something is or what condition it is in.

*Táim.* - I am. (i.e., I exist, I am here.)
*Tá daoine ann.* - There are people. / People exist.

The word `ann` is the 3rd person masculine form of the preposition `i` (`in`), meaning "in it". It is often used idiomatically to mean "there" or "in existence".

### Describing States and Locations

`Bí` is used with prepositions and adjectives to describe temporary states, conditions, and locations.

-   *Tá sé sa teach.* - He is in the house.
-   *Tá sé ar an mbord.* - It is on the table.
-   *Tá sé ina sheasamh.* - He is standing. (Literally: "He is in his standing.")
-   *Tá sé ina chónaí.* - He lives. (Literally: "He is in his living.")
-   *Tá sé fuar.* - It is cold.

### Describing a Role or Profession

A common construction with `bí` is `i` + possessive pronoun + noun. This describes someone being in the state or role of something, often temporarily.

-   *Tá sé ina dhochtúir.* - He is a doctor. (He works as a doctor.)
-   *Tá sé ina fhear anois.* - He is a man now. (He is now in the state of being a man.)

### Expressing Measure

`Bí` is used without a preposition to express measurements like age, weight, size, and time.

-   *Tá sí seacht mbliana déag d’aois.* - She is 17 years old.
-   *Tá sé ceathrú chun a trí.* - It is a quarter to three.

### As an Auxiliary Verb

`Bí` also functions as an auxiliary verb, most notably in the progressive aspect to describe an ongoing action. This usage still carries the sense of being in a temporary state.

-   *Tá sé ag ól.* - He is drinking (right now).

## The Copula `is`

The copula `is` does not describe a state. Its function is to link a subject with a predicate, creating a sentence of classification (X is a type of Y) or identification (X is Y). It corresponds to the equals sign (=) or the set membership symbol (∈) in mathematics.

-   **Classification:** *Is dochtúir é.* - He is a doctor. (He belongs to the class of 'doctors'.)
-   **Identification:** *Is é an dochtúir é.* - He is the doctor. (He is identified as that specific doctor.)

It is grammatically incorrect to use `tá` for this purpose. You cannot say `*Tá mé dochtúir`.

## Comparing `is` and `bí`

While both `Is dochtúir é` and `Tá sé ina dhochtúir` can be translated as "He is a doctor," they have different nuances.

| Construction | Example | Meaning & Nuance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copula `is`** | *Is dochtúir é.* | **Classification**: A statement of fact or permanent identity. "He is classified as a doctor." |
| **Verb `bí`** | *Tá sé ina dhochtúir.* | **State**: A description of a role or condition, which may be temporary. "He is in the state of being a doctor." |

The distinction becomes clearer in the past tense.

-   *Ba mhúinteoir é.* - He was a teacher. (Copula `ba`). This implies a permanent classification that has ended, often meaning the person is no longer alive.
-   *Bhí sé ina mhúinteoir.* - He was a teacher. (Verb `bhí`). This implies he was in the state of being a teacher but has since changed profession or retired.

However, this distinction is not absolute. Adverbs can alter the context. For example, `Is fear é anois` ("He is a man now") uses the copula with a temporal adverb.

## Usage with Adjectives

### Adjectives with `bí`

In modern Irish, predicative adjectives are most commonly used with `bí`. This implies a state, which is often temporary.

-   *Tá an fear tinn.* - The man is ill.
-   *Tá sé te.* - It is hot.

For some subjectively evaluative adjectives, `go` is used.

-   *Tá sé go maith.* - It is good.
-   *Tá sé go dona.* - It is bad.

Traditionally, adjectives describing permanent qualities (like size, length, or colour) were not used predicatively with `bí`. Instead, an attributive construction with the copula was preferred.

-   *Is fear mór é.* - He is a big man. (Instead of `*Tá an fear mór.`)
-   *Is bóthar fada é.* - It is a long road. (Instead of `*Tá an bóthar fada.`)

However, this rule is relaxed if the adjective is modified by a prefix like `an-` (very) or `ró-` (too), or an adverb.

-   *Tá an bord ró-mhór.* - The table is too big.
-   *Tá an t-éan an-bheag.* - The bird is very small.

Adjectives ending in `-ach`, `-úil`, `-mhar`, and verbal adjectives can always be used with `bí`.

-   *Tá sé tuirseach.* - He is tired.
-   *Tá sé buailte.* - He is beaten.

### Adjectives with the Copula

Using the copula with a predicative adjective is less common in modern Irish. When it occurs, it describes a permanent, inherent quality and often has an exclamatory feel, emphasising the adjective.

-   *Is maith é!* - He is good! / How good he is!
-   *Tá sé go maith.* - He is good. (A simple statement of his state.)

## Key Functions of the Copula

The copula is essential for several common grammatical structures:

-   **Classification and Identification:** *Is dochtúir é.* (He is a doctor.)
-   **Origin and Possession:** *Is as Corcaigh é.* (He is from Cork.); *Is le Pól an carr.* (The car belongs to Paul.)
-   **Modal Expressions:** *Ba mhaith le Pól an carr a cheannach.* (Paul would like to buy the car.)
-   **Comparatives and Superlatives:** *an fear is mó* (the bigger/biggest man)
-   **Emphasis (Cleft Sentences):** *Is é an fear a dhéanann na bróga.* (It is the man who makes the shoes.)
-   **Questions with Interrogative Words:** *Cé a rinne sin?* (Who did that? Literally: "Who is it that did that?")

## Grammatical Peculiarities

The copula behaves differently from true verbs.

-   **Forms:** It has only two basic forms: `is` (present/future) and `ba` (past/conditional).
-   **No Imperative or Verbal Noun:** To say "Be a man!", you must use `bí`: *Bí i d’fhear!*
-   **Invariable:** The forms `is` and `ba` do not change for person or number.
-   **Pronouns:** It requires the disjunctive forms of 3rd person pronouns: `é`, `í`, `iad` (not `sé`, `sí`, `siad`).
-   **Fuses with Particles:** The copula often merges with particles like `ní` (not), `an` (question), and `go` (that). For example, `ní` is a fusion of `ní` + `is`.
-   **Word Order:** The standard order is **Copula - Predicate - Subject**.
-   **Definite Nouns:** A definite noun cannot immediately follow the copula. A pronoun (`é`, `í`, `iad`) must be inserted between them.
