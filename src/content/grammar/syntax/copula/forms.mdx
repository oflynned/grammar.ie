The copula has only a few basic forms: `is` for the present tense and `ba` for the past and conditional. There are also the subjunctive forms `gura` and `nára`, which are already fusions with other particles.

Most other forms are created when `is` and `ba` merge with particles, conjunctions, or prepositions. In some cases, the copula is omitted entirely, and another word takes on its function. For example, the negative particle `ní` is a fusion of an older `ní` + `is`. This also applies to many interrogative words, such as `cé` (who is).

The following table provides a comprehensive overview of these forms. Dialectal variants are shown in parentheses.

## Forms Before a Vowel

### The form `ba`

The past/conditional form `ba` changes to `b'` before most vowels.

-   **`ba` is used:**
    -   Before a consonant (except `fh`).
    -   Before the pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, `eisean`, `ise`, `iadsan`, and `éard`. Example: *Ba é an fear é.* (He was the man.)
    -   Before prepositions. Example: *Ba ar an mbord a chuir sé é.* (He put it on the table.)
    -   Before prepositional pronouns. Example: *Ba air a chuir sé é.* (He put it on it.)
    -   Before adverbs. Example: *Ba arú inné a tháinig sé.* (He came the day before yesterday.)
-   **`b'` is used:**
    -   Before nouns starting with a vowel or `fh`. Example: *B’fhear mór óil é.* (He was a great drinking man.)
    -   Before adjectives starting with a vowel. Example: *B’álainn an radharc é.* (It was a beautiful view.)
    -   Before adverbs derived from adjectives. Example: *B’annamh ólta é.* (He was rarely drunk.)
    -   Before the adverb `amhlaidh`. Example: *...agus b’amhlaidh an scéal i Ros Comáin.* (...and that was how the situation was in Roscommon.)
    -   Before the demonstrative pronouns `eo`, `in`, `iúd`. Example: *...agus b’eo ina ndiaidh é.* (...and off he went after them.)

### Forms ending in `-r`

For forms ending in `-r` (like `gur`, `murar`, `dar`), the form used before a vowel depends on the tense.

-   **In the present tense, `gur` becomes `gurb` before vowels in specific cases:**
    -   **`gur` (without `-b`)** is used before most nouns, prepositions, prepositional pronouns, and adverbs not derived from adjectives.
        -   *...gur amadán mé.* (...that I am a fool.)
        -   *...gur anseo atá mé.* (...that I am here.)
    -   **`gurb` (with `-b`)** is used before pronouns (`é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, etc.), demonstratives (`eo`, `in`, `iúd`), adjectives, and adverbs derived from adjectives.
        -   *...gurb é mo mhac é.* (...that he is my son.)
        -   *...gurb álainn an lá é.* (...that it is a beautiful day.)
    -   Other present tense forms ending in `-r` (e.g., `ar`, `dar`, `sular`) usually add a `-b` before a vowel (becoming `arb`, `darb`, `sularb`).
        -   *an fear darb ainm Seán* (a man named Seán)

-   **In the past tense, all forms ending in `-r` add `-bh` before a vowel.**
    -   This includes `gurbh`, `arbh`, `níorbh`, `cérbh`, `sularbh`, etc.
    -   *...gurbh amadán mé.* (...that I was a fool.)

### The form `ní`

The negative form `ní` causes an h-prefix before a vowel in specific cases:

-   Before pronouns: *Ní hé mo mhac é.* (He is not my son.)
-   Before some adjectives: *Ní hionann iad.* (They are not the same.)
-   Before a few nouns (e.g., `ionadh`): *Ní hionadh é sin.* (That is no wonder.)

It does not add an h-prefix before most nouns: *Ní amadán mé.* (I am not a fool.)

## Lenition After the Copula

-   There is **no lenition** after present and subjunctive forms of the copula.
-   There is **lenition** after all past and conditional forms.
    -   Example: `ba` + `maith` → `ba mhaith`.
    -   **Dialect Note:** In Ulster and Connacht, adjectives beginning with `t`, `d`, or `s` are often not lenited after `ba` (e.g., `ba deas`, `ba te`).

## Omission of the Copula

-   The present tense form `is` can be omitted before emphatic pronouns and nouns in identification sentences.
    -   *Is eisean an fear.* → *Eisean an fear.* (He is the man.)
-   In cleft sentences (for emphasis), `is` can be omitted before adjectives and must be omitted before indefinite nouns.
    -   *Is tuirseach atá sé.* → *Tuirseach atá sé.* (He is tired.)
    -   *Fear a rinne sin.* (A man did that.)
-   In exclamatory sentences, `is` is always omitted.
    -   *Grá mo chroí thú!* (You are the love of my heart!)
-   The past tense form `ba` is rarely omitted, but can be in some Munster dialects.
    -   *...a cheart a thabhairt air* (instead of *...ba cheart...*) (...one should call it...)

## Dialectal Forms and Other Notes

-   **Pronunciation of `is`**: Usually pronounced [əs]. The `i` is often dropped in speech when followed by pronouns like `é`, `í`, `iad`, leading to forms written as `sé`, `sí`, `siad` or `’sé`, `’sí`, `’siad`.
-   **`dob’` / `do`**: In Munster, `do` + `b’` is sometimes used, e.g., `dob é` for `ba é`.
-   **Connemara forms**: `ar` or `ab` are often used for the question particle `an`. Past/conditional forms like `ní ba`, `an mba`, `go mba` are common instead of the standard forms with `-r`.
-   **Ulster forms**: The negative particle `cha` (`chan` before vowels) is used instead of `ní`. Generalised forms like `is é`, `ní hé` are used regardless of the gender or number of the subject.
-   **Prepositional combinations**: Forms like `lenar` (`le` + `ar`) and `dar` (`de`/`do` + `ar`) are fusions of a preposition, a relative pronoun, and the copula. They are used in positive indirect relative clauses. Example: *an fear lenar maith é* (the man who likes it).
-   **`níos` variants**: The comparative form `níos` has several dialectal variants, including `níosa` (Munster), `nas` (Ulster), and `tíos`/`díos` (Connemara).
-   **Historical forms**: An old future form, `budh`, is now obsolete.
