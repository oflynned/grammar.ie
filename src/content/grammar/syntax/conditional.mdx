Conditional sentences describe a condition and its consequence, following an "if... then..." structure. In Irish, these sentences consist of two parts: the **protasis** (`an céadbheart`), which is the 'if' clause, and the **apodosis** (`an t-iarbheart`), which is the 'then' clause. The 'if' clause is usually introduced by `má` or `dá` ("if"), or `mura(r)` ("if not"). The 'then' clause typically has no introductory word.

Like English, the order of these clauses is flexible. Irish grammar distinguishes between two main types of conditional sentences: real and unreal.

## Real Conditional Sentences

Real conditional sentences, or `an clásal coinníollach oscailte`, are used when the condition is true, was true, will be true, or is considered possible or probable.

The condition is introduced by `má` for positive statements and `mura(r)` for negative ones.

*   **Positive:** *má fheiceann tú an fear* - if you see the man
*   **Negative:** *murar ól tú fuisce* - if you have not drunk whiskey

After `má`, you must use the independent form of the verb, including the `d'` prefix in the past tense. After `mura`, use the dependent form.

<Warning>
The negative conjunction `mura` is used for both real and unreal conditions. You can tell them apart by the verb form that follows: real conditions use the indicative (`mura dtuigeann sé` - if he does not understand), while unreal conditions use the conditional or subjunctive (`mura dtuigfeadh sé` - if he did not understand).
</Warning>

<Dialect>
  Dialectal variants for `mura` and `murar` include `mara`, `marar`, `muna`, and `munar`.
</Dialect>

### The 'If' Clause (Protasis)

The verb in the 'if' clause is usually in the indicative mood.

A important rule is that `má` is not followed by the future tense. To express a future condition, use the present tense instead.

*   *Má thagann sé amárach, beidh mé anseo.* - If he comes tomorrow, I will be here.

However, after the negative `mura`, the future tense can be used. The present subjunctive can also be used after `mura` to express future uncertainty.

*   *Cad a dhéanfaidh tú mura dtagann an traein?* - What will you do if the train does not come? (Present tense for future)
*   *Cad a dhéanfaidh tú mura dtiocfaidh an traein?* - What will you do if the train does not come? (Future tense)
*   *Cad a dhéanfaidh tú mura dtaga an traein?* - What will you do if the train does not come? (Present subjunctive)

<Note>
In rare cases, the conditional mood can appear after `má` if the verb form is necessary but the condition is still considered 'real'. For example, some modal verbs use the conditional form to express a past ability.
- *Agus má thiocfadh leis obair a dhéanamh, thiocfadh léise fanacht.* - And if he could work, then she could wait.
</Note>

### The 'Then' Clause (Apodosis)

The 'then' clause can be in several moods, depending on the intended meaning.

*   **Indicative:** This is the most common form.
    *   *Má tá do chos briste, caithfidh tú fanacht anseo.* - If your leg is broken, you must stay here.
*   **Imperative:** Used for commands.
    *   *Má tá do chos briste, ná héirigh suas!* - If your leg is broken, do not get up!
*   **Present Subjunctive:** Used for wishes or prayers.
    *   *Má tá do chos briste, go bhfóire Dia ort.* - If your leg is broken, may God help you.

To express particular certainty that the consequence will happen, you can use the "indicative of certainty". This involves using the present tense of `bí` or a perfect construction where a future tense would normally be expected.

*   *Má chloistear thú, tá tú gafa.* - If you are heard, you will certainly be caught.

### Implicative Conditions

For conditions that mean "whenever" or "if ever", the temporal conjunction `nuair` ("when") followed by a direct relative clause is often used instead of `má`.

## Unreal Conditional Sentences

Unreal conditional sentences, or `an clásal coinníollach iata`, are used for hypothetical situations or when a condition is impossible, counter-factual, or very unlikely.

The condition is introduced by `dá` for positive statements and `mura` for negative ones.

*   **Positive:** *dá bhfeicteá an fear* - if you were to see the man
*   **Negative:** *mura bhfeicteá an fear* - if you were not to see the man

### Mutations and Verb Forms

The conjunctions `dá` and `mura` both cause eclipsis and require the dependent form of the verb.

### The 'If' Clause (Protasis)

The verb in an unreal 'if' clause can take several forms.

*   **Subjunctive Preterite:** This is the older standard form, which is identical in form to the imperfect indicative. It is introduced by `dá` or `mura`.
*   **Conditional:** Today, the conditional mood is more commonly used and is synonymous with the subjunctive preterite.
    *   *dá bhfeicteá é* (subjunctive preterite) = *dá bhfeicfeá é* (conditional) = if you were to see him.

Unreal conditional sentences are indifferent to time. The same structure can refer to the past, present, or future.

*   *Dá bhfeicfeá an fear inné/inniu/amárach...* - If you had seen / were to see the man yesterday/today/tomorrow...

<Dialect region="Connacht">
  In Connacht, the 'if' clause can be expressed using the imperative mood without any conjunction.
  - *Teagadh sé abhaile, chuirfeá fáilte roimhe.* - If he were to come home, you would welcome him.

  If the clauses are reversed, `agus` is used to introduce the condition.
  - *Chuirfeá fáilte roimhe agus teagadh sé abhaile.* - You would welcome him if he were to come home.
</Dialect>

### The 'Then' Clause (Apodosis)

The 'then' clause of an unreal sentence is almost always in the **conditional mood**.

The main exception is the "indicative of certainty", which uses a past tense to vividly emphasize that the consequence would have been certain.

*   *Dá bhfáiscfeá a thuilleadh mise, bhí mé marbh agat!* - If you had squeezed me any more, you would have killed me!
*   *Dá mbéadh sé sa teach nuair a dódh é, bhí sé marbh.* - If he had been in the house when it burned, he would be dead.

A pluperfect meaning ("would have been") can be formed with the past tense of `bí` and a verbal adjective.

*   *Murach í, bhí sé imithe.* - If it were not for her, he would certainly have gone.

## Concessive Conditional Sentences

Conditional sentences can have a concessive meaning, like "even if" or "although". These are often marked by adding `féin` or `fiú amháin` to the 'if' clause.

In these sentences, the connection between the clauses is looser; the consequence happens *despite* the condition, not because of it. This affects the grammar:

*   In a real concessive sentence, the future tense can be used after `má` (which is not allowed in a standard real conditional).
*   In an unreal concessive sentence, the 'then' clause can be in the indicative or imperative, not just the conditional.

## Exclamatory Conditional Sentences

Conditional structures can be used as exclamations, where their meaning changes slightly.

### Dá for Wishes

An exclamation with `dá` expresses an unreal wish or a condition where the consequence is left unstated. It often translates as "If only...".

*   *Dá mbeadh a fhios agat!* - If you only knew!

### Má with Negative Meaning

In certain exclamations, `má` surprisingly takes on a negative meaning, equivalent to `ní` ("not").

*   *Ar m’anam má d’aithin mé féin iad!* - By my soul, I did not recognise them myself! (Literally: "if I recognised...")
*   *...agus má ba thaise duit!* - ...and you are no less! / ...and you are just the same!

### Mura for Sarcasm

A real conditional sentence with `mura` can be used to make a sarcastic remark.

*   *Mura glic atá tú!* - Well, if you aren't clever! (Meaning: You're a really clever one!)

## Multiple Conditions

When listing several conditions in a row, only the first one is introduced by `má`, `dá`, or `mura`. Subsequent conditions are joined by `agus` ("and") and can take one of the following forms:

*   **An infinitive construction:** This can use the verbal noun directly, or be a "small clause" where the verbal noun `bheith` is omitted. After `má`/`dá`, these are positive unless negated with `gan`. After `mura`, they are implicitly negative.
*   **A `go`/`nach` clause:** In a real conditional, the future tense can be used after `go`/`nach` even if the first clause used the present. In an unreal conditional, the conditional mood is always used.
*   **The imperative (Connacht):** In Conamara, a further unreal condition can be added using a verb in the imperative mood without a conjunction.
