Irish has several different ways of using numbers depending on the context. You will use one form for abstract counting ("one, two, three..."), another for counting nouns ("one boat, two boats"), a special set for counting people, and another for ordinal numbers ("first, second, third"). This guide covers all four systems for the numbers 1 to 19.

## Abstract Counting (Maolúimhreacha)

When you are counting aloud without referring to any specific objects, you use the abstract numbers, or `maolúimhreacha`. These are usually preceded by the particle `a`.

- 1: `a haon`
- 2: `a dó`
- 3: `a trí`
- 4: `a ceathair`
- 5: `a cúig`
- 6: `a sé`
- 7: `a seacht`
- 8: `a hocht`
- 9: `a naoi`
- 10: `a deich`
- 11: `a haon déag`
- 12: `a dó dhéag`
- 13: `a trí déag`
- 14: `a ceathair déag`
- 15: `a cúig déag`
- 16: `a sé déag`
- 17: `a seacht déag`
- 18: `a hocht déag`
- 19: `a naoi déag`

<Tip>
The numbers from 11 to 19 are formed by adding `déag` to the numbers 1-9. This is similar to the "-teen" suffix in English.
</Tip>

## Counting Nouns (Bunuimhreacha)

When counting objects or things, you use the cardinal numbers, or `bunuimhreacha`. These numbers often cause a mutation (lenition or eclipsis) on the noun that follows, and the noun itself remains in the singular form.

### Numbers 1 and 2

The number one, `aon`, lenites the noun that follows. It is often used with `amháin` ("only" or "one") for clarity. The number two, `dhá`, also lenites the noun.

- *aon bhád amháin* - one boat
- *dhá bhád* - two boats

You can also express "one" by placing `amháin` after the noun.

- *bád amháin* - one boat

### Numbers 3 to 6

The numbers from three to six all cause lenition on the following noun.

- *trí bhád* - three boats
- *ceithre bhád* - four boats
- *cúig bhád* - five boats
- *sé bhád* - six boats

### Numbers 7 to 10

The numbers from seven to ten all cause eclipsis on the following noun.

- *seacht mbád* - seven boats
- *ocht mbád* - eight boats
- *naoi mbád* - nine boats
- *deich mbád* - ten boats

<Note>
Nouns of time, distance, or measurement often use their genitive singular form after numbers 3-10, rather than the standard nominative singular. For example, `bliain` (year) becomes `bliana`.

- *trí bliana* - three years
- *seacht mbliana* - seven years
</Note>

### Numbers 11 to 19

To count nouns from 11 to 19, you place the noun between the unit number (1-9) and the word `déag`. The mutation on the noun is determined by the unit number.

- *aon bhád déag* - eleven boats (from `aon` + lenition)
- *dhá bhád déag* - twelve boats (from `dhá` + lenition)
- *sé bhád déag* - sixteen boats (from `sé` + lenition)
- *seacht mbád déag* - seventeen boats (from `seacht` + eclipsis)

## Counting People (Uimhreacha Pearsanta)

Irish uses a special set of collective nouns for counting people from 2 to 12. For other numbers, the standard cardinal numbers are used, but with a slightly different mutation pattern for 13-19.

### Numbers 1 to 12

For one person, you use the standard cardinal number. From 2 to 12, special forms are used. The noun following these forms is in the singular.

| Number | Form for People | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `aon` ... `amháin` | *aon fhear amháin* | one man |
| 2 | `beirt` | *beirt fhear* | two men |
| 3 | `triúr` | *triúr fear* | three men |
| 4 | `ceathrar` | *ceathrar fear* | four men |
| 5 | `cúigear` | *cúigear fear* | five men |
| 6 | `seisear` | *seisear fear* | six men |
| 7 | `seachtar` | *seachtar fear* | seven men |
| 8 | `ochtar` | *ochtar fear* | eight men |
| 9 | `naonúr` | *naonúr fear* | nine men |
| 10 | `deichniúr` | *deichniúr fear* | ten men |
| 11 | `aon` ... `déag` | *aon fhear déag* | eleven men |
| 12 | `dáréag` | *dáréag fear* | twelve men |

### Numbers 13 to 19

From 13 to 19, you use the standard cardinal numbers with the noun followed by `déag`. However, the mutation pattern is different from counting objects: numbers 13-16 cause lenition, while 17-19 cause eclipsis.

- *trí fhear déag* - thirteen men (lenition)
- *sé fhear déag* - sixteen men (lenition)
- *seacht bhfear déag* - seventeen men (eclipsis of `f` is `bhf`)
- *naoi bhfear déag* - nineteen men (eclipsis of `f` is `bhf`)

## Ordinal Numbers (Orduimhreacha)

Ordinal numbers ("first", "second", "third", etc.) are used to indicate position in a sequence. They are usually preceded by the definite article `an`.

| Number | Ordinal Form | Example | Abbreviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | `an chéad` | *an chéad bhád* | 1ú |
| 2nd | `an dara` | *an dara bád* | 2ú |
| 3rd | `an tríú` | *an tríú bád* | 3ú |
| 4th | `an ceathrú` | *an ceathrú bád* | 4ú |
| 5th | `an cúigiú` | *an cúigiú bád* | 5ú |
| 6th | `an séú` | *an séú bád* | 6ú |
| 7th | `an seachtú` | *an seachtú bád* | 7ú |
| 8th | `an t-ochtú` | *an t-ochtú bád* | 8ú |
| 9th | `an naoú` | *an naoú bád* | 9ú |
| 10th | `an deichiú` | *an deichiú bád* | 10ú |
| 11th | `an t-aonú ... déag` | *an t-aonú bád déag* | 11ú |
| 12th | `an dóú ... déag` | *an dóú bád déag* | 12ú |
| 13th | `an tríú ... déag` | *an tríú bád déag* | 13ú |
| 19th | `an naoú ... déag` | *an naoú bád déag* | 19ú |

<Note>
- `an chéad` (first) lenites the following noun.
- `an dara` (second) does not cause any mutation.
- Ordinal numbers from 3 upwards do not cause mutation.
- A `t-` is prefixed to ordinals beginning with a vowel, such as `an t-ochtú` (the eighth) and `an t-aonú déag` (the eleventh).
</Note>

## Zero

The number zero is usually `náid`. It can also be referred to as `neamhní`.
