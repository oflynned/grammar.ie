When used on their own without a following noun, Irish cardinal numbers act like regular nouns. They have a gender and can be declined with the definite article, `an`. All numbers are treated as masculine nouns except for `náid` (zero), which is feminine.

These forms are used to refer to the number itself, but also for things like bus routes, decades, or playing cards.

- *Tóg an seacht.* - Take the number seven (bus).
- *Ba iad na nóchaidí an ré órga.* - The nineties were the golden era.
- *Tarraing an deich hart.* - Draw the ten of hearts.

<Note>
The numerals from 20 to 90 (except for `daichead`) also have a separate 'personal plural' form used for counting people, which is covered elsewhere.
</Note>

## Declension Forms

The table below shows the main declined forms for numbers when used with the definite article.

| Number | Nominative Singular | Genitive Singular | Nominative Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | `an náid` | `na náide` | `na náideanna` |
| 1 | `an t-aon` | `an aoin` | `na haonta` |
| 2 | `an dó` | `an dó` | `na dónna` |
| 3 | `an trí` | `an trí` | `na tríonna` |
| 4 | `an ceathair` | `an cheathair` | `na ceathaireanna` |
| 5 | `an cúig` | `an chúig` | `na cúigeanna` |
| 6 | `an sé` | `an tsé` | `na séanna` |
| 7 | `an seacht` | `an tseacht` | `na seachtanna` |
| 8 | `an t-ocht` | `an ocht` | `na hochtanna` |
| 9 | `an naoi` | `an naoi` | `na naonna` |
| 10 | `an deich` | `an deich` | `na deicheanna` |
| 11 | `an t-aon déag` | `an aoin déag` | `na haonta déag` |
| 12 | `an dó dhéag` | `an dó dhéag` | `na dónna déag` |
| 13 | `an trí déag` | `an trí déag` | `na tríonna déag` |
| 20 | `an fiche` | `an fhichead` | `na fichidí` |
| 21 | `an fiche a haon` | `an fhichead a haon` | `na fichidí a haon` |
| 30 | `an tríocha` | `an tríochad` | `na tríochaidí` |
| 40 | `an daichead` | `an daichid` | `na daichidí` |
| 40 | `an ceathracha` | `an cheathrachad` | `na ceathrachaidí` |
| 50 | `an caoga` | `an chaogad` | `na caogaidí` |
| 60 | `an seasca` | `an tseascad` | `na seascaidí` |
| 70 | `an seachtó` | `an tseachtód` | `na seachtóidí` |
| 80 | `an t-ochtó` | `an ochtód` | `na hochtóidí` |
| 90 | `an nócha` | `an nóchad` | `na nóchaidí` |
| 100 | `an céad` | `an chéid` | `na céadta` |
| 1,000 | `an míle` | `an mhíle` | `na mílte` |
| 1,000,000 | `an miliún` | `an mhilliúin` | `na milliúin` |

The genitive plural is typically formed by eclipsing the nominative plural form, for example, `na gceathaireanna` (of the fours) or `na bhfichidí` (of the twenties).

<Note>
There is some variation in the genitive singular forms of `seasca` (sixty) and `seachtó` (seventy). While some sources show the t-prefix (`an tseascad`, `an tseachtód`), others state that these masculine nouns do not take it (`an seascad`, `an seachtód`). Both forms may be encountered.
</Note>
