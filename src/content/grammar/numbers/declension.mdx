## Numbers as Nouns

Cardinal numbers can be used as standalone nouns with the definite article, for example, *an deich* (the ten). When used this way, they are treated as regular nouns, each having a gender and a declension class. All are masculine except for `náid` (zero), which is feminine.

These forms are used in several contexts:

-   **Naming the number itself**: *an deich* (the ten)
-   **Referring to numbered objects**: *an seacht* (the number seven, e.g., a bus)
-   **Referring to decades**: *na nóchaidí* (the nineties)
-   **In card games**: *an deich hart* (the ten of hearts)

The numbers from 20 to 90 (except for `daichead`) also have separate forms for counting people, which are covered elsewhere.

### Notes on Declension

¹ According to _Graiméar Gaeilge na mBráithre Críostaí_, the numbers `seasca` (60) and `seachtó` (70) do not take a t-prefix in the genitive singular, despite being masculine (`an seascad`, `an seachtód`). However, the forms with the t-prefix (`an tseascad`, `an tseachtód`) are also found in other sources.
