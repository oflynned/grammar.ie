Irish has two systems for numbers above 20: a modern decimal (base-10) system and a traditional vigesimal (base-20) system. The decimal system is the official standard taught in schools, while the vigesimal system remains common in everyday speech, particularly among native speakers.

This page provides a reference for both systems for the numbers 20 to 99.

## Decimal System (`Córas Deachúlach`)

The decimal system builds numbers logically using the tens (`fiche`, `tríocha`, `daichead`, etc.) followed by the units from 1-9.

-   For abstract counting, the pattern is `(ten) a (unit)`, e.g., `fiche a haon` (twenty-one).
-   When counting nouns, the pattern is `(unit) is (ten)`, e.g., `bád is fiche` (twenty-one boats).

## Grammar Notes

### Abstract vs Counting Numbers

-   **`Maolúimhreacha`** (abstract numbers) are used for counting in sequence, doing maths, or when the number stands alone. The decimal pattern is `(ten) a (unit)`, e.g., `tríocha a cúig` (35).
-   **`Bunuimhreacha`** (cardinal numbers) are used to count nouns. The decimal pattern is `(unit) is (ten)`, e.g., `cúig bhád is tríocha` (35 boats).

### Noun Forms After Numbers

-   After a number, the noun is usually in the singular form, e.g., `fiche bád` (20 boats).
-   The numbers 1-10 within a larger number trigger their usual initial mutations on the following noun:
    -   `dhá` lenites: *dhá bhád is fiche* (22 boats).
    -   `trí` to `sé` lenite: *trí bhád is fiche* (23 boats).
    -   `seacht` to `deich` eclipse: *seacht mbád is fiche* (27 boats).
-   Some nouns, particularly units of time and measurement like `bliain` (year), use a special plural form after the numbers 3-6. For `bliain`, this form is `bliana`:
    -   *trí bliana is fiche* (23 years)
    -   *ceithre bliana is fiche* (24 years)
