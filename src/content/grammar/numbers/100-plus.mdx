This page provides a reference for Irish numbers from 100 upwards. Large numbers in Irish have several forms depending on their function:

- **Abstract numbers** (`maol-uimhreacha`) are used for counting in sequence, reading out digits, or naming years.
- **Cardinal numbers** (`bun-uimhreacha`) are used for counting nouns (e.g., "one hundred boats").
- **Ordinal numbers** (`ord-uimhreacha`) are used to indicate position in a series (e.g., "the hundredth boat").

The table below shows examples of these forms. For clarity, the cardinal and ordinal examples use the masculine noun `bád` (boat). See the usage notes for how other nouns are treated.

## Usage Notes

### Cardinal Numbers (`bunuimhreacha`)

The words `céad` (100), `míle` (1,000), and `milliún` (1,000,000) are masculine nouns. When they are multiplied, they are lenited or eclipsed like any other noun:
- `dhá chéad` (200), `trí mhíle` (3,000)
- `seacht gcéad` (700), `ocht milliún` (8,000,000)

The noun being counted follows the number and usually remains in the singular nominative case:
- *dhá chéad bád* (200 boats)
- *míle bliain* (1,000 years)

For compound numbers like 107 or 223, the conjunction `is` ("and") is used. The rules for the final part of the number (1–99) then apply to the noun. For example, numbers from 3 to 10 require the genitive plural form of certain feminine nouns like `bliain` (year, genitive plural `bliana`):
- *céad is seacht mbád* (107 boats)
- *céad is seacht mbliana* (107 years)

### Ordinal Numbers (`orduimhreacha`)

Ordinal numbers are formed by adding the suffix `-ú` to the cardinal number and are preceded by the definite article `an`.
- `céad` → `an céadú` (the 100th)
- `míle` → `an míliú` (the 1,000th)
- `milliún` → `an milliúnú` (the 1,000,000th)

These can be abbreviated in writing, for example `100ú`, `101ú`, `200ú`.

### Abstract Numbers (`maoluimhreacha`)

Abstract numbers are used for counting in series, for years, or for reading digits. For numbers above 100, the particle `a` is used to connect the parts.
- `céad a haon` (101)
- `dhá chéad a dó` (202)
- The year 1968 is *míle naoi gcéad seasca a hocht*.

### `Billiún` and `Míle Milliún`

The table uses the "long scale" system for very large numbers, where `míle milliún` is 10⁹ (one thousand million) and `billiún` is 10¹² (one trillion in US English). However, usage can vary. As in English, `billiún` is sometimes used to mean 10⁹ (one billion in US English). Context is important for these very large numbers.
