Irish has special words for "half" and "third", and a regular system based on ordinal numbers for all other fractions.

## Fractions (`Na Codáin`)

There are several ways to express fractions, from common words to more mathematical formulas.

### Special Terms: `leath` and `trian`

The most common fractions, "half" and "third", have their own distinct words and usage patterns.

#### Half (`leath`)

The word for "half" is `leath`. It can be used in several ways:

-   As a prefix, causing no mutation:
    -   *leathúll* (a half-apple)
-   As a separate word before a noun in the genitive case:
    -   *leath úill* (half of an apple)
-   With the preposition `de`:
    -   *leath d’úll* (half of an apple)
-   With the definite article:
    -   *leath an úill* (half of the apple)
    -   *leath na n-úll* (half of the apples)
    -   *leath den úll* (half of the apple)

To express multiples, such as "five halves" (5/2), you use the cardinal number followed by `leath`:
-   *cúig leath* (five halves)
-   *cúig leathúll* (five half-apples)

#### Third (`trian`)

The word for "third" is `trian`. Its usage is similar to `leath`.

-   As a separate word before a noun in the genitive case:
    -   *trian úill* (a third of an apple)
-   With the preposition `de`:
    -   *trian d’úll* (a third of an apple)
-   With the definite article:
    -   *trian an úill* (a third of the apple)
    -   *trian na n-úll* (a third of the apples)
    -   *trian den úll* (a third of the apple)

To express multiples, such as "two thirds" (2/3), the cardinal number precedes `trian` and causes the appropriate initial mutation:
-   *dhá thrian* (two thirds)
-   *dhá thrian den úll* (two thirds of the apple)

### General Fractions

Fractions from "a quarter" onwards are formed using the ordinal numbers. The word `cuid` (part, portion) is often used with them, especially when the fraction is definite.

| Fraction | Irish Term |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1/4 | `ceathrú` |
| 1/5 | `cúigiú` |
| 1/6 | `séú` |
| 1/7 | `seachtú` |
| 1/8 | `ochtú` |
| 1/9 | `naoú` |
| 1/10 | `deichiú` |
| 1/20 | `fichiú` |
| 1/100 | `céadú` |
| 1/1000 | `míliú` |

**Examples with nouns:**

-   *an cheathrú cuid den úll* (the quarter of the apple)
-   *an cúigiú cuid den úll* (the fifth of the apple)
-   *an fichiú cuid den úll* (the twentieth of the apple)

**Multiples of fractions:**

When forming multiples, the cardinal number comes first and causes the expected initial mutation on the fraction word.

-   *trí cheathrú* (three quarters)
-   *ceithre chúigiú* (four fifths)
-   *cúig shéú* (five sixths)
-   *seacht ndeichiú* (seven tenths)
-   *naoi n-ochtú* (nine eighths)

### Mathematical Expressions

A more formal or mathematical way to express a fraction is to use the structure `X ar Y` ("X on Y").

The formula is `a [numerator] ar a [denominator]`. For denominators of 20 or higher, the second `a` is usually omitted.

| Fraction | Mathematical Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1/2 | `a haon ar a dó` |
| 2/3 | `a dó ar a trí` |
| 3/4 | `a trí ar a ceathair` |
| 4/5 | `a ceathair ar a cúig` |
| 1/20 | `a haon ar fiche` |
| 13/20 | `a trí déag ar fiche` |

## Mixed Numbers (`Na hUimhreacha Measctha`)

Mixed numbers like "one and a half" are formed using specific conjunctions.

### With `leith` and `trian`

For "and a half" and "and a third", the word `go` is used.

-   `a haon go leith` (one and a half)
-   `a dó go dtrian` (two and a third)

When used with a noun, the noun is placed between the whole number and the fraction.

-   *úll go leith* (one and a half apples)
-   *dhá úll go leith* (two and a half apples)
-   *dhá úll go dtrian* (two and a third apples)

### With Other Fractions

For all other fractions, the word `is` ("and") is used.

-   `a dó is ceathrú` (two and a quarter)
-   `a trí is cúigiú` (three and a fifth)

As with `go`, the noun comes between the whole number and the fraction.

-   *dhá úll is ceathrú* (two and a quarter apples)
-   *trí úll is cúigiú* (three and a fifth apples)
-   *trí úll is seachtú* (three and a seventh apples)

| Number | Irish Term | Example with Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 ½ | `a haon go leith` | `úll go leith` |
| 2 ½ | `a dó go leith` | `dhá úll go leith` |
| 2 ⅓ | `a dó go dtrian` | `dhá úll go dtrian` |
| 2 ¼ | `a dó is ceathrú` | `dhá úll is ceathrú` |
| 3 ⅕ | `a trí is cúigiú` | `trí úll is cúigiú` |
| 3 ⅙ | `a trí is séú` | `trí úll is séú` |
| 3 ⅛ | `a trí is ochtú` | `trí úll is ochtú` |
