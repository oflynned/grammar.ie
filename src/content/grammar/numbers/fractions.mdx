Irish has several ways to express fractions. For common values like a half, a third, and a quarter, there are special dedicated words. Other fractions are typically formed using ordinal numbers, or with a more mathematical structure for precision.

## Special Fraction Words

The most common fractions have unique names rather than being formed from a general rule.

### A Half (`leath`)

The word for "a half" is `leath`. A less common alternative is `leathchuid`. `Leath` can be used in several ways, either compounding with a noun or followed by the genitive case.

*   *leathúll* - a half-apple (compounded)
*   *leath úill* - a half of an apple (genitive)
*   *leath d’úll* - a half of an apple (with `de`)

When referring to a specific item, use the definite article:

*   *leath an úill* - a half of the apple
*   *leath na n-úll* - a half of the apples
*   *leath den úll* - a half of the apple (using `den` for `de + an`)
*   *an leath den úll* - the half of the apple

For multiple halves, such as "five halves" (5/2), you can say `cúig leath` or `cúig cinn de leatha`.

*   *cúig leathúll* - five half-apples

### A Third (`trian`)

The most common word for "a third" is `trian`. The ordinal number `tríú` is also used, often with the word `cuid` (part/share). A less common alternative is `treaschuid`.

*   *trian úill* - a third of an apple
*   *trian d’úll* - a third of an apple
*   *trian an úill* - a third of the apple
*   *tríú cuid an úill* - a third (part) of the apple

For multiples, `trian` is used, and its plural is `trianta` or `treana`.

*   *dhá thrian den úll* - two thirds of the apple
*   *trí treana den úll* - three thirds of the apple
*   *dhá cheann de thrianta* - two (items) of thirds

In some dialects, the eclipsed form `dhá dtrian` is used for "two thirds" instead of the standard lenited `dhá thrian`.
</Dialect>

### A Quarter (`ceathrú`)

The word for "a quarter" or "a fourth" is `ceathrú`. Like `tríú`, it is often used with `cuid`.

*   *an cheathrú den úll* - the quarter of the apple
*   *an ceathrú cuid den úll* - the quarter (part) of the apple

For multiples, you can use `ceathrú` or the plural `ceathrúna`.

*   *trí cheathrú den úll* - three quarters of the apple
*   *trí ceathrúna den úll* - three quarters of the apple
*   *trí cinn de cheathruithe* - three (items) of quarters

## General Fractions

Most other fractions are formed systematically from numbers.

### Using Ordinal Numbers

For most fractions, the system is based on ordinal numbers. The basic fractional unit (e.g., "a fifth", "a sixth") is simply the ordinal number itself.

| Fraction | Ordinal Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1/5 | `cúigiú` |
| 1/6 | `séú` |
| 1/7 | `seachtú` |
| 1/8 | `ochtú` |
| 1/9 | `naoú` |
| 1/10 | `deichiú` |

To express a fraction of something, you can add `cuid` (part) and use the preposition `de`.

*   *(an) cúigiú cuid den úll* - (the) fifth part of the apple

For numerators greater than one, place the cardinal number before the fractional noun. This often causes a mutation.

*   *ceithre chúigiú (cuid) den úll* - four fifths of the apple
*   *cúig shéú (cuid) den úll* - five sixths of the apple
*   *seacht ndeichiú (cuid) den úll* - seven tenths of the apple
*   *naoi n-ochtú (cuid) den úll* - nine eighths of the apple

### Fractions from 11 to 19

Fractions from one eleventh to one nineteenth have two common forms. One is a single compound word, and the other uses the phrase `cuid déag` ("teen part").

| Fraction | Compound Form | `cuid déag` Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1/11 | `aondéagú` | `an t-aonú cuid déag` |
| 1/12 | `dódhéagú` | `an dóú cuid déag` |
| 1/13 | `trídéagú` | `an triú cuid déag` |
| 1/14 | `ceathairdéagú` | `an ceathrú cuid déag` |
| 1/19 | `naoidéagú` | `an naoú cuid déag` |

When the numerator is greater than one, the compound form is typically used.

*   *sé aondéagú* - six elevenths (6/11)
*   *deich ndódhéagú* - ten twelfths (10/12)
*   *dhá thrídéagú* - two thirteenths (2/13)

### Fractions for Tens

Fractions based on tens (twentieth, thirtieth, etc.) also follow a regular pattern based on the cardinal number.

| Fraction | Ordinal Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1/20 | `fichiú` |
| 1/30 | `tríochadú` |
| 1/40 | `daicheadú` |
| 1/50 | `caogadú` |
| 1/60 | `seascadú` |
| 1/70 | `seachtódú` |
| 1/80 | `ochtadú` |
| 1/90 | `nóchadú` |
| 1/100 | `céadú` |
| 1/1000 | `míliú` |

These forms behave like other fractional nouns when used with numerators greater than one.

*   *dhá fichiú cuid den úll* - two twentieths of the apple
*   *trí dhaicheadú den úll* - three fortieths of the apple
*   *naoi gcéadú den úll* - nine hundredths of the apple

## Alternative Constructions

Two other constructions are common, one used for counting fractions as items and another for mathematical contexts.

### Counting with `cinn de`

To count fractions as discrete units, you can use the structure `[number] cinn de [plural fractional noun]`.

*   *ceithre cinn de chúigithe* - four (items) of fifths (4/5)
*   *ocht gcinn de sheachtuithe* - eight (items) of sevenths (8/7)
*   *naoi gcinn d’ochtuithe* - nine (items) of eighths (9/8)
*   *a cúig déag de shédéaguithe* - fifteen (items) of sixteenths (15/16)

### Mathematical Style (`X ar Y`)

A more formal or mathematical way to state a fraction is to say "X over Y", using the formula `a <numerator> ar a <denominator>`. For numbers over 19, the second `a` is often dropped.

*   *a haon ar a dó* - one over two (1/2)
*   *a cúig ar a sé* - five over six (5/6)
*   *a hocht ar a seacht* - eight over seven (8/7)
*   *a trí déag ar fiche* - thirteen over twenty (13/20)
*   *a dó dhéag ar fiche a ceathair* - twelve over twenty-four (12/24)

## Mixed Numbers

Mixed numbers like "one and a half" are formed using `go leith` for halves, `go dtrian` for thirds, and `is` (and) for most other fractions.

| Number | Irish Term | Example with `úll` (apple) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 1/2 | `a haon go leith` | `úll go leith` |
| 2 1/2 | `a dó go leith` | `dhá úll go leith` |
| 2 1/3 | `a dó go dtrian` | `dhá úll go dtrian` |
| 2 1/4 | `a dó is ceathrú` | `dhá úll is ceathrú` |
| 3 1/5 | `a trí is cúigiú` | `trí úll is cúigiú` |
| 3 1/6 | `a trí is séú` | `trí úll is séú` |
| 3 1/7 | `a trí is seachtú` | `trí úll is seachtú` |
| 3 1/8 | `a trí is ochtú` | `trí úll is ochtú` |
| 3 1/9 | `a trí is naoú` | `trí úll is naoú` |
| 3 1/10 | `a trí is deichiú` | `trí úll is deichiú` |
