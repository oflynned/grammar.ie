Irish has several ways of expressing numbers depending on whether you are counting aloud, counting objects, counting people, or putting things in order. This page provides a reference for the numbers from one to nineteen.

## Forms of Numbers 1-19

The table below summarises the main forms for each number. The rules for mutations and the noun forms that follow are explained in the sections below the table.

| # | Counting Form | Cardinal (with Noun) | Personal (for People) | Ordinal | Abbrev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `a haon` | `aon` + L | `duine amháin` | `an chéad` + L | 1ú |
| 2 | `a dó` | `dhá` + L | `beirt` (+ gen. sg.) | `an dara` | 2ú |
| 3 | `a trí` | `trí` | `triúr` (+ gen. sg.) | `an tríú` | 3ú |
| 4 | `a ceathair` | `ceithre` | `ceathrar` (+ gen. sg.) | `an ceathrú` | 4ú |
| 5 | `a cúig` | `cúig` | `cúigear` (+ gen. sg.) | `an cúigiú` | 5ú |
| 6 | `a sé` | `sé` + L | `seisear` (+ gen. sg.) | `an séú` | 6ú |
| 7 | `a seacht` | `seacht` + E | `seachtar` (+ gen. sg.) | `an seachtú` | 7ú |
| 8 | `a hocht` | `ocht` + E | `ochtar` (+ gen. sg.) | `an t-ochtú` | 8ú |
| 9 | `a naoi` | `naoi` + E | `naonúr` (+ gen. sg.) | `an naoú` | 9ú |
| 10 | `a deich` | `deich` + E | `deichniúr` (+ gen. sg.) | `an deichiú` | 10ú |
| 11 | `a haon déag` | `aon ... déag` + L | `aon duine déag` | `an t-aonú ... déag` | 11ú |
| 12 | `a dó dhéag` | `dhá ... déag` + L | `dháréag` (+ gen. sg.) | `an dóú ... déag` | 12ú |
| 13-19 | `a [X] déag` | `[X] ... déag` | `[X] ... déag` | `an [X]ú ... déag` | 13ú etc. |

**Key:**
*   **L** = Lenition (e.g., `bád` → `bhád`)
*   **E** = Eclipsis (e.g., `bád` → `mbád`)
*   **gen. sg.** = Genitive Singular

## Cardinal Numbers (Counting Things)

Cardinal numbers (`bunuimhreacha`) are used to count nouns (objects, animals, concepts). The form of the noun and the initial mutation it undergoes depend on the number.

### Noun Forms
*   **1-2**: The noun is in the singular.
    *   *aon bhád amháin* (one boat)
    *   *dhá bhád* (two boats)
*   **3-6**: The noun is usually in the singular for first declension nouns, but in the genitive plural for nouns of the second to fifth declensions.
    *   *trí bhád* (three boats - singular)
    *   *trí bliana* (three years - genitive plural)
*   **7-10**: The noun is in the plural.
    *   *seacht mbád* (seven boats)
    *   *deich mbliana* (ten years)

### Initial Mutations
*   **1, 2, 6**: Cause **lenition**.
    *   `aon bhád amháin` (one boat)
    *   `dhá chat` (two cats)
    *   `sé bhád` (six boats)
*   **3, 4, 5**: Cause **no mutation**.
    *   `trí bhád` (three boats)
    *   `ceithre chat` (four cats)
    *   `cúig bhád` (five boats)
*   **7-10**: Cause **eclipsis**.
    *   `seacht mbád` (seven boats)
    *   `ocht gcat` (eight cats)
    *   `deich mbád` (ten boats)

### The Number One
The number one has a few forms:
*   `... amháin`: Used after a noun to mean "one" or "only one".
    *   *bád amháin* (one boat)
*   `aon ...`: Used before a noun, it means "any". It causes lenition.
    *   *aon bhád* (any boat)
*   `aon ... amháin`: Used before and after a noun for emphasis, meaning "just one".
    *   *aon bhád amháin* (just one boat)

## Personal Numbers (Counting People)

For counting people, special forms called personal numbers (`uimhreacha pearsanta`) are used for numbers 2-10 and 12.

*   These special forms are followed by the noun in the **genitive singular**.
    *   `beirt fhear` (two men)
    *   `triúr ban` (three women)
    *   `ceathrar páistí` (four children)
*   For **twelve people**, the special form `dháréag` is used.
    *   `dháréag fear` (twelve men)
*   For **13-19 people**, the cardinal number is used, followed by the noun (often genitive singular) and `déag`.
    *   `trí fhear déag` (thirteen men)
    *   `cúig bhean déag` (fifteen women)
*   **Exception for 17-19**: When counting people, the numbers 17, 18, and 19 cause **lenition**, not eclipsis.
    *   `seacht bhfear déag` (seventeen men)
    *   `ocht bhfear déag` (eighteen men)
    *   `naoi bhfear déag` (nineteen men)

## Ordinal Numbers (First, Second...)

Ordinal numbers (`orduimhreacha`) indicate position in a sequence.

*   **First**: `an chéad` causes **lenition**.
    *   *an chéad bhád* (the first boat)
*   **Second**: `an dara` causes **no mutation**.
    *   *an dara bád* (the second boat)
*   **Third onwards**: The suffix `-ú` is added to the cardinal number. These forms cause no mutation.
    *   `an tríú bád` (the third boat)
    *   `an séú bád` (the sixth boat)
*   If the number begins with a vowel, a **t-prefix** is added after the article `an`.
    *   `an t-ochtú bád` (the eighth boat)
    *   `an t-aonú bád déag` (the eleventh boat)

## Numbers 11-19 (`déag`)

The numbers from 11 to 19 are formed by adding `déag` (related to `deich`, ten).

*   **11**: The noun comes first, followed by `déag`. If `aon` is used for emphasis, it precedes the noun.
    *   *bád déag* (eleven boats)
    *   *aon bhád déag* (eleven boats, emphatic)
*   **12-19**: The structure is `[number] [noun] déag`. The mutation rules for the base number (2-10) apply to the noun.
    *   `dhá bhád déag` (twelve boats)
    *   `cúig bhád déag` (fifteen boats)
    *   `seacht mbád déag` (seventeen boats)

## Zero

The number zero is `náid` or `neamhní`.
*   *Tá an teocht ag náid.* (The temperature is at zero.)
