In Irish, compass directions use distinct adverbial forms to express static location, origin (from a direction), and destination (to a direction). There are also noun forms for the directions themselves.

- **Location**: Indicates a static position, e.g., `thuaidh` (in the north).
- **Origin**: Indicates movement from a direction, e.g., `aduaidh` (from the north).
- **Destination**: Indicates movement towards a direction, e.g., `ó thuaidh` (to the north).
- **Relative Location**: Indicates a position beyond or relative to something, e.g., `lastuaidh` (north of).
- **Noun**: The name of the direction as a concept, e.g., `an tuaisceart` (the north).

## Primary Compass Points

The cardinal (N, S, E, W) and intercardinal (NE, SE, SW, NW) directions are the most common.
