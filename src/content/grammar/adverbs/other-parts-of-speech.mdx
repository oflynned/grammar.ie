In Irish, adverbs are invariable, meaning they are not declined or conjugated. However, adverbs derived from adjectives can be graded, using the same forms as the comparative and superlative of the adjective.

## Word Order

The position of an adverb in a sentence depends on what it modifies.

-   **Modifying an adjective or another adverb:** The adverb is placed immediately before the word it modifies.
    -   *réasúnta dona* (quite bad)
    -   *pas beag rómhór* (a little too big)

-   **Modifying a verb or the whole sentence:** The adverb usually goes at the end of the sentence. If there are several adverbs, the typical order is: place, manner, then time.
    -   However, adverbs come **before** simple personal or prepositional pronouns that are objects: *Bhí mo mhac inné agam.* (My son was with me yesterday.)
    -   They come **after** stressed pronouns (e.g., with `-sa`, `féin`, `seo`, `sin`): *Bhí mo mhac agamsa inné.* (My son was with me yesterday.)

-   **Emphasis:** Any part of a sentence, including an adverb, can be moved to the beginning for emphasis using a cleft sentence (a copula construction followed by a direct relative clause).
    -   *(Is) inné a bhí mo mhac agamsa.* (It was yesterday that my son was with me.)

### Adverbs After the Subject

Some adverbs can be placed directly after the subject, regardless of what follows. These include:

-   `anois` (now)
-   `riamh` (ever, before)
-   `i gcónaí`, `choíche` (always)
-   `go fóill`, `fós` (still, yet)
-   `go minic` (often)

*An raibh tú riamh i mBéal Feirste?* (Have you ever been in Belfast?)

If the verb is `bí ... ann` (to be, to exist) and the subject is indefinite, these adverbs can come between the verb and the subject.

-   *Bíonn i gcónaí drochaimsir ann Lá Fhéile Pádraig.* (There is always bad weather on St. Patrick's Day.)

Adverbial interjections like `cinnte` (certainly) and `ar ndóigh` (of course) can also come between the verb and a non-pronominal subject.

-   *Tá cinnte Gardaí ar an sráid.* (There are certainly police officers on the street.)
-   *Tá ar ndóigh daoine a chreideann…* (There are of course people who believe…)

### Adverbs at the Start of a Sentence

Some adverbs, particularly those referring to the whole sentence (time and place), can stand at the beginning of a sentence without changing its structure.

-   *Ar aon chuma, bhí sé ag obair go cúramach.* (In any case, he worked carefully.)
-   *Amárach, beidh muid ag labhairt faoin ábhar sin.* (Tomorrow, we will talk about that topic.)

When such a sentence becomes a subordinate clause with `go`/`nach`, the adverbial phrase comes before the conjunction.

-   *An chéad Nollaig eile tiocfaidh sé aníos.* (Next Christmas he will come up.)
-   *Deiridís an chéad Nollaig eile **go** dtiocfadh sé aníos.* (They said that he would come up next Christmas.)

Other adverbs can only be moved to the front using a cleft sentence. For some, this is common (`minic` - often, `annamh` - rarely) or the general rule (`ar éigean` - hardly).

-   *Is minic a bhí sé ag obair go cúramach.* (He often worked carefully.)
-   *(Is) ar éigean a chreid sé mé.* (He hardly believed me.)

Some adverbs require a `go`/`nach` clause instead of a cleft sentence.

-   *Is gearr go mbeimid abhaile.* (We will soon be home.)
-   *B’fhéidir go bhfuil sé anseo.* (Perhaps he is here.)
-   *Is dócha go dtiocfadh sí ar ais am éicínt.* (She would probably come back sometime.)

## Adverbs of Place and Direction

Irish makes a strict distinction between location, movement towards the speaker, and movement away from the speaker. There are often four related forms for a single concept.

### Key Distinctions

1.  **State of Rest (`th-`):** Marks a defined location where something is.
    -   *Tá sé thuas ansin.* (He is up there.)

2.  **Movement Away from Speaker (`s-`):** Describes movement away from the speaker's position. It can also indicate an undefined location in a certain direction.
    -   *Téigh suas.* (Go up.)
    -   *Tá sé suas ansin.* (He is up there somewhere.)

3.  **Movement Towards Speaker (`an-`):** Describes movement towards the speaker's position.
    -   *Tar aníos.* (Come up.)

4.  **Relative Position (`las-`):** Indicates a position relative to another object, introduced with the preposition `de`.
    -   *lastuas den staighre* (above the stairs)
    -   *laistigh de na ballaí* (inside the walls)

The structure `taobh` + adverb + `de` is a common alternative to `las-` forms.

-   `taobh thiar de` = `laistiar de` (behind, west of)
-   `taobh istigh de` = `laistigh de` (inside of)

### Usage with Verbs of Motion

The choice of adverb must agree with the verb's direction.

-   `tar` (to come) implies movement towards the speaker, so it pairs with `an-` adverbs (`aníos`, `anuas`).
    -   *Tar aníos.* (Come up!)
-   `téigh` (to go) implies movement away from the speaker, so it pairs with `s-` adverbs (`suas`, `síos`).
    -   *Téigh suas.* (Go up!)

An exception is when the movement is shared with the speaker: *Tar suas liom.* (Come up with me!)

### The Cardinal Directions

| Direction | Position (in) | Movement from | Movement to | Relative (of) |
| :-------- | :------------ | :------------ | :---------- | :-------------- |
| North     | `thuaidh`     | `aduaidh`     | `ó thuaidh` | `lastuaidh`     |
| South     | `theas`       | `aneas`       | `ó dheas`   | `laisteas`      |
| East      | `thoir`       | `anoir`       | `soir`      | `lastoir`       |
| West      | `thiar`       | `aniar`       | `siar`      | `laistiar`      |

**Note on `ó thuaidh` and `ó dheas`:** The particle `ó` here means "towards" and comes from an older form `fó` (under, in the direction of). It is not the modern preposition `ó` (from).

**Intermediate Directions:** These are formed by combining the main directions, with East/West coming first.
-   **North-East:** `thoir thuaidh` (in the NE), `anoir aduaidh` (from the NE), `soir ó thuaidh` (to the NE)
-   **South-West:** `thiar theas` (in the SW), `aniar aneas` (from the SW), `siar ó dheas` (to the SW)

**Nominal Forms:** The cardinal directions also have noun forms.
-   `an tuaisceart` (the North)
-   `an deisceart` (the South)
-   `an t-oirthear` (the East)
-   `an t-iarthar` (the West)

### Up and Down

| Position | Movement from | Movement to | Relative (of) |
| :------- | :------------ | :---------- | :------------ |
| `thuas` (up) | `anuas` (from above) | `suas` (upwards) | `lastuas` (above) |
| `thíos` (down) | `aníos` (from below) | `síos` (downwards) | `laistíos` (below) |

### Front and Back

In the traditional Irish worldview, East is "in front" and West is "behind".
-   `thiar` means both "in the west" and "behind".
-   `thoir` means "in the east" but is rarely used for "in front". Phrases like `ar aghaidh` are used instead.

### Left and Right

Following the same logic, South is "to the right".
-   `deas` means right, southern, and also "nice".
-   `clé` means left.

These are adjectives. Adverbial forms include `ar dheis` (to the right) and `ar chlé` (to the left).

Circular movements are described with related terms:
-   `deiseal`: clockwise (lit. "south-wise")
-   `tuathal`: anti-clockwise (lit. "north-wise")

### Beyond and This Side

| Position | Movement from | Movement to | Relative (of) |
| :------- | :------------ | :---------- | :------------ |
| `thall` (beyond) | `anall` (from beyond) | `sall` / `anonn` (to beyond) | `lastall` (beyond) |
| `abhus` (this side) | — | — | `lasbhus` (this side of) |

### In and Out

| Movement to | Position | Relative (of) |
| :---------- | :------- | :------------ |
| `isteach` (in/into) | `istigh` (inside) | `laistigh` (within) |
| `amach` (out/outwards) | `amuigh` (outside) | `lasmuigh` (outside of) |

## Adverbs from Adjectives

### Formation with `go`

The most common way to form an adverb from an adjective is to prefix it with the particle `go`. This causes lenition where possible.

| Form                                | Examples                                 |
| :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- |
| `go` + Adjective                    | `go maith`, `go cliste`, `go sciobtha`    |
| `go` + `h` + Adjective (vowel-initial) | `go hiontach`, `go hard`, `go hannamh` |

The particle `go` is omitted in several situations:
-   When another adverb modifies the adverbial adjective: *D’oibrigh sé réasúnta dona.* (He worked quite badly.)
-   With a series of adverbs not joined by `agus`: *Roinn siad go fial fairsing linn.* (They shared generously and amply with us.)
-   When the adverb modifies an attributive adjective: *an teach measartha mór* (the fairly large house)
-   Before `cuibheasach` and `sách`: *Rinne sé cuibheasach maith é.* (He did it quite well.)
-   Often before the prefix `ró-` (too): *D’oibrigh sé ródhona.* (He worked too badly.)
-   Often in cleft sentences after a copula form: *Ní dona a d’oibrigh sé.* (It was not badly that he worked.)

### Adverbial Use of the Comparative

-   **Comparative:** Use `níos` + the comparative form of the adjective.
    -   *D’oibrigh sé níos measa ná Pól.* (He worked worse than Paul.)
-   **Superlative:** This uses a special relative clause with the copula.
    -   *Is é Seán is luaithe a ritheann.* (Seán runs the fastest.)

## Adverbial Pronouns

Adverbial pronouns (e.g., "therein", "thereupon", "hereby") are formed from prepositions.

### From Simple Prepositions

The 3rd person singular masculine form of a prepositional pronoun can function as an adverbial pronoun.
-   `aige` (at him) can mean "thereby"
-   `air` (on him) can mean "thereupon"
-   `ann` (in him) can mean "therein"

For emphasis or clarity, a demonstrative pronoun (`seo`, `sin`, `siúd`) is added.
-   `air sin`: thereupon
-   `air seo`: hereupon
-   `air siúd`: thereupon (over there)

| Preposition | Form with `sin` (that) | Form with `seo` (this) | Form with `siúd` (yonder) |
| :---------- | :--------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------ |
| `ag`        | `aige sin`             | `aige seo`             | `aige siúd`               |
| `ar`        | `air sin`              | `air seo`              | `air siúd`                |
| `as`        | `as sin`               | `as seo`               | `as siúd`                 |
| `chuig`     | `chuige sin`           | `chuige seo`           | `chuige siúd`             |
| `de`        | `de sin`               | `de seo`               | `de siúd`                 |
| `do`        | `dó sin`               | `dó seo`               | `dó siúd`                 |
| `fara`      | `fairis sin`           | `fairis seo`           | `fairis siúd`             |
| `faoi`      | `faoi sin`             | `faoi seo`             | `faoi siúd`               |

| Preposition | Form with `sin` (that) | Form with `seo` (this) | Form with `siúd` (yonder) |
| :---------- | :--------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------ |
| `i`         | `ann sin`              | `ann seo`              | `ann siúd`                |
| `idir`      | `eatarthu sin`         | `eatarthu seo`         | `eatarthu siúd`           |
| `le`        | `leis sin`             | `leis seo`             | `leis siúd`               |
| `ó`         | `uaidh sin`            | `uaidh seo`            | `uaidh siúd`              |
| `roimh`     | `roimhe sin`           | `roimhe seo`           | `roimhe siúd`             |
| `trí`       | `tríd sin`             | `tríd seo`             | `tríd siúd`               |
| `thar`      | `thairis sin`          | `thairis seo`          | `thairis siúd`            |
| `um`        | `uime sin`             | `uime seo`             | `uime siúd`               |

**Notes:**
-   `fairis sin` is the origin of `freisin` (also), used in Connacht. `leis` is used for "also" in Munster.
-   `ann sin`, `ann seo`, and `ann siúd` are usually written as one word: `ansin` (there, then), `anseo` (here), `ansiúd` (over there).
-   `ann` is frequently used with `bí` to mean "to exist": *Tá teach ann.* (There is a house.)

### From Compound Prepositions

-   **Two-part prepositions** (e.g., `ar son`) form adverbs using the 3rd person masculine possessive pronoun `a`.
    -   `ar son` (because of) -> `ar a shon` (because of that)
    -   `de bharr` (as a result of) -> `dá bharr sin` (as a result of that)
    -   `i ndiaidh` (after) -> `ina dhiaidh sin` (after that)
-   **Three-part prepositions** (e.g., `in aice le`) form adverbs using the 3rd person masculine prepositional pronoun of the final preposition.
    -   `in aice le` (beside) -> `in aice leis` (beside it)
    -   `mar gheall ar` (because of) -> `mar gheall air` (because of it)

## Adverbs from Nouns

Many adverbs originated from a preposition (often `i`) plus a noun. The nominal origin is often no longer obvious.
-   `amuigh` (outside) < `i muigh` (in a plain)
-   `amach` (out) < `i mach` (into a plain)
-   `istigh` (inside) < `is tigh` (in the house)
-   `isteach` (in) < `is teach` (into the house)

Some time adverbs are fossilised forms of a noun and the article.
-   `inniu` (today) < `in-diu` (on the day)
-   `anocht` (tonight) < `in nocht` (the night)

## Adverbial Weekdays

To specify that an action happens on a certain day of the week, the word `Dé` is prefixed to the genitive form of the day's name. This combination acts as a time adverb.

| Day         | Adverbial Form | Meaning       |
| :---------- | :------------- | :------------ |
| `Luan`      | `Dé Luain`     | on Monday     |
| `Máirt`     | `Dé Máirt`     | on Tuesday    |
| `Céadaoin`  | `Dé Céadaoin`  | on Wednesday  |
| `Déardaoin` | `Déardaoin`    | on Thursday   |
| `Aoine`     | `Dé hAoine`    | on Friday     |
| `Satharn`   | `Dé Sathairn`  | on Saturday   |
| `Domhnach`  | `Dé Domhnaigh` | on Sunday     |

To express a recurring event (e.g., "on Mondays"), use `gach` + the nominative form of the day: `gach Luan` (every Monday).

## Adverbs from Numerals

To express "how many times" something happens, Irish uses several constructions.

| Number | Frequency ("... times") | Multiplicative ("... times") | Adjective ("...-fold") |
| :----- | :---------------------- | :--------------------------- | :--------------------- |
| 1      | `(aon) uair (amháin)`   | —                            | `singil`               |
| 2      | `dhá uair`              | `faoi dhó`                   | `dúbailte`             |
| 3      | `trí huaire`            | `faoi thrí`                  | `tréaga`               |
| 4      | `ceithre huaire`        | `faoi cheathair`             | `ceathairfhillte`      |
| 5      | `cúig uaire`            | `faoi chúig`                 | `cúigfhillte`          |
| 10     | `deich n-uaire`         | `faoi dheich`                | `deichfhillte`         |

The forms with `uair` and `faoi` are adverbs. The forms in the final column are adjectives.
