In Irish, some adjectives and other modifying words can be attached to the beginning of a noun or another adjective as a prefix (`réimír`). This changes the meaning of the word and often triggers a sound change called a mutation. Understanding these prefixes is key to building vocabulary and understanding compound words.

While most descriptive adjectives come *after* the noun they modify, a few types always come before it:
- Pronominal adjectives like `gach` (every): *gach teach* (every house)
- Possessive pronouns like `mo` (my): *mo theach* (my house)
- Numbers like `trí` (three): *trí theach* (three houses)

Other descriptive adjectives can only be placed before a noun by turning them into a prefix.

## How Prefixes Work

The way an adjective is used as a prefix depends on the word itself.

### Prefix-only Adjectives

Some adjectives, like `dea-` (good) and `droch-` (bad), appear almost exclusively as prefixes.

- *dea-bhád* - a good boat
- *drochbhád* - a bad boat

### Preferred Prefixes

For some adjectives, the prefix form is strongly preferred. A common example is `sean-` (old).

- *seanfhear* - an old man

<Note>
While you might occasionally see *fear sean*, it is much less common. However, if `sean` is modified by an adverb like `an-` (very) or `ró-` (too), it must come after the noun.
- *fear an-sean* - a very old man
- *fear róshean* - a man who is too old
</Note>

### Prefixes for Fixed Terms

Most adjectives are rarely used as prefixes. When they are, it is usually to form a specific, well-known compound noun or concept. In general use, they follow the noun.

- *ardrí* - High King
- *sliabh ard* - a high mountain

### Intensifying Prefixes

Some prefixes act like adverbs, intensifying the meaning of the adjective or noun they are attached to. Common examples include `an-` (very), `fíor-` (truly), and `ró-` (too).

- *an-mhaith* - very good
- *fíormhaith* - really good
- *rómhaith* - too good

These can also modify nouns directly to create a new meaning.

- *an-charr* - a great car (literally "very-car")

## Mutation and Spelling Rules

Prefixes follow consistent rules for mutations and how they are written.

### Mutations

Nearly all prefixes cause **lenition** on the following word.

- `sean` + `bean` → `seanbhean` (old woman)
- `droch` + `bád` → `drochbhád` (bad boat)

The main exception is the older prefix `éa-` (un-), which causes **eclipsis**.

<Note>
In older orthography, the eclipsed consonant was written, so you may still encounter spellings like `éagcóir` for `éagóir` (injustice).
</Note>

### Hyphenation

Prefixes and the words they modify are usually written as a single word.

- `sean` + `fear` → `seanfhear` (old man)

There are three main exceptions where a hyphen is required:

1.  The prefixes `an-` (very) and `dea-` (good) are always followed by a hyphen.
    - *an-bhean* - a very good woman
    - *dea-cheol* - good music

2.  A hyphen is used when two prefixes are joined together.
    - *droch-sheanbhád* - a bad old boat

3.  A hyphen is used to separate two identical vowels or consonants.
    - *ró-óg* - too young
    - *sean-nós* - old style (a traditional style of singing)

<Warning>
Be careful not to confuse the intensifying prefix `an-` (very) with the negative prefix `an-` (un-).

| Prefix | Meaning | Hyphen? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `an-` | very | Yes | *an-mhaith* (very good) |
| `an-` / `ain-` | un- | No | *anabaí* (unripe) |

The negative prefix `an-` changes to `ain-` before a slender vowel, following the *caol le caol* rule. The intensifier `an-` never changes.
</Warning>

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster Irish, an epenthetic vowel (`-a-`) is often inserted after prefixes ending in `l`, `n`, or `r` when they are followed by a consonant.
- *ana-mhaith* (instead of *an-mhaith*) - very good
- *seanabhean* (instead of *seanbhean*) - an old woman
</Dialect>

## Common Prefixes

This list includes some of the most frequently encountered prefixes.

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ais` | back | *aisbhreathnaigh* | to look back |
| `an-` | very | *an-mhaith*, *an-charr* | very good, a great car |
| `ard` | high, arch- | *ardeaspag* | archbishop |
| `ath` | re-, again | *Athbhliain*, *athbheoigh* | New Year, to revive |
| `corr` | occasional, odd | *corruimhir*, *corrdhuine* | odd number, the odd one out |
| `barr` | excellent, exceedingly | *barrthuirseach* | terribly tired |
| `dea-` | good | *dea-cheol* | good music |
| `dearg` | red, intense | *deargamadán* | complete idiot |
| `dian` | intense, strict | *dianchosc* | strict prohibition |
| `droch` | bad | *drochbhád* | a bad boat |
| `fíor` | truly | *fíormhaith* | truly good |
| `il` | many, poly- | *ilteangach* | multilingual |
| `leath` | half, one of two | *leathchéad*, *leathshúil* | fifty (half a hundred), one eye |
| `oll` | huge, super- | *ollmhargadh*, *ollscoil* | supermarket, university |
| `príomh` | main, primary | *príomhtheanga* | main language |
| `rí` | royal, very | *ríshean* | very old |
| `ró` | too, excessively | *róshean* | too old |
| `sár` | extremely, super- | *sárálainn* | extremely beautiful |
| `sean` | old | *seanbhean* | an old woman |
| `síor` | eternal, continuous | *síordheifir*, *síorghrá* | constant hurry, eternal love |

## Negative Prefixes

These prefixes add a negative meaning like "un-", "dis-", or "non-".

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `amh` / `aimh` | un- | *aimhleas* | harm, damage |
| `an` / `ain` | un- | *anabaí*, *aineolaí* | unripe, ignorant person |
| `dí` | un-, dis- | *dímheas* | disrespect |
| `éa` / `éi` | un-, non- | *éadrom* | light (not heavy) |
| `eas` / `eis` | un- | *easonóir* | dishonour, shame |
| `mí` | un-, mis- | *míshásta* | unhappy, dissatisfied |
| `neamh` | un-, non- | *neamhbhall*, *neamhbheo* | non-member, lifeless |

## Prefixes with Verbal Adjectives

The prefixes `so-`, `do-`, and `in-` are now almost exclusively used with verbal adjectives (participles) to indicate possibility.

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `so` | easily | *sobhriste* | easily broken, fragile |
| `do` | difficult, impossible | *dodhéanta* | impossible to do |
| `in` | -able, possible | *inite* | edible |

## Numbers as Prefixes

Numbers can also function as prefixes, often corresponding to English prefixes like "mono-", "bi-", and "tri-".

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `aon` | one-, mono- | *aondiachas* | monotheism |
| `dá` | two-, bi- | *dátheangach* | bilingual |
| `dé` | two-, bi- | *décheannach* | two-headed |
| `trí` | three-, tri- | *trídhathach* | three-coloured |
| `ceathair` | four- | *ceathairchosach* | four-legged |
| `cúig` | five- | *cúigshiollach* | five-syllable |
| `sé` | six- | *séchosach* | six-legged |
| `seacht` | seven- | *seachtsiollach* | seven-syllable |
| `ocht` | eight- | *ochtfhillte* | eight-fold |
| `naoi` | nine- | *naoicheannach* | nine-headed |
| `deich` | ten- | *deich-cheannach* | ten-headed |
