In Standard Irish, attributive adjectives that follow a noun are declined, meaning their form changes to agree with the noun in gender, number, and case. In the dialects, however, there is a tendency to use adjectives in their base form, especially outside of fixed phrases.

Adjectives that come before a noun are not declined. Predicative adjectives (those that follow a verb like `bí`) are also not declined and remain in their base form.

## General Rules of Agreement

### Lenition of Adjectives

An adjective is lenited in the following situations:

- After a feminine singular noun in the nominative case.
  - *bean mhór* (a large woman)
- After a masculine singular noun in the genitive case.
  - *hata an fhir mhóir* (the hat of the large man)
- After a noun in the dative singular, following a preposition and the article, if the noun itself is mutated.
  - *don tsráid mhór* (to the large street)
  - *ag an mbean mhór* (with the large woman)
  - In Connacht and Munster, the adjective after a masculine noun in this context is often not lenited: *don fhear mór*, *ag an bhfear mór*.
- After a noun in the vocative singular (both masculine and feminine).
  - *A fhir bhig!* (Little man!)
  - *A bhean bheag!* (Little woman!)
- After a weak plural noun that ends in a slender consonant.
  - *fir mhóra* (large men)

Note: The "dentals rule" (where d, t, s resist lenition after d, n, t, l, s) does not apply to attributive adjectives in the standard language.
- *an bhean dheas* (the nice woman)

### Gender Differences

Irish adjectives generally do not have different forms for masculine and feminine nouns in the nominative singular or in the plural. The primary difference is the lenition that occurs after feminine nominative singular nouns.
- *an fear mór* (the large man)
- *an bhean mhór* (the large woman)

Distinct forms for gender appear in the genitive singular and vocative singular.

### Case and Number Forms

- **Genitive Plural**: The form of the adjective depends on whether the noun has a weak or strong plural.
  - With a **weak plural** noun, the adjective takes its **nominative singular** form: *na bhfear mór* (of the large men).
  - With a **strong plural** noun, the adjective takes its **nominative plural** form: *na mbuachaillí móra* (of the large boys).
- **Vocative Singular**:
  - After a masculine noun, the adjective takes its **genitive singular** form: *A fhir mhóir!* (Large man!).
  - After a feminine noun, the adjective takes its **nominative singular** form: *A bhean mhór!* (Large woman!).
- **Vocative Plural**: The adjective takes its **nominative plural** form: *A fheara móra!* (Large men!).
- **Dative Singular**:
  - For 1st declension adjectives, the form is **palatalised** (slenderised): *ag an bhfuinneoig bhig* (at the small window).
  - For 2nd and 3rd declension adjectives, the form is unchanged.
- **Dative Plural**: The adjective always takes its **nominative plural** form: *ag na fuinneoga beaga* (at the small windows).

## First Declension (Consonant Endings)

This class includes most adjectives ending in a consonant, except for those ending in `-úil` or some in `-ir`.

### Subgroup 1: Broad Consonants

This subgroup includes most adjectives ending in a broad consonant, such as `mór` (large), `dearg` (red), and `gorm` (blue).

- **Genitive Singular (Masc.)**: The final consonant is palatalised (made slender). Example: `mór` → `móir`.
- **Genitive Singular (Fem.)**: The final consonant is palatalised and the ending `-e` is added. Example: `mór` → `móire`. Polysyllabic adjectives may be shortened (syncope): `ramhar` → `raimhre`.
- **Nominative Plural**: The ending `-a` is added. Example: `mór` → `móra`. Polysyllabic adjectives may be shortened: `ramhar` → `ramhra`.

### Subgroup 2: Slender Consonants

This subgroup includes all adjectives ending in a slender consonant, such as `maith` (good) and `álainn` (beautiful).

- **Genitive Singular (Masc.)**: The form is the same as the nominative singular (as it is already slender). Example: `maith` → `mhaith`.
- **Genitive Singular (Fem.)**: The ending `-e` is added. Example: `maith` → `maithe`. Polysyllabic adjectives may be shortened: `saibhir` → `saibhre`.
- **Nominative Plural**: The ending `-e` is added. Example: `maith` → `maithe`.

### Subgroup 3: Consonants Resisting Palatalisation

This group includes adjectives ending in `-ll`, `-nn`, `-rr`, or a long vowel + `-ch` (e.g., `-íoch`). It also includes several specific adjectives like `deas` (nice), `trom` (heavy), and `fliuch` (wet).

- **Genitive Singular (Masc.)**: The form is the same as the nominative singular (no palatalisation). Example: `mall` → `mhall`.
- **Genitive Singular (Fem.)**: The final consonant is palatalised and `-e` is added. Example: `mall` → `moille` (irregular palatalisation), `imníoch` → `imníche`.
- **Nominative Plural**: The ending `-a` is added. Example: `mall` → `malla`.

### Subgroup 4: Adjectives in `-ach`

This subgroup includes all polysyllabic adjectives ending in `-ach` or `-each`, such as `salach` (dirty) and `Éireannach` (Irish).

- **Genitive Singular (Masc.)**: The ending changes to `-aigh`. Example: `salach` → `shalaigh`.
- **Genitive Singular (Fem.)**: The ending changes to `-aí`. Example: `salach` → `salaí`.
- **Nominative Plural**: The ending becomes `-acha`. Example: `salach` → `salacha`.

## Second Declension

This class includes adjectives ending in `-úil` and some ending in `-ir`.

### Subgroup 1: Adjectives in `-úil`

The ending `-úil` derives from `amhail` (like).

- **Genitive Singular (Masc.)**: The form is the same as the nominative singular. Example: `bródúil` → `bhródúil`.
- **Genitive Singular (Fem.)**: The ending is depalatalised (made broad) and becomes `-úla`. Example: `bródúil` → `bródúla`.
- **Nominative Plural**: The form is the same as the feminine genitive singular, `-úla`.

### Subgroup 2: Adjectives in `-ir`

This group includes adjectives like `deacair` (difficult), `cóir` (just), and `socair` (calm).

- **Genitive Singular (Masc.)**: The form is the same as the nominative singular. Example: `deacair` → `dheacair`.
- **Genitive Singular (Fem.)**: The ending is depalatalised, shortened, and becomes `-ra`. Example: `deacair` → `deacra`.
- **Nominative Plural**: The form is the same as the feminine genitive singular, `-ra`.

## Third Declension (Vowel Endings)

This class includes all adjectives ending in a vowel, such as `crua` (hard). These adjectives are indeclinable; their form does not change, apart from initial mutations.
