In Irish, descriptive adjectives almost always follow the noun they modify. For example, *sliabh ard* means 'a high mountain'.

However, some adjectives and other modifying words can be used as prefixes (`réimír`), which are joined to the beginning of a noun or another adjective. This is the main exception to the standard word order.

### Types of Prefixed Adjectives

The use of adjectives as prefixes varies:

*   **Prefix-only adjectives**: Some adjectives, like `dea-` (good) and `droch-` (bad), exist only in this prefixed form.
    *   *dea-bhád* - a good boat
    *   *drochbhád* - a bad boat
*   **Preferred as prefixes**: Some adjectives are much more common as prefixes. For example, `sean-` (old) is usually prefixed.
    *   *seanfhear* - an old man (more common than *fear sean*)
    *   However, if `sean` is modified by an adverb, it must follow the noun: *fear an-sean* (a very old man).
*   **Intensifying prefixes**: Some prefixes act like adverbs to intensify an adjective or noun.
    *   *an-mhaith* - very good
    *   *fíormhaith* - really good
    *   *rómhaith* - too good
    *   *an-charr* - a great car (literally 'very-car')
*   **Rare or specialised prefixes**: Most adjectives are rarely used as prefixes. When they are, it is often to form a specific, fixed term.
    *   *ardrí* - High King
    *   But: *sliabh ard* - a high mountain

### Prefixes with Verbal Adjectives

A small set of prefixes—`so-`, `do-`, and `in-`—are now used almost exclusively with verbal adjectives (participles) to indicate possibility or difficulty.

*   *sobhriste* - easily breakable
*   *dodhéanta* - impossible
*   *inite* - edible

## Usage Rules

### Mutations

Most prefixes cause **lenition** on the word that follows them.

*   `dea-` + `ceol` → `dea-cheol` (good music)
*   `sean-` + `bean` → `seanbhean` (old woman)

An exception is the older prefix `éa-` (un-), which causes eclipsis. In modern spelling, the eclipsed consonant is often omitted: `éa` + `cóir` becomes `éagóir` (injustice), from an older spelling `éagcóir`.

### Spelling and Hyphenation

*   Prefixes are usually written as one word with the noun or adjective they modify: `seanbhean`.
*   **`an-`** (meaning 'very') and **`dea-`** ('good') are always followed by a hyphen.
    *   *an-mhaith* - very good
    *   *dea-bhean* - a good woman
*   The negative prefix **`an-`** ('un-') is written without a hyphen and changes to `ain-` before a slender vowel.
*   A hyphen is used to separate consecutive prefixes: *droch-sheanbhád* (a bad old boat).
*   A hyphen is used when a prefix and the following word have the same vowel at their boundary.
    *   *ró-óg* - too young
    *   *sean-nós* - old style

### Vowel Harmony

Most prefixes do not change their form to match the following vowel (the 'caol le caol, leathan le leathan' rule). A notable exception is the negative prefix `an-` / `ain-`.

### Dialect Note: Munster Irish

In Munster Irish, an epenthetic vowel (an extra `-a-`) is often inserted between a prefix ending in `l`, `n`, or `r` and a following consonant.

*   *ana-mhaith* - very good
*   *seana-bhean* - old woman

## Common Prefixes

### General Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ais` | back | `aisbhreathnaigh` | to look back |
| `an-` | very | `an-mhaith`, `an-charr` | very good, great car |
| `ard` | high, arch- | `ardeaspag` | archbishop |
| `ath` | re-, again- | `Athbhliain`, `athbheoigh` | New Year, to revive |
| `barr` | excellent, exceedingly | `barrthuirseach` | terribly tired |
| `corr` | occasional, odd, strange | `corruimhir`, `corrdhuine` | odd number, the odd one out |
| `dea-` | good | `dea-cheol` | good music |
| `dearg` | red, intense | `deargamadán` | complete idiot |
| `dian` | intense, strict, severe | `dianchosc` | strict prohibition |
| `droch` | bad | `drochbhád` | a bad boat |
| `fíor` | truly | `fíormhaith` | truly good |
| `il` | many, poly- | `ilteangach` | polyglot, multilingual |
| `leath` | half, one of two | `leathchéad`, `leathshúil` | half a hundred (50), one eye |
| `oll` | huge, super | `ollmhargadh`, `ollscoil` | supermarket, university |
| `príomh` | first-, main- | `príomhtheanga` | main language |
| `rí` | royal, very, extremely | `ríshean` | very old |
| `ró` | excessively, too much | `róshean` | too old |
| `sár` | extremely | `sárálainn` | extremely beautiful |
| `sean` | old | `seanbhean` | an old woman |
| `síor` | eternal, continuous | `síordheifir`, `síorghrá` | constant hurry, eternal love |

### Negative Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `amh` / `aimh` | un- | `aimhleas` | harm, damage |
| `an` / `ain` | un- | `anabaí`, `aineolaí` | unripe, ignorant person |
| `dí` | un-, dis- | `dímheas` | disrespect, lack of respect |
| `éa` / `éi` | un- | `éadrom` | light (not heavy) |
| `eas` / `eis` | un- | `easonóir` | dishonour, shame |
| `mí` | un- | `míshásta` | unhappy, dissatisfied |
| `neamh` | un-, non- | `neamhbhall`, `neamhbheo` | non-member, lifeless |

### Prefixes with Participles

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |

| `so` | easily possible | `sobhriste` | easily breakable |
| `do` | impossible, difficult | `dodhéanta` | impossible to do |
| `in` | possible | `inite` | edible |
