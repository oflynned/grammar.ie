Irish has two verbs that translate as "to be": the copula `is` and the substantive verb `bí` (usually seen in its present tense form, `tá`). This distinction is a core concept in Irish grammar and a common point of confusion for learners.

The copula `is` is used for permanent identification, classification, and definition. It answers the question "what is it?". It links a subject to a noun or pronoun that defines what it is.

The substantive verb `tá` is used for describing temporary states, locations, and characteristics. It answers the question "how is it?" or "where is it?".

### Core Function: Identification and Classification

The copula's main job is to state that one thing *is* another thing in a defining sense.

*   *Is múinteoir mé.* - I am a teacher.
*   *Is Éireannach í.* - She is an Irish person.
*   *Is madra é sin.* - That is a dog.

<Warning>
Learners often mix up `is` and `tá`. Remember: use `is` to say *what* something is (a noun), and use `tá` to say *how* it is (an adjective) or *where* it is (a location).

| `is` (The Copula) | `tá` (The Substantive Verb) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **For identification (with a noun)** | **For description (with an adjective)** |
| *Is dochtúir í.* (She is a doctor.) | *Tá sí tinn.* (She is sick.) |
| *Is é an fear é.* (He is the man.) | *Tá an fear ard.* (The man is tall.) |
| **For classification (with a noun)** | **For location (with a preposition)** |
| *Is ainmhí an cat.* (The cat is an animal.) | *Tá an cat ar an mbord.* (The cat is on the table.) |

</Warning>

### Sentence Structure

Copular sentences have a different structure from most other Irish sentences. The basic order is: **Copula + Predicate + Subject**.

The **predicate** is the noun that identifies or classifies the subject.

*   *Is [predicate] [subject].*
*   *Is [múinteoir] [mé].* - I am a teacher.
*   *Is [dochtúir] [Síle].* - Síle is a doctor.

When the predicate is a definite noun (i.e., it has the definite article `an`, a possessive adjective like `mo`, or is a proper noun), a pronoun (`é`, `í`, `iad`) must be inserted between the copula and the predicate.

*   *Is é Seán an múinteoir.* - Seán is the teacher.
*   *Is í mo dheirfiúr í.* - She is my sister.

### Forms of the Copula

The copula has different forms depending on the tense and whether the sentence is a statement, a question, or a negative. Unlike regular verbs, it does not conjugate for person.

| Context | Form | Mutation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Affirmative** | `is` | None | *Is bean í.* (She is a woman.) |
| **Present Negative** | `ní` | Lenition | *Ní fear é.* (He is not a man.) |
| **Present Question** | `an` | Eclipsis | *An dochtúir tú?* (Are you a doctor?) |
| **Past Affirmative** | `ba` | Lenition | *Ba dhochtúir é.* (He was a doctor.) |
| **Past Negative** | `níor` | Lenition | *Níor dhochtúir é.* (He was not a doctor.) |
| **Past Question** | `ar` | Lenition | *Ar dhochtúir é?* (Was he a doctor?) |
