The Irish copula is a special verb used for identification and classification, roughly equivalent to "to be" in sentences like "He *is* a doctor". It has only two basic forms: `is` for the present tense and `ba` for the past and conditional. All other forms are created when these basic forms merge with particles, conjunctions, or prepositions. In some cases, the copula is omitted entirely, and a particle like `ní` takes on its function.

This page provides a comprehensive reference for the many forms the copula can take in different grammatical contexts.

## Sentence Forms

This table shows the most common forms of the copula in statements, questions, and clauses. Dialectal variants are shown in parentheses.

| Type | Present (before consonant) | Present (before vowel) | Past / Conditional (before consonant) | Past / Conditional (before vowel) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive** | `is` | `is` | `ba` | `b’` (`dob’`) |
| **Negative** | `ní` (`nín`, `chan`, `cha`) | `níh` (`nín`, `chan`, `cha`) | `níor` (`ní ba`, `char`) | `níorbh` (`ní b’`, `charbh`) |
| **Positive Question** | `an` (`ar`) | `an` (`ar`, `ab`) | `ar` (`an mba`) | `arbh` (`an mb’`) |
| **Negative Question** | `nach` | `nach` (`nab`) | `nár` (`nach mba`) | `nárbh` (`nach mb’`) |

## Usage Before Vowels

The form of the copula often changes depending on whether the following word begins with a vowel or a consonant.

### The form ba

The past/conditional form `ba` has two variants before vowels: `ba` and `b’`.

**Use `ba` before:**
*   The pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, `eisean`, `ise`, `iadsan`, and `éard`.
    *   *Ba é an fear é* - He was the man.
*   Prepositions.
    *   *Ba ar an mbord a chuir sé é* - He put it on the table.
*   Prepositional pronouns.
    *   *Ba air a chuir sé é* - He put it on it.
*   Adverbs.
    *   *Ba arú inné a tháinig sé* - He came the day before yesterday.

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In spoken Irish, a glide sound ([j] or [w]) often appears between `ba` and `é`: `ba é` is pronounced [bəje:] or [bəwe:].
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, `b’é`, `b’í`, `b’iad`, `b’ea` are common. Before `ea`, the form `ab` is often used, even when it is not a relative clause.
</Dialect>

**Use `b’` before:**
*   Nouns.
    *   *B’fhear mór óil é* - He was a great drinking man.
*   Adjectives.
    *   *B’álainn an radharc é* - It was a beautiful view.
*   Adverbs derived from adjectives.
    *   *B’annamh ólta é* - He was rarely drunk.
*   The adverb `amhlaidh` (so, thus).
    *   *Agus b’amhlaidh an scéal i Ros Comáin* - And that was how the situation was in Roscommon.
*   The demonstrative pronouns `eo`, `in`, `iúd`.
    *   *Agus b’eo ina ndiaidh é* - And off he went after them.

### Forms ending in -r

For copula forms ending in `-r`, the rules for adding a `-b` or `-bh` before a vowel depend on the tense.

**In the present tense, use `gur` (without `-b`) before:**
*   Most nouns: *gur amadán mé* - that I am a fool.
*   Prepositions: *gur i nGaillimh atá mé* - that I am in Galway.
*   Prepositional pronouns: *gur inti atá sé* - that it is in her.
*   Adverbs not derived from adjectives: *gur anseo atá mé* - that I am here.
*   The pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, etc., when part of an infinitive construction: *gur iad a chur air is fearr liom* - that I prefer to put them on it.

**In the present tense, use `gurb` (with `-b`) before:**
*   The pronouns `é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, `éard`, etc.: *gurb é mo mhac é* - that he is my son.
*   Demonstrative pronouns `eo`, `in`, `iúd`: *Deir sí gurb in é an fear* - She said that this was the man.
*   Adjectives: *gurb álainn an lá é* - that it is a beautiful day.
*   Adverbs derived from adjectives: *gurb annamh a rinne sé é* - that he rarely did it.
*   The adverb `amhlaidh`: *gurb amhlaidh atá* - that it is so.
*   Certain nouns followed by prepositions, such as `eol do` (known to), `acmhainn do` (able to), `eagal le` (afraid of): *gurb eol dom* - that I know.
*   A few specific nouns like `ionadh` (wonder) and `iontas` (surprise): *gurb ionadh liom é* - that it surprises me.

<Note>
Other present tense copula forms ending in `-r` (like `arb`, `cérb`, `sularb`) usually add `-b` before a vowel in all contexts, including before nouns and prepositions. For example, *an fear darb ainm Seán* - a man named Seán.
</Note>

**In the past tense,** forms ending in `-r` before a consonant **always** add `-bh` before a vowel (`gurbh`, `arbh`, `níorbh`, `cérbh`, `sularbh`).
*   *Gurbh amadán mé* - That I was a fool.

### The form ní

The negative form `ní` adds an h-prefix before a vowel in specific cases:
*   Before pronouns: *Ní hé mo mhac é* - He is not my son.
*   Before some adjectives: *Ní hionann iad* - They are not the same.
*   Before a few nouns (generally the same ones that take `gurb`): *Ní hionadh é sin* - That is no wonder.

It does not add an h-prefix before most other nouns: *Ní amadán mé* - I am not a fool.

## Mutations after the Copula

*   **No lenition** occurs after present and subjunctive forms.
*   **Lenition** occurs after all past and conditional forms.
    *   *ba mhaith* - it was good

<Dialect region="Ulster Connacht">
In Ulster and Connacht, adjectives beginning with `t`, `d`, or `s` are often not lenited after `ba`. For example, `ba deas` (it was nice), `ba te` (it was hot).
</Dialect>

## Omission of the Copula

In certain situations, the copula form can be left out entirely.

*   `is` can be omitted before emphatic pronouns (`eisean`, `mise féin`) and nouns (except in classification sentences).
    *   *Is eisean an fear* → *Eisean an fear* - He is the man.
*   In a normal copula sentence, `is` cannot be omitted before an adjective.
    *   *Is maith liom í* - I like her. (Not: *Maith liom í*)
*   However, in a cleft sentence (used for emphasis), `is` can be omitted before an adjective.
    *   *Is tuirseach atá sé* → *Tuirseach atá sé* - He is tired.
*   In exclamatory identification sentences, `is` and the secondary predicate are always omitted.
    *   *Grá mo chroí thú!* - You are the love of my heart!
*   After some particles and conjunctions (`ní`, `an`, `nach`, `gur`, etc.), `is` is omitted, and the particle itself functions as the copula form.
*   The past form `ba` is rarely omitted.

## Dialectal and Other Variants

*   **Pronunciation of `is`**: Usually pronounced [əs], but with a slender [s′] before pronouns like `é`, `í`, `iad`.
*   **Apheresis of `is`**: In speech, `is` often merges with a following pronoun: `is é` becomes `sé` or `’sé`, `is í` becomes `sí` or `’sí`.
    *   *Is é an fear é* → *’Sé an fear é* - He is the man.
*   **`dob’`/`do`**: In Munster, `dob’` (before a vowel) or `do` (before a consonant) is sometimes used instead of `ba`, especially before pronouns.
*   **Connemara Questions**: `ar` is often used for `an` before consonants and vowels. `ab` is used for `an` before pronouns like `é`, `í`, `iad`.
*   **Connemara Past/Conditional**: Forms like `ní ba`, `an mba`, `nach mba`, `go mba` are common. These are older forms, replaced by forms with `-r` in the standard language.
*   **Ulster `cha`/`chan`**: The negative particle `cha` or `chan` is used instead of `ní`. `chan` is used before pronouns and nouns.
*   **Ulster "Generalised `é`"**: Forms like `is é`, `ní hé`, `ab é` are used regardless of the gender or number of the noun.
    *   *Is é tinn a bhí sí* - She was ill. (Standard: *Is tinn a bhí sí*)
*   **Prepositional Relative Forms**: Forms like `lenar` combine a preposition (`le`), a relative pronoun (`a`), and the copula (`is`).
    *   *An fear lenar maith é* - The man who likes it (literally: the man with-whom-is good it).
    *   This is an alternative to the more common structure: *an fear ar maith leis é*.
*   **`níos` Variants**: The comparative particle `níos` has several dialectal forms: `níosa` (Munster), `nas` (Ulster), and `tíos` or `díos` (Connemara).
