Irish has two distinct verbs that both translate as "to be": the copula `is` and the substantive verb `bí`. Understanding their different roles is fundamental to forming correct sentences. The copula `is` is used for classification and identification, while the verb `bí` (whose present tense form is `tá`) is used to describe states, conditions, and locations.

## The Substantive Verb: Bí

The verb `bí` expresses existence in a particular state or location. Its most common present tense form is `tá`. It is often used with prepositions to describe where something is or what condition it is in.

For example, `táim` means "I am" in the sense of "I exist" or "I am here". It is frequently used with the prepositional pronoun `ann` (from `i` + `é`, meaning "in it") to express "there is" or "there are".

*   *Tá daoine ann.* - There are people.
*   *Tá daoine (ann) sa bhaile nach mbeannódh sa tsráid duit.* - There are people in the village who would not greet you in the street.

While `ann` can be omitted if another location is specified, it is required to express simple existence.

*   *Níl aon Dia ach tú.* - There is no God but you.
*   *Níl aon Dia ann.* - There is no God. (Here, `ann` is necessary).

`Bí` is also used to describe temporary states, such as standing, living, or working in a certain role, as well as with most predicative adjectives.

| Example | Translation | Literal Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Tá sé ann.* | He is there. / He exists. | He is in-it. |
| *Tá sé sa teach.* | He is in the house. | He is in the house. |
| *Tá sé ar an mbord.* | He is on the table. | He is on the table. |
| *Tá sé ina sheasamh.* | He is standing. | He is in his standing. |
| *Tá sé ina chónaí.* | He lives. | He is in his living. |
| *Tá sé ina dhochtúir.* | He is a doctor. | He is in his being a doctor. |
| *Tá sé ina lá.* | It is day. | It is in its being day. |
| *Tá sé fuar.* | It is cold. | It is in a state of being cold. |

### Describing Measurements

`Bí` is used without a preposition to express measurements of size, weight, age, and time.

*   *Tá sí seacht mbliana déag d’aois.* - She is 17 years old.
*   *Tá sí naoi gclocha meáchain.* - She weighs 9 stone.
*   *Tá sé ceathrú chun a trí.* - It is a quarter to three.

### As an Auxiliary Verb

`Bí` also functions as an auxiliary verb, most notably in the progressive aspect to describe an action currently in progress. This usage still carries the sense of a temporary state.

*   *Tá sé ag ól.* - He is drinking (right now).

## The Copula: Is

The copula `is` does not describe a state but simply links a subject to a predicate. It serves to classify or identify something, much like an equals sign (=) in mathematics. Sentences with the copula state that X is a member of group Y (classification) or that X is the same as Y (identification).

## Comparing Bí and Is

While both verbs can sometimes be used to express similar ideas, they carry different nuances. A statement of profession, for example, can be made with either verb, but the meaning changes slightly.

| With the copula (`is`) | With the substantive verb (`bí`) |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Is dochtúir é.* | *Tá sé ina dhochtúir.* |
| He is a doctor. | He is a doctor. |

The copula sentence (`Is dochtúir é`) makes a statement of classification. It defines his identity as a doctor, suggesting a permanent or core characteristic. The sentence with `bí` (`Tá sé ina dhochtúir`) describes his current state or role. It implies he works as a doctor, a condition that could be temporary.

*   *Tá sé ina fhear anois.* - He is a man now. (He was a boy before; his state has changed).
*   *Is fear é anois.* - He is a man now. (A statement of his new classification).

Although the distinction is often described as permanent (`is`) versus temporary (`bí`), this is a guideline rather than a strict rule. Adverbs can alter the context.

*   *Bhí sé ina amadán riamh.* - He was always an idiot. (Using `bí` with an adverb of permanence).

The core difference is that the copula classifies, while `bí` describes a state.

*   *Is fear é.* - He is a man. (He is classified as a man).
*   *Tá sé ina fhear.* - He is a man. (He is in the state of being a man, possessing manly characteristics).

### In the Past Tense

The distinction becomes particularly important in the past tense. The past tense of the copula is `ba`, and the past tense of `bí` is `bhí`.

*   *Ba mhúinteoir é.* - He was a teacher.
*   *Bhí sé ina mhúinteoir.* - He was a teacher.

<Warning>
Using the copula `ba` to describe a permanent classification of a person implies that the person is no longer alive. Because the classification was permanent, the only way for it to be in the past is if the subject no longer exists. Using `bhí`, however, simply implies that the person's state has changed; he was a teacher, but now he has a different profession or is retired.
</Warning>

## Usage with Predicative Adjectives

### Adjectives with Bí

In modern Irish, most predicative adjectives are used with `bí`. This usage frames the quality as a state or condition.

*   *Tá sé te.* - It is hot.
*   *Tá an fear tinn.* - The man is ill.

With subjectively evaluative adjectives, the particle `go` is often placed before the adjective.

*   *Tá sé go maith.* - It is good.
*   *Tá sé go dona.* - It is bad.

<Note>
Older textbooks sometimes state that `bí` cannot be used with adjectives describing permanent characteristics like size, length, or colour. In these cases, an attributive construction was preferred: *Is fear mór é* (He is a big man) instead of *Tá an fear mór* (The man is big).
</Note>

This restriction is less strict in modern Irish, especially when the adjective is modified by an adverb like `ró-` (too) or `an-` (very).

*   *Tá an bord ró-mhór.* - The table is too big.
*   *Tá an t-éan an-bheag.* - The bird is very small.
*   *Tá sé fada a dhóthain.* - It is long enough.

Adjectives derived from verbs or those ending in `-ach`, `-úil`, and `-mhar` can always be used with `bí`.

*   *Tá sé tuirseach.* - He is tired.
*   *Tá sé buailte.* - He is beaten.

<Note>
For colours, a common construction is `tá dath [adjective] ar [noun]`, meaning "[noun] is [colour]".
- *Bhí dath bán ar a haghaidh.* - Her face was white. (Literally: "There was a white colour on her face.")
</Note>

### Adjectives with the Copula

Using the copula with predicative adjectives is less common in modern Irish and is generally restricted to certain contexts. It describes an inherent, permanent quality rather than a temporary state. The meaning is often exclamatory, emphasising the quality itself.

| With the copula (`is`) | With the substantive verb (`bí`) |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Is maith é!* | *Tá sé go maith.* |
| He is good! / How good he is! | He is good. |

## Further Notes

<Details summary="Expressing existence with `is ann do`">
The phrase `is ann do...` is another way to state that something exists. It is a type of cleft sentence derived from a sentence with `bí`. The structure is essentially `Is ann [atá...]`, where the verb `bí` is omitted and the subject takes the dative form with `do`.

*   *Tá tú ann.* → *Is ann atá tú.* → *Is ann duit.* - There you are! / You exist!
*   *Is ann do na tuairimí seo.* - These opinions exist.
*   *Ní hann don mhonarcha a thuilleadh.* - The factory no longer exists.
*   *An ann dó?* - Does it exist?
</Details>

<Details summary="Linguistic comparison with Spanish">
The distinction between two "to be" verbs is not unique to Irish. Spanish, for example, has `ser` (for permanent qualities, like `is`) and `estar` (for temporary states and locations, like `tá`). The similarity in form between `is`/`ser` and `tá`/`estar` is not a coincidence; `tá` derives from an Indo-European root meaning "to stand", which underlines its core meaning of describing a state or location.

A key difference, however, is that Spanish typically uses `ser` with adjectives, whereas modern Irish overwhelmingly prefers `tá`.
</Details>
