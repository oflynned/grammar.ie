The verb `tar` (to come) is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. It uses several different stems across its various tenses and moods:

-   **Present and Subjunctive:** `tag-`
-   **Preterite:** `tháinig`
-   **Future and Conditional:** `tioc-`
-   **Imperative (2nd person singular):** `tar`

Unlike some other irregular verbs, `tar` does not have special dependent forms.

## Notes on Usage and Dialect

### Dialectal Stems

-   **Connemara:** The form `teara` is used instead of `tar`, and the stem `teag-` is used instead of `tag-`.
-   **Ulster:** The stem `tig` is often used instead of `tag-`. In the 3rd person, it is uninflected, e.g., *tig sé*.

### Preterite without Particles (Ulster and Connacht)

In Ulster and parts of Connacht, preterite verbal particles are often omitted with `tar`.

-   `ní tháinig` (instead of standard `níor tháinig`)
-   `an dtáinig` (instead of standard `ar tháinig`)
-   `go dtáinig` (instead of standard `gur tháinig`)

### Synthetic Preterite Forms (Munster)

The following synthetic forms for the preterite are still in common use in Munster, often replacing the standard analytic forms (e.g., `tháinig mé`).

-   **1st sg:** `thángas` (variants: `thánag`, `thánas`, `thána`)
-   **2nd sg:** `thán(g)ais` (variant: `tháinis`)
-   **1st pl:** `thánamair` (more common in Munster than standard `thángamar`)
-   **2nd pl:** `thán(g)abhair`
-   **3rd pl:** `thán(g)adar`

The spellings with `-ng-` represent a more standardised form; in spoken Munster Irish, these are often pronounced with just an `-n-`.

### Pronunciation Notes

-   **`thángamar` / `thángthas`:** In these forms, the `-ng-` is separated by an epenthetic vowel [ə]. `thángamar` is pronounced [ha:nəgəmər] and `thángthas` is [ha:nəkəs]. The `ng` is not pronounced as a single velar nasal sound (as in `teanga`). In Munster, `thánamair` is pronounced [ha:nəmər′].
-   **`tháinig`:** In Ulster and Connacht, the final `g` is often hardened, resulting in a pronunciation that sounds like "tháinic" [ha:n′ək′].

### Historical Forms

The old 2nd/3rd person present subjunctive form `tí` is preserved in the preposition `go dtí` (to). It is rarely seen with other conjunctions, but an example is *sula dtí maidin* (before morning).
