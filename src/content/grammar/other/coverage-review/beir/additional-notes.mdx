The verb `beir` is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. Its meanings include "carry", "hold", "win", "bring", "take", "give birth", and "catch".

It is irregular in two main stems:
-   **Past Tense (Preterite):** uses the stem `rug-`.
-   **Future and Conditional:** uses the stem `béar-`.

Unlike some other irregular verbs, `beir` does not have special dependent forms.

## Usage Notes

When used to mean "to give birth" (for humans), the autonomous form is common, expressing a passive sense.

-   *Rugadh é.* - He was born.
-   *Rugadh é di.* - She gave birth to him. (Literally: "He was born from her.")

## Dialectal Forms

### Ulster

-   **Present Tense:** In the south of Ulster, `beiridh` is used alongside `beireann`. In the north, the endingless form `beir` is used. The form `breitheann` (to throw, to calve) is also found. Autonomous forms include `rugtar`, `rugthar` (South), and `beirtear` (North).
-   **Future Tense:** `beirfidh` is found alongside `béarfaidh`. The autonomous form is `rugfar` (pronounced `rucar`).
-   **Past Tense:** Both `bheir` and `rug` are used. The autonomous form `rugadh` means "was born", while `rugas` (from `*-thas*`) or `beireadh` is used for other meanings, e.g., *beireadh air* (he was caught).
-   **Verbal Noun:** In the north, `rugan` is used alongside `breith`.

### Connacht

-   **Present Tense:** Both `beireann` and `béarann` are found.
-   **Imperfect Tense:** Both `bheireadh` and `bhéaradh` occur. Autonomous forms are `beirtí` and `béartaí` (unlenited).
-   **Future/Conditional:** Only `béarfaidh` (future) and `béarfadh` (conditional) are used. The autonomous conditional is `béarfaí` (unlenited).
-   **Past Tense:** The forms `rug`, `bhéar`, and `bhreith` appear. As in Ulster, the autonomous `rugadh` means "was born". The form `rugas` or `rugús` is used for other meanings, e.g., *Rugús air* (He was caught). The ending `-ús` is a blend of `-adh` and `-(th)as`.

### Munster

-   **Future/Conditional:** The future `béarfaidh` is typically pronounced [b′iarhig′], and the conditional `béarfadh` is [b′iarhax], reflecting a common Munster shift from `éa` to `ia`.
-   **Past Tense:** `bheir` or `riug` (with a slender 'r') are used. However, in Dingle, `rug`/`riug` is now rare.
-   **Verbal Adjective:** The verbal adjective has a broad 'r': `bertha`.

## Related Verbs

The verb `beir` is the root of two other common irregular verbs:

-   **`tabhair`** (to give): This verb was formed from the prefix `do-` + `beir`. The prefix is visible in older forms like `do-bheireann`. When the `do-` was dropped, the lenition remained, distinguishing `bheireann sé` (he gives) from `beireann sé` (he brings/carries).
-   **`abair`** (to say): This verb was formed from the prefix `as-` ("out") + `beir`, literally meaning "to bring out".
