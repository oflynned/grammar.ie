Monosyllabic verbs can end in `-igh` (older forms also use `-idh`). They belong to the first conjugation, not the second, because they form their future tense with the marker `-f-`. The term "monosyllabic" also includes polysyllabic verbs created by composition, such as `ruadhóigh` (to burn up) from `dóigh` (to burn), or `eisiaigh` (to exclude) from `iaigh` (to close).

For verbs with a **long vowel** or diphthong in the stem (e.g., `-á`, `-é`, `-í`, `-ó`, `-ú`, `-eo`, `-ao`, `-ua`), the `-igh` or `-gh` ending is only added in the 2nd person singular imperative and in the past tense (preterite). In the standard language, it is omitted elsewhere. This is a feature of the modern spelling reform; for example, older `dóigheann sé` is now `dónn sé` (he burns).

For verbs with a **short vowel**, the `-igh` or `-gh` is part of the verb root and is not omitted. However, before other endings, it orthographically becomes `-í` since the spelling reform.

There are four main patterns to distinguish:

1.  Verbs ending in `-áigh`, `-óigh`, `-eoigh`, `-úigh`, `-aoigh`, `-uaigh`, `-iaigh`, and `-aeigh`.
2.  Verbs ending in `-éigh`.
3.  Verbs ending in `-ígh`.
4.  Verbs ending in a short vowel + `-igh` or a consonant + `-igh`.

## Verbs with a broad long vowel or diphthong

This group includes verbs ending in `-áigh`, `-óigh`, `-eoigh`, `-úigh`, `-aoigh`, `-uaigh`, and `-iaigh`. The stem vowel is a long broad vowel (`á`, `ó`, `eo`, `ú`, `ao`) or a diphthong ending in a broad vowel (`ua`, `ia`).

Examples include `láigh` (to dawn), `dóigh` (to burn), `clóigh` (to tame), `reoigh` (to freeze), `brúigh` (to press), `glaoigh` (to call), `luaigh` (to mention), and `iaigh` (to close).

### Conjugation Rules

-   Future and conditional endings use a **broad f**: `-faidh`, `-far`, `-faí`.
-   Autonomous and imperfect endings use a **slender t**: `-itear`, `-iteá`, `-ití`.
-   The `-i-` before some endings is purely for slenderisation and is not pronounced as a separate syllable (e.g., `dóim` is pronounced [do:m′], not [do:-im]).
-   The `-igh` is omitted before all endings.
-   In the present subjunctive, `-igh` is also dropped, leaving only the stem vowel (e.g., *go ndó sé*).
-   The `a` is dropped from endings like `-ann`, `-adh`, and `-amar`, resulting in `-nn`, `-dh`, and `-mar` (e.g., `dónn sé`, `dhómar`).

### Verbs ending in `-aeigh`

The rare verbs ending in `-aeigh`, such as `glaeigh` (to glue), also belong to this group. They take the full endings `-ann`, `-amar`, `-adh`, etc., creating the unusual letter sequence `-aea-`. However, future/conditional endings are broad (`-faidh`, `-far`) and endings with `-t-` are slender (`-iteá`, `-itear`).
Examples: `glaeann sé`, `glaeitear`, `ghlaeigh sé`, `ghlaeadh sé`, `glaefaidh sé`.

## Verbs ending in `-éigh`

Examples include `léigh` (to read) and `téigh` (to heat).

### Conjugation Rules

-   Future and conditional endings use a **slender f**: `-ifidh`, `-ifear`, `-ifí`.
-   Autonomous and imperfect endings use a **slender t**: `-itear`, `-iteá`, `-ití`.
-   The `-igh` is omitted before endings.
-   In the present subjunctive, `-igh` is also dropped, leaving only the stem vowel `-é` (e.g., *go lé sé*).

## Verbs ending in `-ígh`

Examples include `maígh` (to assert) and `caígh` (to weep).

### Conjugation Rules

-   Future and conditional endings use a **slender f**: `-fidh`, `-fear`, `-fí`.
-   Autonomous and imperfect endings use a **slender t**: `-tear`, `-teá`, `-tí`.
-   The `-gh` is omitted before endings.
-   The slender endings `-im` and `-inn` lose their vowel, becoming `-m` and `-nn`.
-   Endings after the stem vowel `í` follow standard spelling rules: `-ann` becomes `-onn`, and `-amar` becomes `-omar`.
-   In the present subjunctive, `-gh` is dropped, leaving the stem vowel `-í` (e.g., *go maí sé*).

## Verbs with a short vowel + `-igh`

This group includes verbs ending in a short vowel or consonant followed by `-igh`.
Examples: `nigh` (to wash), `snoigh` (to carve), `gluigh` (to call), `snigh` (to flow), `figh` (to weave), `luigh` (to lie down).

### Conjugation Rules

-   Future and conditional endings use a **slender f**: `-fidh`, `-fear`, `-fí`.
-   Autonomous and imperfect endings use a **slender t**: `-tear`, `-teá`, `-tí`.
-   The ending `-igh` is part of the verb root and is never omitted.
-   According to modern spelling, `-igh` becomes `-í` before most endings. For example:
    -   `-igh` + `-eann` → `-igheann` → `-íann` → `-íonn`.
    -   `-igh` + `-im` → `-ighim` → `-ím`.
    -   `-igh` + `-eamar` → `-igheamar` → `-íamar` → `-íomar`.
    -   `-igh` + `-fidh` → `-ífidh`.
-   Unlike verbs with a long `-ígh` stem, the `i` in the endings `-igh`, `-itear`, `-ití`, and `-iteá` remains short (e.g., `nitear`, not *`nítear`*).
