The verb `bí` (to be) is irregular because it uses several different stems depending on the tense, mood, and whether the form is independent or dependent.

-   **Present Tense:** The independent stem is `tá`.
-   **Habitual Present, Preterite, Imperfect, Imperative:** The stem is `bí`.
-   **Future, Conditional:** The stem is `beidh`/`beadh`.
-   **Dependent Present:** The stem is `fuil`.
-   **Dependent Preterite & Subjunctive:** The stem is `raibh`.

Unlike regular verbs, `bí` does not use preterite particles like `d'`.

## Munster Dialect Forms

The following forms are characteristic of Munster Irish and may differ from the standard language.

### Indicative Mood

**Present Tense**
-   1sg: `táim`, `tá mé`
-   2sg: `tá tú`
-   3sg: `tá sé`
-   1pl: `táimid`
-   2pl: `tá sibh`
-   3pl: `tá siad`, `táid`, `tádar`
-   Autonomous: `táthar`

**Preterite (Past Tense)**
-   1sg: `bhíos`
-   2sg: `bhís`
-   3sg: `bhí sé`
-   1pl: `bhíomar`
-   2pl: `bhíobhair`
-   3pl: `bhíodar`
-   Autonomous: `bhíothas`

**Imperfect (Habitual Past)**
-   1sg: `bhínn`
-   2sg: `bhíteá`
-   3sg: `bhíodh sé`
-   1pl: `bhímis`
-   2pl: `bhíodh sibh`
-   3pl: `bhídís`
-   Autonomous: `bhítí`

**Future Tense**
-   1sg: `bead`
-   2sg: `beidh tú`
-   3sg: `beidh sé`
-   1pl: `beimid`
-   2pl: `beidh sibh`
-   3pl: `beidh siad`
-   Autonomous: `beifear`

### Conditional Mood

-   1sg: `bheinn`
-   2sg: `bheifeá`
-   3sg: `bheadh sé`
-   1pl: `bheimis`
-   2pl: `bheadh sibh`
-   3pl: `bheidís`
-   Autonomous: `bheifí`

### Imperative Mood

-   1sg: `bím`
-   2sg: `bí`
-   3sg: `bíodh sé`
-   1pl: `bímis`
-   2pl: `bígí`
-   3pl: `bídís`
-   Autonomous: `bítear`

### Subjunctive Mood

**Present Subjunctive**
-   1sg: `go raibh mé`
-   2sg: `go raibh tú`
-   3sg: `go raibh sé`
-   1pl: `go rabhaimid`
-   2pl: `go raibh sibh`
-   3pl: `go raibh siad`
-   Autonomous: `go rabhthar`

**Preterite Subjunctive**
-   1sg: `dá mbeinn`
-   2sg: `dá mbeiteá`
-   3sg: `dá mbeadh sé`
-   1pl: `dá mbeimis`
-   2pl: `dá mbeadh sibh`
-   3pl: `dá mbeidís`
-   Autonomous: `dá mbeití`

### Relative Forms

-   **Present:** `atá (sé)`
-   **Future:** `a bheas (sé)`

## Habitual Present and Dependent Forms

### Habitual Present

These forms describe actions that happen regularly.
-   1sg: `bím`
-   2sg: `bíonn tú`
-   3sg: `bíonn sé`
-   1pl: `bímid`
-   2pl: `bíonn sibh`
-   3pl: `bíonn siad`
-   Autonomous: `bítear`
-   Relative: `a bhíos sé`

### Dependent Present Tense (`fuil`)

The stem `fuil` is used in questions and negative statements in the present tense.
-   **Question:** `an bhfuil mé?`, `an bhfuil tú?`, etc.
-   **Negative (`ní`):** `níl mé`, `níl tú`, etc. (`níl` is a contraction of `ní fhuil`).
-   **Negative (`cha`):** `chan fhuil mé`, `chan fhuil tú`, etc. (Mainly Ulster).

### Dependent Preterite (`raibh`)

The stem `raibh` is used in questions and negative statements in the preterite.
-   1sg: `an raibhas`
-   2sg: `an raibhais`
-   3sg: `an raibh sé`
-   1pl: `an rabhamar`
-   2pl: `an rabhabhair`
-   3pl: `an rabhadar`
-   Autonomous: `an rabhthas`

## Notes on Specific Forms and Usage

### First-Person Singular

In the present tense, the analytic form `tá mé` is as common as the synthetic form `táim`. Both are considered standard, along with their dependent counterparts `an bhfuil mé?` / `an bhfuilim?` and `níl mé` / `nílim`.

### Relative Forms

-   The present relative form is `atá` (who/which is). Historically, the verb was always `atá`, and the `a-` prefix has only survived in the relative form. Endings can be added: `atáim`, `atáimid`, `atáthar`.
-   After interrogative words like `cad` and `conas`, the relative particle `a` is often omitted: *Cad (a)tá uait?* (What do you want?), *Conas (a)tá tú?* (How are you?).
-   The habitual present relative form is `a bhíos` (in Conamara `a bhíonns`). In Ulster, it is often unlenited: `a bíos`, `a bíonns`.
-   The future relative form is `a bheas`.
-   These relative forms are widespread in Connacht and Ulster but less so in Munster.

### The Negative Form `níl`

The form `níl` (is not) is a contraction of `ní fhuil`. In Ulster, it is often still pronounced as two syllables, making the spelling `ní fhuil` more representative.

Instead of `mura bhfuil` (if... is not), the form `murab fhuil` occurs in Connacht and Ulster.

### The Negative Particle `cha(n)` in Ulster

In Ulster, the negative particle `cha(n)` is often used instead of `ní`. With the verb `bí`, this results in `chan fhuil`. While `cha` usually causes lenition, forms of `bí` beginning with `b-` are often eclipsed: `cha mbíonn`.

### Subjunctive Forms

In some dialects (Munster, South Connacht), a distinction is made between two present subjunctive forms:
-   **Optative:** `raibh` is used for wishes.
-   **Subjunctive:** `go mbí` (South Connacht) or `go mbé` (Munster) is used after temporal conjunctions like `go`, `nó go`, or `sula`.

In Standard Irish, Ulster, and Mayo, `raibh` is used for both functions.

### Preterite Subjunctive

The preterite subjunctive of `bí` resembles the conditional, whereas for other verbs it resembles the imperfect. Note the difference in the 2nd person singular and autonomous forms:
-   **Preterite Subjunctive:** *dá mbeiteá* (if you were)
-   **Conditional:** *bheifeá* (you would be)
-   **Imperfect:** *bhíteá* (you used to be)

## Verbal Noun and Participle

-   **Verbal Noun:** `bheith`
-   **Participle of Necessity:** `beite`

The form `beite` (older spelling `beithte`) is not used as a verbal adjective ("been"). It functions only as a participle of necessity, meaning "it is necessary to be", though this is rare today.
-   *ní beite a + [Verbal Noun]* (one should not...)
-   *ní beite do [X] a + [Verbal Noun]* (X should not...)

## Grammatical Limitations

-   **Perfect Tense:** The verb `bí` cannot form a perfect tense (e.g., "have been") because it lacks a verbal adjective.
-   **Progressive Aspect:** The progressive aspect (e.g., "I am being") cannot be formed, as the construction `*ag bheith` is not used.
