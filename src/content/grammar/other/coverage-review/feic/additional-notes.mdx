The verb `feic` (to see) is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. Its primary irregularities are in the past tense (preterite), which uses the stem `chonaic` in independent forms and `faca` in dependent forms. Unlike regular verbs, it does not take a preterite particle like `d'`.

## Historical and Dialectal Forms

### Older Forms

Historically, a distinction was made between absolute forms (used independently) and dependent forms (used after certain particles). The absolute forms were based on the stem `(do-)chí-`, while the dependent forms used `feic-`. This distinction is still found in some dialects today. In Connemara and the Official Standard, `feic-` is now used for both.

| Tense | Dependent | Absolute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `feiceann` | `(do-)chíonn` |
| **Imperfect** | `feiceadh` | `(do-)chíodh` |
| **Future** | `feicfidh` | `(do-)chífidh` |
| **Conditional** | `feicfeadh` | `(do-)chífeadh` |

### Dialectal Forms

#### Ulster

*   **Present:** The absolute analytic form is `tí sé` or `tchí sé` (he sees). In the south of the region, `tíonn sé` or `tchíonn sé` is also used. The dependent form is always `ní fheiceann sé`. The autonomous form is `títear`, `tchítear`, `t(ch)íthear`, or `tíonntar`.
*   **Future, Imperfect, Conditional:** These tenses also use the stems `tí-/tchí-` for absolute forms and `feic-` for dependent forms.
*   **Future:** Absolute `tíofaidh` or `tchíofaidh`; in the north also `tiúfaidh sé`. Dependent `ní fheicfidh`. Autonomous `tífear`, `tchífear`; dependent `ní fheicfear`.
*   **Conditional:** Absolute `tífeadh sé` (south) or `thiúfadh sé` (north). Dependent `ní fheicfeadh sé`. Autonomous `tífí`, `thiúfaí`; dependent `ní fheicfí`.
*   **Imperfect:** Absolute `tíodh sé`, dependent `ní fheiceadh sé`.
*   **Preterite:** Absolute `chonnaic`. Dependent `ní fhacaidh`. Autonomous `chonnaictheas`; dependent `ní fhacthas`.
*   **Imperative:** `feic` is uncommon. `Amharc!` or `dearc!` are used instead.
*   **Present Subjunctive:** `go bhfeicidh`.
*   **Verbal Noun:** `feiceáil`, `feiceáilt`.
*   **Verbal Adjective:** `feicthe`, `feiciste`.

#### Connacht

*   The forms largely correspond to the Standard.
*   **Autonomous Present:** Alongside `feictear`, forms like `feicthear`, `feicthears`, and `feictheas` are found.
*   **Idiom:** The form `chítear` appears in the idiom *chítear dhom* (it seems to me).
*   **Preterite:** The form `choinic` or `chainic` is often used instead of `chonaic`. This can sometimes also be used as a dependent form (`níor choinic` instead of `ní fhaca`).
*   **Autonomous Preterite:** Forms include `facthas` and `facthús` (both absolute and dependent), alongside the absolute `chainictheas`. The form `b’fhacthas` (prefixing the copula `ba`) also occurs.

#### Munster

*   **Dingle:** The verb is regularised with the stem `cí-` for all tenses except the preterite (e.g., absolute `cíonn sé`, dependent `ní chíonn sé`).
*   **Other Munster Dialects:**
    *   **Present:** Absolute `chíonn sé` (in Cape Clear also `do-chíonn`). Dependent `ní fhiceann sé` (less commonly `ní fheiceann sé`).
    *   **Future:** Absolute `chífidh`. Dependent `ní fhicfidh`.
    *   **Conditional:** Absolute `chífeadh`. Dependent `ní fhiceadh`.
    *   **Preterite:** The absolute form is based on `(do) chnuc` (from `chonac`), e.g., 1st sg. `(do) chnuc`, 2nd sg. `(do) chnuicís`, 1st pl. `(do) chnuiceamair`. The analytic form is `(do) chnuic`. The dependent form is `ní fheacaidh` (or `ní fheaca` in Waterford).
    *   **Autonomous Preterite:** Absolute `chnuictheas`, `chnucthas`. Dependent `ní fheacthas`.
    *   **Elision:** The `í` of `ní` often drops before `fh-`, e.g., *n’fhiceann sé*, *n’fheacaigh sé*.
    *   **Verbal Noun:** `fiscint`.
    *   **Verbal Adjective:** `ficithe`, `feicithe`. In Dingle and Iveragh, also `fiscithe`.
