### Overview of Irregularity

The verb `faigh` (to get, to find) is one of the irregular verbs in Irish. It uses different stems in certain tenses:
- The preterite (past tense) uses the stem `fuair`.
- The future and conditional use the stem `geobh-`.

It also has special dependent forms in the future and conditional. In the preterite, the stem `fuair` is used for both independent and dependent forms. However, after the negative particle `ní`, eclipsis occurs, and the usual preterite verbal particles (like `d'`) are not used.

### Orthography and Historical Notes

#### The Form `fuair`

The past tense stem `fuair` is never lenited, even after particles that normally cause lenition, like the direct relative particle `a`.
- *An leabhar a fuair mé inné.* (The book I got yesterday.)

The eclipsis seen in `ní bhfuair` is thought to originate from a historical lenited form, `ní fhuair`. The `bh` sound is a glide that arose between `ní` and `fhuair` ([ni:uər′ > ni:wuər′]). The modern spelling `ní bhfuair` reflects this pronunciation, and the lenited form `*fhuair` does not occur in writing.

#### Older Forms

Historically, there were distinct absolute and dependent forms in the present and imperfect tenses.

| Tense | Dependent | Absolute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `faigheann` | `(do-)gheibheann` |
| **Imperfect** | `faigheadh` | `(do-)gheibheadh` |

The lenition seen in the modern future and conditional forms (`gheobhaidh`, `gheobhadh`) comes from the historical prefix `do-`, which was once common with these forms: `(do-)gheobhaidh sé`, `(do-)gheobhadh sé`.

### Dialectal Forms

#### Ulster

- **Present**: The absolute form is usually `gheibh sé` (he gets), with `gheibheann sé` being less common. The dependent form is `ní fhánn sé`. The form `fánn` (from `faghann` or `faghbhann`) is also sometimes used as an absolute form. The autonomous form is `gheibhthear` or `faghthar`.
- **Future**: `gheobhaidh sé` [jo:ə s′e], `gheobhaidh (duine)` [jo:i]. The dependent form is `ní bhfuighidh sé` [N′i: wi: s′e].
- **Imperfect**: `gheibheadh`, with the dependent form `ní bhfuigheadh`.
- **Preterite**: `fuair`. The negative can also be `chan fhuair`. The autonomous form is `fuarthas`.
- **Imperative**: 2nd person singular `fagh` or `faigh`; 3rd person singular `faghadh sé` [fa:ət′ s′e].
- **Present Subjunctive**: `go bhfaghaidh sé` [gə wa: s′e], `go bhfaghaidh (duine)` [gə wa:i].

#### Connacht

- **Present**: Both absolute and dependent forms are most commonly `fánn` (from `faghann`) [fa:N]. Other forms include `gheibheann` [jeN], `geibhfeann` [g′efəN], `gheibhfeann` [jefəN] or [jehəN] (from `gheibhfeann`), and `geobhfann` [g′ofəN]. An always-eclipsed dependent form `bhfaigheann` or `bhfuigheann` [wi:N] or [wəin] also occurs.
- **Preterite**: As a variant for the autonomous `fuarthas` (one got), the form `fríth` is also found.
- **Imperative**: 3rd person `faigheadh` [fɑ:x], `faigheadh sé` [fɑ:t′]. The form `gheobhfeadh` [jo:fəx] also exists.
