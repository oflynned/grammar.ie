The verb `abair` (to say) is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. It is highly irregular, using several different stems across its various tenses and moods.

The main stems are:
*   **Present, Imperfect, Subjunctive:** `deir-`
*   **Preterite:** `dúirt-`
*   **Future, Conditional:** `déar-`
*   **Imperative:** `abair-`

In the standard language, `abair` does not have special dependent forms, but it is notable for not using a preterite particle (like `ar` or `d'`).

#### Present Tense

| Person | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `deirim` |
| 2nd sg. | `deir tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `deir sé / sí` |

The form `deireann` is also possible, particularly as a habitual present.

#### Preterite (Past Tense)

The preterite does not use a verbal particle like `d'` or `ar`.

| Person | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `dúras`, `dúrt` |
| 2nd sg. | `dúraís` |
| 3rd sg. | `dúirt sé / sí` |

| 3rd pl. | `dúradar` |

For dependent forms (e.g. after `ní`, `an`, `go`), the initial `d-` is not lenited, but it is eclipsed where appropriate.

### Usage Notes

#### Lenition of `d-` forms
In the standard language, forms beginning with `d-` (`deir`, `dúirt`, `déar`) are never lenited. This is because they historically began with an `a-` (`adeir`, `adúirt`), which has since been dropped. The direct relative particle `a` also does not cause lenition: `an fear a deir` (the man who says).

The only standard exception is after `nár` in the subjunctive: *nár dheire sé* (that he might not say).

With `cad` (what), the relative particle `a` is often omitted in speech and writing: *Cad (a) deir tú?* (What are you saying?).

#### `ar` and `arsa` for Direct Speech
Instead of `abair`, the defective verb `ar` or `arsa` is often used to introduce a speaker after a line of direct speech.
*   `ar` is used before `seisean`, `sise`, `siadsan`.
*   `arsa` is used before other nouns and pronouns.

Examples:
*   *"Sea, tá ceart go leor agat, a chara," ar sise.* ("Yes, you are quite right, my friend," said she.)
*   *"Is amadán mé, nach ea?" arsa Liam.* ("I am an idiot, aren't I?" said Liam.)

If `abair` is used after direct speech, it takes a relative form: `... a deir sé` or `... a dúirt sé`.

### Older Forms

Historically, `abair` had distinct dependent forms based on the stem `abr-`. These are now mostly found in dialects.

| Tense/Mood | Dependent | Absolute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `abrann` | `deir(eann)` |
| **Imperfect** | `abradh` | `deireadh` |
| **Future** | `abróchaidh` | `déarfaidh` |
| **Conditional** | `abróchadh` | `déarfadh` |
| **Subjunctive** | `go n-abra(idh)` | (no absolute) |

### Dialectal Forms

#### Ulster
*   Forms with `d-` can be lenited or the `d-` can be omitted: `ní dheir sé`, `ní eir sé`.
*   The 1st person singular present is analytic: `deir mé`.
*   The `abr-` stem is used in the present (e.g. `abrann`), both dependently and independently. The autonomous form can be `abartar` or `abraítear`.
*   The preterite `dúirt` is often treated as `d'úirt`. This means the `d-` is seen as the particle `do` and is dropped in dependent forms: `níor úirt`, `gur úirt`.
*   The autonomous preterite is often `húradh` or `húras`.
*   The present subjunctive `go n-abraidh` is more common than `go ndeire`.
*   The verbal noun is `ráit` or `ráidht`.

#### Connacht
*   The `abr-` stem is used, often more frequently in dependent contexts. Forms are conjugated regularly according to the 2nd conjugation (e.g. `abraíonn`).
*   There can be a semantic distinction: `abair` for "to recite" or "sing a song", and `deir` for "to communicate".
*   `deir` is used for both simple and habitual present, while `deireann` is more specifically habitual. `deireann` can be lenited, with the `d-` being dropped (`ní eireann`), but `deir` is not lenited (`ní deir`).
*   The 1st person singular is usually `deir mé`, and the 1st plural is `deireann muid`.
*   The preterite `dúirt` is also treated as `d'úirt`, leading to dependent forms like `níor úirt`, `gur úirt`. The autonomous form is often `húradh`.
*   "Echo" forms exist in the preterite: `dúirteas` (I said), `dúirtis` (you said), `dúirteadar` (they said).

#### Munster
*   `deireann` is used in the 2nd person present in the southwest.
*   The `abr-` stem is rare and only used dependently.
*   The preterite form is often `duairt`. Synthetic forms include `dúras`/`duart` (I said), `dúrais`/`duarais` (you said), etc.
*   The autonomous preterite can be `dúradh`/`duaradh` or `dúrathas`/`duarathas`.
*   In the Ring (An Rinn) dialect, forms with a long `í` are found: `dúraíos`, `dúiríos` (I said).
*   The verbal noun is often pronounced `reá`, with a slender `r`, especially when lenited (e.g. `a reá`).
