The verb "to hear" has two main stems in Irish: `clois` and `cluin`. Their usage varies by dialect. In Ulster, `cluin` is used. In Munster, `clois` is used. In Connacht and the Official Standard, both are found.

This verb is considered irregular because its past tense (preterite) uses a completely different stem: `chuala`. All other tenses are formed regularly from either the `clois` or `cluin` stem, following the patterns of first conjugation verbs.

Unlike some other irregular verbs, `clois`/`cluin` does not have special dependent forms, aside from the usual initial mutations. Notably, the autonomous form of the past tense, `chualathas`, is lenited, which is unusual.

## Dialectal Forms

### Ulster

*   The stem `cluin` is used almost exclusively.
*   The present tense often appears without an ending: *cluin sé* (he hears).
*   This form is often lenited, *chluin sé*, due to the influence of the older form *do-chluin sé*.
*   In South Donegal, `cluineann sé` and `cluinidh sé` also occur, but the dependent form is always without an ending: *An gcluin tú?* (Do you hear?).
*   The future and conditional sometimes switch to the second conjugation: `cluineochaidh sé` (he will hear).
*   In the past tense, preterite particles are often omitted: *Ní chuala sé* (He did not hear) alongside `níor chuala`, and *An gcuala sé?* (Did he hear?) alongside `ar chuala`.
*   The autonomous past tense form can be `cluineadh` (one heard) in addition to `chualas` (a reduced form of `chualathas`).
*   The verbal noun is `cluinstin` or `cluinstean`.
*   The verb `mothaigh` (literally "to feel") is often used instead of `cluin`.
*   For the imperative 2nd person singular, *éist!* (listen!) is commonly used.

### Connacht

*   Both `clois` and `cluin` are used, with `clois` being more common in the south of the region.
*   The present tense is `cloiseann sé` or `cluineann sé`. The dependent form often drops the ending: *An gcluin tú?* or *An gclois tú?* (Do you hear?).
*   The past tense is usually the irregular `chuala`, but a regular form `chlois sé` (he heard) is sometimes found.
*   The autonomous past tense is `cluineadh` or `cloiseadh` (one heard), not `chualathas`.
*   The verbal noun for `clois` is `cloisteáil`. In the north, `cluns` or `clins` are also found, which are hybrid forms from the old verbal noun `clos` and the stem `cluin`.

### Munster

*   Only the stem `clois` is used.
*   In the past tense 1st person singular, the form is often `chuala` (I heard), as in Muskerry, Iveragh, and Dingle. In Carbery and Cape Clear, it is `chualag`.
*   The standard form `chualas` was found in Clare and is rare in Dingle. The form `chualaíos` also comes from Clare and is sometimes heard in Muskerry.
*   A regular past tense form `chlois sé` (he heard) also occurs in Muskerry (with 1st/2nd person forms `chloiseas`, `chloisis`).
*   The analytic past tense form is `chualaigh`, pronounced [-ig] before a noun.
*   The autonomous past tense is `chualathas`, but in Dingle `chualathars`, `chualadh`, and `cualadh` are also found.
*   The verbal noun is `cloisint` or `clos`.
*   The verb `airigh` (literally "to feel") is often used instead of `clois`, especially in Cork and always in Waterford.
