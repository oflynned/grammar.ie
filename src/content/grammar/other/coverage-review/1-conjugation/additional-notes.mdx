## Verb Types in the First Conjugation (`An Chéad Réimniú`)

The first conjugation includes several types of regular verbs.

### Monosyllabic verb stems

This group includes all verbs with a one-syllable stem ending in either a slender or a broad consonant. They form the majority of verbs in this conjugation.

- Examples: `glan` (to clean), `tuig` (to understand).

### Monosyllabic verbs in `-igh`

This group includes one-syllable verbs that end in `-igh`.

- Examples: `nigh` (to wash), `léigh` (to read), `dóigh` (to burn).

### Polysyllabic verbs in `-áil`

This group includes multi-syllable verbs ending in `-áil`. Before an ending is added, the final consonant of the stem is depalatalised (made broad). This also happens before endings containing `-f-` but not before endings containing `-t-`.

- Examples:
  - `sabháil` (to save): `sabhálann sé` (he saves), `sabháiltear` (one saves), `sabhálfar` (one will save).
  - `athchúrsáil` (to recycle).

### Polysyllabic verbs with a final long vowel

This group includes some multi-syllable verbs with a long vowel in the final syllable, typically with a slender final consonant in the root (e.g. `-áin`, `-óil`, `-úir`).

Before endings are added, the final consonant is usually depalatalised, including before `-f-` and `-t-` endings.
- `taispeáin` (to show) becomes `taispeán-`: *taispeánann sé* (he shows), *taispeántar* (one shows).
- `tionóil` (to gather) becomes `tionól-`: *tionólann sé* (he gathers), *tionóltar* (one gathers).

Some verbs in this group do not undergo depalatalisation.
- `tiomáin` (to drive): *tiomáineann sé* (he drives), *tiomáintear* (one drives).

Some verbs already have a broad verb root and do not change.
- `gearán` (to complain): *gearánaim* (I complain), *gearántar* (one complains).

### Other polysyllabic verbs

This group includes a few other multi-syllable verbs with a short vowel in the last syllable, but a final consonant other than `l`, `n`, or `r`.

Depalatalisation of the final consonant often occurs before endings, including before `-f-` and `-t-`.
- `adhlaic` (to bury) becomes `adhlac-`: *adhlacann sé* (he buries), *adhlactar* (one is buried).
- `aisig` (to vomit) becomes `aiseag-`: *aiseagann sé* (he vomits).

Some verbs remain without depalatalisation.
- `comhair` (to calculate): *comhaireann sé* (he calculates), *comhairtear* (one calculates).

Some verbs already have a broad verb root.
- `leigheas` (to heal): *leigheasaim* (I heal), *leigheastar* (one is healed).
- `dearmad` (to forget): *dearmadaim* (I forget).

### Polysyllabic verbs with syncope

Some polysyllabic verbs undergo syncope (the loss of a vowel in an unstressed syllable). Unlike syncopating verbs in the second conjugation, these belong to the first.

- Verbs like `deighil` (to separate), `feighil` (to guard), `saighid` (to incite), and `taighid` (to poke) always show syncope, except in endingless forms. This also occurs before endings with `-t-` and `-f-`, which are slender.
  - Example: *deighleann tú* (you separate), *deighlfidh tú* (you will separate), *dheighil tú* (you separated), *deighiltear* (one separates).
- Other verbs show syncope with depalatalisation of the stem ending. Sometimes there is no syncope before endings with `-t-` and `-f-`, but the stem is depalatalised. The `-t-` and `-f-` endings are broad. This group includes verbs like `achair` (to implore), `adhair` (to adore), `ionnail` (to wash), and `sleabhaic` (to slacken).
  - Example: *adhrann tú* (you adore), *adhrfaidh tú* (you will adore), *d’adhair tú* (you adored), *adhrtar* (one adores).
  - Example: *achrann tú* (you implore), *acharfaidh tú* (you will implore), *d’achair tú* (you implored), *achartar* (one implores).
- The verb `siúil` (to walk), formerly spelled `siubhail`, was disyllabic in writing and underwent syncope (*siubhlann sé*). Before `t` and `f`, there was no syncope (*siubaltar*), so depalatalisation continues in the modern spelling.
  - Example: *siúlann sé* (he walks), *siúlfaidh sé* (he will walk), *shiúil sé* (he walked), *siúltar* (one walks).
- Similarly, the verb `fóin` (to serve), formerly `fóghain`, underwent syncope before `-f-` and `-t-` (*fóghnann sé*, *fóghnfaidh sé*, *fóghntar*).
  - Example: *fónann sé* (he serves), *fónfaidh sé* (he will serve), *d’fhóin sé* (he served), *fóntar* (one serves).

> **Note on pronunciation:** Many of these verbs are disyllabic only in writing. In speech, the middle consonants became silent or merged with vowels into diphthongs, making the verbs monosyllabic. The syncope, therefore, often only affects the spelling. For example: `adhair` [air′], *adhraim* (I adore) [airəm′]; `deighil` [d′ail′], *deighlim* (I divide) [d′ail′əm′].

### The verb `fuirsigh`

The polysyllabic verb `fuirsigh` (to harrow, hurry, scuffle) only takes the ending `-igh` in the past tense and the 2nd person singular imperative.

- Example forms: *fuirseann sé*, *fuirsfidh sé*, *d’fhuirsigh sé*, *fuirstear*.

### Compound verbs

Polysyllabic verbs formed by adding prefixes to the verb types above behave like their uncompounded base verbs.

- `forghabh` (to seize) from `for` + `gabh` -> *forghabhann sé* (he seizes).
- `ruadhóigh` (to singe) from `rua` + `dóigh` -> *ruadhónn sé* (he singes).

However, compounds with the irregular verb `déan` become regular first conjugation verbs.

- `athdhéan` (to redo), `comhdhéan` (to form) -> *chomhdhéan sé* (he formed).
