The second conjugation, or *An Dara Réimniú*, includes regular verbs that are polysyllabic (have more than one syllable). They are categorised by their endings and behaviour.

## Verb Types in the Second Conjugation

### Verbs ending in -igh

This is the largest group and includes all polysyllabic verbs ending in `-igh`.

- Examples: `coinnigh` (to hold), `salaigh` (to soil), `fiafraigh` (to ask).
- **Munster dialect:** Some verbs may end in `-imh` instead of `-igh`, such as `coinnimh` for the standard `coinnigh`.
- **Ulster dialect:** Some verbs may end in `-óigh`, such as `éalóigh` (to flee) and `fostóigh` (to rent), which are `éalaigh` and `fostaigh` in the standard language.

### Syncopated verbs

These are polysyllabic verbs ending in `-il`, `-in`, `-ir`, and `-is` (when following `l`, `n`, or `r`). When conjugated, the second syllable of the stem is dropped (syncopated) before adding an ending.

- `imir` (to play) becomes `imr-`
- `oscail` (to open) becomes `oscl-`
- `seachain` (to avoid) becomes `seachn-`
- `inis` (to tell) becomes `ins-`

Other examples include `codail` (to sleep), `coigil` (to save), `aithin` (to recognise), `cogain` (to chew), `bagair` (to threaten), and `freagair` (to answer).

Note that in the imperative 2nd person singular and the past tense 3rd person singular, the original ending is retained (e.g., `imir`, `d'imir sé`).

Some verbs in this category do not syncopate but instead undergo depalatalisation:
- `freastail` (to serve) -> *freastalaíonn sé*
- `taistil` (to travel) -> *taistealaíonn sé*
- `aithris` (to recite) -> *aithrisíonn sé*

The verb `éirigh` (to rise) is now treated as a standard `-igh` verb (*éiríonn sé*), but historically it was syncopated (`éirgh-`, as in the older form *éirgheann sé*), linking the two groups.

### Other polysyllabic verbs

A smaller group of polysyllabic verbs, often ending in `-im` or `-ing`, also belong to the second conjugation. In the standard language, these verbs do not syncopate.

- Examples: `foghlaim` (to learn), `tarraing` (to pull), `fulaing` (to endure).

### Verbs with an epenthetic vowel

The verb `lorg` (to search) is treated as disyllabic (`[lorəg]`) due to an epenthetic vowel and thus belongs to the second conjugation.

However, other verbs with an epenthetic vowel, such as `morg` (to decompose), `dearg` (to redden), `searg` (to wither), and `tairg` (to offer), belong to the first conjugation in the standard language. Dialectal usage for these verbs can vary.

## Verbs Excluded from the Second Conjugation

- **Verbs ending in -áil:** These belong to the first conjugation.
- **Monosyllabic verbs:** All single-syllable verbs belong to the first conjugation, even those that end in `-igh` (e.g., `nigh`, `guigh`).

## Conjugation Patterns

The following tables show the standard forms for the main types of second-conjugation verbs.

### Verbs with a slender ending (e.g., `coinnigh` - to hold)

**Indicative Mood**

| Person | Present | Preterite | Imperfect | Future |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st Sg. | `coinním` | `choinníos` | `choinnínn` | `coinneod` |
| 2nd Sg. | `coinníonn` tú | `choinnís` | `choinníteá` | `coinneoidh` tú |
| 3rd Sg. | `coinníonn` sé | `choinnigh` sé | `choinníodh` sé | `coinneoidh` sé |
| 1st Pl. | `coinnímid` | `choinníomar` | `choinnímís` | `coinneoimid` |
| 2nd Pl. | `coinníonn` sibh | `choinníobhair` | `choinníodh` sibh | `coinneoidh` sibh |
| 3rd Pl. | `coinníonn` siad | `choinníodar` | `choinnídís` | `coinneoidh` siad |
| Autonomous | `coinnítear` | `coinníodh` | `choinnítí` | `coinneofar` |
| Relative | a `choinníos` | — | — | a `choinneos` |
