## Overview

Verbs that end in `-áil` belong to the first conjugation, even though they are polysyllabic. This group is notable because the `-áil` suffix is highly productive and is often used to form verbs from loanwords, particularly from English.

Common examples include:
*   `glasáil` (to lock/unlock)
*   `iarnáil` (to iron)
*   `candáil` (to auction)
*   `peinteáil` (to paint)
*   `sábháil` (to save)
*   `páirceáil` (to park)

Colloquially, this pattern can be applied to almost any English verb to create an Irish equivalent on the fly, such as `hitchhikeáil` (to hitchhike) or `bicycleáil` (to cycle).

## Stem Changes Before Endings

The key grammatical feature of `-áil` verbs is how the stem changes when endings are added. The final `-il` is slender, but it often becomes broad (`-ál-`) in conjugation.

### Depalatalisation to `-ál-`

Before most endings, the slender `-áil` is depalatalised to the broad `-ál-`. This applies to the present, imperfect, and past tenses.

It also applies before the `-f-` of the future and conditional tenses.

*   `sábháil` → `sábhálann sé` (he saves)
*   `sábháil` → `shábhálainn` (I used to save)
*   `sábháil` → `sábhálfad` (I will save)
*   `sábháil` → `shábhálfainn` (I would save)

### Slender `-áil-` is Retained

The slender `-áil-` stem is kept only when the ending begins with a `t`. This is a crucial exception to the general pattern of depalatalisation.

*   `sábháil` → `sábháiltear` (one saves / is saved)
*   `sábháil` → `shábháilteá` (you used to save)

## Comparison with Other Polysyllabic Verbs

This behaviour contrasts with other polysyllabic verbs of the first conjugation, where depalatalisation is more consistent. For example, with verbs like `taispeáin` (to show) or `tionóil` (to gather), the stem becomes broad before all endings, including those with `t`.

*   `taispeáin` → `taispeánann sé` (he shows) AND `taispeántar` (one shows)
*   `tionóil` → `tionólann sé` (he gathers) AND `tionóltar` (one gathers)

A few other polysyllabic verbs, such as `tiomáin` (to drive), may not undergo depalatalisation at all, though broad forms are sometimes seen.

*   `tiomáin` → `tiomáineann sé` (he drives), `tiomáintear` (one drives)

## Conjugation Example: `sábháil` (to save)

The table below shows the full standard conjugation for `sábháil`. Many tenses use analytic forms, which combine the verb stem with a pronoun (e.g., `sábhálann tú`). Synthetic forms, where the ending itself indicates the person (e.g., `sábhálaim`), are used for specific persons and tenses as shown.

| 1st Sg. | `sábhálaim` | `shábhálas` | `shábhálainn` | `sábhálfad` | `shábhálfainn` | `sábhálaim` | `go sábhála mé` | `dá sábhálainn` |
| 2nd Sg. | `sábhálann tú` | `shábhálais` | `shábháilteá` | `sábhálfaidh tú` | `shábhálfá` | `sábháil` | `go sábhála tú` | `dá sábháilteá` |
| 3rd Sg. | `sábhálann sé/sí` | `shábháil sé/sí` | `shábháladh sé/sí` | `sábhálfaidh sé/sí` | `shábhálfadh sé/sí` | `sábháladh sé/sí` | `go sábhála sé/sí` | `dá sábháladh sé/sí` |
| 1st Pl. | `sábhálaimid` | `shábhálamar` | `shábhálaimis` | `sábhálfaimid` | `shábhálfaimis` | `sábhálaimis` | `go sábhálaimid` | `dá sábhálaimis` |
