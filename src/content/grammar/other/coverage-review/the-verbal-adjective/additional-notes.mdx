## The Verbal Adjective (`an aidiacht bhriathartha`)

The verbal adjective is a verb form that functions primarily as a normal adjective. It corresponds in meaning and use to the English past participle (e.g., broken, washed, seen). Colloquially, it is often called the `rangabháil chaite` (past participle).

As an adjective, it belongs to the third declension (adjectives ending in a vowel), meaning it does not change its form in the genitive case or in the plural. The comparative and superlative forms are identical to the base form but are rarely used.

### Formation

#### First Conjugation Verbs

| Final Sound of Stem | Rule | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `b`, `c`, `f`, `g`, `m`, `p`, `r` | Stem + `-tha` / `-the` | `fág` → `fágtha`, `gearr` → `gearrtha` |
| `ch`, `l`, `n`, `s` | Stem + `-ta` / `-te` | `glan` → `glanta`, `bris` → `briste` |
| `d` | Stem + `-ta` / `-te` | `goid` → `goidte` |
| `t`, `th` | Stem (without `t`, `th`) + `-ta` / `-te` | `sciot` → `sciota`, `ith` → `ite` |
| `bh`, `mh` | Stem (without `bh`, `mh`) + `-fa` / `-fe` | `scríobh` → `scríofa`, `snámh` → `snáfa` |
| `-igh` | Stem (without `gh`) + `-te` | `dóigh` → `dóite`, `léigh` → `léite` |
| `-áil` | Stem + `-te` | `sábháil` → `sábháilte` |

**Spelling and Pronunciation Notes:**

*   When `-tha` is added to a stem ending in `b` or `g`, the consonant becomes voiceless in pronunciation: `-btha` is pronounced /pə/, and `-gtha` is pronounced /kə/.
*   The combination `-dta` (from an older `-dtha`) is pronounced /tə/.
*   When a stem ends in `t` or `th`, the ending is always written as `-ta`, as the letter sequences `-tt-` and `-tht-` are not permitted in Irish spelling.
*   The ending `-fa` is a modern simplification of the older spellings `-bhtha` or `-mhtha` (e.g., `scríobhtha` became `scríofa`).

#### Second Conjugation Verbs

| Final Sound of Stem | Rule | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `-igh` | Stem (without `-gh`) + `-the` | `salaigh` → `salaithe`, `coinnigh` → `coinnithe` |
| `-il` | Stem + `-te` | `oscail` → `oscailte` |
| `-in` | Stem (broadened) + `-ta` | `cosain` → `cosanta` |
| `-ir` | Stem (broadened) + `-tha` | `imir` → `imeartha` |
| `-im` | Stem (broadened) + `-tha` | `foghlaim` → `foghlamtha` |
| `-ing` | Stem + `-the` | `tuirling` → `tuirlingthe` |

**Notes:**

*   For verbs ending in `-in`, `-ir`, and `-im`, the final slender consonant of the stem is made broad before the ending is added (e.g., `imir` → `imear-` + `-tha` = `imeartha`).
*   **Exceptions:** The verbal adjective of `inis` is `inste`, and for `freastail` it is `freastalta`.

#### Irregular Verbs

| Verb | Verbal Adjective | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `abair` | `ráite` | said |
| `beir` | `beirthe` | caught, born |
| `bí` | (`beite`) | (been) |
| `clois` / `cluin` | `cloiste` / `cluinte` | heard |
| `déan` | `déanta` | done, made |
| `faigh` | `faighte` | got, found |
| `feic` | `feicthe` | seen |
| `ith` | `ite` | eaten |
| `tabhair` | `tugtha` | given |
| `tar` | `tagtha` | come |
| `téigh` | `dulta` | gone |

**Notes:**

*   Most of these are formed regularly from a particular stem of the verb.
*   `ráite` and `dulta` are formed from the verbal noun stems (`rá` and `dul`).
*   `tagtha` and `tugtha` are formed from the present tense stems (`tag-` and `tug-`).
*   The verb `bí` (to be) does not have a true verbal adjective. The form `beite` exists only for use as a Participle of Necessity (see below).

#### Dialectal Forms and Pronunciation

*   **Connacht (Cois Fharraige/Conamara):**
    *   The endings `-tha`/`-the` after plosives (like `p`, `t`, `c`, `b`, `d`, `g`) are often pronounced as a long /iː/.
    *   In Conamara, verbs ending in `-m` often take `-ta` instead of the standard `-tha` (e.g., `cumta` instead of `cumtha` for "formed").
*   **Munster:** The `-tha`/`-the` ending is often pronounced /hə/, even after `l` where the standard spelling is `-ta`/`-te`.
*   **Ulster (Gaoth Dobhair):**
    *   The ending `-tha` after a broad consonant is pronounced /iː/, but `-the` after a slender consonant is pronounced /ə/.
    *   For second conjugation verbs ending in `-igh`, the alternative ending `-iste` is common (e.g., `coinniste` for `coinnithe`).
    *   For verbs ending in `-bh`/`-mh`, the verbal adjective ending is often pronounced /uhə/ instead of the standard /fə/ (e.g., `scríobhtha` pronounced `scríofa` in the standard, but /s′k′r′iːuhə/ in Ulster).

### Usage of the Verbal Adjective

#### As a Standard Adjective

The verbal adjective can be used like any other adjective, either before a noun (attributive) or after a verb like `bí` (predicative).

*   **Attributive:** *an cruiscín briste* - the broken jug
*   **Predicative (Passive):** *Tá an bord briste.* - The table is broken.
*   **Predicative (Active state):** *Tá mé ólta.* - I am drunk.

#### Forming a Passive Construction

When used predicatively with `bí`, the verbal adjective of a transitive verb creates a passive sense. The agent (the one who performed the action) can be introduced with the preposition `ag`.

*   *Tá an bord briste agam.* - The table was broken by me. (Literally: "Is the table broken at-me.")
*   This also works with verbs that are intransitive but take a prepositional object, like `beir ar` (to catch). The prepositional object remains, and there is no grammatical subject.
    *   *Tá beirthe agam ort.* - You were caught by me. (Literally: "Is caught at-me on-you.")

#### Forming a Perfect Tense

The same structure (`bí` + verbal adjective + `ag` + agent) is widely used to form a perfect tense, equivalent to "I have done", "she has gone", etc.

*   **With transitive verbs:**
    *   *Tá an bord briste agam.* - I have broken the table.
*   **With intransitive verbs + prepositional object:**
    *   *Tá beirthe agam ort.* - I have caught you. (from `beir ar`)
    *   *Tá curtha isteach acu ar phost.* - They have applied for a post. (from `cuir isteach ar`)
    *   *Tá scríofa agam chugat.* - I have written to you. (from `scríobh chuig`)
*   **With intransitive verbs (no agent with `ag`):**
    *   *Tá mé imithe go hÉirinn.* - I have gone to Ireland.

In conditional clauses, this perfect construction can express the certainty of an event occurring.
*   *Má fheictear thú tá tú gafa.* - If you are seen, then you are caught.

#### Other Uses

*   **With other verbs:** It can be used predicatively with verbs other than `bí`.
    *   *Chonaic mé scríofa mar sin é.* - I saw it written like that.
*   **With `faigh`:** This structure is often an influence from English.
    *   To mean "get something done": *conas rudaí a fháil déanta* - how to get things done.
    *   To form a passive: *Fuair sé gortaithe.* - He got hurt.
*   **With `deireadh` (end):** This construction describes the completion of an action.
    *   *An bhfuil deireadh ite agat?* - Have you finished eating?
    *   *Bhí deireadh ráite aige.* - He had finished speaking.
*   **Anglicisms:** The ending `-áilte` is sometimes added to English adjectives to create Irish ones.
    *   `siúráilte` (from "sure")
    *   `cúláilte` (from "cool")

## The Participles (`na rangabhálacha`)

Irish has several types of "modal participles" which express the possibility or necessity of an action. They function as adjectives and are often equivalent to English adjectives ending in `-able` or `-ible`.

They are typically formed by adding one of the prefixes `so-`, `do-`, or `in-` to a base form. This base is usually identical to the verbal adjective, but can also be the genitive form of the verbal noun. Sometimes both forms are possible (e.g., `doshábhála` from the genitive `sábhála`, and `doshábháilte` from the verbal adjective `sábháilte`).

### Participle of Suitability (`rangabháil na hoiriúnachta`)

This expresses that an action is possible (e.g., `-able`, `-ible`). It is formed with the prefix `in-`.

*   **From verbs:** `inbhriste` (breakable), `inite` (edible).
*   **From nouns (genitive case):** `intráchta` (passable, from `trácht`), `inphionóis` (punishable, from `pionós`).
*   **Usage with the copula:**
    *   *Is intuigthe as sin go...* - From that one can understand that...
    *   *scéal is inchreidte dóibh* - a story they can believe.

### Participle of Ease (`rangabháil na fusachta`)

This expresses that an action is *easily* possible. It is formed with the prefix `so-`.

*   **From verbs:** `sobhriste` (easily breakable).
*   **From nouns (genitive case):** `so-iompair` (portable, from `iompar`), `sotheagaisc` (teachable, from `teagasc`).

### Participle of Difficulty (`rangabháil na deacrachta`)

This expresses that an action is difficult or impossible. It is formed with the prefix `do-`.

*   **From verbs:** `dobhriste` (unbreakable).
*   **From nouns (genitive case):** `dofhaisnéise` (indescribable, from `faisnéis`), `doleithscéil` (inexcusable, from `leithscéal`).

### Participle of Comparison (`rangabháil na comparáide`)

This is an extension of the Participle of Suitability, used to make comparisons of equality. It uses the structure `in-` + noun (genitive) + `le`.

*   *Níl sí incheoil le Pól.* - She is not as musical as Paul.
*   *Níl tú introda le Cathal.* - You are not as quarrelsome as Cathal.

### Participle of Necessity (`rangabháil an riachtanais`)

This is a rare form expressing the necessity or appropriateness of an action. Its form is identical to the verbal adjective (without a prefix). It is almost always used with the copula (usually negated) and the logical subject is introduced with `do`.

*   *Ní gearánta dom ar an aimsir.* - I have no reason to complain about the weather.
*   *Ní beite duit ann.* - You should not be there. (`beite` is the special form for `bí`).
*   *Ní tógtha orm é.* - I am not to be blamed for it.
*   *Ní dulta duit ansin.* - You should not go there.

## Related Adjectival Formations

### Adjectives with `dea-` and `dí-`

Similar to the participles, the prefixes `dea-` (good) and `dí-` (un-) can be combined with the genitive of a noun to form an adjective.

*   `dea-éadaigh` - well-dressed (from `éadach`, clothing)
*   `dí-airm` - unarmed (from `arm`, weapon)

### Adjectives with the Suffix `-ach`

The adjectival suffix `-ach` can be added to the genitive of a verbal noun. The resulting adjective has an active meaning, expressing an ability or tendency.

*   `labharthach` - talkative (from `labhairt`, speaking)
*   `mínitheach` - explanatory (from `míniú`, explaining)
*   `tuisceannach` - understanding (from `tuiscint`, understanding)

## Historical and Etymological Notes

1.  **Present Participle:** Irish has not had a present participle since its earliest stages. Remnants of the old Proto-Celtic participle exist in a few nouns, such as `cara` (friend), which comes from Proto-Celtic `*karantos` (loving).
2.  **Prefixes `so-`/`do-`:** These prefixes were once used productively to form pairs of adjectives with opposite meanings, such as `saor` (free, cheap) and `daor` (unfree, expensive), or `sona` (happy) and `dona` (bad). This is no longer a productive way to form new words.
3.  **Use of the Genitive:** The use of the genitive case of nouns in forming participles is a modern interpretation. Etymologically, it reflects a historical change in the noun's declension class, which resulted in a form that is now identical to the genitive singular in many cases.
