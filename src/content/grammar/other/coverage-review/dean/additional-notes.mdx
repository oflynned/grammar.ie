The verb `déan` means "to do" or "to make". It is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. Its irregularity is most prominent in the past tense, which uses the stem `rinne` in independent forms and `dearna` in dependent forms.

## Indicative Mood

### Present Tense

| Person | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `déanaim` |
| 2nd sg. | `déanann tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `déanann sé`, `sí` |
| 1st pl. | `déanaimid` |
| 2nd pl. | `déanann sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `déanann siad` |
| Autonomous | `déantar` |
| Relative | `a dhéanas` |

### Preterite (Past Tense)

The independent preterite stem is `rinne-`. The forms below are synthetic, common in Munster Irish. In the standard language, analytic forms like `rinne mé` and `rinne siad` are more common.

| Person | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `rinneas` |
| 2nd sg. | `rinne tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `rinne sé`, `sí` |
| 1st pl. | `rinneamar` |
| 2nd pl. | `rinneabhair` |
| 3rd pl. | `rinneadar` |
| Autonomous | `rinneadh` |

A common variant stem, especially in Munster, is `dhein-` (e.g., `dheineas`, `dhein sé`).

## Dependent Preterite Forms

The dependent stem `dearna-` is used in the preterite after particles such as `ní` (not), `an` (question), `go` (that), and `nach` (that...not). Note that the preterite particle `do`/`d'` is not used with these forms.

| Person | Negative (`ní`) | Interrogative (`an`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `ní dhearnas` | `an ndearnas?` |
| 2nd sg. | `ní dhearna tú` | `an ndearna tú?` |
| 3rd sg. | `ní dhearna sé` | `an ndearna sé?` |
| 1st pl. | `ní dhearnamar` | `an ndearnamar?` |
| 2nd pl. | `ní dhearnabhair` | `an ndearnabhair?` |
| 3rd pl. | `ní dhearnadar` | `an ndearnadar?` |
| Autonomous | `ní dhearnadh` | `an ndearnadh?` |

## Dialectal Variations

*   **Munster:** In many tenses, the regular verb `déin` or `dein` is used instead of `déan`. However, the `déan-` stem is still used in the future and conditional. The form `dhein` is also a common variant of `rinne` in the preterite.
*   **Ulster:** In the present tense, the absolute forms `ním` and `níonn sé` (from older `do-ghníonn`) are used. The forms `déanaim` and `déanann` are used only as dependent forms.

## Older Forms

Historically, a distinction was made between absolute (independent) and dependent forms in more tenses. The forms based on `déan-` were originally only dependent.

*   **Present:** The absolute form was `(do-)ghním`, `(do-)ghníonn sé`.
*   **Imperfect:** The absolute form was `(do-)ghnínn`, `(do-)ghníodh sé`.

| Tense | Dependent Form | Older Absolute Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `déanann` | `(do-)ghníonn` |
| Imperfect | `déanadh` | `(do-)ghníodh` |
| Future | `déanfaidh` | `(do-)ghéanaidh` |
| Conditional | `déanfadh` | `(do-)ghéanadh` |
