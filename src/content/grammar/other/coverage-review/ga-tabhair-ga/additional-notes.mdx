The irregular verb `tabhair` means "to give". It uses different stems in its conjugation:

-   **`tug-`**: Used in the Present, Past, and Imperfect tenses.
-   **`tabhar-`**: Used in the Future, Conditional, and the 2nd person singular Imperative.

In the modern standard, `tabhair` does not have separate dependent forms.

## Historical and Dialectal Forms

### Absolute and Dependent Forms

Historically, and still in some dialects, a distinction was made between absolute (independent) and dependent forms of the verb.

| Tense/Mood | Dependent Form | Absolute Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `tabhrann` | `(do-)bheir(eann)` |
| **Imperfect** | `tabhradh` | `(do-)bheireadh` |
| **Future** | `tiobhraidh`, `tabharfaidh` | `(do-)bhéarfaidh` |
| **Conditional** | `tiobhradh`, `tabharfadh` | `(do-)bhéarfadh` |

Some of these forms are still found in Munster and Ulster Irish. In the absolute forms, the prefix `do-` was usually omitted. This meant the forms differed from the verb `beir` (to catch, to bear) only by consistent lenition (e.g., `bhéarfaidh sé` = he will give). In contexts where a mutation would occur anyway, the two verbs could become identical.

In Ulster, preterite verbal particles are often not used, for example, `go dtug` is used instead of the standard `gur thug`.

### Etymology

The verb `tabhair` originated from the verb `beir` prefixed with the preposition `do` (to). This is evident in the older absolute forms like `do-bheir`. The change from `do-` to `ta-` in the dependent form `tabhair` is due to a shift in stress; `tabhair` is a prototonic form (stress on the first syllable), whereas `do-bheir` is deuterotonic (stress on the second syllable, `bheir`).

## Pronunciation

Forms containing `tabhar-` or `tabhair-` are often pronounced with a shortened vowel, such as [toːr]/[toːr′] or [tor]/[tor′].

-   *Tabhair dhom é!* (Give it to me!) can be pronounced [tor′əm e:].
