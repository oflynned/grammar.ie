## What are Echo Forms?

In Irish, **Echo Forms** (`Na Foirmeacha Macallacha`), also known as **Answer Forms** (`foirmeacha an fheagra`), are synthetic verb forms (where the pronoun is included in the ending) that are used in specific contexts, particularly in the dialects of Connacht and Ulster.

In these dialects, analytical verb forms (e.g., `ghlan mé`, "I cleaned") are generally preferred in standard declarative sentences. The synthetic forms (e.g., `ghlanas`, "I cleaned") are reserved for situations where the verb "echoes" a preceding statement or question. This is known as **answer ellipsis**.

## When to Use Echo Forms

Echo forms are typically used in the following situations:

-   **Short answers** to yes/no questions.
    -   *Ar ghlan tú an carr, a Sheáin?* - *Ghlanas.* (Did you wash the car, Seán? - Yes / I did.)
-   **Question tags** at the end of a sentence.
    -   *Ghlan tú an carr, a Sheáin, nár ghlanais?* (You washed the car, Seán, didn't you?)
-   **Affirmative or negative short remarks**.
    -   *Mhuise, rinneas!* (Oh yes, I did!)
    -   *M’anam go gcualas!* (I swear I heard it!)

### When Not to Use Echo Forms

Synthetic echo forms are generally avoided when a longer answer containing an object is given. In this case, the analytical form is used.

-   *Nár ghlan tú an carr?* - *Ach ghlan mé é!* (Didn't you wash the car? - But I did wash it!)

However, it is possible for an echo form to appear alongside a longer answer, either before or after it, to serve as the "yes" or "no".

-   *Is dóichí gur chuala túsa gur …* - *Chualas. Chuala mé caint air.* (You probably heard that... - Yes. I heard talk about it.)
-   *Á ní fhaca mé é, ní fhacas.* (Ah, I didn't see it, no.)

## Dialectal Variation

### Munster Irish

In Munster, synthetic verb endings are common in normal declarative sentences, not just in answers. Therefore, the term "echo form" is not typically used. However, the same principle applies: where a synthetic form is an option (e.g., 2nd person singular present), it is the preferred choice in answer ellipsis.

### Connacht and Ulster Forms

The 2nd person singular endings show notable variation:

-   **Present Tense**: The traditional ending is `-(a)ir`, while `-(a)inns` is common in Connacht.
-   **Future Tense**: The traditional ending is `-f(a)ir`. In Connacht, both `-f(a)ir` and `-f(a)is` are used.
-   **Past Tense (Preterite)**: While the standard ending is `-(a)is`, the form `-(a)ir` also appears in Connacht.

## Verb Form Paradigms

The tables below show the synthetic endings for the 1st and 2nd person singular. Forms in **bold** are part of the Official Standard. Other forms are common dialectal variants.

### First Conjugation (Broad Stem)

Example: `glan` (to clean)

| Person | Present | Preterite | Future | Conjunctive Present |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | **`-aim`** (`glanaim`) | **`-as`** (`ghlanas`) | **`-fad`** (`glanfad`) | **`-ad`** (`go nglanad`) |
| 2nd sg (you) | **`-air`** / `-ainns` (`glanair` / `glanainns`) | **`-ais`** / `-air` (`ghlanais` / `ghlanair`) | **`-fair`** / `-fais` (`glanfair` / `glanfais`) | **`-air`** / **`-ais`** (`go nglanair` / `nglanais`) |

### First Conjugation (Slender Stem)

Example: `tuig` (to understand)

| Person | Present | Preterite | Future | Conjunctive Present |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | **`-im`** (`tuigim`) | **`-eas`** (`thuigeas`) | **`-fead`** (`tuigfead`) | **`-ead`** (`go dtuigead`) |
| 2nd sg (you) | **`-ir`** / `-inns` (`tuigir` / `tuiginns`) | **`-is`** / `-ir` (`thuigis` / `thuigir`) | **`-fir`** / `-fis` (`tuigfir` / `tuigfis`) | **`-ir`** / **`-is`** (`go dtuigir` / `dtuigis`) |

### Second Conjugation (Broad Stem, `-aigh`)

Example: `salaigh` (to soil)

| Person | Present | Preterite | Future | Conjunctive Present |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | **`-aím`** (`salaím`) | **`-aíos`** (`shalaíos`) | **`-ód`** (`salód`) | **`-aíod`** (`go salaíod`) |
| 2nd sg (you) | **`-aír`** / `-aínns` (`salaír` / `salaínns`) | **`-aís`** / `-aír` (`shalaís` / `shalaír`) | **`-óir`** / `-óis` (`salóir` / `salóis`) | **`-aír`** / **`-aís`** (`go salaír` / `salaís`) |

### Second Conjugation (Slender Stem, `-igh`)

Example: `coinnigh` (to hold, to keep)

| Person | Present | Preterite | Future | Conjunctive Present |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | **`-ím`** (`coinním`) | **`-íos`** (`choinníos`) | **`-eod`** (`coinneod`) | **`-íod`** (`go gcoinníod`) |
| 2nd sg (you) | **`-ír`** / `-ínns` (`coinnír` / `coinnínns`) | **`-ís`** / `-ír` (`choinnís` / `choinnír`) | **`-eoir`** / `-eois` (`coinneoir` / `coinneois`) | **`-ír`** / **`-ís`** (`go gcoinnír` / `gcoinnís`) |

## Common Irregular Verbs

### `bí` (to be)

The synthetic present tense forms like `táim` are used as echo forms for the common analytical phrases like `tá mé`. With most other verbs, the `-im` ending is standard for the 1st person singular present tense anyway.

| Form | Present | Habitual Present | Preterite | Future |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg Affirmative | `táim` / `tás` | `bím` | `bhíos` | `bead` |
| 2nd sg Affirmative | `táir` / `táis` | `bír` / `bínns` | `bhís` | `beir` / `beidhis` |
| 1st sg Negative | `nílim` | `ní bhím` | `ní rabhas` | `ní bhead` |
| 2nd sg Negative | `nílir` / `nílis` | `ní bhír` / `bhínns` | `ní rabhais` | `ní bheir` / `bheidhis` |
| 1st sg Interrogative | `an bhfuilim` | `an mbím` | `an rabhas` | `an mbead` |
| 2nd sg Interrogative | `an bhfuilir` / `bhfuilis` | `an mbír` / `mbínns` | `an rabhais` | `an mbeir` / `mbeidhis` |
