In Irish, expressing compass directions involves more than just single words for north, south, east, and west. The language uses different forms to distinguish between a static location, movement towards a direction, and movement from a direction. Understanding these forms is key to talking about geography and travel.

## Forms of Direction

There are five main ways to express a direction, depending on the context.

1.  **Location**: Describes a static position ("in the north").
    *   *Tá an chathair thuaidh.* - The city is in the north.
2.  **From a Direction**: Describes movement originating from a direction ("from the west").
    *   *Séideann an ghaoth aniar.* - The wind blows from the west.
3.  **To a Direction**: Describes movement towards a direction ("going east").
    *   *Táimid ag dul soir.* - We are going east.
4.  **Relative Location**: Describes a position relative to another point ("south of the coast").
    *   *Tá an t-oileán laisteas den chósta.* - The island is south of the coast.
5.  **Noun**: Refers to the direction as a region or concept ("the West of Ireland").
    *   *Tá cónaí orthu san iarthar.* - They live in the west.

<Note>
The most important distinction for beginners is between location (e.g., `thuaidh` - in the north) and movement towards (e.g., `ó thuaidh` - northwards, to the north).

Notice that 'east' and 'west' use different words for movement towards (`soir`, `siar`) than 'north' and 'south' do (`ó thuaidh`, `ó dheas`).
</Note>

## Cardinal Directions

The four main compass points show the basic patterns for each form.

| Direction | Location | From | To | Relative | Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| North | `thuaidh` | `aduaidh` | `ó thuaidh` | `lastuaidh` | `an tuaisceart` |
| East | `thoir` | `anoir` | `soir` | `lastoir` | `an t-oirthear` |
| South | `theas` | `aneas` | `ó dheas` | `laisteas` | `an deisceart` |
| West | `thiar` | `aniar` | `siar` | `laistiar` | `an t-iarthar` |

## Intercardinal Directions

The intermediate directions are formed by combining the cardinal points.

| Direction | Location | From | To | Relative | Noun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Northeast | `thoir thuaidh` | `anoir aduaidh` | `soir ó thuaidh` | `lastoir lastuaidh` | `an t-oirthuaisceart` |
| Southeast | `thoir theas` | `anoir aneas` | `soir ó dheas` | `lastoir laisteas` | `an t-oirdheisceart` |
| Southwest | `thiar theas` | `aniar aneas` | `siar ó dheas` | `laistiar laisteas` | `an t-iardheisceart` |
| Northwest | `thiar thuaidh` | `aniar aduaidh` | `siar ó thuaidh` | `laistiar lastuaidh` | `an t-iarthuaisceart` |

<Tip>
You can decode the more complex directions by understanding how they are built:
- **Intercardinal points** like `thoir thuaidh` are literally "east-north".
- **"By" directions** like "North by East" use `lámh` (hand), as in `thuaidh lámh thoir` ("north hand east").
</Tip>
