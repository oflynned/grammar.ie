Irish can be written using two different alphabets. The modern, standard alphabet is the familiar Roman script (`Cló Rómhánach`), which is used in all modern print and digital media. You may also encounter the traditional Gaelic script (`Cló Gaelach`), which is now mostly used for decorative or ceremonial purposes.

The difference between the two scripts is purely stylistic. The spelling, grammar, and pronunciation of the words remain the same.

## Roman Script (Cló Rómhánach)

The Roman script is the standard writing system for Irish today. Its most notable feature is the use of the letter `h` placed after a consonant to show lenition.

For example, the first line of the Lord's Prayer (`An Phaidir`) is written like this:
*Ár nAthair atá ar neamh, go naofar d'ainm.*

Here, `Athair` is the lenited form of `athair` (father).

## Gaelic Script (Cló Gaelach)

The Gaelic script is a traditional, uncial style of lettering that was used for centuries. Instead of adding an `h` to show lenition, this script places a dot, known as a `ponc séimhithe` or simply `séimhiú`, over the consonant.

The same line from the Lord's Prayer looks like this in the Gaelic script:
*Ár nAṫair atá ar neaṁ, go naofar d'ainm.*

Notice the dot over the `t` in `Aṫair` and the `m` in `neaṁ`.

<Note>
While the `ponc séimhithe` is the most obvious difference, the letterforms themselves are also distinct. For example, the letters `r` and `s` have very different shapes compared to their modern Roman counterparts.
</Note>

### Comparison of Lenited Consonants

The following table shows the direct correspondence between the two systems for lenited consonants.

| Roman (with `h`) | Gaelic (with `ponc`) |
| :--------------- | :------------------- |
| `bh`             | `ḃ`                  |
| `ch`             | `ċ`                  |
| `dh`             | `ḋ`                  |
| `fh`             | `ḟ`                  |
| `gh`             | `ġ`                  |
| `mh`             | `ṁ`                  |
| `ph`             | `ṗ`                  |
| `sh`             | `ṡ`                  |
| `th`             | `ṫ`                  |

## Gaelic Fonts

To type or display text in the traditional Gaelic script, a special font is required. Many "Gaelic" or "uncial" fonts are available that contain the traditional letter shapes. Some operating systems and applications can render the `ponc séimhithe` over standard letters, but dedicated fonts like `Búnchló` or `Gaelchló` are often used to achieve an authentic appearance.
