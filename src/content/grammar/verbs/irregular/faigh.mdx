The verb `faigh` means "to get" or "to find" and is one of the most common irregular verbs in Irish. Its irregularity comes from using different stems in different tenses. The past tense uses the stem `fuair`, while the future and conditional tenses use the stem `geobh-`. It also has special dependent forms that are used after particles like `ní`, `an`, and `go`.

These are the standard, standalone forms of the verb, used in simple positive statements.

Dependent forms are required after particles like `ní` (not), `an` (question), `go` (that), `nach` (that... not), and the indirect relative particle `a`.

## Verbal Noun and Adjective

- **Verbal Noun**: `fáil`
- **Genitive of Verbal Noun**: `fála` (substantive), `faighte` (infinitive use)
- **Verbal Adjective**: `faighte`

## Usage Notes

### The Past Tense Stem fuair

The past tense stem `fuair` is never lenited, even after the direct relative particle `a`, which normally causes lenition.

- *An leabhar a fuair mé inné.* - The book that I got yesterday.

<Details summary="Historical note on 'ní bhfuair'">
The negative form `ní bhfuair` appears to use eclipsis, which is unusual for `ní` in the past tense. This spelling reflects a historical sound change. The original form was `ní fhuair` (with lenition). The sound of `ní fhuair` [ni: uər′] evolved to include a "w" glide sound [ni: wuər′]. The modern spelling `ní bhfuair` was adopted to represent this pronunciation, so the lenited form `*fhuair` is never written.
</Details>

## Dialectal Variations

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, the 3rd person present absolute form is often `gheibh sé` (he gets), with `gheibheann sé` being less common. The dependent form is `ní fhánn sé`. The form `fánn` (from `faghann`) can also be used as an absolute form. The autonomous is `gheibhthear` or `faghthar`.

- **Future**: `gheobhaidh sé` [jo:ə s′e], dependent `ní bhfuighidh sé` [N′i: wi: s′e].
- **Imperfect**: `gheibheadh`, dependent `ní bhfuigheadh`.
- **Past**: `fuair`. The negative can also be `chan fhuair`.
- **Imperative**: 2nd sg. `fagh` or `faigh`; 3rd sg. `faghadh sé` [fa:ət′ s′e].
- **Present Subjunctive**: `go bhfaghaidh sé` [gə wa: s′e].
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In Connacht, the present tense form is often `fánn` (from `faghann`) for both absolute and dependent uses. Other variants include `gheibheann`, `geibhfeann`, `gheibhfeann`, and `geobhfann`. Eclipsed dependent forms like `bhfaigheann` or `bhfuigheann` also occur.

A variant for the autonomous past `fuarthas` is `fríth`. The 3rd person imperative can be `faigheadh` [fɑ:x] or `gheobhfeadh` [jo:fəx].
</Dialect>

## Historical Forms

<Details summary="Historical note on older forms">
Previously, Irish distinguished between absolute (standalone) and dependent (after a particle) forms in the present and imperfect tenses. The absolute forms for `faigh` are now obsolete in the standard language but were based on the stem `geibh-`.

| Tense | Dependent (Modern) | Absolute (Older) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | `faigheann` | `(do-)gheibheann` |
| **Imperfect** | `faigheadh` | `(do-)gheibheadh` |

The lenition seen in the modern future (`gheobhaidh`) and conditional (`gheobhadh`) is a remnant of the old prefix `do-`, which was once common with these forms, e.g., `(do-)gheobhaidh sé`.
</Details>
