The verb `beir` is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. It has a wide range of meanings, including "to carry", "to bring", "to take", "to win", "to catch", and "to give birth". Its irregularity is most apparent in the past tense, which uses the stem `rug-`, and in the future and conditional tenses, which use the stem `béar-`.

## Indicative Mood

### Present Tense

The present tense uses the stem `beir-`.

*Beireann an t-iascaire breac.* - The fisherman catches a trout.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `beirim` |
| 2nd singular | `beireann tú` |
| 3rd singular | `beireann sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `beirimid` |
| 2nd plural | `beireann sibh` |
| 3rd plural | `beireann siad` |
| Autonomous | `beirtear` |
| Relative | `a bheireas` |

### Past Tense (Preterite)

The past tense uses the completely different stem `rug-`.

*Rug sé ar an liathróid.* - He caught the ball.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `rugas` |
| 2nd singular | `rugais` |
| 3rd singular | `rug sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `rugamar` |
| 2nd plural | `rugabhair` |
| 3rd plural | `rugadar` |
| Autonomous | `rugadh` |

### Imperfect Tense

The imperfect tense describes habitual actions in the past and uses the lenited stem `bheir-`.

*Bheirinn abhaile é gach lá.* - I used to carry it home every day.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `bheirinn` |
| 2nd singular | `bheirteá` |
| 3rd singular | `bheireadh sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `bheirimis` |
| 2nd plural | `bheireadh sibh` |
| 3rd plural | `bheiridís` |
| Autonomous | `bheirtí` |

### Future Tense

The future tense uses the stem `béar-`.

*Béarfaidh mé bua.* - I will win.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `béarfad` |
| 2nd singular | `béarfaidh tú` |
| 3rd singular | `béarfaidh sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `béarfaimid` |
| 2nd plural | `béarfaidh sibh` |
| 3rd plural | `béarfaidh siad` |
| Autonomous | `béarfar` |
| Relative | `a bhéarfas` |

## Conditional Mood

The conditional mood uses the lenited stem `bhéar-`.

*Dá mbeadh an t-airgead agam, bhéarfainn leat é.* - If I had the money, I would bring it with you.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `bhéarfainn` |
| 2nd singular | `bhéarfá` |
| 3rd singular | `bhéarfadh sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `bhéarfaimis` |
| 2nd plural | `bhéarfadh sibh` |
| 3rd plural | `bhéarfaidís` |
| Autonomous | `bhéarfaí` |

## Imperative Mood

The imperative is used for commands.

*Beir leat do mhála!* - Bring your bag with you!

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `beirim` |
| 2nd singular | `beir` |
| 3rd singular | `beireadh sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `beirimis` |
| 2nd plural | `beirigí` |
| 3rd plural | `beiridís` |
| Autonomous | `beirtear` |

## Subjunctive Mood

The subjunctive mood is used in certain expressions of wish, doubt, or possibility, often following particles like `go` (that) or `dá` (if).

*Go mbeire an bua ort.* - May you win.

| Person | Present Subjunctive | Past Subjunctive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st singular | `go mbeire mé` | `dá mbeirinn` |
| 2nd singular | `go mbeire tú` | `dá mbeirteá` |
| 3rd singular | `go mbeire sé/sí` | `dá mbeireadh sé/sí` |
| 1st plural | `go mbeirimid` | `dá mbeirimis` |
| 2nd plural | `go mbeire sibh` | `dá mbeireadh sibh` |
| 3rd plural | `go mbeire siad` | `dá mbeiridís` |
| Autonomous | `go mbeirtear` | `dá mbeirtí` |

## Verbal Forms

*   **Verbal Noun**: `breith`
*   **Verbal Adjective**: `beirthe`
*   **Genitive of the Verbal Noun**:
    *   When used as a noun (substantive): `breithe`. For example, *Lá na breithe* (Day of birth / Birthday).
    *   When used as an infinitive: `beirthe`. For example, *ag iarraidh an chorn a bheirthe* (trying to win the cup).

## Usage Notes

### To Give Birth

The verb `beir` is commonly used to mean "to give birth". In this context, it almost always appears in the autonomous form of the past tense, `rugadh`, creating a passive sense.

*Rugadh i mBaile Átha Cliath é.* - He was born in Dublin.

To say who gave birth, the preposition `do` is used.

*Rugadh mac di.* - She gave birth to a son. (Literally: A son was born to her.)

## Dialectal Variations

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In South Ulster, the present tense includes `beiridh` alongside the standard `beireann`. In the north, the endingless form `beir` is used. The form `breitheann` (to throw, or for animals to calve) is also occasionally heard.

Autonomous forms include `rugtar`, `rugthar` (South), and `beirtear` (North). In the future tense, `beirfidh` is found alongside `béarfaidh`, with the autonomous form `rugfar` (pronounced `rucar`).

In the preterite, both `bheir` and `rug` are used. The autonomous form `rugadh` specifically means "was born", while `rugas` or `beireadh` are used for other meanings, such as *beireadh air* (he was caught).

The verbal noun can be `rugan` in the north, as well as the standard `breith`.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In the present tense, both `beireann` and `béarann` are found. In the imperfect, `bheireadh` and `bhéaradh` occur, with autonomous forms `beirtí` and `béartaí` (unlenited).

The future tense is consistently `béarfaidh`, and the conditional is `béarfadh`. The conditional autonomous form is `béarfaí` (unlenited).

In the preterite, the forms `rug`, `bhéar`, and `bhreith` appear. As in Ulster, the autonomous form `rugadh` means "was born". For other meanings, `rugas` or `rugús` is used, e.g., *Rugús air* (He was caught).
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
The future form `béarfaidh` is typically pronounced with `ia` instead of `éa`, as is common in Munster: [b′iarhig′]. The conditional `béarfadh` is similarly pronounced [b′iarhax].

The preterite is `bheir` or `riug` (with a slender 'r'). However, in the Dingle peninsula, `rug`/`riug` is now rarely used. The verbal adjective is pronounced with a broad 'r': `bertha`.
</Dialect>

## Related Verbs

<Details summary="Historical Note: Origins of 'tabhair' and 'abair'">
The verb `beir` is the root of two other irregular verbs: `tabhair` (to give) and `abair` (to say).

*   `tabhair` originated from the prefix `do-` + `beir`, meaning "to-carry" or "to-bring". The prefix is still visible in older forms like `do-bheireann` (he gives). In modern Irish, the `do-` prefix was often dropped, but the lenition it caused remained, distinguishing it from `beir`. Compare:
    *   *beireann sé* - he brings
    *   *bheireann sé* - he gives (now `tugann sé`)

*   `abair` originated from the prefix `as-` + `beir`, meaning "to-bring-out" or "to utter".
</Details>
