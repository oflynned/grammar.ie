The verb `cluin` means "to hear". It is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. Its irregularity is confined to the past tense (preterite), which uses the distinct stem `chuala`. In all other tenses, it follows a regular conjugation pattern.

This verb is primarily used in Ulster Irish; the more common standard verb is `clois`, which shares the same irregular past tense.

## Conjugation

### Present Tense

*Cluinim an ceol sin.* - I hear that music.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st sg. | `cluinim` |
| 2nd sg. | `cluineann tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `cluineann sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `cluinimid` |
| 2nd pl. | `cluineann sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `cluineann siad` |
| Autonomous | `cluintear` |

### Past Tense (Preterite)

The past tense uses the irregular stem `chuala`.

*Chuala mé an scéal aréir.* - I heard the story last night.

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 1st sg. | `chuala mé` |
| 2nd sg. | `chuala tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `chuala sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `chualamar` |
| 2nd pl. | `chuala sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `chuala siad` |
| Autonomous | `chualathas` |

<Note>
Unlike most verbs, the autonomous form in the past tense, `chualathas`, is lenited.
</Note>

### Subjunctive Mood

## Derived Forms

### Verbal Noun

The verbal noun is `cluinstin`.

*Is maith liom a bheith ag cluinstin ceoil.* - I like listening to music.

The genitive case of the verbal noun is `cluinstine`.

### Verbal Adjective

The verbal adjective is `cluinte`. It means "heard" and is often used to form the perfect tenses with the verb `bí`.

*Tá an scéal sin cluinte agam cheana.* - I have heard that story already. (Literally: "That story is heard by me already.")

<Dialect region="Ulster">
The verb `cluin` is the variant of `clois` (to hear) that is commonly used in Ulster. Both verbs share the same irregular past tense form, `chuala`.
</Dialect>
