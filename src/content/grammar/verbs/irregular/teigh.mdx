The verb `téigh` means "to go" and is one of the most common irregular verbs in Irish. Its conjugation is highly irregular, using four different stems across its various tenses and moods: `téigh-`, `chuaigh-`, `rach-`, and `deach-`. Mastering this verb is essential for everyday communication.

## Conjugation

The verb `téigh` uses different stems depending on the tense and context.
- The stem `téigh-` is used for the present, imperfect, and imperative.
- The stem `chuaigh-` is used for the independent past tense.
- The stem `rach-` is used for the future and conditional.

| Person | Present | Past (Independent) | Imperfect | Future | Conditional | Imperative | Present Subjunctive | Past Subjunctive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `téim` | `chuas` | `théinn` | `rachad` | `rachainn` | `téim` | `go dté mé` | `dá dtéinn` |
| 2nd sg. | `téann tú` | `chuais` | `théiteá` | `rachaidh tú` | `rachfá` | `téigh` | `go dté tú` | `dá dtéiteá` |
| 3rd sg. | `téann sé/sí` | `chuaigh sé/sí` | `théadh sé/sí` | `rachaidh sé/sí` | `rachadh sé/sí` | `téadh sé/sí` | `go dté sé/sí` | `dá dtéadh sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `téimid` | `chuamar` | `théimis` | `rachaimid` | `rachaimis` | `téimis` | `go dtéimid` | `dá dtéimis` |
| 2nd pl. | `téann sibh` | `chuabhair` | `théadh sibh` | `rachaidh sibh` | `rachadh sibh` | `téigí` | `go dté sibh` | `dá dtéadh sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `téann siad` | `chuadar` | `théidís` | `rachaidh siad` | `rachaidís` | `téidís` | `go dté siad` | `dá dtéidís` |
| Autonomous | `téitear` | `chuathas` | `théití` | `rachfar` | `rachfaí` | `téitear` | `go dtéitear` | `dá dtéití` |

### Relative Forms

The relative forms are used in relative clauses, typically after the particle `a`.

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | `a théas` | *an fear a théas ann* - the man who goes there |
| Future | `a rachas` | *an fear a rachas ann* - the man who will go there |

## Dependent Past Tense Forms

In the past tense (preterite), `téigh` uses a special dependent stem, `deach-`, after particles like `ní` (not), `an` (question), `go` (that), and `nach` (that...not). Note that the independent past tense forms (e.g., `chuaigh mé`) do not use a preterite particle like `d'`.

| Person | Negative (`ní`) | Interrogative (`an`) |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `ní dheachaigh mé` | `an ndeachaigh mé?` |
| 2nd sg. | `ní dheachaigh tú` | `an ndeachaigh tú?` |
| 3rd sg. | `ní dheachaigh sé/sí` | `an ndeachaigh sé/sí?` |
| 1st pl. | `ní dheachamar` | `an ndeachamar?` |
| 2nd pl. | `ní dheachaigh sibh` | `an ndeachaigh sibh?` |
| 3rd pl. | `ní dheachadar` | `an ndeachadar?` |
| Autonomous | `ní dheachthas` | `an ndeachthas?` |

*Ní dheachaigh mé abhaile.* - I did not go home.
*An ndeachaigh tú go dtí an siopa?* - Did you go to the shop?

## Verbal Forms

- **Verbal Noun:** `dul` (going)
- **Verbal Adjective:** `dulta` (gone)
- **Genitive of Verbal Noun:** `dula` (used as a noun) or `dulta` (used as part of a verb phrase)

## Usage and Dialectal Forms

<Note>
In everyday speech, the 2nd person singular imperative form `téigh` is often replaced by `téir`, `téire`, or `téirigh`. Additionally, the older 3rd person present tense form `téid` is sometimes used instead of `téann`.
</Note>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In Connemara, it is common to use forms derived from the verb `gabh` ("take") instead of `téigh` for "go".
- **Imperative:** `gabh!` (go!), `gabhigí!` (go! pl.)
- **Future/Conditional Stem:** `gabh-`
- **Verbal Noun:** `goil` or `gabháil`
- **Verbal Adjective:** `goite`
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster Irish, the future and conditional stem is often `ragh-` instead of the standard `rach-`. For example, `raghad` instead of `rachad`.
</Dialect>

<Details summary="Historical orthography">
In the older, pre-reform spelling system, the forms `chuaigh` and `deachaigh` were written with a final `-dh` as `chuaidh` and `deachaidh`. The verb `téigh` (to go) was not written with `-dh`. This distinguished it from the regular verb `téigh` (to warm), which was written `téidh` in the old orthography.
</Details>
