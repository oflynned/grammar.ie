The verb `bí` (to be) is one of the most common and most irregular verbs in the Irish language. Its irregularity comes from using several different stems across its various tenses and moods. Understanding these stems is key to mastering its conjugation.

-   **Present Tense**: `tá` (independent), `fuil` (dependent)
-   **Habitual Present, Preterite, Imperfect, Imperative**: `bí`
-   **Future & Conditional**: `beidh` / `beadh`
-   **Subjunctive & Dependent Preterite**: `raibh`

Unlike regular verbs, `bí` does not use preterite particles like `d'`.

<Note>
The form `beite` (older spelling `beithte`) is not used as a verbal adjective meaning "been". It is a rare participle of necessity, used in constructions like `ní beite do dhuine rud a dhéanamh` (a person should not do something).
</Note>

## Usage and Syntax Notes

### First-Person Singular Present

In the first-person singular of the present tense, both the synthetic form (`táim`) and the analytic form (`tá mé`) are common and considered standard. The analytic form is often preferred in modern speech.

- *Táim anseo.* - I am here.
- *Tá mé anseo.* - I am here.

### Relative Forms

The present tense relative form of `bí` is `atá`. This form originates from an older version of the verb where the `a-` prefix was always present. Today, it is only retained in the relative clause.

- *Sin é an fear atá ag teacht.* - That is the man who is coming.

The relative particle `a` is often omitted after interrogative words like `cad` and `conas`.

- *Cad atá uait?* - What do you want?
- *Conas atá tú?* - How are you?

<Dialect>
The relative form for the habitual present is `a bhíos` (or `a bhíonns` in Conamara), and for the future is `a bheas`. These are widespread in Connacht and Ulster, but less common in Munster. In parts of Ulster, the unlenited forms `a bíos` or `a bíonns` are used.
</Dialect>

### Dependent Forms and Negation

The negative present tense form `níl` (is not) is a contraction of the particle `ní` and the dependent stem `fuil`. The full historical progression is `ní` + `fuil` → `ní fhuil` → `níl`.

The construction `mura bhfuil` (if... is not) can appear as `murab fhuil` in Connacht and Ulster.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, the uncontracted form `ní fhuil` is often still pronounced as two syllables. The negative particle `cha(n)` is also common, resulting in `chan fhuil`. While `cha` usually causes lenition, it often causes eclipsis with forms of `bí`, such as `cha mbíonn`.
</Dialect>

### Dialectal Present Tense

<Dialect region="Munster">
In some Munster dialects, the present tense form `tá` is always lenited after the subject, becoming `thá`.
</Dialect>

### Subjunctive Mood

The present subjunctive is used to express wishes or after certain conjunctions. The standard form uses the stem `raibh`.

- *Go raibh maith agat.* - Thank you (literally: May there be good at you).

The negative particle used with the present subjunctive is `ná`, not `nár`.

- *Ná raibh maith agat!* - No thanks to you!

<Dialect>
In Munster and South Connacht, a distinction is sometimes made between the optative (wishes) and the subjunctive (after conjunctions like `sula` or `go`). These dialects use `raibh` for wishes but may use `go mbé` (Munster) or `go mbí` (South Connacht) for the subjunctive.
</Dialect>

### Preterite Subjunctive

The preterite subjunctive of `bí` is formed from the conditional stem `beadh`, unlike other verbs where it resembles the imperfect.

<Note>
A key difference appears in the 2nd person singular and the autonomous form. The conditional's `-f-` is replaced by a `-t-`. Compare the three distinct forms:

-   **Preterite Subjunctive**: `dá mbeiteá` (if you were)
-   **Conditional**: `bheifeá` (you would be)
-   **Imperfect**: `bhíteá` (you used to be)
</Note>

## Grammatical Limitations

The verb `bí` has some important limitations that learners should be aware of.

-   It cannot form a perfect tense (e.g., "have been") because it lacks a verbal adjective.
-   It cannot form a progressive aspect (e.g., "I am being") because the construction `ag bheith` is not used.

Despite these limitations, `bí` is a vital auxiliary verb used to form progressive tenses with all other verbs.
