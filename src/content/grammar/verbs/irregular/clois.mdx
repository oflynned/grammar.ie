The verb "to hear" has two main stems in Irish: `clois` and `cluin`. The choice between them is mostly a matter of dialect, with `cluin` being more common in Ulster and `clois` in Munster. Both are used in the Standard Language.

This verb is considered irregular because its past tense (preterite) uses a completely different stem: `chuala`. In all other tenses, it follows regular conjugation patterns. Unlike many other irregular verbs, it does not have special dependent forms.

<Note>
The only truly irregular part of this verb is the past tense (preterite) stem `chuala`. All other tenses are formed regularly from the stems `clois` or `cluin`.
</Note>

The stem `clois` is more common in the south of Ireland (Munster) and is also a valid form in the Standard Language.

## Dialectal Variations

<Dialect region="Ulster">
*   The stem `cluin` is used almost exclusively.
*   In the present tense, the verb often appears without an ending: `cluin sé` (he hears). It can also be lenited, `chluin sé`, due to the influence of an older form `do-chluin`.
*   Dependent present tense forms are always without an ending: *An gcluin tú?* - Do you hear?
*   The future and conditional can switch to the 2nd conjugation: `cluineochaidh sé` (he will hear).
*   Preterite particles are often omitted: *Ní chuala sé* (He did not hear) is used alongside `níor chuala`, and *An gcuala sé?* (Did he hear?) alongside `ar chuala`.
*   The autonomous preterite can be `chualas` (a reduction of `chualathas`) or `cluineadh`.
*   The verbal noun is `cluinstin` or `cluinstean`.
*   The verb `mothaigh` (to feel) is often used instead of `cluin`.
*   In the imperative, `éist!` (listen!) is commonly used for the 2nd person singular.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
*   Both `clois` and `cluin` are used, with `clois` being more common in the south of the region.
*   Dependent present tense forms often lack an ending: *An gcluin tú?* / *An gclois tú?* - Do you hear?
*   The preterite is usually `chuala`, but a regular form `chlois sé` (he heard) is sometimes used.
*   The autonomous preterite is `cluineadh` or `cloiseadh`, not the standard `chualathas`.
*   The verbal noun for `clois` is `cloisteáil`, but in the north, you may hear `cluns` or `clins`.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
*   Only the stem `clois` is used.
*   The 1st person singular preterite is `chuala` (e.g. Muskerry, Dingle) or `chualag` (e.g. Carbery), not the standard `chualas`. The form `chualas` is rare, found mainly in Co. Clare.
*   A regular preterite form `chlois sé` (he heard) also occurs in Muskerry.
*   The analytic preterite form is `chualaigh`.
*   The autonomous preterite has several forms: `chualathas`, `chualathars`, `chualadh`, or `cualadh`.
*   The verbal noun is `cloisint` or `clos`.
*   The verb `airigh` (to feel) is often used instead, especially in Cork and Waterford.
</Dialect>
