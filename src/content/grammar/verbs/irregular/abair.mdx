`abair` (to say) is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. It is extremely common and essential for everyday conversation. Its irregularity comes from using several different stems for different tenses and moods, such as `deir-`, `dúirt-`, `déar-`, and `abair-`.

## Conjugation

### Present Tense

The present tense uses the stem `deir-`.

*`Deir sé an fhírinne i gcónaí.`* - He always tells the truth.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `deirim` |
| 2nd sg. | `deir tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `deir sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `deirimid` |
| 2nd pl. | `deir sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `deir siad` |
| Autonomous | `deirtear` |
| Relative | `a deir` |

<Note>
In the standard language, the form `deir` is more common than the alternative `deireann`. In some dialects, `deireann` is used to express a habitual action.
</Note>

### Past Tense (Preterite)

The past tense uses the stem `dúirt-`. A key feature of `abair` is that it does not use a preterite particle like `d'`.

*`Dúirt mé leis teacht abhaile.`* - I told him to come home.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `dúras`, `dúrt` |
| 2nd sg. | `dúraís` |
| 3rd sg. | `dúirt sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `dúramar` |
| 2nd pl. | `dúrabhair` |
| 3rd pl. | `dúradar` |
| Autonomous | `dúradh` |

Because `abair` does not use the past tense particles `d'`, `níor`, `ar`, or `gur`, its dependent forms use the particles associated with the present and future tenses (`ní`, `an`, `go`, `nach`).

*`Ní dúirt sé tada.`* - He said nothing.
*`An ndúirt tú leis?`* - Did you tell him?
*`Sílim go ndúirt sé é.`* - I think that he said it.

### Imperfect Tense

The imperfect tense also uses the stem `deir-` to describe past habitual actions.

*`Deirinn leis gach lá, ach níor éist sé.`* - I used to tell him every day, but he didn't listen.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `deirinn` |
| 2nd sg. | `deirteá` |
| 3rd sg. | `deireadh sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `deirimis` |
| 2nd pl. | `deireadh sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `deiridís` |
| Autonomous | `deirtí` |

### Future Tense

The future tense uses the stem `déar-`.

*`Déarfaidh mé leis amárach.`* - I will tell him tomorrow.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `déarfad` |
| 2nd sg. | `déarfaidh tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `déarfaidh sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `déarfaimid` |
| 2nd pl. | `déarfaidh sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `déarfaidh siad` |
| Autonomous | `déarfar` |
| Relative | `a déarfas` |

### Conditional Mood

The conditional mood also uses the stem `déar-`.

*`Déarfainn an fhírinne dá bhfiafródh sé díom.`* - I would tell the truth if he asked me.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `déarfainn` |
| 2nd sg. | `déarfá` |
| 3rd sg. | `déarfadh sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `déarfaimis` |
| 2nd pl. | `déarfadh sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `déarfaidís` |
| Autonomous | `déarfaí` |

### Imperative Mood

The imperative mood uses the stem `abair-`.

*`Abair é arís!`* - Say it again!

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `abraim` (May I say) |
| 2nd sg. | `abair` |
| 3rd sg. | `abradh sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `abraimis` |
| 2nd pl. | `abraigí` |
| 3rd pl. | `abraidís` |
| Autonomous | `abairtear` |

### Subjunctive Mood

The subjunctive mood uses the stem `deir-`.

**Present Subjunctive**
*`Go ndeire tú an fhírinne.`* - May you tell the truth.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `go ndeire mé` |
| 2nd sg. | `go ndeire tú` |
| 3rd sg. | `go ndeire sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `go ndeirimid` |
| 2nd pl. | `go ndeire sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `go ndeire siad` |
| Autonomous | `go ndeirtear` |

**Past Subjunctive**
The past subjunctive is identical in form to the imperfect indicative.
*`Dá ndeirinn an scéal, ní chreidfeá mé.`* - If I were to tell the story, you wouldn't believe me.

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | `dá ndeirinn` |
| 2nd sg. | `dá ndeirteá` |
| 3rd sg. | `dá ndeireadh sé/sí` |
| 1st pl. | `dá ndeirimis` |
| 2nd pl. | `dá ndeireadh sibh` |
| 3rd pl. | `dá ndeiridís` |
| Autonomous | `dá ndeirtí` |

## Verbal Noun and Verbal Adjective

- **Verbal Noun**: `rá`
- **Genitive of Verbal Noun**: `rá` (substantive) or `ráite` (infinitive)
- **Verbal Adjective**: `ráite`

*`Tá sé deacair é sin a rá.`* - It is difficult to say that.
*`Tar éis a ráite dóibh...`* - After it was said by them...
*`An scéal ráite`* - The spoken story

## Mutations

The forms of `abair` that begin with `d-` (`deir`, `dúirt`, `déar-`) are never lenited in the standard language. This is because they historically began with a vowel (`adeir`, `adúirt`).

*`An fear a deir an fhírinne.`* - The man who tells the truth. (No lenition after direct relative `a`)
*`Cad deir tú?`* - What are you saying? (The relative particle `a` is often omitted after `cad`)

The only standard exception is after the negative particle `nár` in the subjunctive mood.

*`D'iarr mé air nár dheire sé focal.`* - I asked him that he might not say a word.

## Usage with Direct Speech

When quoting direct speech, Irish often uses the defective verb `ar` or `arsa` instead of a form of `abair`. `ar` is used before the pronouns `seisean`, `sise`, `siadsan`, while `arsa` is used before nouns or other pronouns.

*`"Tá an ceart agat," ar sise.`* - "You are right," said she.
*`"Is lá breá é," arsa Seán.`* - "It's a fine day," said Seán.

If `abair` is used after direct speech, it must be in a relative clause.

*`"Beidh mé ann," a dúirt sí.`* - "I will be there," she said.

<Note>
In storytelling or reporting speech, the present relative form `a deir` is often used timelessly, even when reporting something said in the past.
*`"Imigh leat," a deir sé liom.`* - "Be off with you," he says/said to me.
</Note>

<Details summary="Historical and Older Forms">
  Older forms of Irish used the stem `abr-` for dependent forms in the present, imperfect, future, and conditional tenses. This created a clear distinction between absolute (independent) and dependent verb forms that is now mostly lost in the standard language, though these forms are still found in some dialects.

| Tense/Mood | Dependent Form | Absolute Form |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | `abrann` | `deir(eann)` |
| **Imperfect** | `abradh` | `deireadh` |
| **Future** | `abróchaidh` | `déarfaidh` |
| **Conditional** | `abróchadh` | `déarfadh` |
| **Subjunctive** | `go n-abra(idh)` | (Always dependent) |
</Details>

## Dialectal Variations

<Dialect region="Ulster">
- Forms beginning with `d-` can be lenited or the `d-` can be dropped entirely: *`ní dheir sé`* or *`ní eir sé`* (he doesn't say).
- The 1st person singular is analytic: *`deir mé`* instead of `deirim`.
- The stem `abr-` is used in the present tense, both dependently and independently (e.g., `abrann`). The autonomous form can be `abartar` or `abraítear`.
- The future and conditional also have `abr-` forms, such as `abróchaidh` and `abróchadh`.
- The preterite form `dúirt` is often treated as `d'úirt`. This means the `d-` is seen as the past particle `do` and is dropped after other particles: *`níor úirt`* (didn't say), *`gur úirt`* (that said).
- The autonomous preterite is often `húradh` or `húras`.
- The present subjunctive `go n-abraidh` is more common than `go ndeire`.
- The verbal noun can be `ráit` or `ráidht`.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
- The stem `abr-` is used, more often in dependent contexts. Verbs with this stem are conjugated regularly according to the second conjugation (e.g., `abraíonn`).
- A distinction is sometimes made: `abair` for "to recite" or "sing a song", and `deir` for "to communicate".
- The form `deir` is used for both simple and habitual present, while `deireann` is more specifically habitual. `deireann` can be lenited, with the `d-` disappearing: *`ní eireann`* (doesn't usually say). The form `deir` is never lenited: *`ní deir`*.
- Analytic forms are common: *`deir mé`* (I say), *`deireann muid`* (we say).
- The preterite `dúirt` is also treated as `d'úirt`, leading to dependent forms like *`níor úirt`* and *`gur úirt`*. The autonomous form is often `húradh`.
- "Echo" forms exist for the preterite: `dúirteas` (I said), `dúirtis` (you said), `dúirteadar` (they said).
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
- In the southwest, `deireann` is used for the 2nd person singular and plural present.
- The stem `abr-` is rare and only used in dependent forms.
- The preterite form is often pronounced `duairt`.
- Synthetic preterite forms are common and varied: `dúras`/`dúrt`/`duart` (I said); `dúrais`/`dúiris`/`duarais` (you said).
- The autonomous preterite can be `dúradh`/`duaradh` or `dúrathas`/`duarathas`.
- In Ring (An Rinn), forms with a long `í` are found: `dúraíos`/`dúiríos` (I said).
- The verbal noun is often pronounced `reá`, with a slender `r`, especially when lenited (e.g., *`a reá`*).
</Dialect>
