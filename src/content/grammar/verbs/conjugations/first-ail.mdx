Verbs ending in the suffix `-áil` form a large and productive group within the first conjugation. Although they have more than one syllable, they follow the standard patterns of the first conjugation with one important sound change.

Many of these verbs are derived from English loanwords, making this a common pattern for new words entering the language.

*   `sábháil` - to save
*   `peinteáil` - to paint
*   `pairceáil` - to park
*   `iarnáil` - to iron

Colloquially, this pattern can be applied to almost any English verb, such as `hitchhikeáil` (to hitchhike) or `bicycleáil` (to cycle).

## The -áil to -ál- Sound Change

The key feature of these verbs is that the final slender consonant cluster `-áil` is "depalatalised" to a broad `-ál-` before most endings are added. This means the `i` is dropped from the stem.

*   *Sábhálann sé an comhad.* - He saves the file.

<Note>
The main exception to this rule is before endings that begin with `t`. In these cases, the slender `-áil-` sound is kept. Compare the broad `l` in `sábhálann` with the slender `l` in `sábháiltear`.
</Note>

*   *Sábháiltear an comhad.* - The file is saved.

This pattern differs slightly from other polysyllabic verbs in the first conjugation, like `taispeáin` (to show). With `taispeáin`, the stem is depalatalised to `taispeán-` before endings with `t` as well, giving `taispeántar`.

## Conjugation Forms

The following tables show the standard endings for verbs in `-áil`, using `sábháil` (to save) as a model.

### Present Tense

| Person | Ending | Example Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st Singular | `-álaim` | `sábhálaim` |
| 2nd Singular | `-álann tú` | `sábhálann tú` |
| 3rd Singular | `-álann sé/sí` | `sábhálann sé` |
| 1st Plural | `-álaimid` | `sábhálaimid` |
| 2nd Plural | `-álann sibh` | `sábhálann sibh` |
| 3rd Plural | `-álann siad` | `sábhálann siad` |
| Autonomous | `-áiltear` | `sábháiltear` |
| Relative | `-álas` | `a shábhálas` |
