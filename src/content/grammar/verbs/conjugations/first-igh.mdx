Some monosyllabic verbs in the first conjugation end in `-igh`. Although the `-igh` ending is often associated with the second conjugation, these verbs belong to the first because they form their future tense with an `-f-` marker. Their conjugation patterns depend on the type of vowel in the verb stem.

There are two main categories. For verbs with a long vowel or diphthong, the `-igh` ending is usually dropped before adding other endings. For verbs with a short vowel, the `-igh` is considered part of the verb's root and is retained, typically changing to `-í-` before an ending.

<Note>
This pattern also applies to longer verbs formed by adding a prefix to these monosyllabic roots. For example, `eisiaigh` (to exclude) is formed from `iaigh` (to close), and `ionsuigh` (to insert) is from `suigh` (to sit). They follow the same conjugation rules as their simple root verb.
</Note>

<Details summary="A note on spelling reform">
Modern Irish spelling simplifies many verb forms. For example, the older spelling `dóigheann sé` is now written as `dónn sé` (he burns). The `-igh-` is dropped in writing, though the pronunciation was already changing in that direction. You may still encounter the older spellings in texts written before the mid-20th century.
</Details>

We can distinguish four groups based on the vowel preceding the `-igh` ending.

## Verbs with a Broad Vowel or Diphthong

This group includes verbs where the stem contains a long broad vowel (`á`, `ó`, `ú`, `ao`) or a diphthong ending in a broad vowel (`eo`, `ua`, `ia`).

Examples include `dóigh` (to burn), `clóigh` (to tame), `reoigh` (to freeze), `brúigh` (to press), `glaoigh` (to call), and `luaigh` (to mention).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-igh` ending is dropped before adding any other ending, except in the 2nd person singular imperative (e.g., `dóigh!`) and the 3rd person singular preterite (e.g., `dhóigh sé`).
*   Future and conditional endings use a broad `f`: `-faidh`, `-fadh`, `-far`, `-faí`.
*   Endings with `-t-` are slender: `-itear`, `-iteá`, `-ití`.
*   In the present subjunctive, the `-igh` is dropped, leaving only the stem vowel, e.g., `go ndó sé` (may he burn).
*   For endings that start with `a-` like `-ann`, `-adh`, and `-amar`, the `a` is dropped after the long vowel of the stem, resulting in `-nn`, `-dh`, and `-mar`. For example, `dóigh` + `-ann` becomes `dónn`.

### Verbs ending in -aeigh

The rare verb `glaeigh` (to glue) also belongs to this group. It is unusual because it takes the full `-ann`, `-amar`, `-adh` endings instead of the shortened forms, creating the sequence `-aea-`. However, future and conditional endings are broad (`-faidh`, `-fadh`), and endings with `-t-` are slender (`-itear`, `-iteá`).

*   *Glaeann sé* - He glues
*   *Ghlaeigh sé* - He glued
*   *Glaefaidh sé* - He will glue

## Verbs Ending in -éigh

This group includes verbs with the long slender vowel `é` in the stem, such as `léigh` (to read) and `téigh` (to heat).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-igh` ending is dropped before adding other endings, except for the 2nd person singular imperative (`léigh!`) and 3rd person singular preterite (`léigh sé`).
*   Future and conditional endings use a slender `f`: `-ifidh`, `-ifeadh`, `-ifear`, `-ifí`.
*   Endings with `-t-` are slender: `-itear`, `-iteá`, `-ití`.
*   In the present subjunctive, the `-igh` is dropped, leaving only the stem vowel `é`, e.g., `go lé sé` (may he read).

## Verbs Ending in -ígh

This group includes verbs with a long `í` in the stem, such as `maígh` (to assert) and `caígh` (to weep, to lament).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-gh` is omitted before endings.
*   Future and conditional endings are slender: `-fidh`, `-feadh`, `-fear`, `-fí`.
*   Endings with `-t-` are also slender: `-tear`, `-teá`, `-tí`.
*   The slender endings `-im` and `-inn` are shortened to `-m` and `-nn` after the long `í`.
*   Endings starting with `a-` change according to spelling rules after `í`: `-ann` becomes `-onn`, and `-amar` becomes `-omar`.
*   In the present subjunctive, the `-gh` is dropped, leaving only the stem vowel `í`, e.g., `go maí sé` (may he assert).

## Verbs with a Short Vowel + -igh

This group includes verbs where `-igh` follows a short vowel or a consonant. Examples include `nigh` (to wash), `figh` (to weave), `luigh` (to lie down), and `snoigh` (to carve).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-igh` ending is part of the verb root and is **never omitted**.
*   Before most endings, the `-igh` is written as `-í-`. This is a spelling rule: `-igh` + `-eann` was historically `-igheann`, which simplifies to `-íann`, and then becomes `-íonn` to follow vowel harmony rules. Similarly, `-igh` + `-im` becomes `-ím`.
*   Future and conditional endings are slender and follow this `-í-`: `-ífidh`, `-ífeadh`, `-ífear`, `-ífí`.
*   Endings with `-t-` (`-itear`, `-iteá`, `-ití`) are an exception: the `i` remains short. For example, `nitear` (one washes), not `*nítear`. This distinguishes them from verbs with a long `í` in the stem.
