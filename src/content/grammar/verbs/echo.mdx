Echo forms, also known as "answer forms" (`foirmeacha an fheagra`), are synthetic verb endings used in specific situations, primarily in Connacht and Ulster Irish. They are called "echo forms" because they typically echo or respond to a preceding question or statement without needing a separate subject pronoun.

In these dialects, it is more common to use analytical verb forms (e.g., `ghlan mé` - "I cleaned"). The synthetic forms (e.g., `ghlanas` - "I cleaned") are often reserved for the contexts described below.

## When to Use Echo Forms

These forms appear in situations known as "answer ellipsis", where a full sentence is unnecessary.

### Short Answers

Echo forms are used to provide short "yes" or "no" answers to questions.

*   *Ar ghlan tú an carr, a Sheáin? - Ghlanas.* - Did you wash the car, Seán? - Yes (I did).

### Question Tags

They are also used in short questions at the end of a statement, similar to English "question tags".

*   *Ghlan tú an carr, a Sheáin, nár ghlanais?* - You washed the car, Seán, didn't you?

### Short Remarks

Finally, they appear in brief affirmative or negative remarks.

*   *Mhuise, rinneas!* - Oh yes, I did!
*   *M’anam go gcualas!* - I swear I heard it!

<Note>
Synthetic forms are generally **not** used when giving a longer answer that includes an object. In that case, the analytical form is preferred.

*Nár ghlan tú an carr? - Ach ghlan mé é!* - Didn't you wash the car? - But I did wash it!

However, it is common for an echo form to appear alongside a longer answer, either before or after it.

*Is dóichí gur chuala túsa gur … - Chualas. Chuala mé caint air.* - You probably heard that... - Yes. I heard talk about it.
*Á ní fhaca mé é, ní fhacas.* - Ah, I didn't see it, no.
</Note>

## Dialectal Differences

The usage and terminology around these forms vary by dialect.

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, synthetic verb endings are common in normal declarative sentences, not just in answers. Because they are a regular feature of everyday speech, they are not specifically referred to as "echo forms". However, the same principle applies: in situations where a synthetic form is optional (like the 2nd person singular present), it is the preferred choice for short answers.
</Dialect>

The second person singular ("you") has several dialectal variants, which you will see in the tables below.

<Dialect region="Connacht">
The traditional ending for the 2nd person singular present and future is `-(a)ir`. In Connacht, however, other forms are common:
- **Present Tense:** `-(a)inns` is often used instead of `-(a)ir`.
- **Future Tense:** Both `-f(a)ir` and `-f(a)is` are used.
- **Past Tense:** The standard ending is `-(a)is`, but `-(a)ir` also occurs.
</Dialect>

## Conjugation Patterns

The following tables show the echo forms for the 1st person singular ("I") and 2nd person singular ("you") across different tenses and verb conjugations. They include both standard and common dialectal variants.

### First Conjugation (Broad Stem)

Example verb: `glan` (to clean)

| Person | Present | Past Tense | Future | Present Subjunctive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | `-aim` | `-as` | `-fad` | `-ad` |
| you | `-air` / `-ainns` | `-ais` / `-air` | `-fair` / `-fais` | `-air` / `-ais` |
| I clean | `glanaim` | `ghlanas` | `glanfad` | `go nglanad` |
| you clean | `glanair` / `glanainns` | `ghlanais` / `ghlanair` | `glanfair` / `glanfais` | `go nglanair` / `nglanais` |

### First Conjugation (Slender Stem)

| Person | Present | Past Tense | Future | Present Subjunctive |

## Irregular Verbs

Two common irregular verbs also have distinct echo forms.

### `Bí` (to be)

| | Present | Habitual Present | Past Tense | Future |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I am | `táim` / `tás` | `bím` | `bhíos` | `bead` |
| you are | `táir` / `táis` | `bír` / `bínns` | `bhís` | `beir` / `beidhis` |
| I am not | `nílim` | `ní bhím` | `ní rabhas` | `ní bhead` |
| you are not | `nílir` / `nílis` | `ní bhír` / `bhínns` | `ní rabhais` | `ní bheir` / `bheidhis` |
| am I? | `an bhfuilim` | `an mbím` | `an rabhas` | `an mbead` |
| are you? | `an bhfuilir` / `bhfuilis` | `an mbír` / `mbínns` | `an rabhais` | `an mbeir` / `mbeidhis` |

<Note>
The synthetic present tense forms `táim`, `nílim`, and `an bhfuilim` serve as the echo forms for the very common analytical forms `tá mé`, `níl mé`, and `an bhfuil mé`. With most other verbs, the `-im` ending is standard for the 1st person singular present tense and is not considered a special echo form.
</Note>
