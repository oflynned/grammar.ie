In Irish, possessive pronouns are typically referred to as `aidiachtaí sealbhacha` (possessive adjectives). This is because they always appear with a noun and cannot stand alone, unlike English "mine" or "yours".

## Forms and Initial Mutations

Possessive pronouns in Irish cause initial mutations on the noun that follows them. The specific mutation depends on the pronoun.

| Person | Pronoun | Mutation | Example (consonant) | Example (vowel) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `mo` | Lenition | *mo chara* (my friend) | *m'athair* (my father) |
| 2nd sg | `do` | Lenition | *do chara* (your friend) | *d'athair* (your father) |
| 3rd sg (m) | `a` | Lenition | *a chara* (his friend) | *a athair* (his father) |
| 3rd sg (f) | `a` | h-prefix | *a cara* (her friend) | *a hathair* (her father) |
| 1st pl | `ár` | Eclipsis | *ár gcara* (our friend) | *ár n-athair* (our father) |
| 2nd pl | `bhur` | Eclipsis | *bhur gcara* (your friend) | *bhur n-athair* (your father) |
| 3rd pl | `a` | Eclipsis | *a gcara* (their friend) | *a n-athair* (their father) |

Note that `mo` and `do` are shortened to `m'` and `d'` before a vowel or `fh-` (e.g., *m'fhocal*, my word).

### Emphatic Forms

To add emphasis, a suffix is attached to the noun (or a following adjective), not the pronoun itself.

- **-sa/-se**: Used for 1st/2nd person singular and 2nd person plural.
- **-san/-sean**: Used for 3rd person singular masculine and 3rd person plural.
- **-na/-ne**: Used for 1st person plural.

Examples:
- *mo charrsa* (MY car)
- *a charrsan* (HIS car)
- *ár gcarrna* (OUR car)
- *mo charr mórsa* (MY big car)

## Using `cuid`

The noun `cuid` (share, part) is frequently used with possessive pronouns, especially in certain contexts.

### When to Use `cuid`

The use of `cuid` is mandatory or very common in the following cases:

- **Plural nouns**: *mo chuid leabhar* (my books). An exception is for inalienable possessions like body parts, e.g., *mo chosa* (my feet).
- **Uncountable nouns**: *do chuid Gaeilge* (your Irish), *ár gcuid airgid* (our money).
- **Specific categories**: It is standard for food (*mo chuid bainne*, my milk), property (*mo chuid talaimh*, my land), and abstract concepts like work or knowledge (*mo chuid oibre*, my work).

Usage can vary by dialect, with `cuid` being more frequent in northern dialects.

### Grammar of `cuid`

- The noun following `cuid` is in the genitive case.
- Despite `cuid` being a feminine noun, it does not cause lenition on the following genitive noun.
- *a cuid gruaige* (her hair, lit. "her share of hair")

When using an emphatic suffix with `cuid`, it can be attached to `cuid` itself or to the following noun:
- *mo chuidse airgid* (MY money)
- *mo chuid airgidse* (my MONEY)

## Expressing "mine", "yours" (Substantival Forms)

Irish does not have single words for "mine", "yours", "his", etc. Instead, it uses a possessive pronoun with a helper noun like `ceann` (one, head), `cuid` (share), or `muintir` (people).

| Meaning | For Countable Singular Nouns | For Plural or Uncountable Nouns |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| mine | `mo cheannsa` | `mo chuidse` |
| yours (sg) | `do cheannsa` | `do chuidse` |
| his | `a cheannsan` | `a chuidsean` |
| hers | `a ceannsa` | `a cuidse` |
| ours | `ár gceann-na` | `ár gcuidne` |
| yours (pl) | `bhur gceannsa` | `bhur gcuidse` |
| theirs | `a gceannsan` | `a gcuidsean` |

- Use `ceann` for countable singular items: *Sin é mo cheannsa.* (That is mine.)
- Use `cuid` for plural or uncountable items: *Sin é mo chuidse.* (Those are mine / That is mine.)
- Use `muintir` for people: *mo mhuintirse* (my people/family).

### Expressing "Belongs to"

To state that something belongs to someone, use the copula with the preposition `le` (with), not a possessive pronoun.

- *Is liomsa an leabhar.* (The book is mine / The book belongs to me.)
- *Is leatsa an carr.* (The car is yours.)
- *Is linne na bróga.* (The shoes are ours.)

## Possessive Pronouns with Prepositions

Prepositions that end in a vowel often merge with a following possessive pronoun.

| | `de` (of) | `do` (to) | `faoi` (under) | `i` (in) | `le` (with) | `ó` (from) | `trí` (through) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **+ mo** | `de mo` | `do mo` | `faoi mo` | `i mo` | `le mo` | `ó mo` | `trí mo` |
| **+ do** | `de do` | `do do` | `faoi do` | `i do` | `le do` | `ó do` | `trí do` |
| **+ a** (his/her) | **`dá`** | **`dá`, `á`** | **`faoina`** | **`ina`** | **`lena`** | **`óna`** | **`trína`** |
| **+ ár** | **`dár`** | **`dár`** | **`faoinár`** | **`inár`** | **`lenár`** | **`ónár`** | **`trínár`** |
| **+ bhur** | `de bhur` | `do bhur` | `faoi bhur` | **`in bhur`** | `le bhur` | `ó bhur` | `trí bhur` |
| **+ a** (their) | **`dá`** | **`dá`, `á`** | **`faoina`** | **`ina`** | **`lena`** | **`óna`** | **`trína`** |

### Notes on Prepositions

- **`do` (to)**: The form `á` is used instead of `dá` when it is the object of a verbal noun. *Tá mé á mholadh.* (I am praising him.)
- **`ar` (on)**: The form `arna` is used before verbal nouns to mean "after". *arna chur in eagar ag...* (edited by...). Otherwise, `ar a` is used.
- **Dialectal Forms**: In Munster, merged forms are common in the 1st and 2nd person singular: `im` for `i mo`, `lem` for `le mo`, `dom` for `do mo`, etc.

## Usage and Syntax

### Genitive of Personal Pronouns
Possessive pronouns often function as the genitive of personal pronouns.
- **Object of a verbal noun**: *Tá tú do mo bhualadh.* (You are hitting me.)
- **Passive meaning**: *Tá an teach á thógáil.* (The house is being built. lit: 'is the house to-its building'.)
- **Reflexive meaning**: *Tá tú do do bhualadh féin.* (You are hitting yourself.)
- **With compound prepositions**: *os mo chionn* (above me, lit: 'above my head').

### Other Common Uses
- **With verbs of state**: *Tá sé ina chónaí i gCorcaigh.* (He lives in Cork. lit: 'He is in his living in Cork'.)
- **Classification**: *Tá mé i mo dhochtúir.* (I am a doctor.)
- **Proleptic `a` (his)**: The pronoun `a` can anticipate a following noun or clause.
  - To mean "how": *a fheabhas atá sí* (how good she is).
  - With pronominal nouns: *a lán* (a lot), *a oiread* (as much).
  - To introduce a clause: *Tá a fhios agam go bhfuil tú anseo.* (I know that you are here.)

### When Not to Use Possessive Pronouns
In some cases, a different structure is required.
- **"One of my friends"**: Use a prepositional pronoun. *cara liom* (a friend of mine), *cara dá chuid* (a friend of his).
- **Direct Address (Vocative)**: Use the vocative particle `a`, not a possessive pronoun. *A chara!* (My friend!).
- **With Ordinals/Comparatives**: Use a construction with `ag`. *an mac is sine agam* (my eldest son), *an chéad mhac aige* (his first son).

## Alternative Possessive Construction

An alternative way to express possession uses the definite article, the noun, a demonstrative pronoun (usually `seo`), and a form of the preposition `ag`.

**Formula**: `an` + noun + `seo` + prepositional pronoun of `ag`

- *an teach seo agamsa* (this house of mine / my house)
- *an carr seo aige* (this car of his / his car)

This construction is often preferred for nouns like `tír` (country), `baile` (home/town), and `teach` (house).
- *an tír seo againne* (our country)

In Ulster, `seo` is often shortened to `s'`: *an teach s'agamsa*.
