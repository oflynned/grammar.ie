## Interrogative Words (`na míreanna ceisteacha`)

Interrogative words, or question words, are used to form questions. Most Irish interrogative words begin with the letter 'c', originating from the Indo-European root "kw-" (compare Irish `cad` with English *what* and Latin *quod*).

The core interrogative words are `cé`, `cá`, and `cad`. Other question words are often combinations of these with other words like nouns or prepositions (e.g., `conas` from `cá ionnas`). Their forms and usage can vary significantly between dialects.

### Syntax of Interrogative Words

Irish interrogative words contain an implied form of the copula. For example, `cé` means "who is", not just "who". This means that questions using these words are fundamentally copular sentences where the interrogative word is the predicate.

The rest of the question forms the subject. If this subject includes a verb, it must be in a relative clause.

-   *`Cé hé sin?`* = Who is that?
    (Literally: "Who-is he that?")
-   *`Cé a deir sin?`* = Who says that?
    (Literally: "Who-is [the one] who says that?")
-   *`Cad a rinne sé?`* = What did he do?
    (Literally: "What-is [the thing] that he did?")

### Indirect Questions

Interrogative words can introduce indirect questions, which are subordinate clauses that function as the object of verbs of asking, knowing, thinking, etc.

-   *`Níl a fhios agam cé hé an fear seo.`* = I don't know who this man is.
-   *`Ní raibh sé cinnte cad a déarfadh sé.`* = He wasn't sure what he would say.
-   *`Smaoinigh mé ar céard a déarfainn.`* = I thought about what I would say.

Unlike in English, Irish interrogative words are not used as general relative pronouns (like "the man who..."). Instead, Irish uses relative particles or phrases like `an rud`.

-   *`Thug mé dó a raibh uaidh.`* = I gave him everything he needed.
-   *`Thug sé dóibh an rud a bhí tuillte acu.`* = He gave them what they deserved.

### Exclamatory Sentences

Interrogative words can also be used in exclamations.

-   *`Cad é mar thubaiste!`* = What a disaster!
-   *`Cad é a mhinice…`* = How often…!
-   *`Cá hiontas!`* = What a wonder!

### `cé`

`Cé` primarily means "who" or "which". Older forms include `cia`. In Connacht, dialectal forms like `té`, `go cé`, or `go dé` can be heard.

#### Forms of `cé` with the Copula

| Context | Present | Present (before vowel) | Past | Past (before vowel) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple** | `cé` | `cé h` | `cér` | `cérbh` |
| **Direct Relative** | `cé is` | `cé is` | `cé ba` | `cé ab` |
| **Indirect Relative** | `cér`, `cé ar` | `cérb`, `cé arb` | `cér`, `cé ar` | `cérbh`, `cé arbh` |

#### `cé` with the Article `an`

| Context | Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **+ Singular Article** | `cén` | which |
| **+ Plural Article** | `cé na` | which |

#### Usage of `cé`

-   **who / whom**:
    -   *`Cé hé an cailín beag?`* = Who is the little girl?
    -   *`Cé atá ann?`* = Who is there?
    -   *`Cé leis é?`* = Whose is it? (To whom does it belong?)
    -   *`Cé as thú?`* = Where are you from?
    -   *`Cé aige a bhfuil an t-arán?`* = Who has the bread?
-   **what** (mainly in Connacht):
    -   *`Cé leis ar scríobh tú?`* = With what did you write?
    -   `cén ní` (`cé` + `an ní`) = what. *`Cén ní baisteadh?`* = What is baptism?
-   **which**:
    -   `cén` (`cé` + `an`): *`Cén fear é?`* = Which man is he? *`Cén t-am é?`* = What time is it?
    -   `cén` can also mean **whose**: *`Cén iníon í sin?`* = Whose daughter is she?
    -   `cén` can also mean **how much/many**: *`Cén bainne?`* = How much milk?
    -   With a superlative: *`Cé is mó, seo nó sin?`* = Which is bigger, this or that?
-   **who of / which of**:
    -   `cé againn/agaibh/acu`: *`Cé agaibh atá ann?`* = Who of you is there?
    -   `cé acu` is often used to mean "which", sometimes written `céacu` (Ulster) or pronounced `ceocu` (Munster).
    -   *`Cé acu fear atá ann?`* = Which man is there?
    -   *`Cé acu de na fir atá ann?`* = Which of the men is there?
-   **whose**:
    -   With an indirect relative clause: *`Cé a bhfuil a leabhar anseo?`* = Whose book is here?
    -   With `leis`: `cé leis`. *`Cé leis an leabhar seo?`* = Whose is this book?
-   **where / how** (in Connemara, often replacing `cá`):
    -   *`Cé raibh tú?`* = Where were you?
    -   `cé mhéad` = how much/many. *`Cé mhéad páiste atá agat?`* = How many children do you have?
-   **Other combinations**:
    -   `cé chomh` + adjective = how. *`Cé chomh mór leis an iasc?`* = How big is the fish?
    -   `cén fáth` = why.
    -   `cén uair` / `cén t-am` = when.
    -   `cén áit` = where.
    -   `cibé` / `pé` / `cé ar bith` = whoever/whatever.

The conjunction `cé go` (although) is likely of a different origin.

### `cá`

`Cá` is a general interrogative adjective meaning "which" or "how". It is usually pronounced short.

#### Forms of `cá` with the Copula

| Context | Present | Present (before vowel) | Past | Past (before vowel) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple** | `cá` | `cá h` / `c’` | `cár` | `cárbh` |
| **Indirect Relative** | `cár` | `cárb` | `cár` | `cárbh` |

#### `cá` with the Indirect Relative Particle (meaning "where")

| Tense | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Present etc.** | `cá` |
| **Past** | `cár` |

#### Usage of `cá`

-   **which** (causes h-prefix before a vowel):
    -   *`Cá haois é?`* = What age is he?
    -   *`C’ainm atá ort?`* = What is your name? (Ulster `c’` for `cá h`)
-   **how**:
    -   With a gradable abstract noun (causes lenition): `cá mhéad` (how much/many), `cá fhad` (how long), `cá mhinice` (how often).
    -   *`Cá mhéad duine?`* = How many people?
    -   With an adjective (no mutation): *`Cá hard é?`* = How high is it?
-   **where / to where** (causes eclipsis in present, lenition in past):
    -   This is a combination of `cá` + an indirect relative particle.
    -   *`Cá bhfuil tú?`* = Where are you? (`cá bhfuil` is often contracted to `cáil`).
    -   *`Cár chuir tú é?`* = Where did you put it?
-   **from where**:
    -   `cá has` / `cárb as`: *`Cárb as dó?`* = Where is he from?
-   **what** (in Ulster, no mutation):
    -   With a prepositional pronoun: *`Cá leis?`* = With what?
-   **Other combinations**:
    -   `cá háit` = where.
    -   `cá huair` / `cá ham` = when.
    -   `cá fhad ó` = since when. *`Cáid ó tháinig tú?`* = How long is it since you came?
    -   `cá fhad go` = when (in the future). *`Cáide go dtiocfaidh an bus?`* = When will the bus come?
    -   `cumá` / `camá` = why (in negative questions). *`Cumá nach suíonn tú?`* = Why don't you sit down?
    -   *`Cá bhfios dom?`* = How should I know?

### `cad`

`Cad` means "what". It is most common in Munster. In Ulster, `cad é` (often `caidé` or `goidé`) is always used.

#### Usage of `cad`

-   **what**:
    -   In copular sentences: *`Cad é do bharúil?`* = What is your opinion? *`Cad é seo?`* = What is this?
    -   With a direct relative clause: *`Cad a dhéanann tú?`* = What are you doing?
    -   With prepositional pronouns and an indirect relative clause: `cad air` (on what), `cad leis` (with what). *`Cad air ar chuir tú é?`* = On what did you put it?
-   **which**:
    -   `cad é an` / `cad í an` / `cad iad na`: *`Cad é an t-am é?`* = What time is it?
-   **from where**:
    -   `cad as`: *`Cad as é?`* = Where is he from?
-   **how**:
    -   `cad é mar` (Ulster: `goidé mar`): *`Goidé mar atá tú?`* = How are you?
    -   `cad é chomh` + adjective: *`Is cuma cad é chomh maith is a bheadh sé.`* = It doesn't matter how good he would be.

### `céard`

`Céard` is the common word for "what" in Connacht, a contraction of `cad é rud` ("what is the thing").

-   *`Céard atá ann?`* = What's up?

Dialectal variants include `go déard` and `téard`.

### `conas`

`Conas` means "how" and is common in Munster and the standard language. It derives from `cá ionnas` ("which way").

-   **how** (in what way), with a direct relative clause:
    -   *`Conas atá tú?`* = How are you?
-   **how** (how is it that), with a `go`-clause:
    -   *`Conas go ndéarfadh sé a leithéid?`* = How could he say such a thing?

### `cathain`

`Cathain` means "when" and is mainly used in Munster. It derives from `cá tan` ("which time").

-   *`Cathain a tharla sé?`* = When did it happen?

### `an`

The particle `an` is used for yes/no questions, but in some combinations it forms questions meaning "how". This `an` is the interrogative form of the copula.

| Tense | Positive | Mutation | Negative | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present etc.** | `an` | Eclipsis | `nach` | Eclipsis |
| **Past** | `ar` | Lenition | `nár` | Lenition |

-   `an mó` = how many (Munster). *`An mó duine atá ann?`* = How many people are there?
-   `an mór` = how much. *`An mór é?`* = How much is it?
-   `an fada` = how long. *`An fada a bhí sé anseo?`* = How long was he here?
-   `an minic` = how often.

### Interrogative Words with Prepositions

These forms, also called interrogative adverbial pronouns, combine `cé`, `cá`, or `cad` with the 3rd person singular masculine form of a prepositional pronoun. They require an indirect relative clause.

| Preposition | `cé +` | `cá +` | `cad +` | Meaning (examples) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ag` | `cé aige` | `cá haige` | `cad aige` | at whom/what (who has) |
| `ar` | `cé air` | `cá hair` | `cad air` | on whom/what |
| `as` | `cé as` | `cá has` | `cad as` | from whom/what (where from) |
| `chuig` | `cé chuige` | `cá chuige` | `cad chuige` | to whom/what (why) |
| `de` | `cé de` | `cá de` | `cad de` | of whom/what |
| `do` | `cé dó` | `cá dó` | `cad dó` | to whom/what |
| `faoi` | `cé faoi` | `cá faoi` | `cad faoi` | under/about whom/what |
| `i` | `cé ann` | `cá hann` | `cad ann` | in whom/what |
| `le` | `cé leis` | `cá leis` | `cad leis` | with whom/what (whose) |
| `ó` | `cé uaidh` | `cá huaidh` | `cad uaidh` | from whom/what |

-   `cé` can refer to people or things.
-   `cá` and `cad` refer only to things.
-   Forms with `cá` are typical of Ulster; forms with `cad` are common in Munster.
-   *`Cad air a bhfuil siad ag breathnú?`* = What are they looking at?
-   *`Cé leis a bhfuil tú ag caint?`* = With whom are you speaking?

For compound prepositions, the 3rd person masculine possessive pronoun `a` (his) is used.

-   `i dtaobh` (concerning) → `cad ina thaobh` (concerning what, why?)
-   `ar son` (because of) → `cad ar a shon` (for what reason?)

## Demonstrative Pronouns (`na forainmneacha taispeántacha`)

The demonstrative pronouns are `seo` (this), `sin` (that), and `siúd` (that yonder).

### Proximal and Distal Deixis

Irish has a three-way distinction for pointing things out:

-   `seo`: Near the speaker (1st person deixis).
-   `sin`: Near the person being spoken to (2nd person deixis), or generally distant from the speaker. It can also refer to something previously mentioned.
-   `siúd` / `úd`: Far from both speaker and listener (3rd person deixis). It implies greater distance, either physical or emotional.

In Munster, `so`, `san`, and `súd` are common after broad consonants.

### Standalone Demonstrative Pronouns

`seo`, `sin`, and `siúd` can stand alone to refer to things or situations, but not living beings.

-   *`Tabhair dom seo!`* = Give me this!
-   *`Rinne mé sin.`* = I did that.

### Adjectival Demonstrative Pronouns

To modify a noun ("this house", "that girl"), the structure `an` + noun + `seo/sin/úd` is used.

-   *`an teach seo`* = this house
-   *`an cailín sin`* = that girl
-   *`an crann úd`* = that tree yonder

### Demonstrative Pronouns with Personal Pronouns

These forms are more specific and can refer to people.

-   `é/í/iad seo` = this one / these
-   `é/í/iad sin` = that one / those
-   `é/í/iad siúd` = that one yonder / those yonder
-   *`Is é seo a rinne é.`* = This one did it.
-   *`Tá sí sin go maith.`* = That one is good.

In certain copular sentences, the order is inverted: `Seo é an leabhar` (This is the book).

### Demonstrative Pronouns with Possessive Pronouns

This construction indicates possession, corresponding to "whose" or "of which". It is only used with the 3rd person possessive `a`.

-   `a theach seo` = whose house (this one's house)
-   `a theach sin` = whose house (that one's house)
-   `a dteach siúd` = their house (those ones' house)

### Demonstrative Pronouns with Prepositions

These are formed using the 3rd person forms of prepositional pronouns.

-   `air seo` = on this
-   `leis sin` = with that
-   `ina dhiaidh sin` = after that
-   `dá bharr sin` = as a result of that

### Demonstrative Adverbs

These are formed with the prefix `an-` (from `ann`, "in it").

-   `anseo` = here (*`Táim anseo.`* = I am here.)
-   `ansin` = there (*`Bhíomar inár gcónaí ansin.`* = We lived there.)
-   `ansiúd` = over there

## Relative Particles and Pronouns (`na míreanna coibhneasta`)

Relative clauses modify a noun (the "antecedent"). Irish primarily uses particles, not pronouns, to introduce them.

### The Direct Relative Particle

The direct relative particle `a` (positive) or `nach`/`nár` (negative) is used when the antecedent is the subject or direct object of the relative clause.

| Tense | Positive | Mutation | Negative | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present etc.** | `a` | Lenition | `nach` | Eclipsis |
| **Past** | `a` | Lenition | `nár` | Lenition |

-   *`An fear a chonaic mé.`* = The man that I saw.
-   *`An scéal nach raibh go maith.`* = The story that was not good.

> **Note on the origin of `a`**: The direct relative particle is a relatively new development. Historically, only the lenition of the verb indicated a relative clause. The particle `a` evolved from the old past tense particle `do`, which was reinterpreted as a relative marker.

### The Indirect Relative Particle

The indirect relative particle is used when the antecedent has a different role, such as the object of a preposition or a possessor. The relationship is shown by a "resumptive" or "reflexive" pronoun later in the clause (e.g., a prepositional pronoun or possessive pronoun).

| Tense | Positive | Mutation | Negative | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present etc.** | `a` | Eclipsis | `nach` | Eclipsis |
| **Past** | `ar` | Lenition | `nár` | Lenition |

-   *`an teach a bhfuil mé ann`* = the house in which I am (reflexive pronoun: `ann`)
-   *`an fear a bhfuil a mhac tinn`* = the man whose son is ill (reflexive pronoun: `a`)

In Munster, `go`/`gur` is often used instead of `a`/`ar`.

### The Reflexive Relative Pronoun

Instead of placing a prepositional pronoun at the end of the clause, the preposition can combine directly with the relative particle at the beginning. This form acts as a true relative pronoun.

| Preposition | with `a` | with `ar` | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `le` | `lena` | `lenar` | with which |
| `de` | `dá` | `dár` | of which |
| `i` | `ina` | `inar` | in which |
| `ó` | `óna` | `ónar` | from which |

-   *`an bord ar a bhfuil an leabhar`* = the table on which the book is.
-   *`an teach ina bhfuil mé`* = the house in which I am.

### The Generalising Relative Pronoun

The particle `a` (present) or `ar` (past) can also mean "all that" or "that which" when there is no antecedent. It requires the dependent verb form.

| Tense | Positive | Mutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present etc.** | `a` | Eclipsis |
| **Past** | `ar` | Lenition |

-   *`Chonaic mé a raibh anseo.`* = I saw all that was here.
-   *`Tabhair leat a bhfuil uait.`* = Take what you need.
-   It combines with prepositions: `dá` (`de` + `a`) means "of all that/who".
    -   *`an fear is cliste dá bhfuil ann`* = the cleverest man of all who exist.

## Other Pronouns

### Determinative Pronouns

These are pronouns that are typically followed by a relative clause.

-   **the one (who/which)**: `an té`.
    -   *`Chonaic mé an té a dúirt é.`* = I saw the one who said it.
    -   *`An té nach bhfuil láidir, ní foláir dó bheith glic.`* = Whoever is not strong must be clever.
-   **those (who/which)**: `na daoine`, `an mhuintir`.
-   **that (which)**: `séard` / `éard` (Connacht), `siod` (Ulster), `é an rud` (Munster). These are used to introduce cleft sentences.
    -   *`Séard a chonaic mé ná capall.`* = What I saw was a horse.
-   **(something/that) which**: `an rud a...` or the generalising relative pronoun `a...`.
    -   *`Dúirt sé liom é a dhíol, rud a rinne mé.`* = He told me to sell it, which I did.
-   **whoever/whatever**: `pé` / `cibé` (+ noun) + relative clause.
    -   *`Cibé mná a bhí ann...`* = Whatever women were there...
    -   `cibé ar bith` / `pé ar bith` = whoever/whatever.

### Indefinite and Other Pronouns

Irish often uses nouns like `duine` (person) or `rud` (thing) combined with adjectives to form indefinite pronouns.

> **Note on Negative Polarity Items**: Words like `aon` and `ar bith` are "negative polarity items". They are used in negative sentences, questions, and conditional clauses to mean "any". In negative sentences, they correspond to English "no" or "none". For example, *`Níl aon duine ann`* means "There isn't anyone there" (i.e., "There is no one there").

-   **(Any)one, someone, something**: `duine`, `rud`, `ní`. In negative/question contexts: `aon duine` (`éinne`), `duine ar bith`, `aon rud`.
-   **Some, a few**: `cúpla` (+ singular noun), `roinnt`, `cuid`.
-   **Certain, specific**: `áirithe` (e.g., `duine áirithe`).
-   **Every, each**: `gach duine`, `gach rud`. `cách` (everyone).
-   **Other**: `eile` (e.g., `duine eile`). `a mhalairt` (a different one).
-   **Same**: `céanna` (e.g., `an lá céanna`).
-   **All, everything**: `an t-iomlán`, `gach uile rud`, `an méid`.
-   **Both**: `an bheirt`, `(siad) araon`, `(siad) beirt`.
-   **No one, none**: `aon duine`, `duine ar bith` (in negative sentences).
-   **Nothing**: `aon rud`, `rud ar bith`, `dada`, `faic` (in negative sentences). `neamhní` is a true negative pronoun.
-   **Such, such a thing**: `a leithéid`. *`Ní chuala mé riamh a leithéid.`* = I have never heard such a thing.
-   **So much**: `a oiread`, `an oiread sin`.
-   **Much/many, more**: `a lán`, `mórán`, `go leor`, `tuilleadh` (more), `níos mó` (more).
-   **Most**: `formhór`, `an chuid is mó`.
-   **Enough**: `go leor`, `dóthain`, `sáith`.
-   **One (impersonal)**: There is no single word. The autonomous verb form is used (`déantar` = one does), or sometimes the 2nd person (`déarfá` = one would say).

## The Pronoun `féin`

`Féin` means "self" and is always used in apposition to another noun or pronoun.

### Retrospective Pronoun

`Féin` is used for emphasis, similar to "myself", "himself", etc.

-   **Emphasis**: *`Rinne sé féin é.`* = He did it himself.
-   **"Own"**: *`mo theach féin`* = my own house.
-   **"By itself"**: *`doras a dhruideann uaidh féin`* = a door that closes by itself.

### Reflexive Pronoun

Combined with personal or prepositional pronouns, `féin` forms the reflexive pronoun.

-   `mé féin` = myself
-   `é féin` = himself
-   `air féin` = on himself
-   *`Ghortaigh sé é féin.`* = He hurt himself.

### The Adverb `féin`

`Féin` can also act as an adverb meaning "even".

-   *`Bhí Pól féin ann.`* = Even Paul was there.
-   *`Ní raibh Pól féin ann.`* = Not even Paul was there.
-   `mar sin féin` = nevertheless, anyway.

To distinguish the pronoun from the adverb, an extra personal pronoun is sometimes inserted: *`Bhí Pól é féin ann`* (Paul himself was there) vs. *`Bhí Pól féin ann`* (Even Paul was there).

## The Reciprocal Pronoun (`a chéile`)

`A chéile` means "each other" or "one another".

-   *`Chonaic siad a chéile.`* = They saw each other.

It combines with prepositions, often with a figurative meaning.

-   `le chéile` = together
-   `ó chéile` = apart, from each other
-   `trína chéile` = confused, mixed up
-   `i ndiaidh a chéile` = one after another
-   `mar a chéile` = the same, alike

## Other Particles

### The Vocative Particle (`a`)

The particle `a` is used before a noun in the vocative case to indicate direct address. It causes lenition.

-   *`A Sheáin!`* = Seán!
-   *`A Mháire!`* = Máire!
-   *`A dhaoine uaisle!`* = Ladies and gentlemen!

Although often unstressed or silent in speech (especially before vowels), the lenition it causes remains.

### The Numeral Particle (`a`)

The particle `a` is used before cardinal numbers when they are not followed by a noun. It causes an h-prefix before numbers starting with a vowel.

-   **Counting**: *`a haon, a dó, a trí...`* = one, two, three...
-   **Telling time**: *`a haon a chlog`* = one o'clock.
-   **Numbering**: *`Bus a haon`* = Bus No. 1.
-   **Titles**: *`Eoin Pól a Dó`* = John Paul II.
