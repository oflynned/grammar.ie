## Overview

Irish has a **definite article** (`an t-alt cinnte`), which corresponds to "the" in English. Its main forms are `an` and `na`.

There is no **indefinite article** (`an t-alt éiginnte`) in Irish. The bare noun is used to mean "a" or "an".
- *fear* - a man
- *an fear* - the man

The definite article is crucial because it changes form and causes initial mutations on the following noun depending on the noun's gender, case, and number.

## Forms and Mutations

The article has two basic forms: `an` in the singular and `na` in the plural. The dual form (used with the number two) is also `an`. The main complexity lies in the initial mutations (lenition, eclipsis) and prefixes (t-, n-, h-) that the article triggers.

The following tables show the form of the article and the mutation it causes in the nominative and genitive cases. The dative case is handled separately under [The Article with Prepositions](#the-article-with-prepositions).

### Masculine Nouns

| Case | Initial Sound of Noun | Article + Mutation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nom. Sg.** | Vowel | `an t-` | *an t-éan* (the bird) |
| | `s` + vowel/l/n/r | `an t-` | *an sagart* > *an tsagart* (the priest) |
| | `d`, `t` | `an` (no mutation) | *an teach* (the house) |
| | Other consonant | `an` (no mutation) | *an fear* (the man) |
| **Gen. Sg.** | Vowel | `an` (no mutation) | *an éin* (of the bird) |
| | `s` + vowel/l/n/r | `an t-` | *an tsagairt* (of the priest) |
| | `d`, `t` | `an` (no mutation) | *an tí* (of the house) |
| | Other consonant | `an` + Lenition | *an fhir* (of the man) |
| **Plural** | Vowel | `na h-` (Nom), `na n-` (Gen) | *na héin* (the birds), *na n-éan* (of the birds) |
| | `s` | `na` (no mutation) | *na sagairt* (the priests/of the priests) |
| | Other consonant | `na` (Nom), `na` + Eclipsis (Gen) | *na fir* (the men), *na bhfear* (of the men) |

### Feminine Nouns

| Case | Initial Sound of Noun | Article + Mutation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nom. Sg.** | Vowel | `an` (no mutation) | *an áit* (the place) |
| | `s` + vowel/l/n/r | `an t-` | *an tsúil* (the eye) |
| | `d`, `t` | `an` (no mutation) | *an deoch* (the drink) |
| | Other consonant | `an` + Lenition | *an bhean* (the woman) |
| **Gen. Sg.** | Vowel | `na h-` | *na háite* (of the place) |
| | `s` | `na` (no mutation) | *na súile* (of the eye) |
| | Other consonant | `na` (no mutation) | *na mná* (of the woman) |
| **Plural** | Vowel | `na h-` (Nom), `na n-` (Gen) | *na háiteacha* (the places/of the places) |
| | `s` | `na` (no mutation) | *na súile* (the eyes/of the eyes) |
| | Other consonant | `na` (Nom), `na` + Eclipsis (Gen) | *na mná* (the women), *na mban* (of the women) |

### Notes on Mutations

- **The Dentals Rule**: Lenition does not occur when the consonants `d`, `n`, `t`, `l`, `s` appear next to each other. This is why `d` and `t` are not lenited after the `n` of `an`.
- **The t-prefix before `s`**: The t-prefix is added before `s` only when it is followed by a vowel, or by `l`, `n`, or `r`. It is not added if another consonant follows the `s`.
  - *an tsláinte* (the health)
  - *an scian* (the knife)

## The Article with Numbers

The form of the article and the mutations it causes can change when used with numbers.

### Cardinal Numbers

- **1 (`aon`) and 11 (`aon déag`)**: The singular article is used. A t-prefix appears before `aon` in the nominative for both genders: `an t-aon`.
  - *an t-aon fhear amháin* (the one man)
  - *an t-aon bhean amháin* (the one woman)
  - Genitive masculine: `an aon fhir amháin` (of the one man)
  - Genitive feminine: `na haon mhná amháin` (of the one woman)

- **2 (`dhá`) and 12 (`dhá déag`)**: The dual article `an` is used. The numeral `dhá` becomes `dá`. The article form does not change for case or gender.
  - *an dá bhád* (the two boats)
  - *clann an dá dheirfiúr* (the children of the two sisters)

- **3–10 and 13–19**: The plural article `na` is used.
  - **Nominative**: `na trí bhád` (the three boats), `na hocht mbád` (the eight boats).
  - **Genitive**: The article causes eclipsis of the numeral: `na dtrí bhád` (of the three boats), `na n-ocht mbád` (of the eight boats).

- **20, 30, 40... (`fiche`, `tríocha`, etc.)**: These are treated as masculine singular nouns. The article is `an`.
  - *an fiche bád* (the twenty boats)
  - Genitive: The numeral is lenited: `an fhiche bád` (of the twenty boats).
  - Before `ochtó` (80), a t-prefix appears in the nominative: `an t-ochtó bád`.

### Ordinal Numbers

Ordinal numbers are usually preceded by the article. The entire phrase (article + ordinal + noun) is typically treated as masculine, except for `chéad` (first).

- **Nominative**: A t-prefix is always used before ordinals beginning with a vowel, regardless of the noun's gender.
  - *an t-ochtú banríon* (the eighth queen)
  - *an t-aonú haithne déag* (the eleventh commandment)
- **Genitive**: The phrase is usually treated as masculine. The article is `an`, and it lenites the ordinal number.
  - *an ochtú bád* (of the eighth boat) becomes *an ochtú báid*.
  - *an chúigiú haithne* (of the fifth commandment)
- **First (`chéad`)**: `chéad` is always lenited after the article, as is the following noun. The phrase follows the gender of the noun.
  - *an chéad bhád* (the first boat)
  - *an chéad bhean* (the first woman)
  - Genitive masculine: *an chéad bháid* (of the first boat)
  - Genitive feminine: *na chéad mhná* (of the first woman)

## The Article with Prepositions

When a preposition is followed by the article, two things can happen:
1.  The preposition and article merge into a single word (e.g., `de` + `an` → `den`).
2.  The preposition takes a special form (e.g., `le` becomes `leis` before `an`).

The mutation that follows depends on the preposition and, significantly, on the dialect. In the dative case (after most simple prepositions), masculine nouns that take `t-` in the nominative (`an t-úll`) lose it (`ar an úll`).

### Standard Forms and Mutations

| Preposition | Form with `an` (sg.) | Mutation (sg.) | Form with `na` (pl.) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ag` | `ag an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `ag na` |
| `ar` | `ar an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `ar na` |
| `as` | `as an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `as na` |
| `chuig` | `chuig an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `chuig na` |
| `de` | `den` | Lenition | `de na` |
| `do` | `don` | Lenition | `do na` |
| `faoi` | `faoin` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `faoi na` |
| `i` | `sa` / `san`\* | Lenition | `sna` |
| `le` | `leis an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `leis na` |
| `ó` | `ón` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `ó na` |
| `roimh` | `roimh an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `roimh na` |
| `trí` | `tríd an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `trí na` |
| `thar` | `thar an` | Eclipsis or Lenition | `thar na` |

\*`san` is used before a vowel or `fh-`.

### Dialectal Variations in Mutations

The choice between lenition and eclipsis after a preposition + article is a major point of dialectal variation.

- **Standard Irish**: For most prepositions, either lenition or eclipsis is acceptable. However, `den`, `don`, and `sa(n)` always cause lenition.
- **Ulster**: Lenition is generally preferred.
  - *ag an fhear* (at the man)
- **Munster**: Eclipsis is generally preferred, including for `d` and `t`.
  - *ag an bhfear* (at the man)
  - *as an dteach* (out of the house)
- **Connacht**: The system is mixed. Eclipsis is common in many areas, but `d` and `t` are often not eclipsed.
  - *ag an bhfear* (at the man)
  - *as an teach* (out of the house)

### Prepositions without Dative Mutations

A few prepositions are followed by the nominative case, so the noun mutates as it would after the simple article in the nominative.

| Preposition | + Singular Article | + Plural Article |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `gan` | `gan an` | `gan na` |
| `go dtí` | `go dtí an` | `go dtí na` |
| `idir` | `idir an` | `idir na` |
| `mar` | `mar an` | `mar na` |

## The Article with `cé`

The interrogative `cé` (which) merges with the singular article `an`.

- `cé` + `an` → `cén`
  - *Cén fear?* (Which man?)
- `cé` + `na` → `cé na` (no merger)
  - *Cé na fir?* (Which men?)

## Pronunciation

The vowel `a` in `an` and `na` is an unstressed schwa sound, [ə].

- **`an`**:
  - Before a consonant, the final `-n` is often dropped: *an fear* is pronounced [ə f′ar].
  - Before a vowel, the `-n` is pronounced: *an obair* is pronounced [ən obər′].
  - The sound of `-n` often assimilates to the following consonant, becoming [m] before `b` or `p`, and [ŋ] before `g` or `c`.
- **`na`**:
  - Pronounced [nə]. *na héisc* is pronounced [nə he:s′k′].

## Special Uses of the Article

The article is used in some contexts where it is not used in English.

- **With demonstratives**: The article is required with `seo`, `sin`, and `úd`.
  - *an teach seo* (this house)
- **With genitive attributes**: If a noun is defined by another definite noun in the genitive, only the second noun takes the article.
  - *teach an dochtúra* (the doctor's house)
  - *fear na mná móire* (the man of the tall woman)
- **With titles**:
  - *an Dochtúir Ó Murchú* (Dr. Murphy)
- **With country and place names**:
  - *an Fhrainc* (France), *an Ghearmáin* (Germany), *an tSionainn* (the Shannon)
- **With abstract and generic nouns**:
  - *an tsláinte* (health), *an bhochtaineacht* (poverty)
  - *an duine* (man, mankind), *an leon* (the lion, as a species)
- **With seasons, days, and months**:
  - *an tEarrach* (Spring), *an Luan* (Monday), *an Feabhra* (February)
- **To mean "per"**:
  - *deich bpunt an ceann* (ten pounds per head)
