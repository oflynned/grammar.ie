Initial mutations are sound changes that affect the first letter of a word. They are a fundamental part of Irish grammar, triggered by the word that comes immediately before. The two most common mutations are lenition (`séimhiú`), which typically softens a consonant, and eclipsis (`urú`), which replaces the original consonant sound with a new one. Understanding when to use them is essential for speaking and writing Irish correctly.

The following table shows how the initial consonants of a word change when lenition or eclipsis is applied.

| Consonant | After Lenition | After Eclipsis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `b` | `bh` | `mb` |
| `c` | `ch` | `gC` |
| `d` | `dh` | `nD` |
| `f` | `fh` | `bhF` |
| `g` | `gh` | `nG` |
| `m` | `mh` | – |
| `p` | `ph` | `bP` |
| `s` | `sh` | – |
| `t` | `th` | `dT` |

The consonants `l`, `n`, and `r` are never affected by lenition or eclipsis.

## Lenition (An Séimhiú)

Lenition, known in Irish as `séimhiú`, is a "softening" of a consonant's sound. In writing, it is marked by adding an `h` after the initial consonant. For example, `b` becomes `bh`, and `c` becomes `ch`.

### Common Triggers for Lenition

Here are some of the most common situations that cause lenition.

#### The Definite Article `an`

Lenition occurs after the singular definite article `an` when it is followed by a feminine noun in the nominative case.

*   *an bhean* - the woman (from `bean`, a feminine noun)
*   *an chathair* - the city (from `cathair`, a feminine noun)

<Warning>
Masculine nouns are not lenited after `an` in the nominative case. For example, *an fear* (the man) does not change.
</Warning>

#### Possessive Pronouns

The possessive pronouns `mo` (my), `do` (your, singular), and `a` (his) all cause lenition.

*   *mo chat* - my cat (from `cat`)
*   *do theach* - your house (from `teach`)
*   *a mhadra* - his dog (from `madra`)

#### Certain Prepositions

A number of simple prepositions cause lenition on the following noun. These include `ar` (on), `de` (from/off), `do` (to/for), `faoi` (under), `ó` (from), `roimh` (before), and `trí` (through).

*   *ar bhord* - on a table (from `bord`)
*   *ó Chorcaigh* - from Cork (from `Corcaigh`)

#### Numbers

The numbers `aon` (one) and `dhá` (two) cause lenition.

*   *aon teach amháin* - one house (from `teach`)
*   *dhá chat* - two cats (from `cat`)

#### The Vocative Case

The vocative particle `a`, used for direct address, causes lenition.

*   *A Sheáin!* - Hello, Seán! (from `Seán`)
*   *A Mháire!* - Hello, Máire! (from `Máire`)

<Warning>
The consonant clusters `sc`, `sm`, `sp`, and `st` at the beginning of a word are never lenited. For example, `mo scéal` (my story), not `mo shcéal`.
</Warning>

## Eclipsis (An tUrú)

Eclipsis, known in Irish as `urú`, involves "eclipsing" or covering the original initial sound of a word with a new sound. In writing, a new letter is placed before the original initial consonant. For example, `b` becomes `mb`, and `c` becomes `gC`.

### Common Triggers for Eclipsis

Here are some of the most common situations that cause eclipsis.

#### The Preposition `i`

The preposition `i` (in) always causes eclipsis.

*   *i mBaile Átha Cliath* - in Dublin (from `Baile Átha Cliath`)
*   *i nGaillimh* - in Galway (from `Gaillimh`)

#### Possessive Pronouns

The possessive pronouns `ár` (our), `bhur` (your, plural), and `a` (their) all cause eclipsis.

*   *ár gcarr* - our car (from `carr`)
*   *bhur dteach* - your house (from `teach`)
*   *a mbád* - their boat (from `bád`)

<Tip>
The word `a` can mean "his", "her", or "their". The mutation it causes is the only way to tell them apart:
- `a chat` (lenition) means "his cat"
- `a cat` (no mutation) means "her cat"
- `a gcat` (eclipsis) means "their cat"
</Tip>

#### Numbers 7–10

The numbers `seacht` (seven), `ocht` (eight), `naoi` (nine), and `deich` (ten) cause eclipsis.

*   *seacht gcailín* - seven girls (from `cailín`)
*   *deich mbád* - ten boats (from `bád`)

#### After Certain Prepositions and the Article

When the definite article `an` follows certain prepositions, it causes eclipsis on the noun. This happens with prepositions like `ar an`, `leis an`, `faoin`, `ón`, and `tríd an`.

*   *ar an mbord* - on the table (from `bord`)
*   *leis an bhfear* - with the man (from `fear`)

### Eclipsis of Vowels

When a word beginning with a vowel is eclipsed, an `n-` is prefixed to it.

*   *i nÉirinn* - in Ireland (from `Éirinn`)
*   *ár n-athair* - our father (from `athair`)

## Other Initial Changes

Besides lenition and eclipsis, there are two other common changes to the start of a word: the h-prefix and the t-prefix.

### The H-Prefix

An `h` is placed before a vowel in certain situations. This is sometimes considered a type of lenition but is often treated as a separate rule. A common trigger is the possessive pronoun `a` (her).

*   *a hainm* - her name (from `ainm`)

It also occurs with the numbers three to six (`trí`, `ceithre`, `cúig`, `sé`).

*   *trí húll* - three apples (from `úll`)

### The T-Prefix

A `t-` is prefixed to masculine singular nouns that begin with a vowel after the definite article `an`.

*   *an t-uisce* - the water (from `uisce`)
*   *an t-arán* - the bread (from `arán`)

A `t-` is also prefixed to feminine nouns beginning with `s` after `an` in the nominative case. This `t-` prevents the `s` from being lenited.

*   *an tsráid* - the street (from `sráid`)
