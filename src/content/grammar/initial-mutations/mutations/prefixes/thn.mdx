In addition to lenition (`séimhiú`) and eclipsis (`urú`), Irish uses three prefixes—`t`, `h`, and `n`—to change the initial sound of certain words, particularly those beginning with a vowel. These are sometimes considered a third type of initial mutation.

## The t-Prefix

The t-prefix is added to words beginning with a vowel or the letter `s`.

### Spelling and Pronunciation

-   **Before a vowel**: A hyphen is used, e.g., `t-a`, `t-e`.
    -   *an t-úll* (the apple)
    -   *an tÉireannach* (the Irishman)
-   **Before `s`**: The `t` is placed directly before the `s`, e.g., `ts`.
    -   *an tsráid* (the street)
    -   When capitalised, it becomes `tS`, e.g., `an tSráid`.
-   **Pronunciation**: When `t` is prefixed to `s`, the `s` sound is dropped.
    -   *an tsráid* is pronounced [an traːdʲ]

### Usage of the t-Prefix

The t-prefix is primarily associated with the definite article `an`. The rules for its use before vowels and before `s` are distinct.

-   **Before masculine nouns beginning with a vowel** after the article `an` in the nominative and accusative cases.
    -   *an t-úll* (the apple)
    -   *an t-uisce* (the water)
    -   It is not used in the genitive or dative: *craiceann an úill* (the skin of the apple), *ar an úll* (on the apple).

-   **Before nouns beginning with `s`** (followed by a vowel, `l`, `n`, or `r`) after the article `an`.
    -   **Nominative/Accusative (feminine nouns)**: *an tsráid* (the street).
    -   **Genitive (masculine nouns)**: *hata an tsagairt* (the priest's hat).
    -   **Dative (after prepositions + `an`)**: Usage varies by dialect.
        -   **Standard/Connacht**: Only with feminine nouns. *ar an tsráid* (on the street), but *ag an sagart* (at the priest's).
        -   **Ulster**: With both feminine and masculine nouns. *ag an tsagart* (at the priest's).
        -   **Munster**: With both, but only after the combined forms `den`, `don`, and `sa`. *don tsagart* (to the priest).

-   **Before numbers beginning with a vowel** after `an` in the nominative/accusative.
    -   *an t-aon bhád amháin* (the one boat)
    -   *an t-ochtú* (the eighth), *an t-ochtar* (the eight people)
    -   This does not apply in the dative: *ar an ochtar* (on the eight people).

-   **Before numbers beginning with `s`**: In the Standard language, no t-prefix is used in the genitive after `an`.
    -   *i dtús an seachtú haois déag* (at the beginning of the 17th century)
    -   An exception is made for the personal numbers `seisear` (six people) and `seachtar` (seven people): *i measc an tseachtair* (among the seven).

-   **Dialectal Usage**: In some dialects, a t-prefix appears before `s` after words ending in `-n` or `-l`, even when it is not the article.
    -   *an-tsean* (very old) (Standard: `an-sean`)
    -   *aon tsúil* (one eye) (Standard: `aon súil`)
    -   It also appears in some place names, such as *Cionn tSáile* (Kinsale).

> **Note**: The form `t'` before a vowel, as in *t'uncail* (your uncle), is a contraction of the possessive adjective `do` (your) and is not a t-prefix.

## The h-Prefix

The h-prefix is only used before words that begin with a vowel. Its main function is to separate two vowel sounds where neither lenition nor eclipsis applies.

### Spelling

The `h` is placed directly before the vowel, with no hyphen.
-   *a hainm* (her name)
-   *Poblacht na hÉireann* (Republic of Ireland)
-   When capitalised, it becomes `hA`, `hE`, etc.

### Usage of the h-Prefix

-   **Before nouns**:
    -   After the article `na`:
        -   In the plural (nominative, accusative, dative): *na héin* (the birds).
        -   In the genitive singular of feminine nouns: *praghas na hoifige* (the price of the office).
    -   After `a` (her): *a huncail* (her uncle). Also after `a dhá` (her two): *a dhá húll* (her two apples).
    -   After the numbers `trí`, `ceithre`, `cúig`, `sé` with plural nouns: *trí huaire* (three hours/times).
    -   After ordinal numbers (except `an chéad`): *an tríú húll* (the third apple).
    -   After non-leniting prepositions like `le` and `go`: *le hÚna* (with Úna), *go hÉirinn* (to Ireland).
    -   After `cá` (which): *cá háit?* (where? / which place?).
    -   In surnames with `Ó`: *Ó hEidhin*. The `h` disappears after leniting particles like `Ní`: *Ní Eidhin*.
    -   After the negative copula `ní` in certain fixed phrases: *Ní hiontas ar bith é sin.* (That is no wonder at all.)

-   **Before pronouns** (`é`, `í`, `iad`, `ea`, etc.):
    -   After the negative copula `ní`: *ní hé* (it is not he), *ní hea* (it is not so).
    -   After `cé` (who): *cé hé?* (who is he?).
    -   After `pé` (whoever): *pé hé tú féin* (whoever you are).

-   **Before adjectives and adverbs**:
    -   After the adverbial particle `go`: *go holc* (badly).
    -   After `chomh` (as/so): *chomh holc* (as bad).
    -   After the negative copula `ní` in certain phrases: *ní hionann iad* (they are not the same).

-   **Before verbs**:
    -   After the negative imperative particle `ná`: *Ná habair é!* (Don't say it!).
    -   In some dialects (especially Munster), after the negative interrogative particle `ná`: *Ná hólann tú é?* (Don't you drink it?).

-   **Before numerals**:
    -   After the counting particle `a`: *a haon* (one), *a hocht* (eight).

-   **In days of the week**:
    -   *Dé hAoine* (Friday).

## The n-Prefix

The n-prefix is used before vowel-initial words in contexts that require eclipsis.

### Spelling

A hyphen is used between the `n` and the vowel.
-   *n-a*, *n-e*, etc.
-   *ár n-athair* (our father)
-   When capitalised, it becomes `nA`, `nE`, etc., as in *Tír na nÓg* (Land of Youth).

### Usage of the n-Prefix

-   **In eclipsing contexts**: The n-prefix is the form eclipsis takes before a vowel.
    -   *ár n-uncail* (our uncle)
    -   *i gcaitheamh na n-aoiseanna* (throughout the ages)
    -   **Note**: If the eclipsing word already ends in `-n` (like the article `an`), the prefix is not written, as it is already present in the sound: *ar an úll* (on the apple), not `*ar an n-úll`.

-   **After the preposition `i`**: Before a vowel, `i` combines with the n-prefix to become `in`.
    -   *in Éirinn* (in Ireland)
    -   This is a modern spelling convention; older texts may show `i nÉirinn`.

-   **With verbal nouns (dialectal)**: In Connacht and Munster, `le` can mean "in order to" and causes an n-prefix on a following verbal noun.
    -   *Cad a bheidh agat le n-ól?* (What will you have to drink?)

## Other Prefixes and Contractions

Other single letters that appear before vowels are typically contractions of other words and are written with an apostrophe. They are not considered initial mutations.

-   **`d’`**: A contraction of the past tense particle `do`.
    -   *d’ól mé* (I drank)
-   **`d’`** or **`t’`**: A contraction of the possessive adjective `do` (your).
    -   *d’athair* or *t’athair* (your father)
-   **`m’`**: A contraction of the possessive adjective `mo` (my).
    -   *m’athair* (my father)
-   **`d’`**: A contraction of the prepositions `de` (from) or `do` (to).
    -   *d’Éirinn* (from/to Ireland)
-   **`b’`**: A contraction of the past/conditional copula form `ba`.
    -   *b’fhéidir* (perhaps, literally "it would be possible")
