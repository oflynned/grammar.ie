Eclipsis, known in Irish as `An tUrú`, is an initial mutation that changes the sound at the beginning of a word. It involves two main processes:

1.  **Voicing**: A voiceless consonant is replaced by its voiced equivalent. For example, `c` [k] becomes `gc` [g], and `p` [p] becomes `bp` [b].
2.  **Nasalisation**: A voiced consonant is replaced by its corresponding nasal sound. For example, `b` [b] becomes `mb` [m], and `d` [d] becomes `nd` [n].

Because of the second process, eclipsis is sometimes referred to as nasalisation.

## The Changes of Eclipsis

When a word is eclipsed, the original initial letter is kept in writing, but a new letter (or letters) is prefixed to it. In speech, only the sound of the new prefix is pronounced.

The following table shows which consonants are affected and how they change.

| Consonant | Eclipsed Form | Pronunciation |
| :-------- | :------------ | :------------ |
| `b`       | `mb`          | `m`           |
| `c`       | `gc`          | `g`           |
| `d`       | `nd`          | `n`           |
| `f`       | `bhf`         | `v` or `w`    |
| `g`       | `ng`          | `ng` (as in "song") |
| `p`       | `bp`          | `b`           |
| `t`       | `dt`          | `d`           |

The exact pronunciation of the eclipsed form depends on whether the original consonant was broad or slender. For example, `mb` is pronounced as a slender `m` before `i` or `e`, and a broad `m` otherwise.

The consonants `l`, `m`, `n`, `r`, and `s` are not affected by eclipsis.

When a word begins with a vowel, eclipsis is marked by prefixing `n-`.

- *`i nÉirinn`* - in Ireland

## Spelling and Capitalisation

In standard text, the eclipsing letter is written in lowercase before the original letter.

- *`ár gcarr`* - our car
- *`i dteach`* - in a house

If the word is capitalised, as in a title or a proper noun, the eclipsing letter remains lowercase while the original letter is capitalised.

- *`i gConnachta`* - in Connacht
- *`i mBéarla`* - in English
- *`ar an mBord`* - on the table

## When to Use Eclipsis

Eclipsis is triggered in a number of specific grammatical contexts.

### After Certain Prepositions

- **After `i` (in) without an article:**
  - *`i dteach`* - in a house
  - *`i mBaile Átha Cliath`* - in Dublin
- **After certain prepositions when followed by the singular article `an`:**
  - This rule applies after `ag`, `ar`, `as`, `le`, `mar`, `ó`, `faoi`, `roimh`, `trí`, `thar`, and `um`.
  - *`ar an mbord`* - on the table
  - *`leis an gcat`* - with the cat (`leis` is the form of `le` before the article)
  - **Dialect note:** This rule is applied consistently in Munster. In Connacht, it does not apply to `d` or `t`. In Ulster, lenition is generally used instead of eclipsis in this context.
- **After `sa` (in the):**
  - *`sa bhfarraige`* - in the sea
  - **Dialect note:** This is standard in Connacht. In Munster, it typically only applies to `f`. In Standard Irish and Ulster, lenition is used instead (*`san fharraige`*).

### After the Genitive Plural Article

Eclipsis occurs after the genitive plural article `na`.

- *`praghas na dtithe`* - the price of the houses
- *`ainmneacha na mban`* - the names of the women

### After Plural Possessive Adjectives

The plural possessive adjectives `ár` (our), `bhur` (your, pl.), and `a` (their) all cause eclipsis.

- *`ár dtír`* - our country
- *`bhur mbord`* - your table
- *`a gcarr`* - their car

### After Numbers 7 to 10

The numbers seven to ten (`seacht`, `ocht`, `naoi`, `deich`) cause eclipsis of the following noun.

- *`seacht mbád`* - seven boats
- *`ocht gcat`* - eight cats
- *`deich dteach`* - ten houses

### After Certain Verbal Particles and Conjunctions

Eclipsis is used on a verb following these particles:

- `an` (interrogative): *`An dtuigeann tú?`* - Do you understand?
- `go` (that): *`Dúirt sé go bhfuil sé anseo.`* - He said that he is here.
- `nach` (that...not / interrogative negative): *`Nach bhfaca tú é?`* - Did you not see it?
- `mura` (if...not): *`mura dtagann sé`* - if he doesn't come
- `dá` (if, in the conditional mood): *`dá mbeadh a fhios agam`* - if I knew
- `sula` (before): *`sula dtagann sé`* - before he comes

### After Relative Particles and Interrogatives

- **After the interrogative `cá` (where?):**
  - *`Cá bhfuil tú?`* - Where are you?
- **After the indirect relative particle `a`:**
  - *`an fear a dtugann tú an leabhar dó`* - the man to whom you give the book
- **After the generalising relative pronoun `a` (all that):**
  - *`a bhfaca mé`* - all that I saw

## Historical and Other Uses

Eclipsis also appears in some fixed forms and historical contexts.

- **After the prefix `éa-` (un-):** In Old Irish, this prefix caused eclipsis. In modern spelling, the original eclipsed letter is usually omitted entirely.
  - `éa-` + `cóir` → `éagóir` (injustice)
  - `éa-` + `trom` → `éadrom` (light, not heavy)
- **In place names:** Some place names retain eclipsis from a time when the preceding noun was of the neuter gender.
  - *`Loch nEachach`* - Lough Neagh
- **Historical spelling:** In older texts, eclipsis was sometimes written by doubling the eclipsed consonant (e.g., `cc` for `gc`, `tt` for `dt`).
  - *`go ffuil`* instead of modern *`go bhfuil`*
- **On adjectives:** It was formerly common to eclipse an adjective following an eclipsed noun in the genitive plural.
  - *`scéal na mban mbocht`* - the story of the poor women
