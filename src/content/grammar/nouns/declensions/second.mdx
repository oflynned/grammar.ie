The second declension is a large and important group of nouns in Irish. It consists almost entirely of feminine nouns, making it a key pattern to learn for correctly using adjectives and pronouns. These nouns can end in either a broad or a slender consonant.

This declension includes:

*   Almost exclusively feminine nouns. The only common masculine nouns in this group are `im` (butter), `sliabh` (mountain), and `teach` (house).
*   Nouns ending in either a broad or a slender consonant.
*   Many polysyllabic feminine nouns ending in `-(e)ach`, such as `báisteach` (rain).

## Genitive Singular

The genitive singular is formed by adding the ending `-e`. The way this happens depends on the final consonant of the noun.

### Nouns Ending in a Slender Consonant

If the noun already ends in a slender consonant, you simply add `-e`.

*   *dath na coille* - the colour of the forest (from `coill`)
*   *dath na súile* - the colour of the eye (from `súil`)

### Nouns Ending in a Broad Consonant

If the noun ends in a broad consonant, the consonant is first made slender (palatalised), and then `-e` is added. This often involves a vowel change in the final syllable.

*   *praghas na bróige* - the price of the shoe (from `bróg`)
*   *cnámh na láimhe* - the bone of the hand (from `lámh`)
*   *barr an tsléibhe* - the top of the mountain (from `sliabh`)

<Note>
The noun `scian` (knife) is slightly irregular. Its genitive is `scine`, where you might expect `*scéine`.
</Note>

### Disyllabic Nouns

In some two-syllable nouns, the second vowel is dropped (a process called syncope). This causes the consonant before it to become slender.

*   *deireadh na hoibre* - the end of the work (from `obair`)
*   *dath na seimre* - the colour of the clover (from `seamair`)

### Nouns Ending in `-(e)ach`

Polysyllabic nouns ending in `-(e)ach`, along with the masculine noun `teach`, form their genitive by adding `-(a)í`.

*   *solas na gealaí* - the light of the moon (from `gealach`)
*   *fuaim na báistí* - the sound of the rain (from `báisteach`)
*   *doras an tí* - the door of the house (from `teach`)

<Details summary="Historical note on the -(a)í ending">
The ending `-(a)í` is a modern spelling of the older form `-(a)ighe`. With the older spelling, these words followed the general rule of "make the consonant slender and add `-e`". For example, `teach` became `tighe` (now `tí`), and `gealach` became `gealaighe` (now `gealaí`).
</Details>

However, monosyllabic nouns ending in `-och`, `-each`, or `-uach` follow the standard pattern of making the final consonant slender and adding `-e`. This results in endings like `-oiche`, `-eiche`, and `-uaiche`.

## Dative Singular

Historically, nouns with a broad consonant formed a distinct dative singular by making the final consonant slender. Today, this form is rare in the standard language but is important for understanding other parts of the grammar.

In Standard Irish, a unique dative form is only consistently used for five common nouns:

*   `lámh` → `láimh` (hand)
*   `bos` → `bois` (palm)
*   `bróg` → `bróig` (shoe)
*   `cluas` → `cluais` (ear)
*   `cos` → `cois` (foot/leg)

*Chuir sé an fáinne ar a láimh.* - He put the ring on her hand.

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In the Connemara dialect, this palatalised dative form is often used as the standard nominative singular for many nouns, for example, `fuinneoig` instead of `fuinneog` (window).
</Dialect>

The dative singular form is identical to the nominative dual, which is used after the number two (`dhá`).

*   *dhá bhróig* - two shoes
*   *dhá láimh* - two hands

## Nominative Plural

Second declension nouns can have either "weak" or "strong" plurals.

### Weak Plurals

The most common plural ending is `-a` or `-e`.

*   If the noun ends in a broad consonant, `-a` is usually added.
    *   `long` → `longa` (ships)
    *   `póg` → `póga` (kisses)
    *   `cloch` → `clocha` (stones)
*   If the noun ends in a slender consonant, `-e` is usually added.
    *   `súil` → `súile` (eyes)
*   Some nouns ending in a slender consonant first make the consonant broad (depalatalise) and then add `-a`.
    *   `binn` → `beanna` (peaks)
    *   `deoir` → `deora` (tears)

### Strong Plurals

Less commonly, nouns form a strong plural with endings like `-anna`, `-acha`, `-ta`, or `-tha`.

*   `áit` → `áiteanna` (places)
*   `craobh` → `craobhacha` (branches)
*   `tonn` → `tonnta` (waves)
*   `tír` → `tíortha` (countries)

A regular pattern exists for polysyllabic nouns ending in a slender consonant: they form a strong plural with the ending `-í`.

*   `aimsir` → `aimsirí` (weathers, times)
*   `oifig` → `oifigí` (offices)
*   `abairt` → `abairtí` (sentences)

## Genitive Plural

The form of the genitive plural depends on whether the nominative plural is weak or strong.

### Nouns with Weak Plurals

For nouns with a weak plural, the genitive plural is identical to the nominative singular, but the final consonant is usually made broad (depalatalised) if it was slender.

*   *dath na súl* - the colour of the eyes (Nom. Sg: `súil`)
*   *fuaim na gceall* - the sound of the churches (Nom. Sg: `cill`)
*   *airde na mbeann* - the height of the peaks (Nom. Sg: `binn`)

### Nouns with Strong Plurals

For nouns with a strong plural, the genitive plural is identical to the nominative plural.

*   *liosta na n-áiteanna* - the list of the places (Nom. Pl: `áiteanna`)
*   *deireadh na n-oibreacha* - the end of the works (Nom. Pl: `oibreacha`)

## Summary of Patterns

### Declension Patterns

This table summarises the main patterns for nouns with weak plurals.

| Nom. Sg. Ending | Gen. Sg. | Dative Sg. | Nom. Pl. | Gen. Pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Broad consonant | Slender consonant + `-e` | Slender consonant | Broad consonant + `-a` | Broad consonant |
| Slender consonant | Slender consonant + `-e` | Slender consonant | Broad consonant + `-a` | Broad consonant |
| Slender consonant | Slender consonant + `-e` | Slender consonant | Slender consonant + `-e` | Broad consonant |

Polysyllabic nouns ending in a slender consonant often have a strong plural in `-í`, which is also used for the genitive plural.

### Common Endings

This table shows how nouns with certain common endings typically decline.

| Nom. Sg. | Gen. Sg. | Dative Sg. | Nom. Pl. | Gen. Pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-ach` | `-aí` | `-aigh` | `-acha` | `-ach` |
| `-each` | `-í` | `-igh` | `-eacha` | `-each` |
| `-óg` | `-óige` | `-óig` | `-óga` | `-óg` |
| `-eog` | `-eoige` | `-eoig` | `-eoga` | `-eog` |
| `-lann` | `-lainne` | `-lainn` | `-lanna` | `-lann` |
| `-óid` | `-óide` | `-óid` | `-óidí` (strong) | `-óidí` (strong) |
| `-ing` | `-inge` | `-ing` | `-ingí` (strong) | `-ingí` (strong) |
