The fourth declension is one of the more straightforward noun classes in Irish. It primarily includes masculine nouns that end in a vowel, as well as all nouns with the diminutive suffix `-ín`. Its most defining feature is that the genitive singular form is identical to the nominative singular.

## Characteristics of the Fourth Declension

This declension includes several distinct groups of nouns:

- **Masculine nouns ending in a vowel**: This is the largest group.
- **Feminine nouns ending in a vowel**: A smaller group of feminine nouns also belongs here.
- **Nouns with the diminutive ending `-ín`**: All nouns with this ending, regardless of their original declension, follow fourth declension rules.
- **A few nouns ending in a consonant**: This group includes common loanwords like `bus`, `club`, and `tobac`, as well as some native Irish words like `ainm` (name) and `lucht` (people).

## Genitive Case

The key feature of the fourth declension is its simplicity in the singular.

- The **genitive singular** has the same form as the nominative singular.
- The **genitive plural** has the same form as the nominative plural.

This means that once you know the nominative singular and plural forms, you know all the case forms for this declension.

## Plural Forms

Fourth declension nouns almost always have "strong" plurals, meaning the form does not change for the genitive plural. The plural is formed by adding a suffix, which depends on the noun's ending.

### Nouns ending in `-ín`

Nouns with the diminutive ending `-ín` add `-í` to form the plural.

- `cailín` → `cailíní` (girls)
- `báidín` → `báidíní` (little boats)

### Nouns ending in `-a` or `-e`

- Nouns ending in `-a` change this to `-aí`.
  - `dalta` → `daltaí` (pupils)
  - `slabhra` → `slabhraí` (chains)
- Nouns ending in `-e` change this to `-í`.
  - `file` → `filí` (poets)
  - `oráiste` → `oráistí` (oranges)

### Nouns ending in `-le` or `-ne`

Nouns ending in `-le` or `-ne` replace the final `-e` with `-te`.

- `baile` → `bailte` (towns)
- `sloinne` → `sloinnte` (surnames)

### Nouns ending in a long vowel

- Nouns ending in `-í`, `-aí`, `-aoi`, or `-é` usually add the ending `-the`.
  - `croí` → `croíthe` (hearts)
  - `garraí` → `garraíthe` (gardens)
  - `saoi` → `saoithe` (wise people)

<Note>
Some nouns ending in `-í` or `-aí`, particularly those referring to people, take the plural ending `-ithe` (with a short `i`) instead.
- `rúnaí` → `rúnaíthe` (secretaries)
- `tiománaí` → `tiománaithe` (drivers)
- `ní` → `nithe` (things)
</Note>

### Other Plural Endings

A few common nouns have other plural endings:

- **Ending `-nna`**:
  - `cú` → `cúnna` (hounds)
  - `fia` → `fianna` (deer)
  - `fleá` → `fleánna` (feasts)
- **Ending `-(n)acha`**:
  - `ainm` → `ainmneacha` (names)

### Irregular and Weak Plurals

A very small number of nouns in this declension have irregular or weak plurals. A weak plural changes its form in the genitive plural.

- `bó` (cow) → `ba` (cows) (Gen. Pl: `na mbó`)
- `grásta` (grace) → `grásta` (graces) (Gen. Pl: `na ngrást`)

## Summary of Endings

This table summarises the most common patterns for the fourth declension.

| Ending | Gender | Gen. Sg. | Nom. Pl. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-a` | m/f | `-a` | `-aí` |
| `-e` | m/f | `-e` | `-í` |
| `-le` | m/f | `-le` | `-lte` |
| `-ne` | m/f | `-ne` | `-nte` |
| `-ire` | m | `-ire` | `-irí` |
| `-ín` | m | `-ín` | `-íní` |
| `-í` | m | `-í` | `-íthe` |
| `-aí` | m | `-aí` | `-aíthe` |
| `-é` | m/f | `-é` | `-éithe` |
| `-aoi` | m/f | `-aoi` | `-aoithe` |
