The first declension is one of five noun classes in Irish. It contains exclusively masculine nouns that end in a broad consonant in their basic form, the nominative singular. This is a very common and regular pattern, so learning its rules is a key step in mastering Irish grammar.

Nouns in this group include:
- Masculine nouns ending in a broad consonant.
- Many common nouns ending in `-ach` and `-adh` (but not verbal nouns in `-adh`).
- Many masculine first names, such as `Seán`, `Séamas`, and `Pól`.

## Genitive Singular

To form the genitive singular, the final broad consonant is made slender. This process is called palatalisation or `caolú`.

- **Adding `-i-`**: For most nouns, you simply insert an `i` before the final consonant.
  - *hata Sheáin* - Seán's hat
- **Ending `-(e)ach`**: This changes to `-(a)igh`. Note the spelling change from `ch` to `gh`.
  - *capall an mharcaigh* - the rider's horse
- **Ending `-(e)adh`**: This changes to `-(a)idh`.
  - *tús an tsamhraidh* - the beginning of the summer

The pronunciation of the `-(a)idh` ending varies by dialect.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster and North Connacht (Mayo), the ending is pronounced /iː/.
</Dialect>
<Dialect region="Connemara">
In Connemara, the ending is pronounced as a neutral schwa /ə/, the same as the nominative `-adh`.
</Dialect>
<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, the ending is pronounced /ɪɟ/.
</Dialect>

Some nouns require more significant vowel changes. The common change from `ia` to `é` does not apply to `Brian` (`Briain`), `fiach` (`fiaigh`), `rian` (`riain`), `srian` (`sriain`), `trian` (`triain`), `cliant` (`cliaint`), `fiar` (`fiair`), or `giar` (`giair`). These simply add an `i`.

<Note>
A few common nouns have irregular genitive forms:
- `mac` (son) becomes `mic`
- `Mícheál` (Michael) becomes `Míchíl`
- `muineál` (neck) becomes `muiníl`
</Note>

## Vocative Singular

The vocative singular, used for direct address, has the same form as the genitive singular. It is always preceded by the particle `a`, which causes lenition.

- *A fhir!* - Man!
- *A Sheáin!* - Seán!

## Nominative Plural

Nouns in the first declension can have either a "weak" or a "strong" plural. The distinction matters for forming the genitive plural.

### Weak Plurals

Weak plurals are formed in one of two ways:
1.  **Palatalisation**: The plural form is identical to the genitive singular.
    - *cat* (a cat) -> *cait* (cats)
2.  **Ending `-a`**: The ending `-a` is added to the nominative singular form.
    - *cleas* (a trick) -> *cleasa* (tricks)

### Strong Plurals

Strong plurals are formed with a variety of endings.
- **Ending `-aí`**: This is common for nouns ending in `-ach` or `-adh`.
  - *bealach* (a way) -> *bealaí* (ways)
  - *cogadh* (a war) -> *cogaí* (wars)
- **Endings `-ta` or `-tha`**: These are often found with monosyllabic nouns ending in `l`, `n`, or `r` after a long vowel.
  - *saol* (a world, life) -> *saolta* (worlds, lives)
  - *scéal* (a story) -> *scéalta* (stories)
- **Syncope and `-e`**: Some polysyllabic nouns drop the vowel in the last syllable (syncope) and add `-e`.
  - *solas* (a light) -> *soilse* (lights)
  - *uasal* (a noble) -> *uaisle* (nobles)
- **Other Endings**: Less common endings include `-anna` and `-acha`.
  - *bás* (a death) -> *básanna* (deaths)

## Genitive Plural

The form of the genitive plural depends on whether the nominative plural is weak or strong.
- For **weak plurals**, the genitive plural is the same as the **nominative singular**.
  - *cosa na gcat* - the legs of the cats (`gcat` is the eclipsed form of `cat`)
- For **strong plurals**, the genitive plural is the same as the **nominative plural**.
  - *stair na gcogaí* - the history of the wars (`gcogaí` is the eclipsed form of `cogaí`)

## Vocative Plural

The vocative plural always takes the ending `-a` and is preceded by the particle `a`.
- *A fheara!* - Men!

## Summary of Patterns

### Core Patterns

This table summarises the most common "weak plural" patterns.

| Case | Pattern 1 (Palatalisation) | Pattern 2 (Ending `-a`) |
|---|---|---|
| **Nom. Sg.** | Ends in a broad consonant | Ends in a broad consonant |
| **Gen. Sg.** | Final consonant becomes slender | Final consonant becomes slender |
| **Nom. Pl.** | Same as Gen. Sg. | Adds `-a` to Nom. Sg. |
| **Gen. Pl.** | Same as Nom. Sg. | Same as Nom. Sg. |

### Common Endings

This table shows how common endings change across the different cases.

| Nom. Sg. | Gen. Sg. | Nom. Pl. | Gen. Pl. | Plural Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-(e)ach` | `-(a)igh` | `-(a)igh` | `-(e)ach` | Weak |
| `-(e)ach` | `-(a)igh` | `-(a)í` | `-(a)í` | Strong |
| `-(e)adh` | `-(a)idh` | `-(a)í` | `-(a)í` | Strong |
| `-as` | `-ais` | `-ais` | `-as` | Weak |
| `-án` | `-áin` | `-áin` | `-án` | Weak |
