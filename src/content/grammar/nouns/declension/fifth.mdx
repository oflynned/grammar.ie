The fifth declension is a complex class of nouns, historically known as "consonantal stems". Its key feature is that the genitive singular always ends in a broad consonant.

This declension includes:
- Mostly **feminine** nouns. Nouns that form their genitive with `-d` are typically masculine.
- Nouns ending in a **slender consonant**, such as `-il`, `-in`, or `-ir`.
- Nouns ending in a **vowel**.
- Nouns ending in `-inn` or `-in`, which were originally dative forms that have become the standard nominative.
- Key **kinship terms** ending in `-ir`, such as `athair` (father), `máthair` (mother), and `bráthair` (brother, in a religious order).

## Genitive Singular

All genitive singular forms in the fifth declension end in a **broad consonant**. There are several ways this is formed, reflecting the noun's historical stem class.

- **Depalatalisation**: The final slender consonant of the nominative becomes broad.
  - *`athair` -> `athar`*
  - *`Albain` -> `Alban`*
  - *`abhainn` -> `abhann`*
  - *`Nollaig` -> `Nollag`*

- **Depalatalisation and `-ach`**: Many nouns ending in `-il`, `-in`, or `-ir` broaden the final consonant and add `-ach`.
  - *`beoir` -> `beorach`*
  - *`cathair` -> `cathrach`*

- **Slender consonant and `-each`**: Polysyllabic nouns often keep the final consonant slender but add `-each`. This process often involves syncope (the loss of a vowel in the middle of the word).
  - *`litir` -> `litreach`* (the `i` is lost)
  - *`uimhir` -> `uimhreach`* (the `i` is lost)

- **Vowel ending and `-n`, `-nn`, or `-d`**: Nouns ending in a vowel add one of these endings.
  - *`cara` -> `carad`* (masculine)
  - *`pearsa` -> `pearsan`*
  - *`Éire` -> `Éireann`*
  - The `-nn` ending is common after `l`, `n`, and `r` (e.g., `Éireann`), while `-n` is more common after other consonants (e.g., `Alban`).

- **Ulster Dialect Forms**: Some nouns that end in a vowel in the standard language retain older forms in Ulster. The nominative may end in `-idh` and the genitive in `-adh`. For example, standard `léine` (genitive `léine`, 4th declension) can be `léinidh` (nominative) and `léineadh` (genitive) in Ulster.

## Dative Singular

Historically, the dative singular of these nouns ended in a slender consonant. While often replaced by the nominative form in modern Irish, some dative forms remain in common use.

- Many nouns ending in `-inn` or `-in` (e.g., `Éirinn`, `abhainn`) are originally dative forms that have become the standard nominative. For these nouns, the nominative and dative are identical.
- Nouns with a genitive in `-n`, `-nn`, or `-d` form the dative by palatalising this ending (making it slender). This is most famously seen with `Éire`:
  - Genitive: `Éireann`
  - Dative: `Éirinn` (as in *`i nÉirinn`* - in Ireland)
- Nouns with a genitive in `-(e)ach` historically formed a dative in `-(a)igh`, but this is now rare.
  - Genitive: `caorach`
  - Dative (older): `caoirigh`

## Nominative Plural

The most common way to form the nominative plural is to add `-a` to the genitive singular form. The common plural endings `-(e)acha` and `-(e)anna` originate from this pattern.

- *`cathair`* (city) -> gen. `cathrach` -> pl. `cathracha`
- *`comharsa`* (neighbour) -> gen. `comharsan` -> pl. `comharsana`

Some important nouns are exceptions:
- *`athair`* (father) -> `aithreacha`
- *`abhainn`* (river) -> `aibhneacha`

## Genitive Plural

The genitive plural is usually identical to the nominative plural. Less commonly, it can be identical to the genitive singular.

## Summary of Patterns

This table shows the most common patterns. The dative singular is omitted as it is usually the same as the nominative in modern usage.

| Nominative Singular | Gender | Genitive Singular | Nominative Plural | Genitive Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ends in slender consonant | f | Ends in broad consonant | Varies | Varies |
| Ends in slender `-l`, `-n`, `-r` | f | Broad consonant + `-ach` | Broad consonant + `-acha` | Broad consonant + `-acha` |
| Ends in slender `-l`, `-n`, `-r` | f | Slender consonant + `-each` | Slender consonant + `-eacha` | Slender consonant + `-eacha` |
| Ends in a vowel | f | Vowel + `-n` or `-nn` | Vowel + `-na` or `-nna` | Vowel + `-n(a)` or `-nna` |
| Ends in a vowel | m | Vowel + `-d` | Varies | Varies |
