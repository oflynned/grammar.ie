The third declension includes both masculine and feminine nouns. Its defining characteristic is a genitive singular that ends in `-a`. Nouns in this class can end in either a broad or a slender consonant in their nominative singular form.

## Genitive Singular

The genitive singular is always formed by adding the ending `-a`. The way this is applied depends on the final consonant of the noun.

### Nouns with a Broad Final Consonant

If the noun ends in a broad consonant, the ending `-a` is simply added.

- `beannacht` (a blessing) → `beannachta`
- `áth` (a ford) → `átha`

### Nouns with a Slender Final Consonant

If the noun ends in a slender consonant, the consonant is first made broad (depalatalised) before the ending `-a` is added.

- `feoil` (meat) → `feola`
- `cuid` (a part, a share) → `coda`
- `fuil` (blood) → `fola`
- `mil` (honey) → `meala`

### Special Endings

A few specific endings have their own rules:

- Nouns ending in `-int` drop the `t`: `canúint` (a dialect) → `canúna`.
- Nouns ending in `-irt` change the `t` to `th`: `imirt` (playing, a game) → `imeartha`.

## Plural Forms

In modern Irish, most third-declension nouns form a **strong plural**, where the nominative and genitive plural forms are identical.

An older **weak plural** ending in `-a` is now rare. In this pattern, the genitive plural is formed by dropping the `-a` from the nominative plural (e.g., nominative plural `béasa`, genitive plural `béas`).

Common strong plural endings include:

### Ending in `-í`

This ending is used for nouns ending in `-aeir`, `-éir`, `-eoir`, `-óir`, and `-úir`.

- `Gaeilgeoir` (an Irish speaker) → `Gaeilgeoirí`
- `dochtúir` (a doctor) → `dochtúirí`

### Ending in `-aí`

This ending is common for nouns ending in `-acht` or `-íocht`. These nouns are almost always feminine.

- `Gaeltacht` (an Irish-speaking area) → `Gaeltachtaí`
- `mallacht` (a curse) → `mallachtaí`

An exception is `comhlacht` (a company), which is masculine.

### Ending in `-anna` or `-aí`

Masculine, monosyllabic nouns often take one of these endings.

- `loch` (a lake) → `lochanna`
- `rud` (a thing) → `rudaí`

### Other Endings

For nouns ending in `-l`, `-n`, or `-r`, the following endings are often found:

- **`-acha`**: `barúil` (an opinion) → `barúlacha`
- **`-ta`**: `bliain` (a year) → `blianta`
- **`-te`**: `móin` (peat, turf) → `móinte`
