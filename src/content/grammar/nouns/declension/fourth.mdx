The fourth declension is primarily for nouns that end in a vowel. Its most distinctive feature is that the genitive singular form is identical to the nominative singular.

## Characteristics of the Fourth Declension

Nouns in this group typically share the following characteristics:
- Most are **masculine**.
- The majority end in a **vowel**.
- All nouns with the diminutive suffix `-ín` belong to this declension.
- A few common nouns ending in a consonant are also included, some of which are loanwords (e.g., `bus`, `club`, `tobac`) and some native Irish words (e.g., `ainm`, `lucht`).

## Declension Patterns

### Genitive Singular

The core rule of the fourth declension is that the genitive singular is always the same as the nominative singular. There is no change in the noun's form.

*   *Hatas an dalta* - The pupil's hat
*   *Dath an choláiste* - The colour of the college

### Plural Forms

Fourth declension nouns almost always have strong plurals, meaning the genitive plural is the same as the nominative plural. The plural is formed by adding a suffix, which depends on the ending of the singular noun.

-   Nouns ending in `-a` or `-e` change their ending to `-aí` or `-í`.
    *   `dalta` → `daltaí`
    *   `coláiste` → `coláistí`
-   Nouns ending in `-le` or `-ne` replace the `-e` with `-te`.
    *   `baile` → `bailte`
    *   `sloinne` → `sloinnte`
-   Nouns ending in `-ín` add `-í`.
    *   `cailín` → `cailíní`
-   Nouns ending in `-í`, `-aí`, `-aoi`, or `-é` usually add `-the`.
    *   `croí` → `croíthe`
    *   `garraí` → `garraíthe`
-   Nouns for people ending in `-aí` often add `-the` with a short `i` (`-ithe`).
    *   `rúnaí` → `rúnaíthe`
-   A small number of nouns add `-nna`.
    *   `cú` → `cúnna`
    *   `fia` → `fianna`
-   A few nouns add `-(n)acha`.
    *   `ainm` → `ainmneacha`

A very small number of nouns have weak plurals, such as `bó` (cow), which becomes `ba` in the nominative plural but reverts to `bó` in the genitive plural (`na mbó`).
