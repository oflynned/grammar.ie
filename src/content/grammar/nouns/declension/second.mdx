The second declension consists almost entirely of feminine nouns, which can end in either a broad or a slender consonant. Its key feature is forming the genitive singular by adding `-e`.

### Characteristics of the Second Declension

- **Gender:** Almost all nouns are feminine. The only common masculine nouns in this class are `im` (butter), `sliabh` (mountain), and `teach` (house).
- **Ending:** The nominative singular can end in a broad or slender consonant.
- **Subgroups:** A notable subgroup includes polysyllabic feminine nouns ending in `-(e)ach`, such as `gealach` (moon) and `báisteach` (rain).

### Paradigm Example

Here is a typical second declension noun, `bróg` (shoe), declined with the definite article.

| Case | Singular | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | `an bhróg` | `na bróga` |
| **Genitive** | `na bróige` | `na mbróg` |
| **Dative** | `don bhróig` | `do na bróga` |

## Case Formation Rules

### Genitive Singular

The genitive singular is typically formed by making the final consonant slender (if it isn't already) and adding the ending `-e`.

- **Nouns ending in a slender consonant:** Simply add `-e`.
  - *`coill`* (forest) → `coille`
  - *`súil`* (eye) → `súile`
- **Nouns ending in a broad consonant:** Make the consonant slender (palatalise) and add `-e`.
  - *`bróg`* (shoe) → `bróige`
  - *`lámh`* (hand) → `láimhe`
  - *`sliabh`* (mountain) → `sléibhe`
  - An irregular example is `scian` (knife) → `scine`.
- **Disyllabic nouns:** The vowel in the second syllable is often dropped (syncope), causing the new final consonant to become slender.
  - *`obair`* (work) → `oibre`
  - *`seamair`* (clover) → `seimre`
- **Nouns ending in `-(e)ach`:** These nouns, along with `teach` (house), take an `-(a)í` ending.
  - *`gealach`* (moon) → `gealaí`
  - *`báisteach`* (rain) → `báistí`
  - *`teach`* (house) → `tí`

> **Note on spelling:** The ending `-(a)í` is the modern spelling for the older `-(a)ighe`. This shows that these nouns historically followed the "palatalise and add `-e`" rule. For example, `teach` became `tighe` (now `tí`), and `gealach` became `gealaighe` (now `gealaí`).

### Dative Singular

For nouns ending in a broad consonant, a distinct dative singular form is created by making the final consonant slender.

- In Standard Irish, this form is only consistently used for five nouns:
  - `lámh` → `láimh`
  - `bos` → `bois`
  - `bróg` → `bróig`
  - `cluas` → `cluais`
  - `cos` → `cois`
- This dative form is identical to the **nominative dual**, used after the number `dhá` (two), e.g., *`dhá bhróig`* (two shoes).
- In the Connemara dialect, this slender dative form is often used for the nominative singular as well, e.g., `fuinneoig` for `fuinneog`.

### Nominative Plural

Second declension nouns can have weak or strong plurals.

- **Weak Plurals:** The most common plural ending is `-a` (after a broad consonant) or `-e` (after a slender consonant).
  - *`long`* (ship) → `longa`
  - *`cloch`* (stone) → `clocha`
  - *`súil`* (eye) → `súile`
  - Some nouns ending in a slender consonant first make the consonant broad (depalatalise) and then add `-a`.
    - *`binn`* (peak) → `beanna`
    - *`deoir`* (tear) → `deora`
- **Strong Plurals:** Less common endings include `-anna`, `-acha`, `-ta`, and `-tha`.
  - *`áit`* (place) → `áiteanna`
  - *`craobh`* (branch) → `craobhacha`
  - *`tonn`* (wave) → `tonnta`
  - Polysyllabic nouns ending in a slender consonant regularly form a strong plural with `-í`.
    - *`aimsir`* (weather) → `aimsirí`
    - *`oifig`* (office) → `oifigí`

### Genitive Plural

The form of the genitive plural depends on whether the nominative plural is weak or strong.

- **Weak Plurals:** The genitive plural is identical to the nominative singular, but if the noun ends in a slender consonant, it is often made broad (depalatalised).
  - *`súil`* (eye) → `súl` (of the eyes)
  - *`cill`* (churchyard) → `ceall` (of the churchyards)
  - *`glúin`* (knee) → `glún` (of the knees)
- **Strong Plurals:** The genitive plural is identical to the nominative plural.
  - *`oifigí`* (offices) → `oifigí` (of the offices)
