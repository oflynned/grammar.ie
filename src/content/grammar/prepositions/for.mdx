There is no single preposition in Irish that directly translates the English word "for". Instead, Irish uses a variety of different prepositions and phrases depending on the context, such as time, purpose, benefit, or exchange. This page outlines the most common ways to express this concept.

## "For" in a Temporal Sense

The correct phrase for temporal "for" depends on the timeframe of the event.

### Total Duration

To emphasise the total duration of an event, whether in the past or future, use `ar feadh` ("for the duration of").

* *Bhí sé anseo ar feadh seachtaine.* - He was here for a week.
* *Tháinig sé anseo ar feadh seachtaine.* - He came here for a week.
* *Beidh mé anseo ar feadh bliana.* - I will be here for a year.

For non-temporal durations, you can also use `le linn` ("during") or `i rith` ("in the course of").

* *Bhí sé ansin le linn an chogaidh.* - He was there for the duration of the war.

### Endpoint in the Past

When the event is entirely in the past, you can use `ar feadh` or `le` ("with").

* *Bhí sé anseo le seachtain agus fuair sé bás.* - He was here for a week and then died.
* *Bhí sé anseo ar feadh seachtaine roimh an gCáisc.* - He was here for a week before Easter.

### Endpoint in the Future

When an event will last for a certain duration starting from now, use `go ceann` ("until the end of").

* *Beidh sé anseo go ceann seachtaine.* - He will be here for a week (from now).

### Continuing to the Present

When an event started in the past and continues into the present, use `le`. This often corresponds to the English present perfect tense.

* *Tá mé anseo le seachtain.* - I have been here for a week.

## "For" a Distance

To express "for" over a physical distance, use `ar feadh`.

* *ar feadh trí mhíle* - for three miles.

## "For" a Benefit or Purpose

Several phrases can mean "for the benefit of" or "for the purpose of".

- **`le haghaidh`** ("with face"):
  * *Cheannaigh mé sin le m’aghaidh.* - I bought that for myself.
- **`i gcomhair`** ("in counting"), **`faoi chomhair`** ("under counting"), or **`faoi choinne`** ("under determination"):
  * *litir faoi do choinne* - a letter for you.
  * *áit faoi choinne leabhar* - a place for books.
  * *Rinne siad féasta faoina chomhair.* - They held a feast for him.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, `fá choinne` is the common form instead of `faoi choinne`.
</Dialect>

## "For" a Favour

To indicate that something is done as a favour for someone, use the preposition `do` ("to").

* *Rinne mé sin dó.* - I did that for him.

## "For" a Goal

To express doing something for a specific goal or reason, use `ar son` ("for the well-being of").

* *Rinne mé ar son an airgid é.* - I did it for the money.
* *Glacaigí agus ithigí uile é seo: óir is é seo mo chorp a thabharfar ar bhur son.* - Take and eat all of this, for this is my body which will be given for you.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, `de gheall ar` or `mar gheall ar` are also used in this context.
</Dialect>

## To Go "For" Something

When going to a place to get something, use `faoi dhéin` ("under fetching") or `le haghaidh`.

* *Bhuail mé isteach sa siopa faoi dhéin siúcra.* - I went into the shop for some sugar.

## "For" a Price or Exchange

When buying, selling, or exchanging items, use the preposition `ar` ("on").

* *Cheannaigh mé ar phunt amháin é.* - I bought it for one pound.
* *Bhéarfaidh mé daoibh ar chúig phunta an ceann iad.* - I will give them to you for 5 pounds each.
* *Thug sé trí ba ar an gcapall sin.* - He gave three cows for that horse.
* *Focal ar fhocal* - Word for word.

## In Return "For"

To express gratitude or payment in return for something, use `as ucht` ("out of breast") or simply `as` ("out of").

* *Go raibh maith agat as an gcabhair.* - Thank you for the help.
* *D’íoc mé fiche punt as.* - I paid 20 pounds for it.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
The phrase `ar son` is more common in Ulster for this meaning.
</Dialect>

## To Work "For"

When talking about employment, use `le` ("with") or `do` ("to").

* *ag obair leis an gcomhlacht árachais* - to work for the insurance company.

## Good or Bad "For"

The choice of preposition depends on the context.

- Use **`do`** ("to") for general statements of benefit or harm.
  * *maith dó* - good for him.
  * *olc duit* - bad for you.
- Use **`ag`** ("at") to describe how someone finds a task or situation.
  * *éasca aici* - easy for her.
  * *maith againn* - good for us.
  * *róchúng ag luch* - too narrow for a mouse.
  * *róthrom ag an bpáiste* - too heavy for the child.

## "For" Meaning "Instead Of"

To mean "instead of" or "in place of", use `thar cheann` ("over head") or `in ionad`.

* *Bhí sé ann thar mo cheann.* - He was there for me (in my place).
* *Labhair sé thar ceann an náisiúin.* - He spoke for the whole nation.

## To Be "For" Something (In Favour Of)

To express support or being in favour of something, use `i leith` ("in side"), `i bhfabhar` ("in favour"), or `ar thaobh` ("on side").

* *Tá mé i leith fanacht anseo.* - I am for staying here.
* *Tá sé ar thaobh Shasana.* - He is for England.

## "For" Meaning "As Regards"

When "for" is used to mean "considering" or "as regards", several phrases are possible.

- **`mar le`** ("as with"):
  * *Is breá an aimsir í, mar le geimhreadh!* - For a winter, it is glorious weather!
- **`maidir le`**:
  * *Maidir le Sasanach níl sé ag déanamh go holc.* - For an Englishman, he is not doing badly.
- **`de`** ("of"):
  * *Is fear maith é d’fhear Sasanach.* - He is a good man for an Englishman.

## "For All That"

The phrase `ar feadh a` can be used to mean "for all that..." or "considering what...", often in a disapproving sense. A similar phrase is `ar scáth a`.

* *ar feadh a bhfuil de thalamh aige* - for all the land he has.
* *Ar feadh a raibh le cur ar an pháipéar d’fhéadfainn an tuairisc a scríobh gan a dhul chun na feise ar chor ar bith.* - For all that was to be put on paper, I could have written the report without going to the festival at all.
* *ar scáth a bhfuil de mhaith ann bheadh sé chomh maith dhó imeacht.* - For all the good he is, he might as well leave.

## Responsible "For"

In official titles, the preposition `um` ("around") is often used to mean "for" in the sense of "responsible for" or "concerning".

* *An Institiúid Éireannach um Chosaint Raideolaíoch* - The Irish Institute for Radiological Protection (Radiological Protection Institute of Ireland).

## "Man for Man" or "Eye for an Eye"

In reciprocal phrases like "man for man" or "eye for an eye", Irish uses `sa` ("in the").

* *fear san fhear* - man for man.
* *súil sa tsúil* - eye for an eye.
