The prepositions `chun` and `chuig` both mean "to" or "towards". The primary difference between them is the grammatical case they require for the following noun. This choice also affects initial mutations and can vary by dialect.

## `chun` (with the Genitive Case)

The preposition `chun` always requires the noun that follows it to be in the **genitive case**. This is the more common form in Munster and in the official standard.

### Initial Mutations with `chun`

The initial mutations after `chun` are determined by the rules of the genitive case.

- **Without an article**: The noun is in the genitive, but there is no mutation.
  - *chun buachalla* - to a boy
- **With the masculine singular article `an`**: The noun is lenited.
  - *chun an bhuachalla* - to the boy
- **With the feminine singular article `na`**: There is no mutation.
  - *chun na mná* - to the woman
- **With the plural article `na`**: The noun is eclipsed.
  - *chun na mban* - to the women

## `chuig` (with the Dative Case)

The preposition `chuig` requires the noun that follows it to be in the **dative case**. This form is most common in Connacht and Ulster, particularly when referring to people or events.

### Initial Mutations with `chuig`

- **Without an article**: There is no mutation.
  - *chuig cailín* - to a girl
- **With the singular article `an`**: The noun is eclipsed (except for `d` and `t` in some dialects).
  - *chuig an gcailín* - to the girl
  - **Munster**: `d` and `t` are also eclipsed.
  - **Connacht**: A t-prefix is added to feminine nouns starting with `s-`, e.g., *chuig an tsúil*.
  - **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis, e.g., *chuig an chailín*.

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

When combined with personal pronouns, `chun` and `chuig` use the same set of conjugated forms.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `chugam` | `chugamsa` | to me |
| 2nd sg | `chugat` | `chugatsa` | to you |
| 3rd sg (m) | `chuige` | `chuigesean` | to him |
| 3rd sg (f) | `chuici` | `chuicise` | to her |
| 1st pl | `chugainn` | `chugainne` | to us |
| 2nd pl | `chugaibh` | `chugaibhse` | to you |
| 3rd pl | `chucu` | `chucusan` | to them |

### Dialectal Variants of Conjugated Forms

- **Munster**: The `g` or `c` is often lenited, resulting in forms like `chugham`, `chughat`, `chuichi`, etc. (but not `chuige`).
- **Connacht/Ulster**: The preposition `ag` is often used instead of `chuig`. The conjugated forms may appear with an initial `h-`, such as `hagam` or `agam` instead of `chugam`.

## Common Uses and Meanings

### Direction and Movement

The most common use is to indicate movement towards a person, place, or thing.

- *Scríobh chugam.* - Write to me.
- *dul chuig an dochtúir* - to go to the doctor
- *chun na Fraince* - to France (for place names with an article)

It can also be used figuratively:

- *cuir chun báis* - to put to death
- *dul chun olcais* - to turn to bad
- *dul chun dearmaid* - to be forgotten
- *chun bealaigh* - on the way

### Purpose or Intention

With a verbal noun, `chun` or `chuig` can mean "in order to". In this construction, `chun` does not trigger the genitive case.

- *chun an fhírinne a rá* - in order to tell the truth
- *chun teanga a fhoghlaim* - in order to learn a language

### Readiness or Suitability

With a verbal noun, `chun` can indicate that something is ready for or suited to an action.

- *tá an féar chun bainte* - the grass is high enough to be cut
- *réidh chun ...* - ready for ...
- *fear chun cainte* - a man for talking (a talkative man)
- *lá chun taistil* - a day for travelling
- *an aimsir chuige* - suitable weather for it

### Temporal Meanings

`chun` is used in telling time and for other temporal expressions.

- *ceathrú chun a dó* - a quarter to two
- *Tá sé chun a dó.* - It is nearly 2 o'clock.
- *chun gur ghlaoigh an coileach* - before the cock crowed
- *Dé Luan seo chugainn* - next Monday

### Other Meanings

- **Reason**: *rinne muid chuig glóire Dé é* - we did it for the glory of God.
- **Proximity**: *chun na gaoithe* - close to the wind; *chun mo laimhe* - near my hand.

## Asking Questions

- `cé chuige?` - to whom?
- `cad chuige?` / `cá chuige?` - for what purpose? why? (In Connacht/Ulster, `cad chuige` is often shortened to `tuige`).

## Pronunciation and Origin

### Etymology

- `chun` derives from the Old Irish verbal noun `dochum` ("to step towards it"), which explains its requirement for the genitive case. It was formerly spelled `chum`.
- `chuig` is a back-formation from `chuige` (to him), which was originally a conjugated form of the preposition `go` (to).

### Pronunciation of `chun`

- **Munster**: Pronounced [xun] or [xun′]. Sometimes spelled `chuin`.
- **Connacht/Ulster**: Often reduced to [un], [ən], or [ə]. Sometimes spelled `’un`, as in *’un an aonaigh* (to the fair).
- **Ulster**: The combination `chun an` or `chun na` can merge into `’na` [nə], e.g., *chun an chnoic* becomes *’na chnoic* (to the hill).
