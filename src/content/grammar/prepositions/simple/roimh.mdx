The preposition `roimh` primarily means "before". It is usually pronounced [riv′].

## Grammatical Rules

### Case

`roimh` is followed by the dative case.

- *Tá sé anseo roimh an gcruinniú.* - He is here before the meeting.

In parts of Munster, the genitive case is sometimes used instead.
- *roimis na Nollag* - before Christmas (lit. "before the Christmas's")

### Initial Mutations

The initial mutation caused by `roimh` depends on whether it is followed by the definite article `an`.

#### Without the Article

When `roimh` is followed directly by a noun, it causes **lenition**.

<MutationCard
  title="roimh + Noun"
  before="roimh + maidin"
  after="roimh mhaidin"
  rule="Lenites the following noun."
/>

- *roimh mhaidin* - before morning

#### With the Singular Article

In the standard language, `roimh an` causes **eclipsis**.

<MutationCard
  title="roimh + an + Noun (Standard)"
  before="roimh + an + bus"
  after="roimh an mbus"
  rule="Eclipses the following noun."
/>

- *roimh an mbus* - before the bus

Dialectal variations are common:
- **Munster**: Eclipsis applies to all consonants, including `d` and `t`, which are normally exempt. The form `roimis` is often used before the article.
  - *roimis an ndalta* - before the pupil
- **Connacht**: A `t-` prefix is added to feminine nouns beginning with `s-`.
  - *roimh an tsúil* - before the eye
- **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis.
  - *roimh an bhus* - before the bus

### Dialectal Forms

- **Ulster**: `roimhe`
- **Munster**: `roim`
- **Before the article**: `roimis` is common, especially in Munster.

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

When `roimh` is followed by a pronoun, they combine into a single word, known as a prepositional pronoun.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | `romham` | `romhamsa` | before me |
| 2nd sg (you) | `romhat` | `romhatsa` | before you |
| 3rd sg (he) | `roimhe` | `roimhesean` | before him |
| 3rd sg (she) | `roimpi` | `roimpise` | before her |
| 1st pl (we) | `romhainn` | `romhainne` | before us |
| 2nd pl (you) | `romhaibh` | `romhaibhse` | before you |
| 3rd pl (they) | `rompu` | `rompusan` | before them |

**Note:** In Munster, `roimis` is often used instead of `roimhe` for "before him".

## Usage

### Temporal 'before'

The most common use of `roimh` is to mean "before" in time, or "earlier than".

- *roimh mhaidin* - before morning
- *Tá mé romhat ann.* - I am there before you.

### Spatial 'before'

`roimh` can mean "before" in a spatial sense (in front of), but this is less common. The preposition `os comhair` is more frequently used for this meaning.

### With Verbs

`roimh` combines with certain verbs to create specific meanings.

- `tar roimh` / `téigh roimh` - to intercept, stop, prevent, or interrupt

### Idiomatic Expressions

`roimh` is used in several common idioms where the English translation uses a different preposition, such as "of" or "to".

- *Tá faitíos orm roimh an madra.* - I am afraid **of** the dog. (lit. "Fear is on-me before the dog.")
- *Tá fáilte romhat.* - You are welcome. (lit. "Welcome is before-you.")

## Asking Questions

- `cé roimhe ...?` - before whom ...? (requires an indirect relative clause)
- `cad roimhe ...?` / `cé roimhe ...?` / `cá roimhe ...?` - before what? (requires an indirect relative clause)
