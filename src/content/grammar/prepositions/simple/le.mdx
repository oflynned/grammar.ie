The preposition `le` primarily means 'with'. Historically, it is a merger of the Old Irish prepositions `le` and `re` (from `fri`, meaning 'against'). In Scottish Gaelic, these two prepositions remain separate.

In Munster, `le` is often pronounced and written as `lé`, with a long 'e'.

## Grammatical Forms and Mutations

### Case and Initial Mutations

The preposition `le` is followed by the dative case. The initial mutation it causes depends on whether the definite article is present.

**Without the article:**
`le` causes no lenition or eclipsis, but it prefixes an 'h' to nouns beginning with a vowel.

<MutationCard title="H-prefix with 'le'" before="le + vowel" after="le h-vowel" rule="The preposition 'le' prefixes 'h' to a following noun that begins with a vowel when there is no article." />

- *le hAoife* - with Aoife

**With the article:**
The form becomes `leis an`. The mutation that follows varies by dialect.

- **Standard/Munster:** Eclipsis.
  - *leis an mbord* - with the table
  - In Munster, `d` and `t` are also eclipsed.
- **Ulster:** Lenition.
  - *leis an bhord* - with the table
- **Connacht:** Eclipsis, but with a t-prefix before feminine nouns starting with `s`.
  - *leis an tsúil* - with the eye

### Forms with the Article

`le` combines with the definite article `an` and `na` to form `leis`.

| Form | Combination | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Singular | `le` + `an` | `leis an` |
| Plural | `le` + `na` | `leis na` |

### Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

Like most prepositions, `le` combines with personal pronouns to form a single word.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `liom` | `liomsa` | with me |
| 2nd sg | `leat` | `leatsa` | with you |
| 3rd sg (m) | `leis` | `leis-sean` | with him |
| 3rd sg (f) | `léi` | `léise` | with her |
| 1st pl | `linn` | `linne` | with us |
| 2nd pl | `libh` | `libhse` | with you |
| 3rd pl | `leo` | `leosan` | with them |

### Forms with Possessive Pronouns

`le` also combines with possessive pronouns.

| Person | Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `le mo` (or `lem`) | with my |
| 2nd sg | `le do` (or `led`) | with your |
| 3rd sg (m) | `lena` (+ lenition) | with his |
| 3rd sg (f) | `lena` (+ no mutation) | with her |
| 1st pl | `lenár` (+ eclipsis) | with our |
| 2nd pl | `le bhur` (+ eclipsis) | with your |
| 3rd pl | `lena` (+ eclipsis) | with their |

The forms `lem` and `led` are common in Munster. The three `lena` forms are distinguished by the initial mutation they cause on the following noun.

### Forms with Relative Clauses

`le` combines with the indirect relative pronoun `a` and the indirect relative form of the copula.

| Tense | Relative Pronoun (`le` + `a`/`ar`) | Copula Form (`le` + `ar`) | Copula (before vowel) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `lena` (+ eclipsis) | `lenar` (+ lenition) | `lenarb` | with which |
| Past | `lenar` (+ lenition) | `lenar` (+ lenition) | `lenarbh` | with which |

These forms are used when the relative clause begins with the preposition.
- *an bhean lena dtéim* - the woman with whom I go

However, it is more common to place the preposition at the end of the clause.
- *an bhean a dtéim léi* - the woman that I go with

## Usage and Idioms

`le` is used in a wide variety of contexts beyond its core meaning of 'with'.

### Core Meanings

- **Accompaniment:** *le Máirtín* - with Martin
- **Means/Instrument:** *le spáid* - with a spade
- **Cause:** *Fuair sé bás leis an ocras.* - He died of hunger.
- **Cause of states:** *leis an tuirse* - from tiredness; *le hól* - due to drinking; *le greann* - for fun

### Purpose and Obligation

`le` is used with a verbal noun to express purpose or obligation.
- *le balla a phéinteáil* - to paint a wall (in order to paint a wall)
- *neart le n-ól* - plenty to drink
- *scéal le hinsint* - a story to tell

Note the `n-` prefix before a vowel-initial verbal noun in this context.

With an adjective, it means 'to' or 'for'.
- *maith le hithe* - good to eat
- *blasta le hól* - tasty to drink
- *crua le cogaint* - hard to chew

### Time and Duration

- **Point in time (at the beginning of):** *leis an lá* - at daybreak; *leis an mí* - at the beginning of the month
- **Duration (for/since):** *le seachtain* - for a week; *le fada* - for a long time

### Location and Movement

- **Location (close to, against):** *leis an mballa* - against the wall; *le tine* - close to the fire
- **Movement (alongside):** *éirigh le balla* - to climb up a wall; *tit le haill* - to fall down a cliff
- **Direction (towards):** *luí le grian* - to lie in the sun; *tabhair do chúl leo* - turn your back to them
- **Moving force:** *le gaoth* - with the wind; *le sruth* - with the current

### Possession and Relationships

With the copula, `le` denotes ownership.
- *Is le Cáit an teach.* - The house belongs to Cáit. (The house is with Cáit.)
- *an teach leis* - his house

It is also used for relationships and authorship.
- *cara liom* - a friend of mine
- *iníon leis* - a daughter of his
- *"Ulysses" le James Joyce* - "Ulysses" by James Joyce

### Opinions and Feelings

`le` is essential for expressing subjective opinions, feelings, and assessments, often with the copula.
- *Is maith liom é.* - I like it. (It is good with me.)
- *Ba mhaith liom tae.* - I would like tea.
- *Is fearr liom caife.* - I prefer coffee.
- *Is fuath liom an aimsir seo.* - I hate this weather.
- *Is ionadh liom é sin.* - I find that strange. (It is a wonder with me.)
- *Is cuma liom.* - I don't care.
- *Is dóigh liom go...* - I think that... (It is an opinion with me that...)
- *Is fada liom an lá.* - I find the day long.

### Comparison

`le` is used in comparisons with `chomh` (as).
- *chomh mór leat* - as big as you
- *i gcomparáid le* - in comparison with
- *inchurtha le* - comparable to

### Common Phrases and Verbs

- **Behaviour towards others:** *bí deas le duine* - be nice to someone; *fearg a bheith ort le duine* - to be angry with someone
- **Waiting:** *fan le duine* - to wait for someone
- **Meeting:** *buail le duine* - to meet someone
- **Helping:** *cuidigh le duine* - to help someone
- **Listening:** *éist le ceol* - to listen to music
- **Ability:** *Is féidir liom snámh.* - I can swim.
- **Success:** *D'éirigh liom.* - I succeeded.
- **Continuing an action:** *Abair leat.* - Continue speaking. *D'oibrigh mé liom.* - I continued working.
- **Turn to do something:** *Is leatsa imirt.* - It's your turn to play.
- **Bringing/Taking:** *An bhfuil do mhála leat?* - Do you have your bag with you?

### Compound Prepositions

`le` forms part of several common compound prepositions.
- `maidir le` - regarding, concerning, as for
- `in aice le` - next to
- `in éineacht le` - together with, alongside

### Adverbial Use

In some dialects, particularly Munster, `leis` is used as an adverb meaning 'also' or 'too', as an alternative to `freisin` or `fosta`.
- *Bhí mé ann, leis.* - I was there, too.

### Questions

- `cé leis?` - with whom?
- `cad leis?` / `cé leis?` / `cá leis?` - with what?
- `Cé leis an leabhar?` - To whom does the book belong? / Whose book is it?
