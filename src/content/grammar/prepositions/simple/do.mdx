The simple preposition `do` primarily means "to" or "for". It is one of the most fundamental prepositions in Irish, most notably used to mark the indirect object of a verb, equivalent to the dative case in other languages.

In most dialects, `do` is pronounced identically to the preposition `de`. Both are typically pronounced /gə/ in Connacht and /də/ in Munster and Ulster. The Connacht pronunciation /gə/ is distinguished from the preposition `go` (to, until) because `do` causes lenition, whereas `go` does not.

In verbal noun constructions, the preposition `a` often appears as a short form of `do`.

## Initial Mutations

The preposition `do` causes lenition of the following noun.

- *do Sheán* - to Séan

When combined with the singular definite article (`an`), it becomes `don` and still causes lenition.

- *don mháthair* - to the mother

If the noun begins with 's', a t-prefix is added instead of lenition.

- *don tsagart* - to the priest

## Forms of 'do'

### Before Vowels and 'fh-'

Before a word beginning with a vowel or with `fh-` followed by a vowel, `do` contracts to `d'`. This form is identical to the contracted form of `de`.

- *d'Úna* - to Úna
- *d'fhear* - to a man

### With the Definite Article

| Combination | Result |
| :--- | :--- |
| `do` + `an` | `don` |
| `do` + `na` | `do na` (also `dos na`, `dosna`) |

### Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

Like all simple prepositions, `do` combines with personal pronouns to form a single word. It also has distinct forms when used with possessive pronouns.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning | With Possessive Pronoun | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `dom` | `domhsa` | to me | `do mo` (`dom`) | to my |
| 2nd sg | `duit` | `duitse` | to you | `do do` (`dod`) | to your |
| 3rd sg (m) | `dó` | `dósan` | to him | `dá` | to his |
| 3rd sg (f) | `di` | `dise` | to her | `dá` | to her |
| 1st pl | `dúinn` | `dúinne` | to us | `dár` | to our |
| 2nd pl | `daoibh` | `daoibhse` | to you (pl) | `do bhur` | to your (pl) |
| 3rd pl | `dóibh` | `dóibhsean` | to them | `dá` | to their |

#### Dialectal Variations

- The conjugated forms are often lenited, especially after vowels and slender consonants (e.g., `dhom` instead of `dom`, `dhá` instead of `dá`).
- **Connacht:** Short forms are common, e.g., `'om`, `'uit`, `'ó`, `'i`, `'úinne`, `'aoibh`, `'óibh`.
- **Ulster:** `domh` or `damh` is used instead of `dom`. `dona` is used instead of `dá` (to his/her/their).
- **Munster:** `dom` and `dod` are the standard forms for `do mo` and `do do`.
- When `dá` is the object of a verbal noun, it is often replaced by `á`.

### With Relative Pronouns

`Do` combines with the indirect relative particle `a` and the past tense particle `ar`.

| Tense | Relative Particle | Copula Form | Copula Form (before vowel) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `dá` | `dar` | `darb` | to which/whom |
| Past | `dár` | `dar` | `darbh` | to which/whom |

These forms are also used with the generalising relative pronoun, meaning "to everything that" or "to all those who".

## Usage

### Indirect Object (Dative)

The primary function of `do` is to mark the indirect object of a verb. This corresponds to the dative case and often translates to English "to" or "for", or the indirect object without a preposition.

- *Thug mé an leabhar dó.* - I gave the book to him. / I gave him the book.
- *Inis an scéal dom.* - Tell the story to me. / Tell me the story.
- *Thaispeáin sí an pictiúr dúinn.* - She showed the picture to us. / She showed us the picture.

This is distinct from `chuig`, which indicates a direct spatial destination:
- *Scríobh mé litir duit.* - I wrote a letter for you. / I wrote you a letter.
- *Chuir mé litir chugat.* - I sent a letter to you.

If an action disadvantages someone (the "dative of disadvantage"), the preposition `ar` is used instead of `do`:
- *Bhris sé an bord orm.* - He broke the table on me (to my disadvantage).

### In Copula Sentences

`Do` is used with the copula and an adjective to describe the person affected by a quality or state. It is also used in constructions that function like modal verbs.

- *Is deacair dom é a thuiscint.* - It is difficult for me to understand it.
- *Ní gá duit imeacht.* - It is not necessary for you to leave.
- *Is fiú dóibh iarracht a dhéanamh.* - It is worthwhile for them to make an effort.

Modal-like constructions:
- **must**: `b’éigean do...` (it was necessary for...), `ní mór do...` (it is essential for...)
- **should**: `is ceart do...`, `ba chóir do...` (it is right for...)

### With Verbal Nouns

`Do` marks the agent (the person performing the action) in certain verbal noun phrases, particularly those indicating time.

- **after**: *tar éis an obair a dhéanamh dom* - after I had done the work.
- **during**: *le linn an litir a scríobh duit* - while you were writing the letter.
- **before**: *roimh an doras a oscailt dó* - before he opened the door.

### Proximity and Relationship

`Do` can indicate closeness, relationship, or belonging.

- *Tá an siopa cóngarach don teach.* - The shop is close to the house.
- *Is mac dom é.* - He is a son of mine.
- *Cara duit* - A friend of yours.
- *Seomra dom féin* - A room for myself.

### In Common Questions and Phrases

`Do` is essential in many fixed questions and expressions, often with the copula.

- *Cad is ainm duit?* - What is your name?
- *Seán is ainm dom.* - My name is Seán.
- *Cá haois duit?* - How old are you?
- *Carb as duit?* - Where are you from?

### Other Uses

- **Spatial Destination:** Less common than `chun` or `chuig`, but used in some phrases.
  - *Téigh don siopa.* - Go to the shop.
  - *Don diabhal leat!* - Go to hell!
- **In Wishes:**
  - *Oíche mhaith duit!* - Good night to you!
  - *Nollaig faoi mhaise daoibh.* - A happy Christmas to you (all).
- **In the Progressive Aspect:** Used with a possessive pronoun to mark the object.
  - *Tá mé do do bhualadh.* - I am hitting you.
  - *Tá mé do mo bhualadh aige.* - I am being hit by him. (passive)
- **With the Verbal Adjective:**
  - *Ní gearánta dom.* - I have no reason to complain.
- **Telling Time:**
  - *Tá sé ceathrú don a trí.* - It is a quarter to three.
- **As an Infinitive Marker (Munster):** In Munster Irish, `do` (often as `d'`) can still be seen where other dialects use the particle `a` before a verbal noun.
  - *chun Gaeilge d’fhoghlaim* - to learn Irish.
