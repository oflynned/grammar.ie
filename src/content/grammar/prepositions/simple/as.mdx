The preposition `as` primarily means "out of" or "from".

Historically, this preposition was often just `a`, especially in older literature. The form `as` was originally only used before the article and in its conjugated forms, but it has since become the standard form in most contexts.

In some Munster dialects (e.g., Cork), `a` (often written `a'`) is still used before a consonant.
- *a' ceann* (out of a head)

## Grammatical Features

### Case

`as` is followed by the dative case. In modern Irish, this is only distinct from the nominative for certain nouns.

### Initial Mutations

The mutation caused by `as` depends on whether it is followed by the definite article `an`.

**Without the article:**
`as` causes no initial mutation.
- *as baile* (out of a town)

**With the article (`an`):**
In Standard Irish, `as an` causes **eclipsis**.

- *as an mbaile* (out of the town)

**Dialectal Variations:**
- **Munster:** Eclipsis applies to all consonants, including `d` and `t`.
- **Connacht:** A `t-` prefix is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s-`: *as an tsúil* (out of the eye).
- **Ulster:** Lenition is used instead of eclipsis: *as an bhaile* (out of the town).

## Question Forms

`as` is used with question words to ask about origin.

- `cé as...?` (from whom...?)
- `cad as...?` / `cé as...?` / `cá has...?` (from what...?)
- `cad as...?` / `cé as...?` / `cá has...?` (where is ... from?)

The question word is typically followed by an indirect relative clause or used in a copula sentence.

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

When `as` is followed by a pronoun, they combine into a single word.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | `asam` | `asamsa` | out of me |
| 2nd sg (you) | `asat` | `asatsa` | out of you |
| 3rd sg (he/it) | `as` | `as-san` | out of him, it |
| 3rd sg (she/it) | `aisti` | `aistise` | out of her, it |
| 1st pl (we) | `asainn` | `asainne` | out of us |
| 2nd pl (you) | `asaibh` | `asaibhse` | out of you |
| 3rd pl (they) | `astu` | `astusan` | out of them |

## Usage and Meanings

### Spatial and Figurative: 'out of'

This is the core meaning of `as`, indicating movement from inside something.
- *Tháinig sé as an teach.* (He came out of the house.)

### Origin: 'from'

Used with the copula to state a person's or thing's place of origin.
- *Is as Béal Feirste é.* (He is from Belfast.)
- *Is as Éirinn mé.* (I am from Ireland.)

### Starting Point: 'from'

Indicates the starting point of a movement or range.
- *as sin go hÉirinn* (from there to Ireland)
- *i bhfad as seo* (far from here)

### Composition: 'of'

Indicates the material something is made from.
- *déanta as adhmad* (made of wood)
- *geansaí as olann* (a jumper made of wool)

### Source: 'from', 'by', 'after'

`as` can indicate the source of a perception, name, action, or emotion.

- **Source of a perception:**
  - *Tá boladh as an madadh.* (The dog smells. / There is a smell from the dog.)
  - *Tá cor as an gcáca.* (The cake is moving.)
- **Source of a name:**
  - *Baisteadh as a athair é.* (He was named after his father.)
- **Source of knowledge or a conclusion:**
  - *Aithním as a shúil é.* (I recognised him by his eye.)
  - *Tuigim as sin go...* (I understand from that that...)
- **Psychological source of an action:**
  - *Rinne sé as fearg é.* (He did it out of anger.)
- **Source of pride, thanks, or trust:**
  - *Tá bród orm asat.* (I am proud of you.)
  - *Go raibh maith agat as an obair seo.* (Thank you for this work.)
  - *Tá muinín agam aisti.* (I trust her.)

### Medium: 'in' or 'with'

Used to describe the medium in which an action takes place.
- *nigh as uisce é* (wash it in/with water)

### Language: 'in'

Used to state the language something is said or written in.
- *as Gaeilge* (in Irish)
- *as Gearmáinis* (in German)

### Location of a Quality: 'at'

Can specify where a quality is located on an object.
- *géar as an mbarr* (sharp at the top)

### General: 'outside' or 'beyond'

Indicates being outside a certain range or state.
- *as amharc* (out of sight)
- *as cuimse* (beyond measure)

### Function: 'out of order'

Describes something that is not functioning correctly.
- *as ordú* (out of order)
- *as úsáid* (out of use)
- *as bealach* (off the path, in error)

### Lack: 'without' or 'not to have'

Indicates a lack or absence of something.
- *as obair* (without work, unemployed)
- *Tá airgead as.* (He has no money. / He is out of money.)

### Removal: 'out of' or 'away from'

Indicates removal from a situation.
- *as guais* (out of danger)
- *as an imirt* (out of the game)

### Selection: 'from' or 'of'

Used when making a choice from a group.
- *bain rogha as* (to choose from)
- *duine as beirt* (one of two)

### Engagement: 'in'

The phrase `tá as rud` means to be engaged in something.
- *Tá sé as an obair.* (He is engaged in the work.)

### Adverbial: 'off' or 'out'

Used adverbially to mean "off" or "extinguished".
- *Tá an raidió as.* (The radio is off.)
- *Chas mé an tine as.* (I turned off the fire.)

### Adverbial: 'alone'

Combined with `féin` (self), it means "alone".
- *Tá mé ag siúl as féin.* (I am walking alone.)
- *Tá asainn féin anois.* (We are alone now.)
