The preposition `fara` means "together with" or "beside". It is less common in modern standard Irish but is found in certain dialects and in older texts.

## Grammar Rules

### Case
`fara` is followed by the dative case. In modern Irish, this is only distinct from the nominative for certain nouns after the singular definite article.

### Initial Mutations
`fara` does not cause any initial mutation, either with or without the definite article.

## Forms of 'fara'

### With the Article
When combined with the definite article `an` or `na`, `fara` becomes `fairis` (or `farais`).

- `fara` + `an` → `fairis an`
- `fara` + `na` → `fairis na`

### Prepositional Pronouns
Like other simple prepositions, `fara` combines with personal pronouns to form a conjugated paradigm.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. (I) | `faram` | `faramsa` | beside me |
| 2nd sg. (you) | `farat` | `faratsa` | beside you |
| 3rd sg. (he/it) | `fairis` | `fairis-san` | beside him |
| 3rd sg. (she/it) | `farae` | `faraese` | beside her |
| 1st pl. (we) | `farainn` | `farainne` | beside us |
| 2nd pl. (you) | `faraibh` | `faraibhse` | beside you |
| 3rd pl. (they) | `faru` | `farusan` | beside them |

## Dialectal and Alternative Forms

Several alternative constructions and dialectal forms exist:

- The forms of `le` can be used alongside `fara`, for example, `fara liom` instead of `faram`, `fara leat` instead of `farat`, and so on.
- The concept can be expressed entirely with `in éineacht le` ("together with").
- A related preposition, `fre`, has forms similar to `le`, such as `freis` instead of `leis`.
- In Cois Fhairrge, forms of `fros` are used: `frum`, `frat`, `frois`, `frae`, `fruinn`, `fraoib`, `frób`.
- In Munster, `fara` can combine with possessive pronouns, e.g., `farana` ("beside his/her/their").

## Usage and Examples

### Together with / In the company of
This is the most direct meaning of `fara`.

- *Bhí Mícheál faru.* (Michael was with them.)
- *Buachaill amháin atá fanta faram anso sa bhaile.* (One boy has remained with me here in the village.)
- *Ná suífeá farainn?* (Won't you sit with us?)
- *Tháinig siad chun Eoin agus dúirt siad leis: "A raibí, an té a bhí farat lastall den Iordáin... féach, tá seisean ag déanamh baiste..."* (They came to John and said to him: "Rabbi, he who was with you across the Jordan... look, he is baptising...")

### As well as / Besides
`fara` can mean "in addition to" or "besides".

- `fara cabhair Dé` (so help me God; lit. "besides God's help")
- `punt fara do cheart` (a pound more than is due to you; lit. "a pound besides your right")

### Compared with / Instead of
It can also be used to introduce a comparison.

- *is beag é fara a bhfuair tusa* (it is little compared with what you received)
- `fara a bheith...` (instead of being...)

## Origin of 'freisin'
The common adverb `freisin` ("also", "too") derives from a form of `fara`. The development was:

`fairis sin` (beside that) → `freis sin` → `freisin`
