The preposition `ionsar` means "to" or "towards". It is primarily used in Ulster Irish and is considered a more advanced or dialectal form.

## Usage and Meaning

`Ionsar` indicates direction towards a person, place, or thing.

- *Tar ionsar an teach.* - Come to the house.
- *Druid ionsar.* - Turn towards.

## Initial Mutations

`Ionsar` causes initial mutations on the noun that follows it. The specific mutation depends on whether the definite article `an` is present and, in some cases, on the dialect.

### Without the Article

When `ionsar` is followed directly by a noun, it causes lenition.

<MutationCard title="ionsar + noun" before="ionsar teach" after="ionsar theach" rule="Lenites the following noun when there is no article." />

- *ionsar theach Bhriain* - to Brian's house

### With the Singular Article

When `ionsar` is followed by the singular definite article `an`, the mutation rule varies by dialect.

#### Ulster Irish

In Ulster, `ionsar an` causes lenition.

<MutationCard title="ionsar + an (Ulster)" before="ionsar an gairdín" after="ionsar an ghairdín" rule="Lenites the following noun after the singular article 'an'." />

- *ionsar an ghairdín* - to the garden

#### Other Dialects

Outside of Ulster, where `ionsar` is less common, it typically causes eclipsis (except on words beginning with `d` or `t`).

<MutationCard title="ionsar + an (Other dialects)" before="ionsar an gairdín" after="ionsar an ngairdín" rule="Eclipses the following noun (except d/t) after the singular article 'an'." />

- *ionsar an ngairdín* - to the garden

## Conjugated Forms

Like most prepositions, `ionsar` combines with personal pronouns to form a set of prepositional pronouns.

| Person | Standard form | Emphatic form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `ionsorm` | `ionsormsa` | to me |
| 2nd sg | `ionsort` | `ionsortsa` | to you |
| 3rd sg (m) | `ionsair` | `ionsairsean` | to him |
| 3rd sg (f) | `ionsuirthi` | `ionsuirthise` | to her |
| 1st pl | `ionsorainn` | `ionsorainne` | to us |
| 2nd pl | `ionsoraibh` | `ionsoraibhse` | to you |
| 3rd pl | `ionsorthu` | `ionsorthusan` | to them |

## Asking Questions

To ask "to whom?" or "to what?", you can use the forms `cé ionsair?` or `cá hionsar?`.

## Etymology and Alternative Forms

`Ionsar` originates from the phrase `d’ionsaí ar` (or older `d’ionsaighe ar`), which literally means "to approach on". This phrase combines the verbal noun `ionsaí` ("attack", "approach") and the preposition `ar`.

This origin is reflected in alternative spellings such as `ionns’ar`. Another variant form is `inseair`.

Historically, `d’ionsaí` could also be used on its own, without `ar`, as a preposition governing the genitive case.
