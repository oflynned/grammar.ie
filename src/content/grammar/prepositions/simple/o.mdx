The simple preposition `ó` primarily means "from" or "since". It is used to indicate a starting point in space or time, as well as origin, cause, and removal.

Like most simple prepositions, `ó` requires the dative case, though this is only noticeable with certain nouns.

## Initial Mutations

The preposition `ó` causes different initial mutations depending on whether it is followed by the definite article.

### Dialectal Variations with the Article

- **Munster:** Eclipsis applies to `d` and `t` as well as other consonants.
- **Connacht:** A `t-` prefix is added to feminine nouns beginning with `s-`, e.g., `ón tsúil` (from the eye).
- **Ulster:** Lenition is used instead of eclipsis, e.g., `ón Ghearmáin` (from Germany).

## Forms of 'ó'

The preposition `ó` combines with articles, pronouns, and other particles to create various forms.

### With the Definite Article

| Form | Combination |
| --- | --- |
| `ón` | `ó` + `an` (singular article before a consonant) |
| `ó na` | `ó` + `na` (plural article) |

### Prepositional Pronouns (Conjugated Forms)

When `ó` is followed by a personal pronoun, they merge into a single word known as a prepositional pronoun.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st sg (I) | `uaim` | `uaimse` | from me |
| 2nd sg (you) | `uait` | `uaitse` | from you |
| 3rd sg (he/it) | `uaidh` | `uaidhsean` | from him/it |
| 3rd sg (she/it) | `uaithi` | `uaithise` | from her/it |
| 1st pl (we) | `uainn` | `uainne` | from us |
| 2nd pl (you) | `uaibh` | `uaibhse` | from you |
| 3rd pl (they) | `uathu` | `uathusan` | from them |

### With Possessive Pronouns

| Pronoun | Combination | Mutation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mo` (my) | `ó mo` | Lenition | `ó mo chara` (from my friend) |
| `do` (your) | `ó do` | Lenition | `ó do theach` (from your house) |
| `a` (his) | `óna` | Lenition | `óna phost` (from his job) |
| `a` (her) | `óna` | h-prefix | `óna hoifig` (from her office) |
| `ár` (our) | `ónár` | Eclipsis | `ónár gcairde` (from our friends) |
| `bhur` (your pl.) | `ó bhur` | Eclipsis | `ó bhur dtuismitheoirí` (from your parents) |
| `a` (their) | `óna` | Eclipsis | `óna dteach` (from their house) |

### With the Relative Particle and Copula

`ó` combines with the indirect relative particle `a`/`ar` and the indirect relative forms of the copula.

| Tense | Relative Particle (`ó` + `a`/`ar`) | Relative Copula (`ó` + `ar`) | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Present | `óna` | `ónar` (`ónarb` before a vowel) | from which... |
| Past | `ónar` | `ónar` (`ónarbh` before a vowel) | from which... |

### With the Numeral Particle 'a'

`ó` can combine with the particle `a` used before numbers.

- *óna trí go dtí a hocht a chlog* - from 3 to 8 o'clock

## Usage and Meaning

### Spatial Starting Point (from)

Indicates the point of origin for movement.

- *Tar ón bposta.* - Come from the post office.
- *fear ó Thrá Lí* - a man from Tralee

### Temporal Starting Point (from, since)

Marks the beginning of a period of time.

- *ón Nollaig* - since Christmas
- *ó mhaidin* - since this morning
- *ó Lá Fhéile Eoin amach* - from St. John's Day onwards

It can also act as a conjunction meaning "since" or "as".

- *ó bhí sí tinn* - since she was ill

### Source or Origin (from)

Indicates the source of an object or action.

- *litir uait* - a letter from you
- *cabhair ó chairde* - help from friends

### Distance (from)

Used to specify distance from a point.

- *míle uaidh* - a mile from it

### Cause or Reason (from)

Explains the reason for a state or condition.

- *bodhar ó thorann* - deaf from the noise
- *tuirseach ó shiúil* - tired from walking

### Removal, Absence, or Freedom (from)

Can imply being without something or free from it.

- *bí ó bhaol* - to be free from danger
- *Is cuma liom agam nó uaim é.* - I don't care if I have it or not.
- *Is fearr d’fhear bean chéile uaidh.* - A man is better off without a wife.

### Limitation (from ... onwards)

Sets a lower bound for a range.

- *ó 10 míle suas* - from 10 miles upwards
- *ó cúig phunt go deich bpunt* - from 5 to 10 pounds

### Expressing Need or Want

The structure `tá/teastaíonn [object] ó [subject]` is used to express need or want.

- *Teastaíonn spórt uaim.* - I want fun.
- *Tá bainne uait.* - You need milk.
- *Teastaíonn uaim labhairt leat.* - I want to speak with you.

### Common Phrases

`ó` appears in many common phrases and idioms.

- **With adjectives:** `deas uait` (kind of you), `maith uainn` (good of us)
- **To be good at something:** `is breá uait é`
- **From ... to:** The structure `ó...go` is very common.
  - *ó thús go deireadh* - from beginning to end
  - *ó lá go lá* - from day to day
  - *ó am go ham* - from time to time

### In Arithmetic (minus)

In subtraction, the order is reversed compared to English.

- *a dó óna trí sin a haon* - 3 - 2 = 1 (literally "two from the three, that is one")

## Interrogative Forms

To ask "from whom?" or "from what?", the prepositional pronoun `uaidh` is used with an interrogative word.

- `cé uaidh?` (+ indirect relative clause) - from whom...?
- `cad uaidh?` / `cé uaidh?` / `cá (h)uaidh?` (+ indirect relative clause) - from what...?
