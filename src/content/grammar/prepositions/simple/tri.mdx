The preposition `trí` primarily means "through". It describes movement or presence that penetrates a space, a period of time, or a group.

## Grammatical Rules

### Case

`trí` is followed by the dative case. In modern Irish, this is only noticeable in a few fixed phrases, as the dative form of most nouns is identical to the nominative.

### Forms with the Article

When `trí` is followed by the singular definite article `an`, it changes form to `tríd`.

-   `trí` + `an` → `tríd an`

It remains `trí` before the plural article `na`.

-   `trí` + `na` → `trí na`

## Initial Mutations

`trí` causes different initial mutations depending on whether a definite article is present.

### Dialectal Variations in Mutation

-   **Munster**: Eclipsis also applies to `d` and `t`.
-   **Connacht**: A t-prefix is added to feminine nouns beginning with `s-`, e.g., *tríd an tsúil* (through the eye).
-   **Ulster**: Lenition is used instead of eclipsis after `fríd an` (the common Ulster form), e.g., *fríd an fhuinneog* (through the window).

## Prepositional Pronouns

When `trí` combines with a pronoun, it forms a prepositional pronoun.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `tríom` | `tríomsa` | through me |
| 2nd sg | `tríot` | `tríotsa` | through you |
| 3rd sg (m) | `tríd` | `trísean` | through him |
| 3rd sg (f) | `tríthi` | `tríthise` | through her |
| 1st pl | `trínn` | `trínne` | through us |
| 2nd pl | `tríbh` | `tríbhse` | through you |
| 3rd pl | `tríothu` | `tríothusan` | through them |

Note: All forms of `trí`, including the prepositional pronouns, are often lenited in speech and writing, e.g., `thríom`, `thríot`.

## Other Grammatical Combinations

### With Possessive Adjectives

| Pronoun | Combination | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| my | `trí mo` | through my |
| your (sg) | `trí do` | through your |
| his / her / their | `trína` | through his / her / their |
| our | `trínár` | through our |
| your (pl) | `trí bhur` | through your |

### With the Relative Pronoun and Copula

| Tense | Relative Pronoun (`trí` + `a`/`ar`) | Copula Form (`trí` + `ar`) | Copula Form (before vowel) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `trína` | `trínar` | `trínarb` | through which |
| Past | `trínar` | `trínar` | `trínarbh` | through which |

### Forming Questions

-   `cé tríd...?` (followed by an indirect relative clause) means "through whom...?"
-   `cad tríd...?` / `cé tríd...?` / `cá tríd...?` (followed by an indirect relative clause) means "through what...?"

## Usage and Meanings

### Spatial Penetration (through)

This is the most common meaning, referring to movement through an opening or space.
-   *trí fhuinneoig* (through a window)
-   *Chuaigh an piléar trína gualainn.* (The bullet went through her shoulder.)

Note: For crossing open terrain, `trasna` (across) is more common: *trasna garraí* (across a field).

### Temporal Duration (through, during)

`trí` can describe the duration of an event.
-   *tríd an oíche* (through the night)
-   *tríd an scéal* (during the story)

### Among or Mixed With

It can mean "amongst" a group or "mixed with" a substance.
-   *Tá bó amháin trí na caorigh.* (There is one cow among the sheep.)
-   *Cuir ola tríd.* (Mix it with oil.)
-   *beoir trí fhíon* (beer with wine)

### Cause or Means (because of, by means of)

`trí` can indicate the cause of something or the medium by which something is done.
-   *Trí bháisteach a bhí an féar fliuch.* (The grass was wet because of the rain.)
-   *tríomsa* (because of me)
-   *tríd é a dhéanamh* (by doing it)
-   *múineadh trí Ghaeilge* (to teach through the medium of Irish)

### Common Phrases

-   `trí thine`: on fire
-   `trí gach scéal`: in summary
-   `trína chéile`: confused, mixed up
-   `tríd síos`: throughout

## Forms and Variants

Besides the standard form `trí`, you may encounter the following variants:

-   `thrí`: A common lenited form.
-   `tré`, `thré`: Older or literary forms.
-   `fríd`: The standard form in Ulster Irish.
