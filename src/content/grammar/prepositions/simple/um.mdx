The preposition `um` means "around", "about", or "concerning". It is primarily used in Munster Irish (especially Muskerry) and in formal or official language.

In many contexts, especially in Connacht and Ulster, the preposition `faoi` is used instead. This is due to a historical development where `um` evolved into `má` and then `fá`, which became associated with `faoi`. For example, *to speak about me* is commonly *labhairt fúm* (using `faoi`).

## Grammar Rules

Like other simple prepositions, `um` combines with nouns and pronouns and causes initial mutations. It governs the dative case, though this is only noticeable through the mutations it causes after the definite article.

### Initial Mutations

The mutation following `um` depends on whether the noun is preceded by the definite article `an`.

- *um banríon* - around a queen

There are significant dialectal variations to this rule:
- **Munster:** Eclipsis applies to all mutable consonants, including `d` and `t`.
- **Connacht:** Feminine nouns beginning with `s-` take a `t-` prefix: *um an tsúil* (around the eye).
- **Ulster:** Lenition is used instead of eclipsis after `um an`: *um an fhear* (around the man).

## Conjugated Forms

When `um` is followed by a pronoun, it combines to form a prepositional pronoun.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg | `umam` | `umamsa` | around me |
| 2nd sg | `umat` | `umatsa` | around you |
| 3rd sg (m) | `uime` | `uimesean` | around him, about it |
| 3rd sg (f) | `uimpi` | `uimpise` | around her |
| 1st pl | `umainn` | `umainne` | around us |
| 2nd pl | `umaibh` | `umaibhse` | around you |
| 3rd pl | `umpu` | `umpusan` | around them |

## Asking Questions

- `cé uime...?` - about whom...?
- `cad uime...?` / `cá huime...?` - about what...? / why...?

## Usage

### Temporal Usage (at, around)

`um` can specify a point in time.
- *um Nollaig* - at Christmas
- *um thráthnóna* - in the afternoon
- *um an am sin* - at that time

Note that this is for specific times and is not used for vague approximations in the way "around" can be in English (e.g., "around Christmas time").

### Locative Usage (around, along, against)

- *D’fhiaigh siad um an rí.* - They gathered around the king.
- *a bhéirt mhac uime* - his two sons accompanying him (literally, "around him")
- *um Shionainn* - along the Shannon
- *um an gcarraig* - against the rock

### Idiomatic Expressions

- **`cuir um`**: to put on, to dress.
  - *Chuir sí an cóta uimpi.* - She put on the coat.
- **`is uime sin`**: therefore, for that reason.
- **`fáth um...`**: the reason why... (though `um` is often omitted).
  - *Cén fáth (um) a bhfuil tú anseo?* - Why are you here?

### Abstract Usage (for, about, concerning)

`um` is frequently used in formal and official contexts to mean "for", "about", or "concerning". It often appears in the names of organisations and legislation.

- *An tÚdarás um Ard-Oideachas* - The Higher Education Authority (HEA)
- *An tAcht um Chosaint Sonraí* - The Data Protection Act
- *grá um...* - love for...
- *ag fonóid um...* - to laugh at...
- *troid um...* - to fight for...

### Expressing Purpose

In modern Irish, `um` is not used to express purpose in the sense of "in order to". For this meaning, use `le` or `chun`.
