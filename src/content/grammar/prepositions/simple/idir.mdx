The simple preposition `idir` primarily means "between" or "among". It is often used with `agus` to form constructions like "both... and..." or "partly... partly...".

## Grammar and Mutations

### Case

Historically, `idir` was followed by the accusative case in the singular and the dative case in the plural. In modern Irish, this distinction is rarely apparent except in older fixed phrases.

### Initial Mutations

The mutation that follows `idir` depends on its meaning and whether it is followed by the definite article.

#### Without the Article

*   **No Lenition** is used when `idir` means "between" in the sense of distance, time, difference, or contrast.
    *   *idir Corcaigh agus Baile Átha Cliath* - between Cork and Dublin
*   **Lenition** is used when `idir` means "among", "both... and...", or "partly... partly...".
    *   *idir mhná* - among women
    *   This lenition often extends to the word following `agus` or `is` in the same phrase.
        *   *idir fhear is bhean* - both man and woman
    *   However, lenition does not apply to `d`, `t`, or `s` after `agus` or `is` (following the DNTLS rule).
        *   *idir shúgradh agus dáiríre* - partly in jest, partly in earnest
    *   **Dialect Note:** In Munster (specifically Cork), lenition typically occurs only on the word directly after `idir`, not on the word after `agus`.

#### With the Article

When `idir` is followed by the definite article (`an`, `na`), the standard mutation rules for the article apply.

*   **Singular:** The noun mutates as it would in the nominative/accusative case.
    *   *idir an t-oileán agus an mhór-roinn* - between the island and the mainland (Here, `t-` prefixes the masculine noun `oileán`, and lenition applies to the feminine noun `mór-roinn`).
*   **Plural:** The noun mutates as it would in the plural after `na`, which means an h-prefix is added to vowels.
    *   *idir na hÉireannaigh* - among the Irish

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

`Idir` only has conjugated forms for the plural pronouns. For singular pronouns, the construction `idir` + pronoun + `agus` + pronoun is used.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st pl (we) | `eadrainn` | `eadrainne` | between us |
| 2nd pl (you) | `eadraibh` | `eadraibhse` | between you |
| 3rd pl (they) | `eatarthu` | `eatarthusan` | between them |

The singular forms (`eadram` for "between me", `eadrat` for "between you", etc.) are now considered archaic. Instead, you would say:
*   *idir mise is tusa* - between me and you

**Dialect Note:** In Munster and Connacht, the conjugated plural forms are often not used in enumerations with `agus`. The simple preposition is used instead.
*   *idir iad agus na daoine eile* - between them and the other people
*   *Dia idir sinn is an t-olc* - God (be) between us and evil

## Asking Questions

*   **Between whom...?**: `cé eatarthu` (+ indirect relative clause)?
*   **Between what...?**: `cad eatarthu` / `cé eatarthu` / `cá (h)eatarthu` (+ indirect relative clause)?

## Usage and Meanings

### Spatial: between

*   *idir Baile Átha Cliath agus Gaillimh* - between Dublin and Galway

### Temporal: between

*   *idir a dó agus a trí a chlog* - between two and three o'clock

### Figurative: between

*   *cogadh idir Sasana agus an Fhrainc* - a war between England and France

### Paired Enumeration: both... and...

Used with `agus` or `is`, typically with nouns in the singular.
*   *idir fhear agus bhean* - both men and women
*   *idir bhalla agus cheann* - walls and roof (i.e., the whole house)
*   *idir shean agus óg* - both young and old

### Partly... partly...

*   *idir mhagadh is dáiríre* - half in jest, half in earnest
*   *idir gháire is ghol* - between laughter and weeping

### Among

*   *rún idir mhná* - a secret among women
*   *an bhean is áille idir Éireannaigh* - the most beautiful woman among the Irish
*   *eadrainn féin an chaint seo* - this conversation is to stay between ourselves

### Idiomatic Expressions

*   *idir an dá linn* - in the meantime
*   *idir amanna* - now and then, from time to time
*   *idir eatarthu* - so-so, half and half
*   *idir lámha* - in progress, underway
*   *idir chamáin* - under discussion, in dispute
