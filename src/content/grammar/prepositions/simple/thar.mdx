The preposition `thar` primarily means "over", "past", or "beyond". It is used to describe movement, time, quantity, and comparison.

## Core Grammar

### Case

`thar` is followed by the dative case. In modern Irish, the dative form of a noun is often identical to the nominative.

### Initial Mutations

The mutation caused by `thar` depends on whether the definite article `an` is present and on the dialect.

#### Without the Article

`thar` causes **lenition**.

- *thar bhosca* (over a box)

An exception is made for some general terms, which are not lenited.

- *thar muir* (overseas)

#### With the Singular Article (`an`)

The mutation after `thar an` varies significantly between dialects.

- **Standard/Connacht:** Causes **eclipsis** on consonants, except for `d` and `t`.
  - *thar an mbosca* (over the box)
  - In Connacht, a `t-` is prefixed to feminine nouns starting with `s-`: *thar an tsúil* (past the eye).
- **Munster:** Causes **eclipsis** on all eclipseable consonants, including `d` and `t`.
- **Ulster:** Causes **lenition**.
  - *thar an bhosca* (over the box)

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

When `thar` is followed by a pronoun, they combine to form a single word known as a prepositional pronoun.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg (I) | `tharam` | `tharamsa` | past me |
| 2nd sg (you) | `tharat` | `tharatsa` | past you |
| 3rd sg (he/it) | `thairis` | `thairis-sean` | past him/it |
| 3rd sg (she/it) | `thairti` | `thairtise` | past her/it |
| 1st pl (we) | `tharainn` | `tharainne` | past us |
| 2nd pl (you) | `tharaibh` | `tharaibhse` | past you |
| 3rd pl (they) | `thartu` | `thartusan` | past them |

### Special Form: `thart`

The form `thart` means "past it" and is often used adverbially to mean "over" or "past" in a general sense.

## Usage and Meaning

### Spatial Movement

`thar` describes movement towards and beyond something, either horizontally ("past") or vertically ("over").

- *thar an droichead* (over the bridge)
- *thar an teach* (past the house / over the house)

### Time

It can refer to passing a point in time.

- *Bhí sé thar am agat.* (It was high time for you. Literally: "It was over the time at you.")

### Figurative Use

`thar` can mean "despite" or "against".

- *thar chomhairle a athar* (despite his father's advice. Literally: "over his father's advice.")

### Quantity and Excess

It is used to indicate "more than" or "beyond" a certain amount.

- *thar bhliain* (over a year / more than a year)
- *D’ól sé braon thar an gceart.* (He drank a drop too much. Literally: "...a drop over the right.")

### Comparison

`thar` is used in special comparative constructions to mean "more than" or "exceedingly". The form `thar is` (sometimes contracted to `tharas`) can also be used.

- *thar a bheith go maith* (more than good / exceedingly good)
- *thar (is) mar a shíl mé* (more than I thought)
- *tharas aon duine eile* (more than anyone else)

## Asking Questions with `thar`

To ask "past whom?" or "past what?", use the following structures followed by an indirect relative clause:

- `cé thart ...?` (past whom ...?)
- `cad thart ...?` / `cé thart ...?` / `cá thart ...?` (past what ...?)
