The simple preposition `i` primarily means "in". It is one of the most common prepositions in Irish and has several forms and important grammatical functions.

Despite its spelling, `i` is pronounced as a short schwa sound, [ə], similar to the 'a' in "about". The spelling is historical, deriving from the older form `in`.

Like many prepositions, `i` requires the dative case. In modern Irish, the dative form of a noun is usually identical to the nominative, except for a few specific nouns. A key feature of `i` is that it does not distinguish between location ("in") and movement ("into").

- *sa phota* - in the pot / into the pot

## Forms of 'i'

The preposition `i` changes its form depending on the word that follows it.

### The form 'in'

Before a vowel, `i` becomes `in`. This form does not cause any initial mutation.

- *in Éirinn* - in Ireland
- *in Albain* - in Scotland

It is also used in a few other specific contexts:
- Before the number `dhá` (two): *in dhá bhróig* (in two shoes)
- Before the possessive pronoun `bhur` (your, plural): *in bhur dteach* (in your house)
- Before titles of books or quoted text: *in "Cré na Cille"* (in "Graveyard Clay")

### With the Definite Article

When `i` is followed by the definite article (`an` or `na`), they combine into a single word.

| Combination | Result |
| :--- | :--- |
| `i` + `an` (before a consonant) | `sa` |
| `i` + `an` (before a vowel) | `san` |
| `i` + `na` (plural) | `sna` |

In some dialects, the forms `ins an` and `ins na` are used instead of `sa`/`san` and `sna`.

### The forms 'ins' and 'is'

In dialectal speech, `i` can appear as `ins` or `is` before `gach` (every) and its variants like `chuile` and `chaon`. In Standard Irish, `i ngach` is used.

- *ins gach bliain* - in every year (Standard: `i ngach bliain`)
- *sa chuile áit* - everywhere

## Initial Mutations

The preposition `i` and its combined forms cause different initial mutations.

### After 'i'

When used alone (without the article), `i` causes **eclipsis**.

- *i mBaile Átha Cliath* - in Dublin
- *i gCorcaigh* - in Cork
- *i dteach* - in a house

Note: The form `in` (used before vowels) does not cause any mutation.

### After 'sa', 'san', and 'sna'

When combined with the article, the mutation rules are more complex and vary by dialect.

- **Standard Irish**: Causes **lenition**. A **t-prefix** is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s`.
  - *sa teach* - in the house
  - *sa tsráid* - in the street (sráid is feminine)
- **Ulster**: Causes **lenition**. A **t-prefix** is added to both masculine and feminine singular nouns beginning with `s`.
  - *sa Ghearmáin* - in Germany
  - *sa tseomra* - in the room (seomra is masculine)
- **Munster**: Causes **lenition**, but `f` is **eclipsed** to `bhf`. A **t-prefix** is added to both masculine and feminine singular nouns beginning with `s`.
  - *sa bhfarraige* - in the sea
- **Connacht**: Causes **eclipsis** (except for `d` and `t`). A **t-prefix** is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s`.
  - *sa nGearmáin* - in Germany
  - *sa tsúil* - in the eye (súil is feminine)

## Conjugated and Possessive Forms

Like all simple prepositions, `i` combines with pronouns to form prepositional pronouns. It also combines with possessive pronouns.

### Prepositional Pronouns (Conjugated Forms)

These forms mean "in me", "in you", etc.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `ionam` | `ionamsa` | in me |
| 2nd sg. | `ionat` | `ionatsa` | in you |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `ann` | `annsan` | in him, in it |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `inti` | `intise` | in her, in it |
| 1st pl. | `ionainn` | `ionainne` | in us |
| 2nd pl. | `ionaibh` | `ionaibhse` | in you |
| 3rd pl. | `iontu` | `iontusan` | in them |

### With Possessive Pronouns

These forms mean "in my", "in your", etc. They are crucial for expressing states and conditions.

| Person | Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `i mo` (`im`) | in my |
| 2nd sg. | `i do` (`id`) | in your |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `ina` | in his |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `ina` | in her |
| 1st pl. | `inár` | in our |
| 2nd pl. | `in bhur` | in your |
| 3rd pl. | `ina` | in their |

**Pronunciation and Dialect Notes:**
- The forms `ina` and `inár` are often pronounced and written as `'na` and `'nár`, especially after a vowel.
- In Munster, `im` and `id` are common for `i mo` and `i do`.
- In Ulster and Connacht, `in mo` and `in do` can also be found.

## Usage

The preposition `i` has a wide range of uses beyond simple location.

### Location and Time

- **Spatial**: To indicate location *in* a space.
  - *sa chistin* - in the kitchen
- **Relative Spatial**: To indicate location *at* or *in* a part of something.
  - *i mbun an chnoic* - at the bottom of the hill
  - *in imeall na foraoise* - at the edge of the forest
- **Temporal**: To indicate a point or period *in* time.
  - *san oíche* - in the night
  - *i mí Eanáir* - in January

### Describing Qualities and States

- **Concerning**: To specify the part of something being described.
  - *briste sa lár* - broken in the middle
  - *bán sa ghruaig* - fair-haired (literally, "white in the hair")
- **Inherent Quality**: To describe a quality that is part of something or someone.
  - *Tá éirim an cheoil sa ghasúr.* - The boy has a musical aptitude.
  - *an té a bhfuil an cam ann* - the one who is naturally crooked
- **State or Condition**: To describe a temporary state.
  - *i bpian* - in pain
  - *in imní* - in anxiety

### The 'ina' Construction

A very common structure uses `bí` + `i` + possessive pronoun + noun to describe a state, role, or condition.

- **Progressive of State Verbs**: With verbs like sleeping, sitting, standing, and living.
  - *Tá mé i mo chodladh.* - I am sleeping.
  - *Tá sí ina cónaí i nGaillimh.* - She lives in Galway.
- **Classification (without the copula)**: To state a temporary or newly acquired role or profession.
  - *Tá mé i mo dhochtúir.* - I am a doctor.
  - *Tá tú i do fhear anois.* - You are a man now.
- **Becoming**: Used with the verb `éirigh` (become).
  - *Éireoidh mé i mo dhochtúir.* - I will become a doctor.
- **Describing a Mess or State**:
  - *Tá an seomra ina phraiseach.* - The room is in a mess.

### Existence

The 3rd person singular form `ann` is used with the verb `bí` to express existence, meaning "there is" or "there are".

- *Tá teach ann.* - There is a house. (A house exists.)
- *An bhfuil Gaeilge agat? Tá beagán ann.* - Do you have Irish? There's a little.

### Other Uses

- **Membership**: *sna Gardaí* (in the police), *i mbanna ceoil* (in a band)
- **Capability**: *in ann* + verbal noun means "able to".
  - *Tá mé in ann snámh.* - I am able to swim.
- **Rate**: *deich bpunt sa lá* (ten pounds per day)
- **Distribution**: *fear san fhear* (man for man)
- **With Verbs**: *glaoigh in ainm* (to call by name)

## Advanced Forms

### Adverbial Forms

The form `ann` combines with `seo`, `sin`, and `siúd` to create adverbs of place.

- `ann` + `seo` → `anseo` (here)
- `ann` + `sin` → `ansin` (there; then)
- `ann` + `siúd` → `ansiúd` (over there, yonder)

These are written as one word when used as adverbs. When meaning "in this" or "in that", they are written separately: `ann seo`, `ann sin`.

Be careful to distinguish these similar-sounding forms:
- `annsan`: in *him* (emphatic)
- `ann sin`: in *that*
- `ansin`: there / then

### In Relative Clauses

`i` combines with the indirect relative particle `a` and the past tense copula form `ar`.

| Tense | Relative Pronoun (`i` + `a`/`ar`) | Copula Form (`i` + `ar`) | Copula Form (before vowel) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `ina` | `inar` | `inarb` | in which |
| Past | `inar` | `inar` | `inarbh` | in which |

### Interrogative Forms (Questions)

- *cé ann ...?* - in whom ...? (+ indirect relative clause)
- *cad ann / cá hann ...?* - in what ...? (+ indirect relative clause)
