The preposition `ar` primarily means "on". Although written as `ar`, it is commonly pronounced as if it were spelt `oir` or `eir` ([er′]).

Historically, `ar` in Modern Irish is a coalescence of the Old Irish prepositions `ar`, `for`, and `iar`.

## Grammar Rules

### Case

The preposition `ar` is followed by the dative case. In modern Irish, this is only distinct from the nominative for certain nouns.

### Initial Mutations

The mutation caused by `ar` depends on whether the definite article `an` is present.

#### Without the Definite Article

*   **Lenition** occurs with specific, non-general nouns.
    *   *ar mhuir* - on a sea
    *   *ar bhord* - on a table
    *   *ar chíos mór* - for a high rent
*   **No lenition** occurs with abstract, general, or metaphorical terms, or when describing a state without further attributes.
    *   *ar muir* - at sea
    *   *ar bord* - on board
    *   *ar cíos* - for rent

#### With the Definite Article

*   The standard rule is **eclipsis** (except for `d` and `t`).
    *   *ar an mbord* - on the table
*   **Dialectal variations** exist:
    *   In Munster, `d` and `t` are also eclipsed.
    *   In Connacht, a t-prefix is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s-`: *ar an tsúil* (on the eye).
    *   In Ulster, **lenition** is used instead of eclipsis: *ar an bhord*.

### Forming Questions

*   **On whom...?**: `cé air` + indirect relative clause
*   **On what...?**: `cad air` / `cé air` / `cá (h)air` + indirect relative clause

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

When `ar` is followed by a pronoun, they combine to form a single word known as a prepositional pronoun.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `orm` | `ormsa` | on me |
| 2nd sg. | `ort` | `ortsa` | on you |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `air` | `airsean` | on him, on it |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `uirthi` | `uirthise` | on her, on it |
| 1st pl. | `orainn` | `orainne` | on us |
| 2nd pl. | `oraibh` | `oraibhse` | on you (pl.) |
| 3rd pl. | `orthu` | `orthusan` | on them |

### Combination with Possessive Pronouns

The forms `arna` and `arnár` are used with a verbal noun to mean "after". They replace the archaic `iar n-a` and `iar n-ár`.

*   *arna chríochnú dom* - after I had finished it
*   *an lá arna mhárach* - on the following day

In all other contexts, the simple forms `ar a`, `ar ár`, etc., are used.

*   `ar mo` - on my
*   `ar do` - on your
*   `ar a` - on his / on her / on their
*   `ar ár` - on our
*   `ar bhur` - on your (pl.)

The forms `arna` and `arnar` ("on which" or "on all who") can also appear with reflexive and general relative pronouns, but `ar a` and `ar ar` are more common.

## Usage and Idioms

The preposition `ar` has a wide range of uses beyond its literal meaning of "on".

### Location and Position

*   **On top of something**: *ar an mbord* (on the table)
*   **In a place** (especially cities): *ar an mbaile* (in the town)
*   **Wearing clothes**: *Tá cóta air.* (He is wearing a coat.) To put on clothes is `cuir ... ar ...`.
*   **Fastened to**: *ar teaghrán* (on a rope), *ar srian* (on the rein)
*   **Within range**: *ar amharc* (within sight), *ar éisteacht* (within earshot)
*   **Direction**: *ar aghaidh* (forward), *amach ar an doras* (out through the door)

### States, Emotions, and Appearance

*   **Physical and psychological states**: *Tá áthas orm.* (I am happy. lit: Joy is on me.), *Tá slaghdán uirthi.* (She has a cold.)
*   **Having a name**: *Máirtín atá air.* (He is called Martin.)
*   **External appearance**: Used to describe colours, looks, and features.
*   **Acquiring a state**: *Chuir sé fearg orm.* (He made me angry.)
*   **Abstract states**: *ar meisce* (drunk), *ar buile* (furious), *ar fheabhas* (excellent)
*   **Probability**: *Tá báisteach air.* (It looks like rain.), *Tá cogadh air.* (It looks like there will be a war.)

### Possession, Obligation, and Responsibility

*   **Parts of an object**: *Tá doirse ar an teach.* (The house has doors.)
*   **Obligation ('to have to')**: *Tá orm bainne a ól.* (I have to drink milk.)
*   **Responsibility or authority**: *Tá a báis ort.* (You are responsible for her death.), *rí ar Sasana* (King of England)

### Relationships and Interactions

*   **Indirect object with `tá ... ag`**:
    *   *Tá aithne agam ar Cháit.* - I know Cáit.
    *   *Tá grá agam ort.* - I love you. (lit: I have love on you.)
    *   *Tá airgead agam ort.* - You owe me money. (lit: I have money on you.)
*   **With certain verbs**: *féach ar* (to look at), *iarr ar* (to ask someone), *tosaigh ar* (to begin), *glaoigh ar* (to call someone)
*   **With certain nouns**: *feabhas ar* (improvement of), *scéal ar* (a story about), *ainm ar* (a name for)
*   **With certain adjectives**: *santach ar* (greedy for), *maith ar* (good for)
*   **Advantage or disadvantage (dative)**:
    *   *Bhris sé an claí uirthi.* - He broke the fence on her (to her disadvantage).
    *   *Tá bliain agam air.* - I am one year older than him. (lit: I have a year's advantage over him.)

### Time, Numbers, and Measurement

*   **Time**: *ar maidin* (in the morning), *ar a dó a chlog* (at 2 o'clock)
*   **Sequence (with verbal noun)**: *arna críochnú dom* (after I had finished it)
*   **Counting**: *duine ar fhichid* (21 people, lit: one person on twenty)
*   **Arithmetic (division)**: *a sé ar a trí sin a dó* (6 divided by 3 is 2)
*   **Dimensions**: *ar airde* (in height), *ar fad* (in length)
*   **Price**: *ar phunt* (for a pound), *Tá punt ar an úll.* (The apple costs a pound.)

### Comparisons and Classifications

*   **Similarity or likeness**: *ar dhath an róis* (rose-coloured), *ar dhéanamh báid* (boat-shaped)
*   **Equality**: *fear ar d’ainm* (a man of your name / called the same as you)
*   **Classification (part of a set)**: *Tá sé ar na fir is mó.* (He is one of the greatest men.)
*   **Identification (with superlatives/ordinals)**:
    *   *Tá sé ar an bhfear is mó acu.* - He is the greatest man of them.
    *   *Bhí sé ar an dara fear a tháinig.* - He was the second man who came.

### Other Idiomatic Uses

*   **Participation**: *ar scoil* (at school)
*   **Activity (with verbal noun)**: *ar an obair* (at work, working)
*   **Dependence**: *ag maireachtáil ar arán* (living on bread)
*   **Reason or cause**: *ar ghrá Dé* (for the love of God), *ar a thuarastal* (for his salary)
*   **Result**: *ar a iarratas* (on his application)
*   **Reinforcing an imperative**: *Scríobh ort!* (Write on! / Go on, write!)
