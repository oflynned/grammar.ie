The preposition `le` is one of the most common in Irish, with a core meaning of "with". It is used to express accompaniment, possession, comparison, and features in a wide range of idiomatic phrases. It combines with pronouns to form a set of conjugated prepositions.

<Dialect region="Munster">
  In Munster, `le` is often pronounced and written as `lé`, with a long vowel.
</Dialect>

<Details summary="Historical note">
  The modern Irish preposition `le` is a merger of two older prepositions: *le* ("with") and *re* (from Old Irish *fri*, "against"). These forms remain separate in Scottish Gaelic.
</Details>

## Grammar and Mutations

Like most simple prepositions, `le` requires the dative case for the noun that follows it, although this is only noticeable with a few nouns in the singular. It also causes initial mutations, which depend on whether the noun is preceded by the definite article.

### Without the Article

When `le` is followed directly by a noun, it causes no lenition or eclipsis. However, it adds an **h-prefix** to nouns that begin with a vowel.

*   *le hAoife* - with Aoife
*   *le Máirtín* - with Martin
*   *le spáid* - with a spade

### With the Article

When `le` is followed by the definite article `an` or `na`, it changes to the form `leis`. The mutation that follows depends on the dialect.

*   *le + an* becomes `leis an`
*   *le + na* becomes `leis na`

<Tip>
Remember that `le` always becomes `leis` before the article `an` or `na`. This is a consistent rule.
</Tip>

The standard rule is that `leis an` causes **eclipsis**.

*   *leis an mbord* - with the table

However, there are significant dialectal variations.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
  In Ulster, `leis an` causes **lenition** instead of eclipsis.
  
  *   *leis an bhord* - with the table
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
  In Connacht, `leis an` causes eclipsis. It also prefixes `t-` to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s`.
  
  *   *leis an tsúil* - with the eye
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
  In Munster, `leis an` causes eclipsis, including the eclipsis of the consonants `d` and `t`.
  
  *   *leis an dtigh* - with the house
</Dialect>

## Conjugated and Possessive Forms

Like all simple prepositions, `le` combines with personal and possessive pronouns to create a single word.

### With Personal Pronouns

These are the conjugated forms of `le`, also known as prepositional pronouns.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `liom` | `liomsa` | with me |
| 2nd sg. | `leat` | `leatsa` | with you |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `leis` | `leis-sean` | with him |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `léi` | `léise` | with her |
| 1st pl. | `linn` | `linne` | with us |
| 2nd pl. | `libh` | `libhse` | with you |
| 3rd pl. | `leo` | `leosan` | with them |

### With Possessive Pronouns

| Person | Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `le mo` | with my |
| 2nd sg. | `le do` | with your |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `lena` | with his |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `lena` | with her |
| 1st pl. | `lenár` | with our |
| 2nd pl. | `le bhur` | with your |
| 3rd pl. | `lena` | with their |

The three `lena` forms are distinguished by the initial mutation they cause on the following noun:
*   `lena` (his) causes **lenition**.
*   `lena` (her) causes **no mutation**.
*   `lena` (their) causes **eclipsis**.

<Dialect region="Munster">
  The forms `lem` (for `le mo`) and `led` (for `le do`) are common in Munster.
</Dialect>

## Asking Questions

To ask questions using `le`, you use the following forms:

*   **`cé leis?`** - With whom?
*   **`cad leis?` / `cé leis?` / `cá leis?`** - With what?
*   **`cé leis?`** (in a copular sentence) - To whom does... belong?
    *   *Cé leis an teach sin?* - To whom does that house belong?

## Relative Clause Forms

When `le` is used at the start of an indirect relative clause, it combines with the relative particle `a`.

| Tense | Relative Pronoun (`le` + `a`/`ar`) | Copula Form (`le` + `ar`) | Copula Form (before Vowel) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `lena` | `lenar` | `lenarb` | with which |
| Past | `lenar` | `lenar` | `lenarbh` | with which |

*   `lena` is followed by **eclipsis**.
*   `lenar` is followed by **lenition**.

These forms are used when the preposition comes before the relative clause.

*   *an bhean lena dtéim* - the woman with whom I go

However, it is more common in modern Irish to move the preposition to the end of the clause, using its conjugated form.

*   *an bhean a dtéim léi* - the woman that I go with her

## Usage and Meanings

The preposition `le` has a wide variety of uses beyond its core meaning of "with".

### Accompaniment

This is the most direct meaning, indicating that someone or something is accompanied.

*   *Tá mé le Máirtín* - I am with Martin.

### Means or Instrument

`le` is used to show the tool or means by which something is done.

*   *Scríobh mé an litir le peann* - I wrote the letter with a pen.
*   *Fuair sé bás leis an ocras* - He died of hunger.

### Purpose or Obligation

When used with a verbal noun, `le` can indicate purpose or something that needs to be done.

*   *Tá neart le n-ól ann* - There is plenty to drink there.
*   *Sin scéal le hinsint* - That is a story to tell.
*   *Tháinig mé le balla a phéinteáil* - I came to paint a wall.

<Note>
When `le` is used for purpose with a verbal noun beginning with a vowel, an `n-` prefix is added: `le n-ól` (to drink), `le n-ithe` (to eat).
</Note>

It can also be used in adjectival phrases.

*   *Tá sé maith le hithe* - It is good to eat.
*   *Tá sé crua le cogaint* - It is hard to chew.

### Moving Force

`le` can describe being moved by a natural force.

*   *ag seoladh le gaoth* - sailing with the wind
*   *ag snámh le sruth* - swimming with the current

### Point in Time

It can mean "at the beginning of" or "at the break of".

*   *leis an lá* - at daybreak
*   *leis an mí* - at the beginning of the month

### Cause of States

`le` is used to express the cause of a physical or psychological state.

*   *bás a fháil leis an tuirse* - to die from tiredness
*   *ag caoineadh le hól* - crying due to drinking
*   *rinne sé le greann é* - he did it for fun
*   *ní féidir liom codladh leis an ngleo* - I can't sleep because of the noise

### Behaviour Towards Others

It indicates the target of behaviour or emotion.

*   *Bí deas leis* - Be nice to him.
*   *Tá fearg orm leat* - I am angry with you.

### Location and Movement

`le` can mean "close to", "against", or "alongside".

*   *Tá an cathaoir leis an mballa* - The chair is against the wall.
*   *Sheas sé le tine* - He stood close to the fire.
*   *D'éirigh sé le balla* - He climbed up a wall.
*   *Thit sé le haill* - He fell down a cliff.

### Direction

It can indicate direction "towards" something.

*   *luí le grian* - to lie in the sun (towards the sun)
*   *Thug sé a chúl leo* - He turned his back to them.
*   *Tá súil agam le dea-scéala* - I hope for good news.

### Support or Reason

`le` can mean "for" or "in favour of".

*   *paidir le duine* - a prayer for someone
*   *cúis le rud* - a reason for something
*   *fianaise le scéal* - evidence for a story

### Duration

It is used to express a duration of time, meaning "for" or "since".

*   *Tá mé anseo le seachtain* - I have been here for a week.
*   *Ní fhaca mé le fada thú* - I haven't seen you for a while.

### Subjective Assessment

With the copula, `le` is used to express opinions, preferences, and feelings. This is a very common structure.

*   **`is maith liom`** - I like ("it is good with me")
*   **`ba mhaith liom`** - I would like
*   **`is fearr liom`** - I prefer
*   **`is fuath liom`** - I hate
*   **`is ionadh liom`** - I wonder / I find it strange
*   **`is dóigh liom`** - I think / I am of the opinion
*   **`is cuma liom`** - I don't care
*   **`is fada liom an lá`** - I find the day long.
*   **`is minic liom a dhéanamh`** - I do it often.

### Possession

With the copula, `le` indicates ownership.

*   *Is le Cáit an teach* - The house belongs to Cáit.
*   *An leatsa an peann seo?* - Is this pen yours?

This extends to relationships and authorship.

*   *cara liom* - a friend of mine
*   *iníon leis* - a daughter of his
*   *leabhar le Séamus* - a book by Séamus

### Ability

The phrase `is féidir le` means "can" or "is able to".

*   *An féidir leat cabhrú liom?* - Can you help me?

### Agreement and Success

The meaning of `tá ... le` changes depending on the structure.

*   *Tá mé leat faoi sin* - I agree with you about that.
*   *D'éirigh liom sa scrúdú* - I succeeded in the exam. (Literally, "success rose with me")

### Comparison

`le` is essential for forming comparisons of equality with `chomh` ("as").

*   *Tá sé chomh mór leat* - He is as big as you.
*   *i gcomparáid le* - in comparison with
*   *inchurtha le* - comparable to

### Continuation

Used with a verb, `le` can mean to continue an action.

*   *Abair leat* - Continue speaking. / Say on.
*   *D'oibrigh mé liom* - I continued working.

### With Specific Verbs

Many verbs require `le` to connect to their object.

*   `fan le` - to wait for
*   `buail le` - to meet
*   `cuidigh le` - to help
*   `éist le` - to listen to
*   `labhair le` - to speak with
*   `aontaigh le` - to agree with

### Taking Turns

The phrase `is leat` means "it's your turn".

*   *Is leatsa anois é* - It's your turn now.

### Taking or Bringing

The verb `tá` with a conjugated form of `le` can mean to take or bring something along.

*   *An bhfuil do mhála leat?* - Do you have your bag with you?
*   *Tá sé liom* - I have it with me / I'll bring it.

### Compound Prepositions

`le` is a component of several common compound prepositions.

*   **`maidir le`** - regarding, concerning, as for
*   **`in aice le`** - next to, beside
*   **`in éineacht le`** - together with, along with

### Adverbial Use

<Dialect region="Munster">
  In Munster especially, the form `leis` is often used as an adverb meaning "also" or "too", as an alternative to `freisin` or `fosta`.
  
  *   *Bhí mé ann, leis* - I was there, too.
</Dialect>
