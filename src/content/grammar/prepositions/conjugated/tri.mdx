The preposition `trí` primarily means "through". Like many Irish prepositions, it causes initial mutations on the following noun and combines with pronouns to form a unique set of conjugated forms. It has several common variants and a range of uses beyond its literal meaning.

Grammatically, `trí` is followed by the dative case, though in modern Irish this is mainly visible through the initial mutations it causes on the following noun.

## Forms and Variants

The standard form is `trí`, but you will also encounter several other forms in writing and speech.

-   `thrí`: A common lenited form.
-   `tré` / `thré`: Alternative forms, often found in older texts or certain dialects.
-   `fríd`: The typical form used in Ulster Irish.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
  In Ulster, `fríd` is used instead of `trí`. It still means "through" and generally follows the same mutation patterns as Ulster `trí` (i.e., lenition).
</Dialect>

## Initial Mutations

The mutation caused by `trí` depends on whether it is followed by an article.

### Without an Article

When `trí` is followed directly by a noun, it causes **lenition**.

-   *trí fhuinneoig* - through a window
-   *trí choill* - through a forest

### With the Singular Article an

When `trí` is followed by the singular article `an`, it becomes `tríd an` and causes **eclipsis**.

-   *tríd an bhfuinneoig* - through the window
-   *tríd an gcathair* - through the city

<Dialect region="Munster">
  In Munster, the consonants `d` and `t` are also eclipsed after `tríd an`, which is not always the case in the standard language.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
  In Connacht, a `t-` is prefixed to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s-`.
  - *tríd an tsúil* - through the eye
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Ulster">
  In Ulster, `fríd an` causes **lenition** instead of eclipsis.
  - *fríd an fhuinneog* - through the window
</Dialect>

### With the Plural Article na

When followed by the plural article `na`, the form is `trí na` and it causes no mutation.

-   *trí na fuinneoga* - through the windows
-   *trí na cathracha* - through the cities

## Conjugated and Possessive Forms

Like most prepositions, `trí` has a set of conjugated forms used when the object is a pronoun.

### Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st sg. | `tríom` | `tríomsa` | through me |
| 2nd sg. | `tríot` | `tríotsa` | through you |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `tríd` | `trísean` | through him |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `tríthi` | `tríthise` | through her |
| 1st pl. | `trínn` | `trínne` | through us |
| 2nd pl. | `tríbh` | `tríbhse` | through you |
| 3rd pl. | `tríothu` | `tríothusan` | through them |

<Note>
The base form `trí` and all its conjugated forms are often lenited in speech and writing, e.g., `thríom`, `thríot`, etc.
</Note>

### With Possessive Adjectives

Here are the combined forms of `trí` with possessive adjectives (like "my", "your", "our").

| Pronoun | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| my | `trí mo` | through my |
| your (sg) | `trí do` | through your |
| his / her | `trína` | through his / her |
| our | `trínár` | through our |
| your (pl) | `trí bhur` | through your |
| their | `trína` | through their |

## Usage and Meanings

### Spatial (Through)

This is the most common meaning, referring to movement into and out of a space or opening. For open terrain, `trasna` (across) is often preferred.

-   *Chuaigh sé isteach trí fhuinneoig.* - He went in through a window.
-   *Chuaigh an piléar trína gualainn.* - The bullet went through her shoulder.

Compare with `trasna`:
-   *Shiúil siad trasna na páirce.* - They walked across the field.

### Temporal (During)

`trí` can indicate duration, meaning "through" or "during" a period of time.

-   *d'fhan siad ag caint tríd an oíche* - they stayed up talking through the night
-   *tríd an scéal* - during the story

### Among or Between

It can mean "among" or "between" when something is mixed in with a group.

-   *Tá bó amháin trí na caorigh.* - There is one cow among the sheep.

### Mixing With

This extends the idea of "among" to mean "mixed with".

-   *Cuir ola tríd.* - Mix it with oil.
-   *beoir trí fhíon* - beer with wine

### Cause or Means

`trí` can express the cause of something ("because of") or the means by which something is done ("by means of").

-   *Trí bháisteach a bhí an féar fliuch.* - The grass was wet because of the rain.
-   *Tharla sé tríomsa.* - It happened because of me.
-   *D'fhoghlaim sé é tríd é a dhéanamh.* - He learned it by doing it.
-   *múineadh trí Ghaeilge* - to teach through (by means of) Irish

### Idiomatic Phrases

`trí` appears in several common idioms.

-   *trí thine* - on fire
-   *trína chéile* - confused, mixed up, upset
-   *trí gach scéal* - in summary, all in all
-   *tríd síos* - throughout

## Advanced Forms

### Question Words

To ask "through whom?" or "through what?", `trí` combines with question words. These constructions often require an indirect relative clause.

-   **Through whom...?**: `cé tríd...?`
-   **Through what...?**: `cad tríd...?` / `cé tríd...?` / `cá tríd...?`

### Relative Clauses

`trí` combines with the indirect relative particle `a` and the copula to mean "through which".

| Tense | Relative Pronoun (`trí` + `a`/`ar`) | Copula Form (`trí` + `ar`) | Copula Form (before Vowel) | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Present | `trína` | `trínar` | `trínarb` | through which |
| Past | `trínar` | `trínar` | `trínarbh` | through which |
