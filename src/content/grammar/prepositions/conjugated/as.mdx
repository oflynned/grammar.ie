The preposition `as` primarily means "out of" or "from". It is a very common preposition that you will encounter in phrases describing origin, location, and a wide variety of idiomatic expressions. Like many Irish prepositions, it combines with pronouns to form a single word and causes initial mutations on the following noun.

<Details summary="Historical note on 'a' vs 'as'">
  Historically, the preposition was often just `a`, which could be confused with the preposition `i` ("in"). The `-s` was only preserved before the definite article (e.g. `as an teach`) and in the conjugated forms (e.g. `asam`). Over time, the form `as` was extended to all contexts in Standard Irish.
</Details>

<Dialect region="Munster">
  In some parts of Munster, particularly Cork, the older form `a` (often written `a'`) is still used before a consonant.
  
  - *a' ceann* - out of a head
</Dialect>

## Case

The preposition `as` is always followed by the dative case. In modern Irish, the dative form of a noun is usually identical to the nominative, except after the singular definite article, where lenition occurs on feminine nouns and slenderisation may occur on masculine nouns.

## Initial Mutations

The mutation caused by `as` depends on whether it is followed by the definite article `an`.

### Without the Article

When `as` is followed by a noun without the article, it causes no initial mutation.

- *as baile* - out of a town
- *as Corcaigh* - from Cork

### With the Article

In Standard Irish, `as` followed by the singular definite article `an` causes **eclipsis**.

- *as an mbaile* - out of the town
- *as an gcathair* - out of the city

There are several dialectal variations to this rule.

<Note>
  In Standard Irish, `d` and `t` are not eclipsed after `as an`. However, in Munster, they are eclipsed.
  
  - Standard: *as an teach* - out of the house
  - Munster: *as an dteach* - out of the house
</Note>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
  In Connacht, a `t-` prefix is added to feminine nouns beginning with `s-` after `as an`.
  
  - *as an tsúil* - out of the eye
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Ulster">
  In Ulster, `as an` causes **lenition** instead of eclipsis.
  
  - *as an bhaile* - out of the town
</Dialect>

## Asking Questions with 'as'

To ask questions about origin or source using `as`, you use question words like `cé`, `cad`, or `cá`.

- **`cé as`?** - from whom...?
- **`cad as` / `cá has`?** - from what...? / from where...?

When used in a copula sentence, these phrases ask about a person's or thing's origin.

- *Cá has tú?* - Where are you from?
- *Is as Baile Átha Cliath mé.* - I am from Dublin.

## Conjugated Forms

When `as` is followed by a pronoun, they combine to form a single word.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. (I) | `asam` | `asamsa` | out of me |
| 2nd sg. (you) | `asat` | `asatsa` | out of you |
| 3rd sg. (he/it) | `as` | `as-san` | out of him, it |
| 3rd sg. (she/it) | `aisti` | `aistise` | out of her, it |
| 1st pl. (we) | `asainn` | `asainne` | out of us |
| 2nd pl. (you) | `asaibh` | `asaibhse` | out of you |
| 3rd pl. (they) | `astu` | `astusan` | out of them |

## Common Uses of 'as'

The preposition `as` has a wide range of meanings beyond its core sense of "out of".

### Location, Origin, and Movement

- **Spatial "out of"**:
  - *as amharc* - out of sight (the opposite of `ar amharc`, "in sight")
- **Origin "from"**: Used with the copula to state where someone is from.
  - *Is as Béal Feirste é.* - He is from Belfast.
- **Starting point "from"**:
  - *as sin go hÉirinn* - from there to Ireland
  - *i bhfad as seo* - far from here
- **Direction "away from"**:
  - *as guais* - out of danger
  - *as an imirt* - out of the game
- **Position "at"**: To indicate a quality at a specific point.
  - *géar as an mbarr* - sharp at the top

### Source and Cause

- **Source of a perception**:
  - *Tá boladh as an madadh.* - The dog smells. (Literally: "A smell is out of the dog.")
- **Source of a name ("after")**:
  - *Baisteadh as a athair é.* - He was named after his father.
- **Source of knowledge ("by")**:
  - *Aithním as a shúil é.* - I recognise him by his eye.
- **Source of a conclusion ("from that")**:
  - *Tuigim as.* - I understand from that / Therefore I understand.
- **Psychological source ("out of")**:
  - *as fearg* - out of anger
- **Source of pride, thanks, or trust ("of", "for")**:
  - *Tá bród orm asat.* - I am proud of you. (Literally: "Pride is on-me out-of-you.")
  - *Go raibh maith agat as an obair seo.* - Thank you for this work.
  - *Tá muinín agam aisti.* - I trust her. (Literally: "Trust is at-me out-of-her.")

### Composition, Selection, and Medium

- **Composition ("of")**: Used for materials.
  - *déanta as adhmad* - made of wood
- **Selection ("from", "of")**:
  - *bain rogha as* - to choose from
  - *duine as beirt* - one of two
- **Medium ("in", "with")**:
  - *Nigh as uisce é.* - Wash it in water.
- **Language ("in")**:
  - *as Gaeilge* - in Irish
  - *as Béarla* - in English

### State and Condition

- **Out of function**:
  - *as ord* - out of order
  - *as úsáid* - out of use
- **Mistaken or lost**:
  - *as bealach* - off the path, mistaken
- **Lack ("without")**:
  - *as obair* - without work, unemployed
- **Turned off**:
  - *Tá an raidió as.* - The radio is off.
  - *Chas mé an tine as.* - I turned off the fire.
- **Alone**: The phrase `as ... féin` means "alone".
  - *Tá mé ag siúl as féin.* - I am walking alone.
  - *Tá asainn féin anois.* - We are alone now.
