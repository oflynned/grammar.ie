The preposition `ó` primarily means "from" or "since". It is used to talk about spatial, temporal, and figurative starting points. Like many Irish prepositions, it combines with pronouns to form a single word and causes initial mutations on the following noun.

## Grammar and Mutations

### Case

Historically, `ó` governs the dative case. In modern standard Irish, the dative form of a noun is usually identical to the nominative, so this rule is only noticeable with a few specific nouns.

### Initial Mutations

The mutation caused by `ó` depends on whether it is followed by the article `an`.

When `ó` is used without an article, it causes lenition.

<MutationCard
  title="ó + noun"
  before="ó Ciarraí"
  after="ó Chiarraí"
  rule="ó causes lenition on a following noun when there is no article."
/>

*Tá sé ag teacht ó Chiarraí.* - He is coming from Kerry.

When `ó` is followed by the singular article `an`, they combine to form `ón`. This new form causes eclipsis in the standard language.

<MutationCard
  title="ón + noun (singular)"
  before="ón Gearmáin"
  after="ón nGearmáin"
  rule="ón causes eclipsis on a following singular noun."
/>

*Tháinig an litir ón nGearmáin.* - The letter came from Germany.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
  In Ulster Irish, `ón` causes lenition instead of eclipsis.
  <br/>
  *Tháinig an litir ón Ghearmáin.* - The letter came from Germany.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
  In Munster, eclipsis after `ón` also applies to `d` and `t`, which are not eclipsed in the standard language.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
  In Connacht, a `t-`prefix is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s-` after `ón`.
  <br/>
  *ón tsúil* - from the eye
</Dialect>

When `ó` is followed by the plural article `na`, they remain separate (`ó na`) and no mutation occurs.

*Faigh cabhair ó na comharsana.* - Get help from the neighbours.

## Forms with Pronouns (Conjugated Forms)

When `ó` is followed by a pronoun like "me", "you", or "them", they merge into a single word. These are often called prepositional pronouns or conjugated prepositions.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. (I) | `uaim` | `uaimse` | from me |
| 2nd sg. (you) | `uait` | `uaitse` | from you |
| 3rd sg. (he/it) | `uaidh` | `uaidhsean` | from him/it |
| 3rd sg. (she/it) | `uaithi` | `uaithise` | from her/it |
| 1st pl. (we) | `uainn` | `uainne` | from us |
| 2nd pl. (you) | `uaibh` | `uaibhse` | from you |
| 3rd pl. (they) | `uathu` | `uathusan` | from them |

*Fuair mé litir uait.* - I got a letter from you.
*Chonaic mé uaim é.* - I saw it from me (i.e., from my perspective).

## Forms with Possessive Adjectives

When `ó` is followed by a possessive adjective (my, your, his, etc.), the forms can change and cause different mutations.

- **`ó mo`** (from my) + lenition: *ó mo chara* - from my friend
- **`ó do`** (from your) + lenition: *ó do theach* - from your house
- **`óna`** (from his) + lenition: *óna athair* - from his father
- **`óna`** (from her) + no mutation: *óna máthair* - from her mother
- **`ónár`** (from our) + eclipsis: *ónár scoil* - from our school
- **`ó bhur`** (from your, pl.) + eclipsis: *ó bhur dteach* - from your house
- **`óna`** (from their) + eclipsis: *óna gcairde* - from their friends

## Usage and Common Phrases

### Spatial and Temporal Origin
`ó` indicates a starting point in space or time.

- **Spatial:** *Tar ón bposta.* - Come from the post office.
- **Temporal:** *Tá mé anseo ó mhaidin.* - I have been here since this morning.
- **Conjunction:** It can also act as a conjunction meaning "since" or "as".
  *ó bhí sí tinn* - since she was ill

### Figurative Origin and Source
It can also indicate a non-physical source.

- *Fuair mé cabhair ó chairde.* - I got help from friends.
- *Is fear ó Thrá Lí é.* - He is a man from Tralee.
- *Tá sé míle uaidh.* - It is a mile from it.

### Expressing Need or Want
A very common idiom uses `ó` to express need or want, often with the verb `teastaigh` or `tá`. The structure is: `Tá [object] ó [subject]`.

- *Tá bainne uait.* - You need milk. (Literally: "Milk is from you")
- *Teastaíonn spórt uaim.* - I want fun.
- *Teastaíonn uaim labhairt leat.* - I want to speak with you.

### Cause or Reason
`ó` can show the reason for a state or feeling.

- *Bhí sé bodhar ó thorann.* - He was deaf from the noise.
- *Tá mé tuirseach ó shiúil.* - I am tired from walking.

### In Common Phrases
`ó` appears in many fixed expressions.

- **With adjectives:** *Tá sé deas uait.* - That is kind of you.
- **From... to... (`ó...go`):** *ó thús go deireadh* - from beginning to end; *ó am go ham* - from time to time.
- **With numbers:** *ó chúig phunt go deich bpunt* - from five pounds to ten pounds.
- **With time:** *óna trí go dtí a hocht a chlog* - from 3 o'clock to 8 o'clock.

### Removal or Absence
`ó` can imply something is away from or absent from someone.

- *Is cuma liom agam nó uaim é.* - I don't care if I have it or not. (Literally: "...at me or from me...")
- *Bí ó bhaol.* - Be free from danger.

### In Mathematics (Subtraction)
`ó` is used for subtraction, but the word order is different from English. The number being subtracted comes first.

- *a dó óna trí sin a haon* - 3 - 2 = 1 (Literally: "two from the three, that is one")

<Warning>
The word order in Irish subtraction is the reverse of English. You say "two from three" (`dó ó thrí`) where English says "three minus two".
</Warning>

## Advanced Forms

### In Relative Clauses
When used with the indirect relative particle `a` or `ar`, `ó` combines to form `óna` or `ónar`.

| Tense | Relative Form | Copula Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | `óna` | `ónar(b)` | from which |
| Past | `ónar` | `ónar(bh)` | from which |

- *An fear óna bhfuair tú an t-airgead.* - The man from whom you got the money.
- *An t-am ónar thosaigh sé.* - The time from which it started.

### Asking Questions
To ask "from whom?" or "from what?", you use the conjugated forms of `ó` with an interrogative word.

- *Cé uaidh a fuair tú é?* - From whom did you get it?
- *Cad uaidh a tharla sé?* - From what did it happen?
