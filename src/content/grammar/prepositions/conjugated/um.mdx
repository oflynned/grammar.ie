The preposition `um` generally means "around" or "about". While it is used in Standard Irish, particularly in formal contexts and set phrases, it is most commonly heard in everyday speech in Munster. In other dialects, the preposition `faoi` often replaces it.

<Details summary="Historical note on 'um' and 'faoi'">
The preposition `um` underwent a sound change in some dialects, becoming `má` and then `fá`. This form `fá` was then often associated with `faoi` ("under", "about"). As a result, many speakers now use `faoi` (or its dialectal variant `fú` or `fá`) to mean "about". For example, *labhairt fúm* ("to speak about me") is far more common than *labhairt umam*. Ulster Irish often maintains a distinction between `fá` ("about") and `faoi` ("under").
</Details>

## Initial Mutations

The preposition `um` causes different mutations depending on whether it is followed by the definite article `an`.

### Without the Article

When `um` is followed by a noun without the article, it causes lenition on certain consonants.

<MutationCard
  title="Lenition after 'um'"
  before="c, d, f, g, s, t"
  after="ch, dh, fh, gh, sh, th"
  rule="Lenites c, d, f, g, s, and t. Does not affect b, m, or p."
/>

*um Nollaig* - at Christmas
*um Shionainn* - along the Shannon

Note that `b`, `m`, and `p` are not lenited after `um`.
* *um banríon* - around a queen

### With the Article

When `um` is followed by the definite article `an`, it causes eclipsis in the standard language.

<MutationCard
  title="Eclipsis after 'um an'"
  before="b, c, d, f, g, p, t"
  after="mb, gc, nd, bhf, ng, bp, dt"
  rule="Causes eclipsis on nouns following 'um an'."
/>

* *um an mbaile* - around the town
* *um an gcoirthe* - against the stone

Dialectal forms vary significantly.

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, `d` and `t` are consistently eclipsed after `um an`.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In Connacht, a t-prefix is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s-`.
* *um an tsúil* - around the eye
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, lenition is used instead of eclipsis after `um an`.
* *um an fhear* - around the man
</Dialect>

## Forms with Pronouns

Like all simple prepositions, `um` combines with pronouns to create a single word. These are known as prepositional pronouns or conjugated prepositions.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. (I) | `umam` | `umamsa` | around me |
| 2nd sg. (you) | `umat` | `umatsa` | around you |
| 3rd sg. (he/it) | `uime` | `uimesean` | around him, about it |
| 3rd sg. (she) | `uimpi` | `uimpise` | around her |
| 1st pl. (we) | `umainn` | `umainne` | around us |
| 2nd pl. (you) | `umaibh` | `umaibhse` | around you |
| 3rd pl. (they) | `umpu` | `umpusan` | around them |

*Chuir sí an cóta uimpi.* - She put the coat on.
*D’fhiafraigh siad uime.* - They asked about him.

## Question Words

The preposition `um` combines with question words to ask "about what?" or "about whom?". These forms are followed by an indirect relative clause.

- **cé uime** - about whom...?
- **cad uime / cá huime** - about what...? / why...?

*Cá huime a bhfuil tú anseo?* - Why are you here?

## Common Uses of 'um'

### Time

`um` can mean "at" a specific time or event.

* *um Nollaig* - at Christmas
* *um thráthnóna* - in the afternoon
* *um an am sin* - at that time

<Warning>
Unlike "around" in English, `um` is not used for approximate times (e.g., "around 5 o'clock"). For that, you would use `thart ar`.
</Warning>

### Location and Movement

It can mean "around", "along", or "against".

* *D’iadh siad um an rí.* - They gathered around the king.
* *a bhéirt mhac uime* - his two sons accompanying him (literally, "around him")
* *um Shionainn* - along the Shannon
* *um an gcoirthe* - against the stone

### Expressing 'to put on'

The verb `cuir` combined with `um` means "to put on" clothing.

* *Chuir sí an cóta uimpi.* - She put on the coat. (Literally: She put the coat around herself.)

### Expressing 'about' or 'concerning'

This usage is common in formal and official language, such as in the titles of organisations and laws.

* *An tÚdarás um Ard-Oideachas* - The Higher Education Authority
* *An tAcht um Chosaint Sonraí* - The Data Protection Act

It can also appear with certain nouns and verbs:
* *grá um* - love for
* *ag fonóid um* - laughing at
* *troid um* - to fight for

### Idiomatic and Formal Uses

`is uime` means "therefore" or "for that reason".

* *Is uime sin a tháinig mé.* - That is why I came.

The phrase `fáth um` means "the reason why", though `um` is often omitted in modern Irish.

* *Cén fáth (um) a bhfuil tú anseo?* - Why are you here?

<Warning>
To express purpose ("in order to"), Irish uses `le` or `chun`, never `um`.
</Warning>
