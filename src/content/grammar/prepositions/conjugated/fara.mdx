The preposition `fara` means "together with" or "beside". While less common in modern standard Irish than alternatives like `in éineacht le`, it is still found in certain dialects and set phrases. It requires the dative case and does not cause any initial mutations on the following noun.

## Case and Initial Mutations

`fara` takes the dative case. It does not cause lenition or eclipsis on the noun that follows it, whether the noun is preceded by the definite article or not.

- **Without the article:** no mutation
- **With the article:** no mutation

## Forms with the Article

When `fara` is followed by the definite article `an` or `na`, it combines to form `fairis` or `farais`.

- `fara` + `an` becomes **`fairis an`** (or `farais an`)
- `fara` + `na` becomes **`fairis na`** (or `farais na`)

## Conjugated Forms (Prepositional Pronouns)

Like many Irish prepositions, `fara` combines with personal pronouns to create a single conjugated form.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. (I) | `faram` | `faramsa` | beside me |
| 2nd sg. (you) | `farat` | `faratsa` | beside you |
| 3rd sg. (he/it) | `fairis` | `fairis-san` | beside him/it |
| 3rd sg. (she/it) | `farae` | `faraese` | beside her/it |
| 1st pl. (we) | `farainn` | `farainne` | beside us |
| 2nd pl. (you) | `faraibh` | `faraibhse` | beside you |
| 3rd pl. (they) | `faru` | `farusan` | beside them |

<Note>
In many contexts, you are more likely to hear `fara liom`, `fara leat`, etc., using the conjugated forms of `le`. The phrase `in éineacht le` is also a very common alternative to express "together with".
</Note>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In the Cois Fharraige dialect of Connacht, the related preposition `fros` has the following forms: `frum`, `frat`, `frois`, `frae`, `fruinn`, `fraoib`, `frób`. Alternatively, `fru liom`, `fru leat`, etc. may be used.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, combined forms with possessive pronouns can occur, such as `farana` for "beside his".
</Dialect>

## Usage and Examples

### Together with

The primary meaning of `fara` is "together with" or "in the company of".

- *Bhí Mícheál faru.* - Michael was with them.
- *Buachaill amháin atá fanta faram anso sa bhaile.* - One boy has remained with me here in the village.
- *Ná suífeá farainn?* - Won't you sit with us?
- *Tháinig siad chun Eoin agus dúirt siad leis: "A raibí, an té a bhí farat lastall den Iordáin... féach, tá seisean ag déanamh baiste..."* - They came to John and said to him: "Rabbi, he who was with you across the Jordan... look, he is baptising..." (John 3:26)

### As well as / Besides

`fara` can also mean "as well as" or "in addition to".

- *fara cabhair Dé* - so help me God (literally, "besides the help of God")
- *punt fara do cheart* - a pound more than your due (literally, "a pound besides your right")

### Compared with / Instead of

In a comparative sense, `fara` can mean "compared with" or "instead of".

- *Is beag é fara a bhfuair tusa.* - It is little compared with what you received.
- *fara a bheith...* - instead of being...

### Origin of 'freisin' (also)

The common Irish adverb `freisin`, meaning "also" or "too", derives from `fara`.

<Tip>
The word `freisin` is a contraction of the phrase `fairis sin`, which literally means "beside that" or "in addition to that". Over time, `fairis sin` evolved into `freis sin` and finally into the modern form `freisin`.
</Tip>
