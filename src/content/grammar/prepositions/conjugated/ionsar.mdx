The preposition `ionsar` means "to" or "towards". It is a compound preposition primarily used in Ulster Irish that triggers specific sound changes, known as initial mutations, on the noun that follows it.

<Details summary="Etymology and spelling">
  The form `ionsar` comes from the older phrase `d'ionsaí ar`, which literally means "to approach on". It is a combination of the verbal noun `ionsaí` ("approaching") and the preposition `ar` ("on"). Because of this origin, it is sometimes written as `ionns'ar`. Other historical or alternative forms include `inseair` and `ionnsaighe`.
</Details>

## Initial Mutations

`Ionsar` causes the following noun to mutate. The type of mutation depends on whether the noun is preceded by the definite article `an`.

### Without an Article

When `ionsar` is followed directly by a noun, it causes lenition.

*   *ionsar theach Bhriain* - to Brian's house

### With the Singular Article

When `ionsar` is followed by the singular definite article `an`, the mutation that follows depends on the dialect.

<Dialect region="Ulster">
  In Ulster, where `ionsar` is most common, it causes lenition after the article.

  *   *ionsar an ghairdín* - to the garden
</Dialect>

In other dialects, if `ionsar` is used at all, it typically causes eclipsis (except on words beginning with `d` or `t`).

*   *ionsar an ngairdín* - to the garden

## Question Forms

To ask "to whom?" or "to what?", you can use the following phrases:

*   **`cé ionsair?`** - to whom?
*   **`cá hionsar?`** - to what? / where to?

## Conjugated Forms

Like many Irish prepositions, `ionsar` combines with pronouns to create a single word. These are known as conjugated prepositions or prepositional pronouns.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. (I) | `ionsorm` | `ionsormsa` | to me |
| 2nd sg. (you) | `ionsort` | `ionsortsa` | to you |
| 3rd sg. (he) | `ionsair` | `ionsairsean` | to him |
| 3rd sg. (she) | `ionsuirthi` | `ionsuirthise` | to her |
| 1st pl. (we) | `ionsorainn` | `ionsorainne` | to us |
| 2nd pl. (you) | `ionsoraibh` | `ionsoraibhse` | to you |
| 3rd pl. (they) | `ionsorthu` | `ionsorthusan` | to them |

## Usage

The use of `ionsar` is largely confined to Ulster Irish. It appears in phrases indicating direction or movement towards something.

*   *druid ionsar* - turn towards
*   *tar ionsar an teach* - come to the house
