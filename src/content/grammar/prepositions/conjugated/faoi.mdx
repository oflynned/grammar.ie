The preposition `faoi` primarily means "under", but it also has a very common secondary meaning of "about". It triggers different initial mutations depending on whether it is followed by the definite article, and it combines with pronouns to form a distinct set of words known as conjugated prepositions.

## Forms and Dialect Variants

The standard form of this preposition is `faoi`, which is also the most common form in Connacht. Other dialectal forms exist and are widely used.

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, the form `fé` is common.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, both `faoi` and `fá` are used, but with different meanings. `faoi` is used for "under", while `fá` is used for "over" or "around".
</Dialect>

<Note>
The form `fá` is also an older standard form that you may encounter in older texts. Less common variants include `fó` and `fí`.
</Note>

## Initial Mutations

`faoi` causes different mutations depending on whether it is followed by the definite article (`an`).

### Without the Definite Article

When `faoi` is followed directly by a noun, it causes lenition.

<MutationCard
  title="Lenition"
  before="faoi + bord"
  after="faoi bhord"
  rule="Causes lenition on the following noun."
/>

### With the Definite Article

When `faoi` is combined with the singular definite article (`an` becomes `faoin`), it causes eclipsis.

<MutationCard
  title="Eclipsis"
  before="faoin + bord"
  after="faoin mbord"
  rule="Causes eclipsis on the following noun."
/>

*   *faoin mbord* - under the table

There are several dialectal exceptions to this rule.

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, `d` and `t` are also eclipsed after `faoin`, which does not happen in the standard language.
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In Connacht, a `t-`prefix is added to feminine singular nouns beginning with `s-`.
*   *faoin tsúil* - under the eye
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Ulster">
In Ulster, `faoin` causes lenition instead of eclipsis.
*   *faoin bhord* - under the table
</Dialect>

## Question Forms

To ask "under whom?" or "under what?", you use the following structures, which require an indirect relative clause.

*   `cé faoi...?` - under whom...?
*   `cad faoi / cé faoi / cá faoi...?` - under what...?

## Combining with the Definite Article

`faoi` merges with the singular definite article `an`, but not with the plural `na`.

*   `faoi` + `an` = `faoin`
*   `faoi` + `na` = `faoi na`

## Conjugated Forms (with Pronouns)

Like all Irish prepositions, `faoi` combines with personal pronouns to create a single word.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `fúm` | `fúmsa` | under me |
| 2nd sg. | `fút` | `fútsa` | under you |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `faoi` | `faoisean` | under him |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `fúithi` | `fúithise` | under her |
| 1st pl. | `fúinn` | `fúinne` | under us |
| 2nd pl. | `fúibh` | `fúibhse` | under you |
| 3rd pl. | `fúthu` | `fúthusan` | under them |

`faoi` does not merge with possessive pronouns.

| Person | Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st sg. | `faoi mo` | under my |
| 2nd sg. | `faoi do` | under your |
| 3rd sg. (m) | `faoina` | under his |
| 3rd sg. (f) | `faoina` | under her |
| 1st pl. | `faoinár` | under our |
| 2nd pl. | `faoi bhur` | under your |
| 3rd pl. | `faoina` | under their |

## Combining with the Relative Pronoun

`faoi` can combine with the relative pronoun `a` and the copula. This structure is less common in modern speech than placing the preposition at the end of the clause.

| Tense | Relative Pronoun (`faoi + a/ar`) | Copula Form (`faoi + ar`) | Copula Form (before Vowel) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Present | `faoina` | `faoinar` | `faoinarb` | under which |
| Past | `faoinar` | `faoinar` | `faoinarbh` | under which |

*   *an chathaoir faoina bhfuil mo leabhar* - the chair under which my book is
*   *an crann faoina raibh fás* - the tree that grew (literally, "the tree under which there was growth")

<Note>
A more common way to phrase the first example is: *an chathaoir a bhfuil mo leabhar faoi*.
</Note>

The older form `fá` has relative forms without the linking `-n-`.

*   *A Róisín, ná bíodh brón ort fár éirigh duit.* - My Róisín, do not be sad about what happened to you.

## Usage and Meaning

`faoi` has a wide range of uses beyond its core spatial meaning.

### Spatial: Under

*   *faoin gcathaoir* - under the chair
*   *faoi uisce* - under water
*   *faoi thalamh* - underground
*   *faoin oileán* - on the leeward side of the island
*   *faoin dtuath* - to/in the countryside

### Support or Burden

It can describe something supporting a weight or someone bearing a burden.

*   *bheith faoin ualach* - to bear a burden
*   *na colúin atá faoi* - the pillars that support it

### Contact: Against

*   *faoin mballa* - against the wall

### Driving Force or Urge

`faoi` is used idiomatically to express an internal drive, haste, or natural process like growth.

*   *Tá siúl fúm.* - I am walking quickly.
*   *Tá fuadar fút.* - You are in a hurry.
*   *Tá fás faoin gcrann.* - The tree is growing.

### Intention

The phrase `Tá fúm...` means "I intend to...".

*   *Tá fút teacht.* - You intend to come.

### Topic: About

This is a very common use of `faoi`.

*   *caint faoi...* - to speak about...
*   *imní faoi...* - worry about...
*   *áthas faoi...* - joy about...
*   *brón faoi...* - sorrow for/about...
*   *magadh faoi...* - mockery of...
*   *gáire faoi...* - laughter about...

### Time: Around or At

*   *faoi Nollaig* - at Christmas
*   *faoi Bhealtaine* - in May
*   *faoi seo* - now
*   *faoin am seo* - at this time

### Numbers and Arithmetic

`faoi` is used to mean "times" when counting occurrences or in multiplication.

*   *faoi dhó* - twice
*   *faoi sé* - six times
*   *A trí faoina ceathair sin a dó dhéag.* - Three times four is twelve.

### State or Condition

It can describe being covered by, enveloped in, or in a particular state.

*   *faoi bhláth* - in bloom (literally, "under bloom")
*   *faoi sholas an lae* - in daylight
*   *faoi bhrón* - sad (literally, "under sorrow")
*   *faoi shéan* - happy (literally, "under a good omen")
