The preposition `idir` primarily means "between" or "among". Its meaning can change the mutation it causes on the following word. Unusually for a preposition, its conjugated forms are only used for plural pronouns in modern standard Irish.

## Mutations after idir

The initial mutation caused by `idir` depends on its meaning and whether it is followed by the definite article `an`.

### Without the Article

When `idir` is not followed by the article, the mutation depends on the context:

*   **No lenition** for distance, time, difference, or contrast.
    *   *idir Corcaigh agus Baile Átha Cliath* - between Cork and Dublin

*   **Lenition** when it means "among", "both... and...", or "partly... partly...".
    *   *idir mhná* - among women

When used with `agus` or `is` to mean "both... and...", the lenition also applies to the word after `agus`/`is`.
*   *idir fhear is bhean* - both man and woman

However, lenition does not apply to `d`, `t`, or `s` after `agus`/`is`.
*   *idir shúgradh agus dáiríre* - partly in jest, partly in earnest

<Dialect region="Cork">
In the dialect of Cork, lenition usually occurs only on the word directly after `idir`, not on the word following `agus`. This happens regardless of the meaning.
</Dialect>

### With the Article

When `idir` is followed by the definite article, the standard mutation rules for the article apply.

*   **In the singular**, the noun mutates as it would in the nominative case.
    *   *idir an t-oileán agus an mhór-roinn* - between the island and the mainland (`oileán` is masculine, `mór-roinn` is feminine)

*   **In the plural**, the noun mutates as it would in the plural nominative case, which means a h-prefix is added to vowels.
    *   *idir na hÉireannaigh* - among the Irish

## Conjugated Forms (Plural Only)

In modern Irish, `idir` is only combined with pronouns in the plural. To express "between me and you", you use the preposition with the standalone pronouns: *idir mise agus tusa*.

| Person | Standard Form | Emphatic Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1st Plural (we) | `eadrainn` | `eadrainne` | between us |
| 2nd Plural (you) | `eadraibh` | `eadraibhse` | between you |
| 3rd Plural (they) | `eatarthu` | `eatarthusan` | between them |

<Note>
Singular forms like `eadram` (between me), `eadrat` (between you), and `eidirthi` (between her) are now considered archaic.
</Note>

In Munster and Connacht, the conjugated plural forms are not typically used in enumerations with `agus`. Instead, the preposition is repeated or implied with the standalone pronoun.

*   *idir iad agus na daoine eile* - between them and the other people
*   *Dia idir sinn is an t-olc* - God (be) between us and evil

## Usage and Examples

### Spatial: "between"

*   *idir Baile Átha Cliath agus Gaillimh* - between Dublin and Galway

### Temporal: "between"

*   *idir an dó agus an trí* - between two and three (o'clock)

### Figurative: "between"

*   *cogadh idir Sasana agus an Fhrainc* - a war between England and France

### Paired Enumeration: "both... and..."

This construction uses `idir` with `agus` or `is` and triggers lenition.

*   *idir fhear agus bhean* - both men and women
*   *idir balla agus ceann* - walls and roof
*   *idir shean agus óg* - both young and old

### Partly... partly...

*   *idir mhagadh is dáiríre* - half in jest, half in earnest
*   *idir gháire is ghol* - between laughter and weeping

### Among

*   *eadrainn féin an chaint seo* - this is to stay between us (literally, "among ourselves this talk")
*   *rún idir mhná* - a secret among women
*   *an bhean is áille idir Éireannaigh* - the most beautiful woman among the Irish

### Idiomatic Phrases

*   *idir an dá linn* - in the meantime
*   *idir amanna* - now and then
*   *idir eatarthu* - so-so, half and half
*   *idir lámha* - in progress, underway
*   *idir chamáin* - under discussion

## Asking Questions

To ask "between whom?" or "between what?", you use the conjugated form `eatarthu` with the appropriate question word. This construction requires an indirect relative clause.

*   *Cé eatarthu...?* - Between whom...?
*   *Cad eatarthu / Cé eatarthu / Cá (h)eatarthu...?* - Between what...?

<Details summary="Historical note on case">
Historically, `idir` governed the accusative case in the singular and the dative case in the plural. In modern Irish, the accusative singular is identical to the nominative for almost all nouns. The distinct dative plural forms (ending in `-ibh`) are now archaic and only survive in fixed phrases, so for learners, this distinction is not a practical concern.
</Details>
