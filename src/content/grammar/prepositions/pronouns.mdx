In Irish, prepositions like 'at' (`ag`) or 'on' (`ar`) merge with personal pronouns like 'me' or 'you' to form a single word. These combined forms are known as **prepositional pronouns** or, more technically, **conjugated prepositions** (`réamhfhocail réimnithe`). This is a core feature of Irish grammar, and you will encounter these forms in almost every sentence.

## Reference Table

The table below provides a comprehensive reference for the most common prepositional pronouns. Note that some prepositions, like `idir`, only conjugate in the plural forms.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ag` (at) | `agam` | `agat` | `aige` | `aici` | `againn` | `agaibh` | `acu` |
| `ar` (on) | `orm` | `ort` | `air` | `uirthi` | `orainn` | `oraibh` | `orthu` |
| `as` (out of) | `asam` | `asat` | `as` | `aisti` | `asainn` | `asaibh` | `astu` |
| `chuig` (to) | `chugam` | `chugat` | `chuige` | `chuici` | `chugainn` | `chugaibh` | `chucu` |
| `de` (of, from) | `díom` | `díot` | `de` | `di` | `dínn` | `díbh` | `díobh` |
| `do` (to, for) | `dom` | `duit` | `dó` | `dí` | `dúinn` | `daoibh` | `dóibh` |

## Common Patterns

While the forms for the third person vary, there are consistent endings for the first and second persons, which can help you learn them more quickly.

-   1st person singular (I) forms always end in `-m`.
-   2nd person singular (you) forms always end in `-t`.
-   1st person plural (we) forms always end in `-inn` or `-ainn`.
-   2nd person plural (you) forms always end in `-ibh` or `-aibh`.

<Tip>
Think of the endings as markers for the person: `m` for *mé*, `t` for *tú*. The plural endings `-inn` and `-ibh` are also very consistent across the verb system.
</Tip>

The third person forms are more irregular because they derive from older dative and accusative case forms of the pronouns.

## Dialect and Historical Notes

You may encounter variations in spelling and pronunciation in different dialects or older texts.

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In the Irish of Connemara, the 3rd person plural ending `-u` is often pronounced /əb/, and the ending `-ibh` is pronounced /b/. For example, `acu` sounds like *acab*, `orthu` like *orthab*, and `dóibh` like *dób*.
</Dialect>

Some forms found in older literature are now obsolete but are useful to recognise.

<Details summary="Historical forms and spellings">
The preposition `idir` ("between") used to have conjugated singular forms like `eadram` (for `idir mé`) and `eadrat` (for `idir thú`), which are no longer in common use.

Other prepositions that are now rare or obsolete also had conjugated forms, such as `seach` ("past"), which gave `seacham`, `seachat`, etc., and `uas`/`os` ("over"), which gave `uasam`, `uasat`, etc.

The 3rd person plural ending was also commonly spelt with `-a` instead of `-u` in the past (e.g., `aca` instead of `acu`, `ortha` instead of `orthu`).
</Details>
