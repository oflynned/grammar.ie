Compound prepositions are phrases that function as a single preposition. They are formed by combining a simple preposition with a noun, and sometimes another preposition. While their meaning can sometimes be guessed from their parts, it is often idiomatic.

## Two-Part Compound Prepositions

The most common type of compound preposition consists of two parts: a simple preposition followed by a noun. The noun that follows this structure is put into the genitive case.

*ar nós na ndaoine* - like the people (lit. "on manner of the people")

<Note>
When using a pronoun with these prepositions, you use a possessive pronoun before the noun part of the phrase, not a personal pronoun after it. For example, to say "like me", you say `ar mo nós` (lit. "on my manner").

However, a few temporal prepositions like `ar feadh`, `go ceann`, and `i rith` do not typically use pronouns.
</Note>

Some compound prepositions can also function as conjunctions, often followed by `go` ("that").

*d'ainneoin go raibh sé anseo* - despite the fact that he was here

### Prepositions with `ar`

| Preposition | Translation | Literal Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ar aghaidh` | opposite | on face | *ar aghaidh an dorais* - opposite the door |
| `ar ceann` | at the head/end of, before | on head | *na daoine ar ár gceann* - the people before us |
| `ar chúl` | behind | on back | *ar chúl an tí* - behind the house |
| `ar feadh` | for (the duration of), all along | on extent | *ar feadh seachtaine* - for a week |
| `ar fud` | throughout, amidst | on region | *ar fud na tíre* - throughout the country |
| `ar nós` | like, after the fashion of | on manner | *ar mo nós* - like me |
| `ar son` | because of, for the sake of | on welfare | *ar son Dé* - for God's sake |
| `ar tí` | on the track of | on track | *ar mo thí* - after me |

## Dialectal Forms and Variations

In spoken Irish, some compound prepositions are often simplified or contracted.

<Dialect region="Munster">
In Munster, `ar feadh` is often shortened to `feadh` or `fan`.
*fan na seachtaine* - for the week
*fan chósta an oirthir* - along the west coast

Similarly, `de réir` becomes `dréir` or `déir`.
*déir na haimsire* - according to the season
</Dialect>

<Dialect region="Connacht">
In Connacht, `de réir` can be shortened to `réir` or `léir`.
*léir na haimsire* - according to the season

The preposition `le haghaidh` is often contracted to `l'aghaidh`.
</Dialect>

<Note>
The preposition `i ndiaidh` ("after") has several common variants, including `as diaidh`, `i ndéidh`, and `in éis`.
</Note>

## Other Two-Part Forms

A small number of two-part prepositions have a simple preposition as their second component. These behave like three-part prepositions, as the final preposition governs the following noun.

- `maidir le` - concerning, regarding
  - *maidir leis an litir* - concerning the letter
- `mar le` - concerning, according to (a shortened form of `maidir le`)
- `láimh le` - beside (lit. "hand with")
  - *láimh le droichead* - beside a bridge
- `laistiar de` / `taobh thiar de` - behind
  - *taobh thiar den teach* - behind the house
- `diomaite de` - except
  - *diomaite den Ghaeilge* - except for Irish

<Details summary="Etymological note">
The preposition `maidir le` is likely a shortened form of the older phrase *mar a deir le*, meaning "as you say to".
</Details>

## Three-Part Compound Prepositions

A less common structure consists of three parts: **Preposition + Noun + Preposition**. With these forms, the final preposition determines the case and mutation of the following noun, so the genitive case is not used.

*in aice leis an doras* - next to the door

| Preposition | Translation | Literal Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ar mhaithe le` | for, for the good of | on welfare with | *ar mhaithe leis an tír* - for the good of the country |
| `mar gheall ar` | because of, concerning | like pledge on | *mar gheall ar an gceist* - because of the question |
| `fá dtaobh de` | about (Ulster) | over side of | *fá dtaobh díom* - about me |
| `in aice le` | near, next to | in proximity with | *in aice leis an doras* - next to the door |
| `in éineacht le` | together with | in one-time with | *in éineacht leis an bhfoirm seo* - together with this form |
| `in éindí le` | together with | in one-house with | *in éindí leis an bhfear* - together with the man |
| `i ndáil le` | in relation to | in meeting with | *i ndáil leis an sampla sin* - in relation to that example |
| `i dtaca le` | concerning, with regard to | in support with | *i dtaca leis an chéad cheist de* - as regards the first question |
| `i gcomórtas le` | compared with | in comparison with | *i gcomórtas leis an mbád seo* - compared with this boat |

<Details summary="Etymological note">
The word `éineacht` in `in éineacht le` comes from the Old Irish *aoinfheacht*, a combination of `aon` ("one") and `feacht` ("time, occasion").
</Details>
