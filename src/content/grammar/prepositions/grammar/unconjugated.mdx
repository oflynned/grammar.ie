Unconjugated prepositions, also known as simple prepositions, do not merge with personal pronouns to form prepositional pronouns. Instead, the pronoun follows the preposition directly, for example, `gan mé` (without me). This page lists the most common unconjugated prepositions and their usage.

## `go / go dtí` (to, until, towards)

The form `go dtí` literally means "until I come", where `tí` is an old subjunctive form of the verb `tar` (to come). In speech, `go` is sometimes omitted, and `go dtí` is pronounced [di:].

Dialectal variants of `go dtí` include:
- `go dtige` (Connacht)
- `go nuige` (from Old Irish `conicci`, "until you meet")
- `go ruige` (from Old Irish `co-ricci`, "until you reach")

### Initial Mutations

- **Without an article**: `go` is typically used and causes no mutation.
  - *go hÉirinn* (to Ireland)
- **With an article**: `go dtí` is always used and follows the standard mutation rules for the nominative case. In the Dingle peninsula, however, it always causes eclipsis.
  - *go dtí an teach* (to the house)
  - *go dtí an t-oileán* (to the island)

### Pronominal Forms

In Standard Irish, there are no special pronominal forms. The preposition `go dtí` is followed by the standard personal pronoun.
- *go dtí mé* (to me)
- *go dtí tú* (to you)
- *go dtí é* (to him)

Historically, the pronominal forms of `chun/chuig` (e.g., `chugam`, `chugat`) belong to `go`.

In Munster, dialectal forms using possessive pronouns can be found, such as `go dtína` (until his/her).

### Usage

- **Destination of movement**:
  - *go dtí an teach* (to the house)
  - `go` is used with place names that do not take an article: *go Meireacá* (to America).
- **Temporal extent (until)**:
  - *go dtí an lá sin* (until that day)
  - `go` is used in some idioms: *go maidin* (until morning).
- **Comparison or exception (up to, except for)**:
  - *Níor fheabhas go dtí é.* (There was nothing better than it.)
  - *Níor mhadra go dtí é.* (There was no dog like him.)
  - *Tá mo chuidse díobh ite go dtí dhá cheann.* (I have eaten my share except for two.)

## `go` (with)

This preposition is distinct from `go` (to). It is rarer and often found in older texts or fixed expressions. It is etymologically related to the Latin "cum".

### Initial Mutations

- **Without an article**: Causes **eclipsis**.
  - *go n-onóir* (with honour)

### Pronominal Forms

This preposition only combines with possessive pronouns.

| Context | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| with my | `go mo` |
| with your | `go do` |
| with his/her/their | `gona` |
| with our | `gonár` |
| with your (pl.) | `go bhur` |

### Usage

- **Basic meaning "with"** (rare or archaic):
  - *gona laigí daonna uile* (with all his human weaknesses)
  - *gona chuid gealseol* (with his white sails)
- **Measurements**: Used to mean "...and a half" in the form `go leith`.
  - *trí chéad go leith punt* (350 pounds)
- **Idioms**:
  - *go bhfios dom* (as far as I know)
  - *go gcuimhin liom* (as far as I remember)

## `gan` (without)

### Initial Mutations

- **Without an article**: Causes **lenition**, but with several exceptions. Lenition typically occurs before abstract or general terms.
  - *gan phósadh* (unmarried)
  - *gan chiall* (senseless, without any sense)
  - *gan phingin* (penniless)
- **No lenition** occurs in the following cases:
  - Before `d`, `s`, `t`, and `f`: *gan fear* (without a man).
  - In infinitive constructions: *gan pósadh* (without marrying).
  - When a noun is used elliptically: *gan clú (a bheith agat)* (without (having) fame).
  - When the noun has another attribute: *gan pingin ina phóca* (without a penny in his pocket).
  - Before proper nouns: *gan Pól* (without Paul).
- **With an article**: Follows standard mutation rules.

### Pronominal Forms

There are no special pronominal forms. `gan` is followed by the personal pronoun.
- *gan mise* (without me)
- *gan tusa* (without you)
- *gan é* (without him)

### Usage

- **Basic meaning "without"**:
  - *gan obair* (without work)
- **`gan ... gan` or `gan ... ná` (neither ... nor)**:
  - *gan obair gan arán* (neither work nor bread)
  - *gan obair ná arán* (neither work nor bread)
- **In semi-negative expressions (with only)**:
  - *gan ach aon alt amháin* (with only one article)
  - *gan níos mó ná trí scillinge ina phóca aige* (with only 3 shillings in his pocket)
- **Negation in infinitive constructions (not)**:
  - *Aontaím gan labhairt leis.* (I agree not to speak to him.)
- **Negation in adverbial infinitive constructions (without)**:
  - *D'imigh mé gan labhairt leis.* (I left without speaking to him.)

## `mar` (like, as)

### Initial Mutations

- **Without an article**: Causes **lenition**.
  - *mar fhear* (like a man)
- **With an article**: Usually causes no mutation, but can rarely cause **eclipsis**.
  - *mar an fear* (like the man)
  - *mar an ngrian* (like the sun)
  - *mar an gcéanna* (likewise, the same)

### Pronominal Forms

There are no special pronominal forms.
- *mar mise* (like me)

### Usage

- **Comparison (like, as)**:
  - *mar bhean* (as a woman, like a woman)
- **Manner (according to)**:
  - *gach éinne mar a dhúchas* (everyone according to their nature)
- **Clarification**: `mar atá` means "as is" or "namely".
- **Apposition**:
  - *Tá sé fairsing is fionnuar, mar sheomra.* (It is spacious and cool, the room.)
- **Classification**:
  - *Bhí sí mar Leas-Uachtarán ar an ngrúpa.* (She was Vice-President of the group.)
- **Compound prepositions**:
  - `mar gheall ar` (because of): *mar gheall ar an mbád* (because of the boat)
  - `mar le` (as for, in view of): *Is breá an aimsir í, mar le geimhreadh.* (It is fine weather for a winter.)

## `amhail` (like)

This is a more formal or literary alternative to `mar`.

### Initial Mutations

`amhail` causes no initial mutations.
- *amhail fear* (like a man)
- *amhail an fear* (like the man)

### Pronominal Forms

There are no special pronominal forms.
- *amhail mise* (like me)
- An exception is `amhlaidh` (so, thus), which derives from `amhail` + `ea`.

### Usage

- **Comparison (like)**:
  - *amhail carraig* (like a rock)

## `os` (over)

`os` is an old preposition now found only in fixed expressions. It does not cause any initial mutations.

### Usage

- `os cionn` (over, above): *os mo chionn* (above me)
- `os comhair` / `os coinne` (opposite)
- `os ard` (aloud, openly)
- `os íseal` (in a low voice, secretly)
- `os meán` (above the measuring range)

## `seachas` (except, other than)

`seachas` is a compound of `seach` and the copula `is`. It implies a comparison or distinction.

### Usage

- **Other than**:
  - *an teach seachas an ceann seo* (a different house than this one)
- **Besides / except for**:
  - *daoine seachas ise* (others besides her)
  - *seachas thú féin* (except yourself)
- **Rather than**:
  - *seachas a chéile* (rather than the other)
- **Correction (and not)**:
  - *Rinne an fear sin, seachas an bhean.* (That man did it, not the woman.)
  - *Bearna seachas Barna* (Bearna and not Barna)

Note the difference between `seachas` and `ach amháin` (except). `seachas` makes a relative distinction, while `ach amháin` indicates an absolute exception.
- *seachas mise* (others besides me)
- *ach amháin mise* (no one but me)

## `murach` (without; if it were not for)

This preposition is used in conditional contexts to express what would have happened without a particular person or thing.

## `is` (ago, until)

This preposition is only used with the article `an` as `is an`. It derives from Old Irish `i san` (in that).

### Usage

- **Temporal (ago)**:
  - *mí is an lá inniu* (a month ago today)
  - *bliain is an t-am seo* (a year ago at this time)

## `a` (to)

This particle is almost exclusively used before a verbal noun and is a reduced form of the preposition `do` (to). It is pronounced as a schwa [ə] and is often silent before a vowel.

In Munster and older texts, the full form `do` may appear: *chun na Gaeilge d'fhoghlaim* (to learn Irish). In the Dingle peninsula and Ulster, a `dh'-` prefix is often inserted before a verbal noun beginning with a vowel: *beoir a dh'ól* (to drink beer).

### Initial Mutations

- Causes **lenition**.
  - *rud a dhéanamh* (to do something)

### Usage

- **Infinitive constructions**: Connects an object (or subject) to a verbal noun.
  - *Bhí mé sásta míle a shiúl.* (I was content to walk a mile.)
- **Progressive aspect in relative clauses**: Replaces `ag` when the object of the verbal noun is the relative antecedent.
  - *an litir atá an fear a scríobh* (the letter the man is writing)
  - *Céard atá tú a dhéanamh?* (What are you doing?)
- **Progressive aspect with separated object**: Replaces `ag` when the object does not immediately follow the verbal noun.
  - *Níl an fear a scríobh ach an litir.* (The man is only writing the letter.)
- **Infinitive of purpose (Ulster)**: Replaces `ag` after verbs of motion, beginning, ending, etc.
  - *Tháinig sé a chónaí sa tsráidbhaile.* (He came to live in the village.)
- **Telling time**: In the phrase `a chlog` (o'clock).
  - *Tá sé a trí a chlog.* (It is 3 o'clock.)

## `dar` (by)

A less common preposition used in oaths, affirmations, and exclamations.

### Usage

- *dar Dia!* (by God!)
- *dar m'anam!* (by my soul!)
- *dar m'fhocal!* (by my word!)
