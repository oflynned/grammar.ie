A small number of simple prepositions in Irish require the noun that follows them to be in the genitive case. These are known as the *réamhfhocail ghinideacha*.

Many of these prepositions were originally nouns themselves. For example, `timpeall` once meant "a round". Some are derived from compound prepositions where the first part has been lost, leaving a single word that was originally a noun in a specific case, such as `tigh` (from `teach`, house) or `dála` (from `dáil`, meeting).

## `chun` (to, towards)

This preposition indicates direction towards a place or person.

- *chun an tí* - to the house
- *ag teacht chugainn* - coming towards us (note: `chun` conjugates)

## `trasna` (through, across, over, opposite)

- **Through, across**: *trasna an gharraí* (through the field, across the field)
- **Opposite**: *trasna na habhann* (opposite the river), *trasna an bhóthair* (opposite the road)
- **Adverbial use**: *cúig troithe trasna* (five feet wide/deep), *teacht trasna ar dhuine* (to contradict someone)
- **Adjectival use**: *barra trasna* (crossbar)

## `timpeall` (around, approximately)

- **Spatial**: *timpeall an tí* (around the house), *inár dtimpeall* (around us, in our vicinity)
- **Temporal**: *timpeall na Nollag* (around Christmas), *timpeall fiche bliain* (approximately 20 years)

## `fearacht` (like)

This preposition means "like" or "in the manner of".

- *fearacht an fhir seo* - like this man
- *a fhearacht sin* - like that

## `tar éis` (after)

This preposition is primarily temporal. It is a compound of `tar` and the noun `éis` (trace), and is often pronounced as a single word. Dialectal forms include `thar éis`, `thréis`, `théis`, `d'éis`, and `in éis`.

- **Temporal**: *tar éis an chogaidh* (after the war)
- **Telling time**: *ceathrú tar éis a dó* (quarter past two), *leathuair tar éis a dó* (half past two)
- **With verbal nouns**: It is used to express a recently completed action (the perfect aspect).
  - *Tá mé tar éis imeacht.* - I have just left.

## `cois` (beside, at the foot of)

`Cois` is the dative form of the noun `cos` (foot) and is a common shortening of the compound preposition `le cois`.

- *cois cnoic* - at the foot of the hill
- *cois abhann* - beside the river

## `tigh` (at someone's place)

`Tigh` is the old dative form of the noun `teach` (house). It is used similarly to "chez" in French, often for homes, pubs, or businesses.

- *tigh Sheáin* - at Seán's (place)
- *tigh Mhic Dhónaill* - at McDonald's
- *Cheannaigh mé tigh Woolworth é.* - I bought it at Woolworth's.

## `dála` (like)

`Dála` comes from the noun `dáil` (meeting, situation). Dialectal forms include `dálta` or `dáltha`.

- *mo dhála féin* - like myself
- *dála an scéil* - like the story (used to mean "by the way" or "incidentally")

## `toisc` (because of)

This preposition originated from the phrase `de thoisc` (from circumstance). As a conjunction, `toisc go` means "because".

- *toisc a hiníne* - because of her daughter
