Compound prepositions, or `na réamhfhocail chomhshuite`, are phrases that function as a single preposition. They are formed from two or three words, usually including a simple preposition and a noun.

The meaning of a compound preposition is often idiomatic and cannot always be guessed from its individual parts. Some of the nouns used in these phrases, like `aice` (proximity) or `coinne` (meeting), are rarely seen outside of these constructions.

## Two-Part Compound Prepositions

These prepositions consist of a **simple preposition + a noun**. The noun that follows this compound preposition must be in the genitive case.

*   *ar nós na ndaoine* (like the people)

When using a pronoun, a possessive adjective is placed before the noun part of the compound preposition.

*   *ar mo nós* (like me, lit. "on my manner")
*   *ar ár nos* (like us, lit. "on our manner")

However, some temporal prepositions do not typically use possessive adjectives, such as `ar feadh`, `go ceann`, `go fiú`, `i dtrátha`, `i gcaitheamh`, and `in imeacht`.

Some compound prepositions can also function as conjunctions, often followed by `go` (that).

*   *d’ainneoin go...* (despite the fact that...)

### Common Two-Part Prepositions

#### Starting with `ar`

*   **`ar aghaidh`** (opposite)
    *   Literally: "on face"
    *   Example: *ar aghaidh an dorais* (opposite the door)
*   **`ar ceann`** (at the head/end of, before)
    *   Literally: "on head"
    *   Example: *na daoine ar ár gceann* (the people before us)
*   **`ar chúl`** (behind)
    *   Literally: "on back"
    *   Example: *ar chúl an tí* (behind the house)
*   **`ar feadh`** (for the duration of, all along)
    *   Literally: "on extent"
    *   Example: *ar feadh seachtaine* (for a week)
*   **`ar fud`** (throughout, amidst)
    *   Literally: "on region"
    *   Example: *ar fud na tíre* (throughout the country)
*   **`ar nós`** (like, in the manner of)
    *   Literally: "on manner"
    *   Example: *ar mo nós* (like me)
*   **`ar son`** (for the sake of, for the benefit of)
    *   Literally: "on welfare"
    *   Example: *ar son Dé* (for God's sake)
*   **`ar tí`** (on the track of, about to)
    *   Literally: "on track"
    *   Example: *ar mo thí* (after me)

#### Starting with `as`

*   **`as siocair`** (because of)
    *   Literally: "out of cause"
    *   Example: *as siocair na drochaimsire* (because of the bad weather)
*   **`as ucht`** (for the benefit of, because of)
    *   Literally: "out of breast"
    *   Example: *as ucht na tíre* (for the good of the country)

#### Starting with `de`

*   **`d’ainneoin`** (despite)
    *   Literally: "of unwillingness"
    *   Example: *de m’ainneoin* (despite me)
*   **`de bharr`** (as a result of, owing to)
    *   Literally: "of top"
    *   Example: *de bharr báis* (owing to death)
*   **`de bhuíochas`** (thanks to)
    *   Literally: "of thanks"
    *   Example: *dá bhuíochas* (thanks to him)
*   **`(de) cheal`** (for lack of)
    *   Literally: "of lack"
    *   Example: *de cheal airgid* (for lack of money)
*   **`de chois`** / **`de chóir`** (near)
    *   Literally: "of foot" / "of proximity"
    *   Example: *de chois an tí* (near the house)
*   **`de dheasca`** (as a consequence of, due to)
    *   Literally: "of consequences"
    *   Example: *de dheasca na trioblóidí* (as a consequence of the troubles)
*   **`de réir`** (according to)
    *   Literally: "of will"
    *   Example: *de réir an leabhair* (according to the book)
*   **`de thairbhe`** (because of, due to)
    *   Literally: "of benefit"
    *   Example: *de thairbhe a mbeartas tacaíochta* (due to their support policy)
*   **`d’uireasa`** (without, for lack of)
    *   Literally: "of lack"
    *   Example: *dá huireasa* (without her)

#### Starting with `faoi`

*   **`faoi bhun`** (below)
    *   Literally: "under ground"
    *   Example: *faoi bhun pingine* (below a penny)
*   **`faoi cheann`** (in, at the end of) (temporal)
    *   Literally: "under head"
    *   Example: *faoi cheann seachtaine* (in a week)
*   **`faoi choinne`** / **`faoi chomhair`** / **`faoi dhéin`** (for, intended for)
    *   Literally: "under meeting" / "under counting" / "under fetching"
    *   Example: *faoina coinne* (for her)
    *   Example: *faoi chomhair an phobail* (for the people)

#### Starting with `gan`

*   **`gan fiú`** (without even)
    *   Literally: "without value"
    *   Example: *gan fiú na mbróg* (even without shoes)

#### Starting with `go`

*   **`go ceann`** (for, until) (temporal)
    *   Literally: "until head"
    *   Example: *go ceann bliana* (for a year)
*   **`go fiú`** (up to, including)
    *   Literally: "until value"
    *   Example: *go fiú na gcnáipí* (up to the buttons)

#### Starting with `i`

*   **`in aghaidh`** / **`in éadan`** (against)
    *   Literally: "in face" / "in forehead"
    *   Example: *inár n-aghaidh* (against us)
*   **`in aice`** (near)
    *   Literally: "in proximity"
    *   Example: *in aice na Gaillimhe* (near Galway)
*   **`in áit`** / **`in ionad`** (instead of)
    *   Literally: "in place"
    *   Example: *i m’áit* (in my place)
*   **`in imeacht`** (in the course of)
    *   Literally: "in going away"
    *   Example: *in imeacht an lae* (in the course of the day)
*   **`i gcaitheamh`** (during)
    *   Literally: "in course"
    *   Example: *i gcaitheamh oíche* (during a night)
*   **`i gceann`** (at the end of)
    *   Literally: "in head"
    *   Example: *i gceann an bheallaigh* (at the end of the road)
*   **`i gcoinne`** (against)
    *   Literally: "in meeting"
    *   Example: *i gcoinne an tsrutha* (against the current)
*   **`i gcomhair`** (for, intended for)
    *   Literally: "in counting"
    *   Example: *i gcomhair an phobail* (for the people)
*   **`i gcuideachta`** (in the company of)
    *   Literally: "in company"
    *   Example: *i do chuideachta* (in your company)
*   **`i gcúl`** (in the back of)
    *   Literally: "in back"
    *   Example: *i gcúl an chairr* (in the back of the car)
*   **`i ndiaidh`** (after)
    *   Literally: "in end"
    *   Example: *i ndiaidh an scéil* (after the story)
*   **`i bhfianaise`** (in sight of, in the presence of)
    *   Literally: "in witness"
    *   Example: *i bhfianaise na farraige* (in sight of the sea)
*   **`i bhfochair`** (together with)
    *   Literally: "in proximity"
    *   Example: *in bhur bhfochair* (together with you)
*   **`i bhfoisceacht`** / **`i ngiorracht`** (within)
    *   Literally: "in proximity" / "in shortness"
    *   Example: *i bhfoisceacht trí mhíle* (within three miles)
*   **`i lár`** (in the middle of)
    *   Literally: "in middle"
    *   Example: *i lár an bheallaigh* (in the middle of the road)
*   **`i láthair`** (in the presence of)
    *   Literally: "in presence"
    *   Example: *i láthair Dé* (in the presence of God)
*   **`i leith`** (regarding, for)
    *   Literally: "in side"
    *   Example: *i leith na saoirse* (for freedom)
*   **`i measc`** (among)
    *   Literally: "in tangle"
    *   Example: *i measc na ndaoine* (among the people)
*   **`i rith`** (during)
    *   Literally: "in run"
    *   Example: *i rith na seachtaine* (during the week)
*   **`i dtaobh`** (concerning, about)
    *   Literally: "in side"
    *   Example: *i dtaobh an ruda* (concerning the matter)
*   **`i dteannta`** (together with)
    *   Literally: "in support"
    *   Example: *i do theannta* (together with you)
*   **`i dtrátha`** (around) (temporal)
    *   Literally: "in time"
    *   Example: *i dtrátha na Nollag* (around Christmas)

#### Starting with `le`

*   **`le haghaidh`** (for)
    *   Literally: "with face"
    *   Example: *lena aghaidh* (for him)
*   **`le hais`** (beside, in comparison with)
    *   Literally: "with back"
    *   Example: *lena hais* (beside her)
*   **`le cois`** (beside, at the foot of)
    *   Literally: "with foot"
    *   Example: *le cois na habhann* (by the river)
*   **`le linn`** (during)
    *   Literally: "with duration"
    *   Example: *le linn na seachtaine* (during the week)
*   **`le taobh`** (at the side of, along)
    *   Literally: "with side"
    *   Example: *le mo thaobh* (at my side)
*   **`le teann`** (out of, from)
    *   Literally: "with strength"
    *   Example: *le teann feirge* (out of anger)

#### Starting with `os`

*   **`os cionn`** (over, above)
    *   Literally: "over head"
    *   Example: *os cionn an tí* (over the house)
*   **`os coinne`** / **`os comhair`** (opposite, before, in the presence of)
    *   Literally: "over meeting" / "over counting"
    *   Example: *os mo choinne* (before me)
    *   Example: *os comhair na fuinneoige* (opposite the window)

#### Starting with `thar`

*   **`thar ceann`** (beyond)
    *   Literally: "over head"
    *   Example: *thar ceann an ghoirt* (beyond the field)
*   **`thar cheann`** (on behalf of, instead of)
    *   Literally: "over head"
    *   Example: *thar mo cheann* (instead of me)
*   **`tar éis`** (after)
    *   Literally: "over track"
    *   Example: *tar éis an scéil* (after the story)

### Other Two-Part Prepositions

A few two-part prepositions have a different structure: they end with a simple preposition. These behave like three-part prepositions, where the object is governed by the final preposition.

*   **`maidir le`** / **`mar le`** (concerning, regarding)
    *   Example: *maidir leis an litir* (concerning the letter)
*   **`láimh le`** (beside)
    *   Literally: "hand with"
    *   Example: *láimh le droichead* (beside a bridge)
*   **`laistiar de`** / **`taobh thiar de`** (behind)
    *   Example: *taobh thiar den teach* (behind the house)
*   **`diomaite de`** (except for)
    *   Example: *diomaite den Ghaeilge* (except for Irish)

## Three-Part Compound Prepositions

These prepositions consist of a **simple preposition + a noun + another simple preposition**.

Unlike two-part prepositions, they do not take the genitive case. The noun that follows is governed by the final simple preposition in the phrase.

*   *in aice leis an doras* (next to the door)

### Common Three-Part Prepositions

*   **`ar mhaithe le`** (for the good of)
    *   Literally: "on welfare with"
    *   Example: *ar mhaithe leis an tír* (for the good of the country)
*   **`mar gheall ar`** (because of, concerning)
    *   Literally: "like pledge on"
    *   Example: *mar gheall ar an gceist* (because of the question)
*   **`fá dtaobh de`** (about) (Ulster)
    *   Literally: "over side of"
    *   Example: *fá dtaobh díom* (about me)
*   **`in aice le`** (next to, near)
    *   Literally: "in proximity with"
    *   Example: *in aice leis an doras* (next to the door)
*   **`in éineacht le`** / **`in éindí le`** (together with)
    *   Literally: "in one-time with" / "in one-house with"
    *   Example: *in éineacht leis an bhfoirm seo* (together with this form)
*   **`i ndáil le`** (in relation to)
    *   Literally: "in meeting with"
    *   Example: *i ndáil leis an sampla sin* (in relation to that example)
*   **`i dtaca le`** (with regard to, concerning)
    *   Literally: "in support with"
    *   Example: *i dtaca leis an chéad cheist de* (as regards the first question)
*   **`i gcomórtas le`** (compared with)
    *   Literally: "in comparison with"
    *   Example: *i gcomórtas leis an mbád seo* (compared with this boat)

## Dialectal Forms

In spoken Irish, compound prepositions are often simplified or contracted.

*   **Munster:**
    *   `ar feadh` can become `feadh` or `fan`: *fan na seachtaine* (for the week).
    *   `de réir` can become `dréir` or `déir`: *déir na haimsire* (according to the season).
*   **Connacht:**
    *   `de réir` can become `réir` or `léir`: *léir na haimsire* (according to the season).
    *   `le haghaidh` is often contracted to `l'aghaidh` (pronounced roughly as *lai* in Cois Fharraige).
*   **General Variants:**
    *   `i ndiaidh` has several variants, including `as diaidh`, `i ndéidh`, and `in éis`.
    *   In some dialects, personal pronouns are used instead of possessive adjectives: *ar nós mé féin* (in my own way).
