The English preposition 'for' has a wide range of meanings, covering time, purpose, benefit, exchange, and more. Irish does not have a single equivalent preposition. Instead, the correct choice of preposition or phrase depends entirely on the context.

## Time and Duration

### Total Duration

To express the total duration of an event, whether in the past or future, use `ar feadh` (for the duration of).

- *`Bhí sé anseo ar feadh seachtaine.`* - He was here for a week.
- *`Tháinig sé anseo ar feadh seachtaine.`* - He came here for a week.
- *`Beidh mé anseo ar feadh bliana.`* - I will be here for a year.

For non-temporal spans, `le linn` (during) or `i rith` (in the course of) can also be used.
- *`Bhí sé ansin le linn an chogaidh.`* - He was there for the duration of the war.

### Duration with a Past Endpoint

When an event lasted for a period and ended in the past, you can use either `ar feadh` or `le`.

- *`Bhí sé anseo le seachtain agus fuair sé bás.`* - He was here for a week and then died.
- *`Bhí sé anseo ar feadh seachtaine roimh an gCáisc.`* - He was here for a week before Easter.

### Duration into the Future

To describe a duration that starts now and extends into the future, use `go ceann`.

- *`Beidh sé anseo go ceann seachtaine.`* - He will be here for a week (from now).

### Duration up to the Present

When an action or state began in the past and continues into the present, use `le`. This often corresponds to the English present perfect tense.

- *`Tá mé anseo le seachtain.`* - I have been here for a week.

### Distance

To express 'for' a certain distance, use `ar feadh`.

- *`ar feadh trí mhíle`* - for three miles.

## Purpose, Benefit, and Intention

### For the Benefit or Purpose of

To show that something is for someone's benefit or for a specific purpose, several phrases can be used.

`le haghaidh` is very common:
- *`Cheannaigh mé sin le m’aghaidh.`* - I bought that for myself.

Other options include `i gcomhair`, `faoi chomhair`, and `faoi choinne` (Ulster: `fá choinne`).
- *`litir faoi do choinne`* - a letter for you
- *`áit faoi choinne leabhar`* - a place for books
- *`Rinne siad féasta faoina chomhair.`* - They held a feast for him.

### As a Favour or Intended For

When an action is done as a favour for someone, use the simple preposition `do`.

- *`Rinne mé sin dó.`* - I did that for him.

### For a Goal or For the Sake of

To express a higher purpose or goal, use `ar son`.

- *`Rinne mé ar son an airgid é.`* - I did it for the money.
- *`Glacaigí agus ithigí uile é seo: óir is é seo mo chorp a thabharfar ar bhur son.`* - Take and eat all of this, for this is my body which will be given for you.

In Ulster, `de/mar gheall ar` can also be used in this context.

## Fetching or Acquiring

When going to get something, use `faoi dhéin` or `le haghaidh`.

- *`Bhuail mé isteach sa siopa faoi dhéin siúcra.`* - I went into the shop for some sugar.

## Price and Exchange

### For a Sum of Money or in Exchange

To state the price of something or what was given in exchange, use the preposition `ar`.

- *`Cheannaigh mé ar phunt amháin é.`* - I bought it for one pound.
- *`Bhéarfaidh mé daoibh ar chúig phunta an ceann iad.`* - I will give them to you for 5 pounds each.
- *`Thug sé trí ba ar an gcapall sin.`* - He gave three cows for that horse.
- *`Focal ar fhocal`* - Word for word.

### In Return For

To express thanks or payment in return for something, use `as ucht` or simply `as`.

- *`Go raibh maith agat as an gcabhair.`* - Thank you for the help.
- *`D’íoc mé fiche punt as.`* - I paid 20 pounds for it.

In Ulster, `ar son` is more common in this context.

## Relationships and Roles

### Working For

To say you work for a company or person, use `le` or `do`.

- *`ag obair leis an gcomhlacht árachais`* - to work for the insurance company.

### In Place Of or On Behalf Of

To say someone is acting as a substitute or representative, use `thar cheann` or `in ionad`.

- *`Bhí sé ann thar mo cheann.`* - He was there for me (in my place).
- *`Labhair sé thar ceann an náisiúin.`* - He spoke for the whole nation.

### Responsible For (Formal)

In official or formal contexts, the preposition `um` can mean 'for' in the sense of 'concerning' or 'responsible for'.

- *`An Institiúid Éireannach um Chosaint Raideolaíoch`* - The Irish Institute for Radiological Protection (Radiological Protection Institute of Ireland).

## Judgements and Opinions

### Good, Bad, or Easy For

When making a judgement about how something affects someone, use `do` for general qualities and `ag` for specific abilities or situations.

With `do`:
- *`maith dó`* - good for him
- *`olc duit`* - bad for you

With `ag`:
- *`éasca aici`* - easy for her
- *`maith againn`* - good for us
- *`róchúng ag luch`* - too narrow for a mouse
- *`róthrom ag an bpáiste`* - too heavy for the child

### Expressing Support ('to be for something')

To express support for an idea or side, use `i leith`, `i bhfabhar`, or `ar thaobh`.

- *`Tá mé i leith fanacht anseo.`* - I am for staying here.
- *`Tá sé ar thaobh Shasana.`* - He is for England / on England's side.

### As Regards or Considering

To mean 'for' in the sense of 'considering what it is', use `mar le`, `maidir le`, or `de`.

- *`Is breá an aimsir í, mar le geimhreadh!`* - For a winter, it is glorious weather!
- *`Maidir le Sasanach níl sé ag déanamh go holc.`* - For an Englishman, he is not doing badly.
- *`Is fear maith é d’fhear Sasanach.`* - He is a good man for an Englishman.

## Idiomatic and Advanced Uses

### For All That / Despite

In a slightly disapproving sense, `ar feadh a` and `ar scáth a` can mean 'for all that' or 'despite'.

- *`ar feadh a bhfuil de thalamh aige`* - for all the land he has
- *`Ar feadh a ndearnadh de dhochar duit`* - for all the harm that was done to you
- *`ar scáth a bhfuil de mhaith ann bheadh sé chomh maith dhó imeacht`* - For all the good he is, he might as well leave.

### Idiomatic Comparisons

In phrases like 'man for man' or 'eye for an eye', Irish uses the structure `noun + sa + noun`.

- *`fear san fhear`* - man for man
- *`súil sa tsúil`* - eye for an eye
