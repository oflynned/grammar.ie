Verbal particles are small words that stand immediately before a verb. They are a core part of Irish syntax, as they cause initial mutations (lenition or eclipsis) and help to form questions, negatives, and different types of clauses.

Dependent verb forms are used after most verbal particles. However, the positive direct relative particle and the past particle are exceptions and take independent forms. Irregular verbs such as `bí`, `abair`, `déan`, `faigh`, `feic`, and `téigh` do not use the past tense forms of verbal particles that end in `-r`. Instead, they often have special dependent forms, for example, `tá sé` (he is) becomes `nach bhfuil sé` (that he is not).

When used with the copula, verbal particles often combine to create single forms, or the copula itself is omitted.

## Negation and Prohibition

These particles are used to negate a statement or issue a negative command.

### The Negative Particle: ní / cha

The most common negative particle is `ní`, which is used in all dialects. In the past tense, it becomes `níor`. The particle `cha` is specific to Ulster Irish.

**Standard Forms (`ní`)**

| Tense                 | Particle | Mutation | Example                                                          |
|:----------------------|:---------|:---------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Present, Future, etc. | `ní`     | Lenition | *Ní ólfaidh tú fuisce arís.* (You will not drink whiskey again.) |
| Preterite (Past)      | `níor`   | Lenition | *Níor ól tú fuisce.* (You did not drink any whiskey.)            |

In the Dingle dialect of Munster, `ní` is sometimes shortened to `n'` before verbs like `feadair` (to know), `féad` (can), and `feacaigh` (saw). For example, `n’fheadar` (I don't know).

**Ulster Forms (`cha`)**

| Tense & Context                   | Particle | Mutation | Example                                                          |
|:----------------------------------|:---------|:---------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. before b,c,f,g,m,p,s | `cha`    | Lenition | *Cha chreideann sé é.* (He does not believe it.)                 |
| Present etc. before d,t           | `cha`    | Eclipsis | *Cha ndéanaim a leithéid.* (I do not do such a thing.)           |
| Present etc. before vowel, fh     | `chan`   | Lenition | *Chan ólann tú fuisce arís.* (You will not drink whiskey again.) |
| Preterite (Past)                  | `char`   | Lenition | *Char ól tú fuisce.* (You did not drink any whiskey.)            |

The particle `cha` is primarily used in Ulster, sometimes replacing `ní` and other times used alongside it for emphasis. After `cha`, the present tense is often used to express a future meaning. Mutation rules can vary significantly across Donegal.

### The Prohibitive Particle: ná

The particle `ná` is used to form a negative command (the prohibitive). It is only used with the imperative mood.

| Context            | Particle | Mutation    | Example                                 |
|:-------------------|:---------|:------------|:----------------------------------------|
| Before a consonant | `ná`     | None        | *Ná déan é!* (Don't do it!)             |
| Before a vowel     | `ná`     | h-prothesis | *Ná hól fuisce!* (Don't drink whiskey!) |

## Interrogatives

As Irish has a fixed Verb-Subject-Object word order, questions that can be answered with "yes" or "no" must begin with an interrogative particle.

### The Interrogative Particle: an / nach

These particles introduce a question. `An` is used for positive questions, and `nach` is used for negative questions. They can be thought of as "if" or "whether" in indirect questions, such as *Níl a fhios agam an ólann sé fuisce* (I don't know if he drinks whiskey).

**Positive Questions**

| Tense                 | Particle | Mutation | Example                                      |
|:----------------------|:---------|:---------|:---------------------------------------------|
| Present, Future, etc. | `an`     | Eclipsis | *An ólfá fuisce?* (Would you drink whiskey?) |
| Preterite (Past)      | `ar`     | Lenition | *Ar ól tú fuisce?* (Did you drink whiskey?)  |

**Negative Questions**

| Tense                 | Particle | Mutation | Example                                              |
|:----------------------|:---------|:---------|:-----------------------------------------------------|
| Present, Future, etc. | `nach`   | Eclipsis | *Nach n-ólann tú fuisce?* (Don't you drink whiskey?) |
| Preterite (Past)      | `nár`    | Lenition | *Nár ól tú fuisce?* (Didn't you drink whiskey?)      |

**Munster Variant (`ná`)**

In Munster, `ná` is often used instead of `nach`. It is unique because it takes a dependent verb form without causing lenition or eclipsis.

| Tense & Context               | Particle | Mutation    | Example                                         |
|:------------------------------|:---------|:------------|:------------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. before consonant | `ná`     | None        | *Ná fuil sé anso?* (Is he not here?)            |
| Present etc. before vowel     | `ná`     | h-prothesis | *Ná hólair fuisce?* (Don't you drink whiskey?)  |
| Preterite (Past)              | `nár`    | Lenition    | *Nár ólais fuisce?* (Didn't you drink whiskey?) |

## Tense and Aspect Markers

These particles help to situate the verb in time.

### The Past Particle: do

The particle `do` historically marked the past tenses (Preterite, Imperfect, and Conditional). Today, its usage has changed.

| Context               | Particle | Mutation | Example                                         |
|:----------------------|:---------|:---------|:------------------------------------------------|
| Before consonants     | (`do`)   | Lenition | *Thuig sé an scéal.* (He understood the story.) |
| Before vowels and `f` | `d'`     | Lenition | *D’ól tú fuisce.* (You drank whiskey.)          |

-   The full form `do` is now mostly found in Munster, and even there it is declining. In other dialects, only the lenition it caused remains.
-   The form `d'` is always used before a vowel or `fh-` in all dialects.
-   This particle is only used with independent verb forms and does not appear after particles that require a dependent form (e.g., `níor ól mé`, not `níor d'ól mé`).

### The Perfect Particle: ro

The particle `ro` no longer exists as a standalone word. It has merged with other particles to create their past tense forms, identified by the `-r` suffix.

-   It is found in particles like `níor` (from `ní + ro`), `ar` (from `an + ro`), and `gur` (from `go + ro`).
-   It is only used in the preterite tense and in the negative present subjunctive.
-   Some irregular verbs absorbed `ro` into their verb stem, which is why they do not use particles ending in `-r`. For example, `ro + bhí` became `raibh`.

## Relative Clauses and Conjunctions

These particles introduce relative clauses or connect clauses together.

### The Direct Relative Particle

The direct relative particle `a` corresponds to "who", "which", or "that" when it is the subject or object of the verb.

| Tense                   | Particle | Mutation | Example                                                            |
|:------------------------|:---------|:---------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. (Positive) | `a`      | Lenition | *an fear a ólann fuisce* (the man who drinks whiskey)              |
| Preterite (Positive)    | `a`      | Lenition | *an fear a d’ól fuisce* (the man who drank whiskey)                |
| Present etc. (Negative) | `nach`   | Eclipsis | *an fear nach n-ólann fuisce* (the man who does not drink whiskey) |
| Preterite (Negative)    | `nár`    | Lenition | *an fear nár ól fuisce* (the man who did not drink whiskey)        |

-   The positive particle `a` requires an independent verb form. With the verb `tá`, it combines to form `atá`.
-   The negative forms `nach` and `nár` require a dependent verb form.

### The Indirect Relative Particle

The indirect relative particle is used when the relative pronoun has a more complex relationship with the verb, corresponding to "whose", "of which", "in which", etc.

| Tense                   | Particle | Mutation | Example                                                                             |
|:------------------------|:---------|:---------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. (Positive) | `a`      | Eclipsis | *an teach a n-ólann tú fuisce ann* (the house in which you drink whiskey)           |
| Preterite (Positive)    | `ar`     | Lenition | *an teach ar ól tú fuisce ann* (the house in which you drank whiskey)               |
| Present etc. (Negative) | `nach`   | Eclipsis | *an teach nach n-ólann tú fuisce ann* (the house in which you do not drink whiskey) |
| Preterite (Negative)    | `nár`    | Lenition | *an teach nár ól tú fuisce ann* (the house in which you did not drink whiskey)      |

-   The indirect relative particle always requires a dependent verb form.
-   In Munster, `go` and `gur` are used instead of `a` and `ar`.

### Verbal Conjunctions

Some conjunctions behave like verbal particles, causing mutations and changing form in the past tense.

**go (that)**

| Tense                   | Form   | Mutation | Example                                                  |
|:------------------------|:-------|:---------|:---------------------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. (Positive) | `go`   | Eclipsis | *go n-ólann tú fuisce* (that you drink whiskey)          |
| Preterite (Positive)    | `gur`  | Lenition | *gur ól tú fuisce* (that you drank whiskey)              |
| Present etc. (Negative) | `nach` | Eclipsis | *nach n-ólann tú fuisce* (that you do not drink whiskey) |
| Preterite (Negative)    | `nár`  | Lenition | *nár ól tú fuisce* (that you did not drink whiskey)      |

**sula (before)**

| Tense        | Form    | Mutation | Example                              |
|:-------------|:--------|:---------|:-------------------------------------|
| Present etc. | `sula`  | Eclipsis | *sula dtiocfaidh mé* (before I come) |
| Preterite    | `sular` | Lenition | *sular tháinig mé* (before I came)   |

**mura (if not, unless)**

| Tense        | Form    | Mutation | Example                                         |
|:-------------|:--------|:---------|:------------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. | `mura`  | Eclipsis | *Mura bhfeiceann sé é* (If he does not see him) |
| Preterite    | `murar` | Lenition | *Murar bhuail sé leis* (If he has not met him)  |

**ach a (as soon as)**

| Tense        | Form     | Mutation | Example                                   |
|:-------------|:---------|:---------|:------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. | `ach a`  | Eclipsis | *ach a dtiocfaidh mé* (as soon as I come) |
| Preterite    | `ach ar` | Lenition | *ach ar tháinig mé* (as soon as I came)   |

## Other Particles and Dialectal Forms

### The Optative Particle: go

This particle is used to express wishes, blessings, or curses. It is identical in form to the conjunction `go`.

| Form     | Particle | Mutation | Example                                                   |
|:---------|:---------|:---------|:----------------------------------------------------------|
| Positive | `go`     | Eclipsis | *Go n-éirí an bóthar leat.* (May the road rise with you.) |
| Negative | `nár`    | Lenition | *Nár fheicimid arís é.* (May we never see him again!)     |

When negating a wish with the verb `bí`, `ná` is used instead of `nár` because the historic particle `ro` is already contained in its subjunctive form, `raibh`. For example, *Ná raibh maith agat.* (No thanks to you.)

### The Locative Interrogative: cá

The question word `cá` (where) is a combination of `cá` and the indirect relative particle.

| Tense        | Particle | Mutation | Example                                       |
|:-------------|:---------|:---------|:----------------------------------------------|
| Present etc. | `cá`     | Eclipsis | *Cá gcuirfidh tú é?* (Where will you put it?) |
| Preterite    | `cár`    | Lenition | *Cár chuir tú é?* (Where did you put it?)     |

### Dialectal Variations Summary

-   **Munster:**
    -   Use of `do` before consonants in past tenses (e.g., *do thuig sé*).
    -   A `dh'` prefix is added before vowels after leniting particles (e.g., *Ní dh'aontaím*).
    -   The `-r` is often dropped from past tense particles (e.g., *A' thuig tú?* for *Ar thuig tú?*).
    -   `ná` is used instead of `nach`.
    -   `go` is used as the indirect relative particle instead of `a`.
-   **Ulster:**
    -   `cha` is used as a negative particle, either instead of or alongside `ní`.
-   **General:**
    -   Forms like `sara` (for `sula`) and `mara`/`muna` (for `mura`) are found in different regions.
