## Overview

The verbal adjective, or `an aidiacht bhriathartha`, is most often translated as a past participle, such as *broken*, *washed*, or *carried*. Colloquially, it is sometimes called `rangabháil chaite` (past participle).

It has two primary functions:
- It can be used like a normal adjective.
- It is used to form passive constructions and perfect tenses.

As it belongs to the third declension of adjectives (those ending in a vowel), its form does not change in the genitive case or the plural.

## Formation

The verbal adjective is formed by adding a suffix to the verb stem. The specific suffix depends on the final consonant(s) of the stem.

### First Conjugation Verbs

| Final Consonant(s)                | Rule                                   | Example(s)                         |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| `b`, `c`, `f`, `g`, `m`, `p`, `r` | Add `-tha` / `-the`                    | *fág → fágtha, gearr → gearrtha*   |
| `ch`, `l`, `n`, `s`               | Add `-ta` / `-te`                      | *glan → glanta, bris → briste*     |
| `d`                               | Add `-ta` / `-te`                      | *goid → goidte*                    |
| `t`, `th`                         | Remove `t`/`th` and add `-ta` / `-te`  | *sciot → sciota, ith → ite*        |
| `bh`, `mh`                        | Remove `bh`/`mh` and add `-fa` / `-fe` | *scríobh → scríofa, snámh → snáfa* |
| `igh`                             | Remove `gh` and add `-te`              | *dóigh → dóite, léigh → léite*     |
| `áil`                             | Add `-te`                              | *sábháil → sábháilte*              |

- The ending `-tha` devoices preceding `b` and `g`. For example, `-btha` is pronounced [-pə] and `-gtha` is pronounced [-kə].
- The ending `-dta` is pronounced [-tə].
- The suffix `-fa` is a modern spelling for the older `-bhtha` or `-mhtha` (e.g., *scríobhtha* became *scríofa*).

### Second Conjugation Verbs

| Final Consonant(s) | Rule                             | Example(s)                                 |
|--------------------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| `igh`              | Remove `gh` and add `-the`       | *salaigh → salaithe, coinnigh → coinnithe* |
| `il`               | Add `-te`                        | *oscail → oscailte*                        |
| `in`               | Depalatalise stem and add `-ta`  | *cosain → cosanta*                         |
| `ir`               | Depalatalise stem and add `-tha` | *imir → imeartha*                          |
| `im`               | Depalatalise stem and add `-tha` | *foghlaim → foghlamtha*                    |
| `ing`              | Add `-the`                       | *tuirling → tuirlingthe*                   |

- Some verbs require the stem to be depalatalised (made broad), such as `imir` becoming `imeartha`.
- Exceptions include `inis`, which becomes `inste`, and `freastail`, which becomes `freastalta`.

### Irregular Verbs

| Verb      | Verbal Adjective |
|-----------|------------------|
| `abair`   | `ráite`          |
| `beir`    | `beirthe`        |
| `bí`      | (`beite`)        |
| `clois`   | `cloiste`        |
| `cluin`   | `cluinte`        |
| `déan`    | `déanta`         |
| `faigh`   | `faighte`        |
| `feic`    | `feicthe`        |
| `ith`     | `ite`            |
| `tar`     | `tagtha`         |
| `tabhair` | `tugtha`         |
| `téigh`   | `dulta`          |

Most of these are formed regularly from a different stem of the verb. For instance, `ráite` (said) and `dulta` (gone) derive from the verbal noun stems `rá` and `dul`. The verb `bí` (to be) does not have a true verbal adjective; the form `beite` is only used for the Participle of Necessity.

## Core Functions

The verbal adjective is a versatile form used in several key grammatical structures.

### As an Adjective

It can be used attributively (before a noun) or predicatively (after a verb like `bí`).
- *an crúiscín briste* (the broken jug)
- *Tá an bord briste.* (The table is broken.)
- *Tá mé ólta.* (I am drunk.)

### Forming the Passive Voice

Used with the verb `bí`, the verbal adjective creates a passive sense. The agent (the one performing the action) is introduced with the preposition `ag`.
- *Tá an bord briste agam.* (The table was broken by me.)
- *Tá beirthe agam ort.* (You were caught by me. From `beir ar`, to catch.)

### Forming the Perfect Tense

The same structure used for the passive voice can also express the perfect tense ("have done something").
- **Transitive Verbs:** *Tá an bord briste agam.* (I have broken the table.)
- **Verbs with Prepositions:** *Tá beirthe agam ort.* (I have caught you.)
- **Intransitive Verbs:** *Tá mé imithe go hÉirinn.* (I have gone to Ireland.)

In conditional clauses, this structure can express the certainty of an event's completion.
- *Má fheictear thú tá tú gafa.* (If you are seen, then you are caught.)

### Idiomatic Uses

The verbal adjective appears in several common idiomatic phrases.

With `faigh`, it can mean "to get something done", similar to the English construction.
- *Conas rudaí a fháil déanta?* (How to get things done?)
- *Fuair sé gortaithe.* (He got hurt.)

With `deireadh`, it signifies the completion of an action.

| Concept        | Formula                     | Example                        | Translation (Literal)                           |
|----------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Finished doing | `Tá deireadh` + VA + `agat` | *An bhfuil deireadh ite agat?* | Are you finished eating? (Is end eaten at-you?) |

## Modal Participles

Modal participles are adjectives that express the possibility of an action, similar to English suffixes like "-able" and "-ible". They are formed by adding a prefix (`in-`, `so-`, `do-`) to the verbal adjective or the genitive form of a noun.

### Participle of Suitability (-able/-ible)

The prefix `in-` means "possible".
- *inbhriste* (breakable)
- *inite* (edible)
- *intráchta* (passable, from `trácht`, traffic)

When used with the copula, it indicates capability.
- *Is intuigthe as sin go…* (From this it can be understood that…)
- *scéal is inchreidte dóibh* (a story that they can believe)

### Participle of Facility (Easily -able)

The prefix `so-` means "easily possible".
- *sobhriste* (easily breakable)
- *so-iompair* (portable, from `iompar`, transport)
- *so-bhlasta* (tasty)

### Participle of Difficulty (Un-able)

The prefix `do-` means "difficult" or "impossible".
- *dobhriste* (unbreakable)
- *dofhaisnéise* (indescribable, from `faisnéis`, information)
- *do-bhlasta* (bad-tasting)

### Participle of Comparison (As ... as)

The prefix `in-` combined with the preposition `le` expresses a degree of equality.
- *Níl sí incheoil le Pól.* (She is not as musical as Paul.)
- *Ní raibh aon fhear inchamáin le Pól.* (There was no man who handled the hurley as well as Paul.)

### Participle of Necessity (Should be)

This is a rare form, now mostly found in fixed phrases. It is identical to the verbal adjective and is used with the copula (usually negated) to express a lack of necessity or inappropriateness. The logical subject takes a preposition, often `do`.
- *Ní gearánta dom ar an aimsir.* (I have no reason to complain about the weather.)
- *Ní tógtha orm é.* (I am not to be blamed for it.)
- *Ní dulta duit ansin.* (You should not go there.)

## Related Adjectival Forms

Other prefixes and suffixes can be used to create adjectives from nouns and verbal nouns.

### Adjectives from Nouns with Prefixes

The prefixes `dea-` (good) and `dí-` (un-) can combine with the genitive of a noun to form an adjective.
- *dea-éadaigh* (well-dressed, from `éadach`, clothing)
- *dí-airm* (unarmed, from `arm`, weapon)

### Adjectives from Verbal Nouns with Suffixes

The suffix `-ach` can be added to the genitive of a verbal noun to create an adjective with an active meaning, expressing an ability or tendency.
- *labhair* (to speak) → `labharthach` (talkative)
- *mínigh* (to explain) → `mínitheach` (explanatory)
- *tuig* (to understand) → `tuisceannach` (understanding)

## Advanced Notes

### Dialectal Variations

Pronunciation of verbal adjective endings varies significantly across dialects.
- **Cois Fharraige/Conamara:** Endings like `-tha`/`-the` after plosives are often pronounced as a long vowel [iː]. After `-m`, `-ta`/`-te` is common, as in `cumta` instead of the standard `cumtha` (formed).
- **Munster:** The `-tha` sound [hə] can occur after `-l`, even where `-ta`/`-te` is written.
- **Ulster:** For verbs ending in `-bh`/`-mh`, the consonant is often vocalised, leading to an ending like [uhə] instead of the standard [fə]. For example, `scríobhtha` is pronounced [s′k′r′iːuhə]. The alternative ending `-iste` is also used for some second conjugation verbs, e.g., `coinniste` for `coinnithe`.

### Etymology and Usage Notes

- The verbal adjective often shares its form with the genitive of the verbal noun (e.g., `pósadh` → genitive/VA `pósta`).
- A present participle has not existed in Irish for centuries. A remnant of the Proto-Celtic participle is the word `cara` (friend), from *karantos* (loving).
- The ending `-áilte` is sometimes humorously appended to English words to create Irish-sounding adjectives, such as `siúráilte` (sure) or `cúláilte` (cool).
- Unlike in some other languages, the verbal adjective cannot be used to form temporal or modal participial clauses (e.g., "The work done, I rested"). These concepts are expressed using the verbal noun.
