In the dialects, the forms developed differently. The Standard, in turn, established forms that are partly no longer used as such in the dialects but can be explained by the older forms (e.g. `-amar` as the Preterite form instead of the dialectal, more common `-amair`).

### Endings with -t- or -th-

The conjugational endings with `-t-` (i.e. `-t(e)á`, `-t(e)ar`, `-t(a)í`) originally only occurred when `-th-` was not possible due to delenition.
- **-t-** occurred after `d, l, n, s, t, th`.
- **-th-** occurred in all other cases.

The voiced consonants `b` and `g` thereby became voiceless [p] and [k].
- *scuabthaí* [scuapi:] = one swept
- *scuabthar* [scuapər] = one sweeps
- *scuabthá* [scuapa:] = you swept

In the Standard today, `-t-` is almost always used: *scuabtaí*, *scuabtar*, *scuabtá*. In many dialects, the old forms are more or less alive. In Munster, `-th-` even occurs after `-l`.

### Differences between the 1st and 2nd Conjugation

The decisive difference between the two conjugations is the future marking (`-f-` vs. `-ó-`). In Ulster, forms of the Future and Conditional have been preserved in the 2nd conjugation which align more with the old orthography, as the consonant [x] was retained.
- Future **-óchaidh** [axi, ahi, ahə] and Conditional **-óchadh** [axu, ahu], e.g., *sáróchaidh sé* (he will surpass).

In other dialects, the endings were shortened to `-óidh` (Future) and `-ódh` (Conditional), e.g., *sáróidh sé*. The pronunciation of the ending as [oː] in the Future in Connacht occasionally leads to it being written as `-ó`, e.g., *sáró sé*.

In north Donegal, the common stem ending of the 2nd conjugation, `-igh`, is elided when other endings are added, making the forms resemble the 1st conjugation. For example, the verb `ceannaigh` (to buy) in the Present is *ceannann sé* (he buys) and not, as elsewhere, *ceannaíonn sé*.

### Indicative Present and Habitual Present

#### Analytic Forms
The forms in `-(e)ann` were originally only dependent forms. In Classical Irish, the independent form with `-idh` corresponded to the dependent forms without an ending or with `-ann`.
- *buailidh sé* = he hits
- *ní bhuail sé* / *ní bhuaileann sé* = he does not hit

The older form `-(a)idh` is still found in Ulster, especially as a "historic present". Dependent forms without an ending are now common with only a few verbs: *An meas tú?* (Do you think?), *An gclois tú?* (Do you hear?).

The relative form `-(e)as` is no longer found in Munster but is common in Ulster and Connacht, although mostly as `-(e)anns`. In the official Standard, it is optional (e.g., *a leanas* = that follows).

#### Synthetic Forms
The 1st person singular `-(a)im` is found regularly in all dialects: *buailim* (not *buaileann mé*). Only with the verb `tá` is *táim* or *tá mé* possible.

In west Munster and the Standard, the 1st person plural `-(a)imid` is also obligatory (*buailimid*), whereas in Ulster and Connacht, the analytic form is more frequent (*buaileann muid*).

The form `-(a)ir` in the 2nd person singular is rare in Munster and has been replaced by analytic forms. In Connacht, `-(a)inns` is used as the echo form of the 2nd person singular instead of `-(a)ir` (*buailinns* instead of *buailir*).

The form `-(a)id` in the 3rd person plural is common in Munster and Connemara (*buailid* = they hit).

### Indicative Preterite

#### Analytic Forms
The analytic forms are always endingless. The stem ending `-igh` of the 2nd conjugation is pronounced [ig′] in Munster. Some verbs of the 2nd conjugation take the ending `-imh` [iv′] instead in Munster (West Cork), seemingly derived from the verbal noun.
- *do sheasaimh sé* = he stood (Standard: *sheas sé*, Ulster: *sheasaigh sé*)

#### Synthetic Forms
The plural forms of the 1st and 2nd person appear in spoken Munster Irish with a slender -r: `-(e)amair`, `-(e)abhair`. The 3rd person plural has retained a broad -r: `-(e)adar`.

The 1st and 3rd person plural forms occur in Connacht and Munster; the 2nd person plural only in Munster. The singular synthetic forms occur regularly in Munster but mostly only in echo forms elsewhere.

### Indicative Imperfect

The Imperfect forms are largely preserved in the Standard. Instead of `-(a)imis`, `-(e)amais` or `-(e)amaois` can be found. In Connacht, the 1st person plural is uncommon, and the analytic form occurs instead: *bhuaileadh muid* (we used to hit).

### Indicative Future

Synthetic Future forms are now only in use in Munster. In Connacht and Ulster, only the 1st person plural is common, with others used only in echo forms. In Connacht, `-f(a)is` sometimes occurs as the echo form of the 2nd person singular instead of `-f(a)ir`.

The relative form `-f(e)as` is no longer found in Munster but is common in Ulster and Connacht.

### Conditional

The synthetic forms are preserved in the Standard (except for the obsolete 2nd person plural). In Ulster, the old consonant sequence `-chth-` is still present in pronunciation in the 2nd conjugation: `-óchá` instead of `-ófá`, and `-óchaí` instead of `-ófaí`.

### Imperative

The 2nd person plural in the Standard today is `-igí` instead of the original `-(a)idh`. The older `-(a)idh` survived longer in Munster. In many dialect descriptions, however, both forms exist side by side.

### Present Subjunctive

The Present Subjunctive resembles the Indicative Future in its forms, but without the future marker `-f-`.

#### Analytic Forms
The ending `-(a)idh` is written `-a, -e` in the Standard today. In some dialects (Ulster, West Muskerry), the older forms with `-(a)idh` were preserved in pronunciation.
- *go dtagaidh do ríocht* / *go dtaga do ríocht* = thy kingdom come

#### Synthetic Forms
Only the 1st person plural form is obligatory in the Standard. The other synthetic endings are in optional use in Munster.

### Past Subjunctive

The Past Subjunctive and the Indicative Imperfect have identical forms today, except for the verb `bí`. In the Subjunctive, however, the particle `do` is lacking; instead, other particles like `dá` (if) are used, which cause eclipsis. With `bí`, the Past Subjunctive and the Conditional resemble each other: always *dá mbeadh*, never *dá mbíodh*.

## The Conjugational Endings

The endings listed in the table above largely determine the tense and person. Based on their forms, three series of endings can be identified.

### 1st Series

This series occurs in the **Indicative Present, Indicative Future, and Present Subjunctive**.
It originally comprised the basic forms `-im/-ad, -ir, -idh/-ann, -imid, -tidh, -id, -tar` in the 1st conjugation.

- The 1st person singular Present ending `-im` is also found similarly in Latin, but there in the Subjunctive (cf. Lat. *amem*).
- The 2nd person ending `-ir` originally derives from an old 1st person form in `-ar`.
- The 3rd person ending `-idh` is related to the Latin 3rd person `-t` (cf. *amat*). The form `-idh` was displaced in the Indicative Present by `-ann` but is preserved in the Future as `-fidh` and in the Subjunctive as `-a/-e`.
- The 1st person plural form `-imid` contains the consonant `-m-` as a typical Indo-European core (cf. Lat.: *amamus*).
- The 3rd person plural form `-id` is related to Lat. `-ant` (cf. Lat. *amant*).
- The autonomous form `-tar` has passive roots (cf. Lat.: *amatur*).

### 2nd Series

This series occurs in the **Indicative Imperfect, Past Subjunctive, Conditional**, and partly the **Imperative**. It comprises the basic forms `-inn, -tá, -adh, -imis, -ídís, -tí` in the 1st conjugation. In the Conditional, the future marker `-f-` precedes the ending.

### 3rd Series

This series occurs only in the **Indicative Preterite**. The basic forms are `-as, -is, -, -amar, -abhar, -adar, -adh`. The endings derive in the singular (`-as, -is, -`) from the Old Irish s-preterite, but in the plural (`-amar, -abhar, -adar`) from the formerly deponent (passive) conjugation.
