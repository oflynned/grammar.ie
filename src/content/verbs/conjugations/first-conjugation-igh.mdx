## Overview

Monosyllabic verbs ending in `-igh` (or the older form `-idh`) belong to the first conjugation. This is because they form their future tense with the `-f-` marker, which is characteristic of this group.

These verbs can be divided into four main categories based on the vowel that comes before the `-igh` ending. The conjugation rules, particularly how the `-igh` is handled, change depending on the category.

A key point to remember is the effect of the 1957 Official Standard and spelling reform. In many cases, the `-igh` ending is dropped or changes to `-í-` before another ending is added. For example, the older form `dóigheann sé` is now written as `dónn sé` (he burns).

## Verbs with a Broad Vowel

This group includes verbs with a long broad vowel (`á`, `ó`, `ú`, `ao`) or a diphthong ending in a broad vowel (`eo`, `ua`, `ia`) before the `-igh`.

Examples include `dóigh` (to burn), `clóigh` (to tame), `brúigh` (to press), `glaoigh` (to call), and `luaigh` (to mention).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-igh` ending is dropped before adding other endings, except in the 2nd person singular imperative (`dóigh`) and the 3rd person singular preterite (`dhóigh sé`).
*   In the present subjunctive, the `-igh` is also dropped, leaving only the stem vowel (e.g., `go ndó sé`).
*   Endings that start with `a-` (like `-ann`, `-adh`, `-amar`) drop the `a` when attached to the stem (e.g., `dónn`, `dhódh`, `dhómar`).
*   Future and Conditional endings use a broad `f` (e.g., `-faidh`, `-far`).
*   Endings with `t` use a slender `t` (e.g., `-itear`, `-iteá`). The `-i-` here is just to make the consonant slender and is not pronounced as a separate syllable.

### The case of -aeigh

The rare verbs ending in `-aeigh`, such as `glaeigh` (to glue), also belong to this group. Unlike the others, they take the full `-ann`, `-amar`, and `-adh` endings, leading to the sequence `-aea-`. However, future endings remain broad (`-faidh`) and endings with `t` are slender (`-itear`).

## Verbs in -éigh

This group includes verbs with the long slender vowel `é` before the `-igh`. Examples include `léigh` (to read) and `téigh` (to heat).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-igh` is dropped before adding other endings.
*   In the present subjunctive, the `-igh` is also dropped, leaving only the stem vowel `é` (e.g., `go lé sé`).
*   Future and Conditional endings use a slender `f` (e.g., `-ifidh`, `-ifear`).
*   Endings with `t` use a slender `t` (e.g., `-itear`, `-iteá`).

## Verbs in -ígh

This group includes verbs with a long `í` in the stem before the `-igh`. Examples include `maígh` (to claim) and `caígh` (to cry).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-gh` is dropped before endings are added.
*   The slender endings `-im` and `-inn` lose their vowel, becoming `-m` and `-nn`.
*   Endings that start with `a-` change according to spelling rules after `í`, so `-ann` becomes `-onn` and `-amar` becomes `-omar`.
*   Future and Conditional endings use a slender `f` (e.g., `-fidh`, `-fear`).
*   Endings with `t` use a slender `t` (e.g., `-tear`, `-teá`).
*   In the present subjunctive, the `-gh` is dropped, leaving only the stem vowel `í` (e.g., `go maí sé`).

## Verbs with a Short Vowel

This group includes verbs with a short vowel or just a consonant before the `-igh`. Examples include `nigh` (to wash), `luigh` (to lie down), and `figh` (to weave).

### Conjugation Rules

*   The `-igh` ending is considered part of the verb root and is never dropped.
*   Before most endings, `-igh` fuses and is written as `-í-`. For example, `-igh` + `-eann` becomes `-íonn`, and `-igh` + `-im` becomes `-ím`.
*   **Crucially**, in forms where the `-igh` is retained (imperative) or in endings with `-t-`, the vowel `i` remains short (e.g., `nigh`, `nitear`). This distinguishes them from the `-ígh` verbs above.
*   Future and Conditional endings use a slender `f` (e.g., `-ífidh`, `-ífear`).
*   Endings with `t` use a slender `t` (e.g., `-itear`, `-iteá`).

## Compound Verbs

These conjugation rules also apply to polysyllabic verbs that are formed by adding a prefix to one of these monosyllabic verbs. For example:
*   `eisiaigh` (to exclude) is formed from `iaigh` (to close).
*   `ionsuigh` (to insert) is formed from `suigh` (to sit).
