### Explanations

Since many of these forms appear primarily in older texts, the old orthography is also provided for the 2nd conjugation. Note that spelling in the old orthography often varied. For example, the autonomous imperfect ending [tiː] could be written as `-t(h)aí`, `-t(h)aoi`, `-t(h)aidhe`, `-t(h)aighe`, `-t(h)ithe`, or `-tithi`.

In the past tenses, the particle `do` is always given here. Today it is often omitted (except partly in Munster), but it always appears as the prefix `d’-` before a vowel and is the cause of the constant lenition in these tenses.

Parentheses indicate additions according to a slender or broad stem-final consonant, e.g., `-f(a)idh` becomes `-faidh` after a broad consonant (*dúnfaidh*) or `-fidh` after a slender one (*buailfidh*).

In the Future/Conditional of the 2nd conjugation, the reformed spelling uses `-eo-` with a slender stem-final consonant, whereas the old orthography always used `-eó-`.
- `-óidh` e.g., *salóidh sé* (he will soil)
- `-eoidh` e.g., *éireoidh sé* (he will rise)

These conjugation forms are no longer used everywhere in this complete manner. In individual dialects, different forms have been preserved, with a constant trend towards analytic forms, more so in the north than in the south.
