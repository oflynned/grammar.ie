## Overview

The first conjugation, or `An Chéad Réimniú`, is the largest category of regular verbs in Irish. It primarily includes verbs with a single-syllable stem, but also covers specific types of multi-syllable verbs. The conjugation pattern depends on whether the final consonant of the verb stem is broad or slender.

## Verb Categories

Verbs in the first conjugation can be grouped by their stem structure.

### Monosyllabic Verbs

This is the most common type of verb in the first conjugation.

*   **Verbs with a single-syllable stem** ending in a broad or slender consonant.
    *   *glan* (to clean)
    *   *tuig* (to understand)
*   **Verbs with a single-syllable stem** ending in `-igh`.
    *   *nigh* (to wash)
    *   *léigh* (to read)
    *   *dóigh* (to burn)

### Polysyllabic Verbs

Some multi-syllable verbs also follow first conjugation patterns. Many of these undergo **depalatalisation**, where a final slender consonant in the stem becomes broad before an ending is added.

*   **Verbs ending in `-áil`**: The final consonant is depalatalised before most endings.
    *   *sabháil* (to save) -> *sabhálann sé* (he saves)
    *   *athchúrsáil* (to recycle) -> *athchúrsálfar* (one will recycle)
*   **Verbs with a long vowel in the final syllable** (e.g., `-áin`, `-óil`, `-úir`): Depalatalisation is common.
    *   *taispeáin* (to show) -> *taispeánann sé* (he shows)
    *   *tionóil* (to collect) -> *tionólann sé* (he collects)
*   Some verbs do not depalatalise, such as `tiomáin` (to drive) -> *tiomáineann sé*. Others, like `gearán` (to complain), already have a broad ending.

### Syncopated Verbs

Syncope is the loss of an unstressed vowel in the middle of a word when an ending is added. In modern Irish, this change is often only reflected in the spelling, as the spoken forms may already be monosyllabic.

*   Verbs like `deighil` (to separate) and `feighil` (to watch over) always show syncope.
    *   *deighil* -> *deighleann tú* (you separate)
*   The verb `siúil` (to walk), formerly spelt `siubhail`, shows depalatalisation due to historical syncope.
    *   *siúil* -> *siúlann sé* (he walks), *siúlfar* (one will walk)

### Compound Verbs

Verbs formed by adding a prefix to a first conjugation verb generally follow the same conjugation rules as the base verb.

*   *for + gabh* -> *forghabh* (to seize) -> *forghabhann sé*
*   *rua + dóigh* -> *ruadhóigh* (to singe) -> *ruadhónn sé*

When the irregular verb `déan` is part of a compound, it becomes a regular first conjugation verb.

*   *athdhéan* (to do again)
*   *comhdhéan* (to form) -> *chomhdhéan sé* (he formed)

## Conjugation Patterns

The endings for first conjugation verbs are determined by the quality of the final consonant of the verb's root.

### Broad Consonant Endings

Verbs with a stem ending in a broad consonant (a, o, u) use the following endings.

|             | Present     | Preterite | Imperfect   | Future        | Conditional  | Imperative | Present Subjunctive | Preterite Subjunctive |
|:------------|:------------|:----------|:------------|:--------------|:-------------|:-----------|:--------------------|:----------------------|
| **1.Sing.** | `-aim`      | `-as`     | `-ainn`     | `-fad`        | `-fainn`     | `-aim`     | `-a mé`             | `-ainn`               |
| **2.Sing.** | `-ann` tú   | `-ais`    | `-tá`       | `-faidh` tú   | `-fá`        | -          | `-a tú`             | `-tá`                 |
| **3.Sing.** | `-ann` sé   | - sé      | `-adh` sé   | `-faidh` sé   | `-fadh` sé   | `-adh` sé  | `-a sé`             | `-adh sé`             |
| **1.Plur.** | `-aimid`    | `-amar`   | `-aimis`    | `-faimid`     | `-faimis`    | `-aimis`   | `-aimid`            | `-aimis`              |
| **2.Plur.** | `-ann` sibh | `-abhair` | `-adh` sibh | `-faidh` sibh | `-fadh` sibh | `-aigí`    | `-a sibh`           | `-adh sibh`           |
| **3.Plur.** | `-ann` siad | `-adar`   | `-aidís`    | `-faidh` siad | `-faidís`    | `-aidís`   | `-a siad`           | `-aidís`              |
| **Auto.**   | `-tar`      | `-adh`    | `-taí`      | `-far`        | `-faí`       | `-tar`     | `-tar`              | `-taí`                |
| **Rel.**    | `-as` (sé)  | -         | -           | `-fas` (sé)   | -            | -          | -                   | -                     |

### Slender Consonant Endings

Verbs with a stem ending in a slender consonant (e, i) use the following endings.

|             | Present      | Preterite  | Imperfect    | Future       | Conditional   | Imperative | Present Subjunctive | Preterite Subjunctive |
|:------------|:-------------|:-----------|:-------------|:-------------|:--------------|:-----------|:--------------------|:----------------------|
| **1.Sing.** | `-im`        | `-eas`     | `-inn`       | `-fead`      | `-finn`       | `-im`      | `-e mé`             | `-inn`                |
| **2.Sing.** | `-eann` tú   | `-is`      | `-teá`       | `-fidh` tú   | `-feá`        | -          | `-e tú`             | `-teá`                |
| **3.Sing.** | `-eann` sé   | - sé       | `-eadh` sé   | `-fidh` sé   | `-feadh` sé   | `-eadh` sé | `-e sé`             | `-eadh sé`            |
| **1.Plur.** | `-imid`      | `-eamar`   | `-imis`      | `-fimid`     | `-fimis`      | `-imis`    | `-imid`             | `-imis`               |
| **2.Plur.** | `-eann` sibh | `-eabhair` | `-eadh` sibh | `-fidh` sibh | `-feadh` sibh | `-igí`     | `-e sibh`           | `-eadh sibh`          |
| **3.Plur.** | `-eann` siad | `-eadar`   | `-idís`      | `-fidh` siad | `-fidís`      | `-idís`    | `-e siad`           | `-idís`               |
| **Auto.**   | `-tear`      | `-eadh`    | `-tí`        | `-fear`      | `-fí`         | `-tear`    | `-tear`             | `-tí`                 |
| **Rel.**    | `-eas` (sé)  | -          | -            | `-feas` (sé) | -             | -          | -                   | -                     |
