## Overview

Verbs ending in `-áil` belong to the first conjugation, even though they have more than one syllable. This verb type is very common and productive in Irish.

Many of these verbs are formed from loanwords, particularly from English. This makes `-áil` a useful suffix for creating new verbs.

*   *paint -> peinteáil* (to paint)
*   *save -> sábháil* (to save)
*   *park -> pairceáil* (to park)

Colloquially, almost any English verb can be adapted into Irish using this ending.

*   *hitchhike -> hitchhikeáil* (to hitchhike)
*   *bicycle -> bicycleáil* (to cycle)

## Spelling Rules

The final syllable of the verb stem changes depending on the ending that follows it.

### The -áil Suffix

*   The slender `-áil` changes to a broad `-ál-` before most endings.
*   The broad `-ál-` is also used before endings containing a broad `f` (found in the future and conditional tenses).
*   The slender `-áil-` is kept before endings containing a slender `t` (found in the autonomous forms).

### Comparison with Other Verbs

This pattern is unique to `-áil` verbs. Other polysyllabic verbs in the first conjugation also change their final syllable from slender to broad, but they do so differently.

For verbs like `tionóil` (to collect) or `taispeáin` (to show), the syllable becomes broad before all endings, including those with `t`.

*   *taispeáin -> taispeánann sé* (he shows)
*   *taispeáin -> taispeántar* (one shows)

Notice the broad `t` in `taispeántar`, compared to the slender `t` in `sábháiltear`.

A few polysyllabic verbs, like `tiomáin` (to drive), do not always undergo this change: `tiomáineann sé` (he drives).
