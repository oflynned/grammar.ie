## Overview

The verb `tabhair` means "to give" and is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. It is notable for using two different stems: `tug` for the Present, Preterite, and Imperfect tenses, and `tabhar` for the Future and Conditional tenses. It does not have special dependent forms in the modern standard.

## Historical Forms and Etymology

The verb `tabhair` developed from the verb `beir` ("to bring" or "to catch") by prefixing the preposition `do` ("to"). This origin is visible in older absolute forms of the verb.

In Old Irish, the stress pattern determined the form of the verb. When the stress was on the first syllable (prototonic), the form became `tabhair`. When the stress was on the second syllable (deuterotonic), the form was `do-bheir`.

Older forms distinguished between dependent and absolute forms, which are still in use in some Munster and Ulster dialects.

| Tense | Dependent | Absolute |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | <ga>tabhrann</ga> | <ga>(do-)bheir(eann)</ga> |
| **Imperfect** | <ga>tabhradh</ga> | <ga>(do-)bheireadh</ga> |
| **Future** | <ga>tiobhraidh</ga> / <ga>tabharfaidh</ga> | <ga>(do-)bhéarfaidh</ga> |
| **Conditional** | <ga>tiobhradh</ga> / <ga>tabharfadh</ga> | <ga>(do-)bhéarfadh</ga> |

In the absolute forms, the prefix `do-` was always unstressed and often omitted. This resulted in forms that differed from the verb `beir` only by the presence of constant lenition (e.g., `bhéarfaidh sé` = "he will give"). In contexts where lenition or eclipsis would occur anyway, the two verbs became indistinguishable.

A notable feature in Ulster Irish is the tendency to omit preterite verbal particles, using `go dtug` instead of the standard `gur thug`.

## Pronunciation

The forms `tabhar` and `tabhair` are commonly pronounced [toːr] and [toːr′] respectively, or sometimes with a short vowel as [tor] and [tor′].

- *Tabhair dhom é!* (Give it to me!) can be pronounced as [tor′əm e:].
