The verb `déan` means "to do" or "to make" and is one of the most common irregular verbs in Irish. Its irregularity is most notable in the Preterite (past tense), which uses the stem `rinne` in independent forms and `dearna` in dependent forms.

## Conjugation

The tables below show the full conjugation of `déan`. Note the two different forms for the Preterite, where Form 1 (`rinne`) is the standard independent form, and Form 2 (`dhein`) is a common variant, particularly in Munster.

|:---------------|:-------------------|:-------------------|:-------------------|:--------------|:---------------|
| **1.sg.**      | déanaim            | rinneas            | dheineas           | dhéanainn     | déanfad        |
| **2.sg.**      | déanann tú         | rinne tú           | dheinis            | dhéantá       | déanfaidh tú   |
| **3.sg.**      | déanann sé         | rinne sé           | dhein sé           | dhéanadh sé   | déanfaidh sé   |
| **1.pl.**      | déanaimid          | rinneamar          | dheineamar         | dhéanaimis    | déanfaimid     |
| **2.pl.**      | déanann sibh       | rinneabhair        | dheineabhair       | dhéanadh sibh | déanfaidh sibh |
| **3.pl.**      | déanann siad       | rinneadar          | dheineadar         | dhéanaidís    | déanfaidh siad |

## Preterite Dependent Forms

In the Preterite, `déan` uses a special dependent stem, `dearna`, when used with particles like `ní` (not), `an` (question), `go` (that), and `nach` (that...not).

|                | Negative (`ní`) | Interrogative/Subordinate (`an`, `go`, etc.) |
|:---------------|:----------------|:---------------------------------------------|
| **1.sg.**      | ní dhearnas     | an ndearnas                                  |
| **2.sg.**      | ní dhearna tú   | an ndearna tú                                |
| **3.sg.**      | ní dhearna sé   | an ndearna sé                                |
| **1.pl.**      | ní dhearnamar   | an ndearnamar                                |
| **2.pl.**      | ní dhearnabhair | an ndearnabhair                              |
| **3.pl.**      | ní dhearnadar   | an ndearnadar                                |
| **Autonomous** | ní dhearnadh    | an ndearnadh                                 |

## Dialectal and Historical Variations

### Dialectal Forms

- **Munster:** The regular verb `déin` or `dein` is often used instead of `déan` in most tenses. However, the future and conditional tenses still use the `déan-` stem. The form `dhein` is also a common independent preterite form.
- **Ulster:** The absolute forms `ním` and `níonn sé` are used in the present tense, derived from the older form `do-ghníonn`. In this dialect, `déanaim` and `déanann` function only as dependent forms.

### Historical Forms

Historically, Irish verbs had distinct "absolute" forms (used independently) and "dependent" forms (used with particles). While this distinction has largely disappeared for `déan` in the standard language, remnants survive in dialects and older texts.

| Tense           | Dependent | Absolute       |
|:----------------|:----------|:---------------|
| **Present**     | déanann   | (do-)ghníonn   |
| **Imperfect**   | déanadh   | (do-)ghníodh   |
| **Future**      | déanfaidh | (do-)ghéanaidh |
| **Conditional** | déanfadh  | (do-)ghéanadh  |

The historical root `ghní-` is the source of related words like `gníomh`, meaning "deed" or "action".
