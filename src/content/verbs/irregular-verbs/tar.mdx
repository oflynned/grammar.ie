## Overview

The verb `tar`, meaning "to come", is an irregular verb. It uses different stems for various tenses and moods:
- **Present and Subjunctive:** `tag-`
- **Preterite:** `tháinig`
- **Future and Conditional:** `tioc-`
- **Imperative (2nd person singular):** `tar`

This verb has no special dependent forms.

## Historical Notes

An old form of the 2nd/3rd person Present Subjunctive, `tí`, is preserved in the preposition `go dtí` (to). It is rarely seen with other conjunctions.

- *sula dtí maidin* (before morning comes)

## Dialectal Variations

### Regional Stems and Forms

In Connemara, `teara` is used instead of `tar`, and the stem `teag-` is used instead of `tag-`.

In Ulster, the stem `tig` is used instead of `tag-`. The 3rd person present form is endingless.

- *tig sé* (he comes)

### Preterite Usage in Ulster and Connacht

In Ulster and parts of Connacht, preterite verbal particles such as `níor`, `ar`, and `gur` are not used with `tar`.

- *ní tháinig* (instead of *níor tháinig*)
- *an dtáinig* (instead of *ar tháinig*)
- *go dtáinig* (instead of *gur tháinig*)

### Munster Preterite Forms

In Munster, synthetic preterite forms are still in common use. Forms with `-ng-` are more standardised, while forms with `-n-` are more common in speech.

- **1st sg:** `thángas` (also `thánag`, `thánas`, `thána`) for *I came*.
- **2nd sg:** `thán(g)ais` or `tháinis` for *you came*.
- **1st pl:** `thánamair` is more common than the standard `thángamar` for *we came*.
- **2nd pl:** `thán(g)abhair` for *you (pl.) came*.
- **3rd pl:** `thán(g)adar` for *they came*.

### Pronunciation Notes

The pronunciation of certain forms can vary by dialect.

- In `thángamar` and `thángthas`, the `-ng-` is separated by an epenthetic vowel (`[ə]`) and is not pronounced as a single velar nasal sound (as in `teanga`).
- In Munster, forms like `thánamair` reflect a simpler `-n-` sound.
- In Ulster and Connacht, the final consonant of `tháinig` is often devoiced, sounding like "tháinic" `[ha:n′ək′]`.
