## Overview

The verb `bí` is one of the most fundamental and frequently used verbs in Irish. It is highly irregular, employing different stems across its various tenses and moods.

-   **Present Tense Stem:** `tá`
-   **Habitual Present, Preterite, Imperfect, Imperative Stems:** `bí`
-   **Future & Conditional Stems:** `beidh` / `beadh`
-   **Dependent Present Stem:** `fuil`
-   **Dependent Preterite & Subjunctive Stem:** `raibh`

Unlike regular verbs, `bí` does not use preterite particles like `d'`.

## Present Tense Forms

The present tense of `bí` has three distinct forms: the simple present `tá`, the habitual present `bíonn`, and the dependent form `fuil`.

### Simple Present Tense

Used for current states and actions. The following forms are common, with some variations typical of Munster Irish.

| Person     | Form(s)                               |
|:-----------|:--------------------------------------|
| 1st sg.    | `táim`                                |
| 2nd sg.    | `tá(nn) tú`, `tás tú`, `táir`, `taoi` |
| 3rd sg.    | `tá sé`, `tá sí`                      |
| 1st pl.    | `táimid`                              |
| 2nd pl.    | `tá(nn) sibh`, `tathaoi`              |
| 3rd pl.    | `táid`                                |
| Autonomous | `táthar`, `táthars`                   |

### Habitual Present Tense

Used for repeated or habitual actions.

| Person     | Form(s)                |
|:-----------|:-----------------------|
| 1st sg.    | `bím`                  |
| 2nd sg.    | `bíonn tú`, `bír`      |
| 3rd sg.    | `bíonn sé`, `bíonn sí` |
| 1st pl.    | `bímid`                |
| 2nd pl.    | `bíonn sibh`, `bíthí`  |
| 3rd pl.    | `bíd`                  |
| Autonomous | `bítear`               |

### Dependent Present Tense

Used after certain particles like `an`, `go`, `nach`, and `ní`. The stem is `fuil`.

| Person     | Interrogative (`an`)                  | Negative (`ní`)           |
|:-----------|:--------------------------------------|:--------------------------|
| 1st sg.    | `an bhfuilim`                         | `nílim`                   |
| 2nd sg.    | `an bhfuil(eann) tú`, `an bhfuilir`   | `níl(eann) tú`, `nílir`   |
| 3rd sg.    | `an bhfuil sé`, `an bhfuil sí`        | `níl sé`, `níl sí`        |
| 1st pl.    | `an bhfuilimid`                       | `nílimid`                 |
| 2nd pl.    | `an bhfuil(eann) sibh`, `an bhfuiltí` | `níl(eann) sibh`, `níltí` |
| 3rd pl.    | `an bhfuilid`                         | `nílid`                   |
| Autonomous | `an bhfuiltear`                       | `níltear`                 |

## Past Tense Forms

The past tense also distinguishes between a single event (preterite) and a repeated action (imperfect). The forms below include the preverbal particle `do`, which is common in Munster.

### Simple Past (Preterite)

Used for a single, completed action in the past.

| Person     | Form(s)                  |
|:-----------|:-------------------------|
| 1st sg.    | `do bhíos`               |
| 2nd sg.    | `do bhís`                |
| 3rd sg.    | `do bhí sé`, `do bhí sí` |
| 1st pl.    | `do bhíomair`            |
| 2nd pl.    | `do bhíobhair`           |
| 3rd pl.    | `do bhíodar`             |
| Autonomous | `do bhíothas`            |

The dependent preterite uses the stem `raibh`. For example, `an raibh tú?`

| Person     | Dependent Form               |
|:-----------|:-----------------------------|
| 1st sg.    | `an rabhas`                  |
| 2nd sg.    | `an rabhais`                 |
| 3rd sg.    | `an raibh sé`, `an raibh sí` |
| 1st pl.    | `an rabhamair`               |
| 2nd pl.    | `an rabhabhair`              |
| 3rd pl.    | `an rabhadar`                |
| Autonomous | `an rabhthas`                |

### Habitual Past (Imperfect)

Used for repeated or habitual actions in the past.

| Person     | Form(s)                        |
|:-----------|:-------------------------------|
| 1st sg.    | `do bhínn`                     |
| 2nd sg.    | `do bhíteá`                    |
| 3rd sg.    | `do bhíodh sé`, `do bhíodh sí` |

| Person     | Present Subjunctive (Simple) | Present Subjunctive (Habitual) | Past Subjunctive |
|:-----------|:-----------------------------|:-------------------------------|:-----------------|
| 1st sg.    | `go rabhad`                  | `go mbíod`                     | `dá mbínn`       |

## Dialectal Notes

*   In Munster (excluding Waterford), the particle `ná` is used instead of `nach` as a negative interrogative particle (e.g., `Ná fuil sé anseo?` - "Isn't he here?"). `Ná` causes no initial mutation.
*   The forms of `tá` are often pronounced `thá` [hɑː] in An Rinn (Waterford) and sometimes in West Munster.
*   The `-nn` ending in the present tense forms `tánn`, `níleann`, and `(bh)fuileann` typically only occurs in the 2nd person (`tánn tú`, `tánn sibh`).

## Verbal Forms

-   **Verbal Noun:** `bheith`
-   **Participle of Necessity:** `beite`

The verb `bí` lacks a verbal adjective, meaning a perfect tense ("have been") cannot be formed directly. Similarly, a progressive aspect like "I am being" is not possible, as `ag bheith` is ungrammatical.

## Grammatical Usage

### Analytic and Synthetic Forms

In the first person singular present, the analytic form (`tá mé`) is as common, and often preferred, over the synthetic form (`táim`). Both are considered standard.

-   *Tá mé anseo.* (I am here.)
-   *Táim anseo.* (I am here.)

### Relative Forms

The relative form of `tá` is `atá`. Historically, the verb was always `atá`, but the `a-` prefix is now only retained in the relative form.

-   *An fear atá ag siúl.* (The man that is walking.)

The relative particle `a` in `atá` is often omitted after interrogative words like `cad` and `conas`.

-   *Cad atá uait?* (What do you want?)
-   *Conas atá tú?* (How are you?)

Other relative forms include `a bhíos` for the Habitual Present and `a bheas` for the Future, which are common in Connacht and Ulster.

### Negative and Dependent Forms

The common negative form `níl` is a contraction of the particle `ní` and the dependent stem `fuil`.

-   `ní` + `fuil` -> `ní fhuil` -> `níl`

## Dialectal Variations

### Munster

In some Munster dialects, the present form `tá` is always lenited to `thá`.

### Ulster

In Ulster, the uncontracted form `ní fhuil` is often used instead of `níl`. The negative particle `cha(n)` is also common, used as `chan fhuil`.

### Connacht and Ulster

The form `murab fhuil` or `marab fhuil` is sometimes used instead of `mura bhfuil` (if... not).

### Subjunctive Forms

A distinction is sometimes made between the optative (wishes) and the subjunctive (after temporal conjunctions).

-   **Standard, Ulster, Mayo:** `raibh` is used for both.
-   **Munster, South Connacht:** An optative `raibh` is used for wishes, while a subjunctive `mbí` or `mbé` is used after conjunctions like `go`, `nó go`, or `sula`.

## Subjunctive Nuances

The Past Subjunctive of `bí` is identical to the Conditional, which is different from other verbs where it matches the Imperfect. Note the slight difference in the 2nd person singular and autonomous forms:

-   **Past Subjunctive:** *dá mbeiteá* (if you were)
-   **Conditional:** *bheifeá* (you would be)
-   **Imperfect:** *bhíteá* (you used to be)

For negation in the Present Subjunctive, `ná` is used instead of the `nár` found with other verbs.

-   *Ná raibh maith agat.* (No thanks to you.)
