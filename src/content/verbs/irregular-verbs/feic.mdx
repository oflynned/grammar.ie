## Overview

The verb `feic` means "to see". It is one of the eleven irregular verbs in Irish. Its irregularity is most notable in the Preterite (Past Tense), where it uses the independent stem `chonaic` and the dependent stem `faca`. Unlike regular verbs, it does not use a preterite particle like `d'`.

## Historical Forms

Historically, a distinction was made between absolute (independent) and dependent forms in the Present, Imperfect, and Future/Conditional tenses. The absolute forms used a stem like `(do-)chí`, while the `feic-` stem was reserved for dependent forms.

Today, Standard Irish and the Connacht dialect use `feic-` for both, but the older forms are still common in other dialects.

|                 | Dependent   | Absolute        |
|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Present**     | `feiceann`  | `(do-)chíonn`   |
| **Imperfect**   | `feiceadh`  | `(do-)chíodh`   |
| **Future**      | `feicfidh`  | `(do-)chífidh`  |
| **Conditional** | `feicfeadh` | `(do-)chífeadh` |

## Dialectal Variations

### Ulster

*   **Present:** The absolute form is `tí sé` or `tchí sé` (he sees). The dependent form uses the `feic-` stem, e.g., `ní fheiceann sé`.
*   **Future:** The absolute form is `tíofaidh` or `tchíofaidh`, while the dependent is `ní fheicfidh`.
*   **Conditional:** Absolute forms include `tífeadh sé` and `thiúfadh sé`, with the dependent being `ní fheicfeadh sé`.
*   **Imperfect:** The absolute is `tíodh sé` and the dependent is `ní fheiceadh sé`.
*   **Preterite:** The absolute form is `chonnaic`, and the dependent is `ní fhacaidh`.
*   **Imperative:** Instead of `feic`, the commands `amharc!` or `dearc!` are more common.
*   **Verbal Noun:** `feiceáil` or `feiceáilt`.
*   **Verbal Adjective:** `feicthe` or `feiciste`.

### Connacht

The forms in Connacht largely align with the Standard. Key differences include:

*   **Autonomous Present:** Alongside `feictear`, you may hear `feicthear`, `feicthears`, or `feictheas`. The form `chítear` appears in the idiom `chítear dhom` (it seems to me).
*   **Preterite:** A slender `n` is often used, resulting in `choinic` or `chainic` instead of `chonaic`. This form is sometimes used dependently as well (`níor choinic`).
*   **Autonomous Preterite:** The form `facthas` or `facthús` is used for both absolute and dependent contexts. The form `b’fhacthas` (from the copula `ba`) also occurs.

### Munster

*   **General:** In Dingle, the verb is often regularised using the stem `cí-` for both absolute and dependent forms (e.g., `cíonn sé`, `ní chíonn sé`), except in the Preterite.
*   **Present:** Elsewhere in Munster, the absolute form is `chíonn sé` and the dependent is `ní fhiceann sé`.
*   **Future:** The absolute is `chífidh` and the dependent is `ní fhicfidh`.
*   **Preterite:** The absolute form `chonaic` is often shortened to `(do) chnuc`. The dependent form is `ní fheacaidh`.
*   **Negatives:** The `í` in `ní` is often elided before `fh-`, resulting in forms like `n’fhiceann sé` or `n’fheacaigh sé`.
*   **Verbal Noun:** `fiscint`.
*   **Verbal Adjective:** `ficithe`, `feicithe`, or `fiscithe`.
