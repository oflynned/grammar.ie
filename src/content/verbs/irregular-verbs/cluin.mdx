## Overview

The verb `cluin` means "to hear". It is irregular only in the preterite tense, where it uses the stem `chuala`. Note that the autonomous form in the preterite, `chualathas`, is also lenited. This verb has no special dependent forms.

## Dialectal Variations

### Ulster

*   Only the stem `cluin` is generally used.
*   In the present tense, the verb is often uninflected, e.g., *cluin sé* (he hears), and can be lenited, e.g., *chluin sé*.
*   The dependent present form is always uninflected, e.g., *An gcluin tú?* (Do you hear?).
*   The future and conditional tenses sometimes use 2nd conjugation endings, e.g., *cluineochaidh sé* (he will hear).
*   In the preterite, verbal particles are often omitted, e.g., *Ní chuala sé* instead of *Níor chuala sé*.
*   The autonomous preterite form is `cluineadh` in addition to `chualas` (a shortened form of `chualathas`).
*   The verbal noun is `cluinstin` or `cluinstean`.
*   The verb `mothaigh` (to feel, to notice) is often used as a substitute.
*   The command "listen!" is usually expressed with *éist!*.

### Connacht

*   Both `clois` and `cluin` are used, with `clois` being more dominant in the south of the province.
*   The dependent present form is often uninflected, e.g., *An gcluin tú?* or *An gclois tú?*.
*   The preterite mainly uses the irregular stem `chuala`, but a regular form *chlois sé* (he heard) is also found.
*   The autonomous preterite is typically `cluineadh` or `cloiseadh` rather than the standard `chualathas`.
*   The verbal noun for `clois` is `cloisteáil`, but in the north, hybrid forms like `cluns` or `clins` also exist.

### Munster

*   Only the stem `clois` is used.
*   In the preterite 1st person singular, the uninflected form `chuala` (I heard) is common, along with `chualag` in some areas. The standard form `chualas` is rare.
*   A regular preterite form *chlois sé* (he heard) also occurs in some areas like Muskerry.
*   The analytic preterite form (used before nouns) is `chualaigh`.
*   The autonomous preterite has several forms, including `chualathas`, `chualathars`, `chualadh`, and `cualadh`.
*   The verbal noun is `cloisint` or `clos`.
*   The verb `airigh` (to feel, to notice) is often used as a substitute, especially in Cork and Waterford.
