## Overview

The verb `abair` is one of the most common irregular verbs in Irish. It is highly suppletive, meaning it uses several different stems for its various tenses and moods.

The primary stems are:
- **`deir`**: Used for the Present, Imperfect, and Subjunctive.
- **`dúirt`**: Used for the Preterite (Past).
- **`déar`**: Used for the Future and Conditional.
- **`abair`**: Used for the Imperative.

In the standard language, `abair` does not have special dependent forms, though these are found in dialects. Uniquely, the preterite form `dúirt` does not use a preterite particle like `do` or `d'`.

## Grammatical Rules

### Mutation and Particles

Forms beginning with `d-` (e.g., `deir`, `dúirt`, `déar`) are never lenited in the standard language. This is because they historically began with an `a-` (`adeir`, `adúirt`).

- Lenition only occurs in the Subjunctive after `nár`.
  - *nár dheire sé* (that he may not say)
- After the direct relative particle `a`, `d-` is not lenited.
  - *a deir*
  - *a dúirt*
- After `cad`, the relative particle `a` is often omitted in speech and writing.
  - *Cad (a) deir tú?* (What do you say?)

In the present tense, `deir` is more common than the alternative form `deireann`.

### Reporting Speech

Instead of `abair`, the defective verb `ar` or `arsa` is often used to introduce direct speech. `Ar` is used before `seisean`, `sise`, and `siadsan`, while `arsa` is used before all other nouns and pronouns.

- *"Is amadán mé, nach ea?" arsa Liam.* ("I am an idiot, am I not?" says/said Liam.)
- *"Sea, tá ceart go leor agat, a chara" ar sise.* ("Yes, you are right, my friend," says/said she.)

If `abair` is used after direct speech, it must be in a relative clause (`a deir`, `a dúirt`). Often, `a deir` is used in a timeless sense, even when referring to past events where `a dúirt` would be expected.

## Historical and Dialectal Variations

### Older Forms

Historically, dependent forms using the `abair` stem were common across more tenses. These forms are still preserved in some dialects.

| Tense | Dependent | Absolute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present** | `abrann` | `deir(eann)` |
| **Imperfect** | `abradh` | `deireadh` |
| **Future** | `abróchaidh` | `déarfaidh` |
| **Conditional** | `abróchadh` | `déarfadh` |
| **Subjunctive** | `go n-abra(idh)` | |

### Dialectal Forms

#### Ulster
- Forms with `d-` can be lenited or the `d-` is elided: `ní dheir sé` or `ní eir sé`.
- The 1st person singular is analytic: `deir mé`.
- Forms with `abr-` appear in the Present, Imperfect, Future, and Conditional.
- The preterite form `dúirt` is often rendered as `d'úirt`. The initial `d` is treated as the particle `do` and is omitted in dependent forms: `níor úirt`, `gur úirt`.
- The autonomous preterite is `húradh` or `húras`.
- The subjunctive `go n-abraidh` is more common than `go ndeire`.
- The verbal noun is `ráit` or `ráidht`.

#### Connacht
- Forms with `abr-` are used both dependently and independently. `Abair` can mean "to recite" or "to sing a song", while `deir` means "to relate" or "inform".
- `Deir` is used for both the simple and habitual present, while `deireann` is more strictly habitual.
- `Deireann` can be lenited, with the `d-` elided (`ní eireann`), but `deir` is never lenited (`ní deir`).
- The 1st person singular is `deir mé`, and the 1st person plural is `deireann muid`.
- The preterite form `d'úirt` is common, leading to dependent forms without `d-`: `níor úirt`, `gur úirt`.
- The autonomous preterite is `húradh`.
- Alternative preterite forms exist: `dúirteas` (I said), `dúirtis` (you said), `dúirteadar` (they said).

#### Munster
- `Deireann` is used in the 2nd person present in the southwest.
- Forms with `abr-` are rare and only used dependently.
- The preterite is `dúirt` or `duairt`. Synthetic forms are common: `dúras`/`dúrt` (I said), `dúrais` (you said), etc.
- The autonomous preterite is `dúradh`/`duaradh` or `dúrathas`/`duarathas`.
- In Ring (An Rinn), forms with a long `í` are found: `dúraíos`/`dúiríos` (I said).
- The verbal noun is often pronounced `reá`, with a slender `r`.
