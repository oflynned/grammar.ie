## Overview

The verb `téigh` is an irregular verb meaning "to go". It is highly suppletive, meaning it uses different root stems for different tenses and moods.

- **`téigh-`**: Used for the Present, Imperative, and Imperfect tenses.
- **`chuaigh`**: The independent stem for the Preterite (Past) tense. The preterite particle is not used with this form.
- **`deach-`**: The dependent stem for the Preterite (Past) tense, used after particles like `ní`, `an`, `go`, etc.
- **`rach-`**: Used for the Future and Conditional tenses.

## Alternative and Dialectal Forms

### Common Alternatives

- In the Imperative 2nd person singular, the forms `téir`, `téire` or `téirigh` are more common than `téigh`.
- In the Present 3rd person, `téid` is sometimes used instead of `téann`.

### Regional Variations

- **Connemara:** Future and Conditional forms often use the stem `gabh-`. The imperative forms `gabh!` and `gabhigí!` are also common. In this dialect, the verbal noun is `goil` (from `gabháil`), and the verbal adjective is `goite`.
- **Munster:** The stem `ragh-` is often used in the Future and Conditional tenses instead of the standard `rach-`.

### Historical Orthography

In older spelling systems, the past tense forms were written with a `-dh`: `chuaidh` and `deachaidh`.

The verb form `téigh` (to go) was not written with `-dh`. This distinguished it from the homophonous regular verb `téidh` (to warm).
