## Overview

`Beir` is an irregular verb with several meanings, including to carry, hold, win, bring, take, give birth, or catch. It uses the stem `rug` in the Preterite and `béar` in the Future and Conditional tenses. It has no special dependent forms.

## Core Functions

### To Give Birth

When used to mean "to give birth" in relation to humans, the autonomous form is most common and implies a passive sense.

- *Rugadh é.* - He was born.
- *Rugadh é di.* - She gave birth to him (literally, "he was born to her").

## Etymology and Related Verbs

The verb `beir` is the root of two other common irregular verbs, `tabhair` (to give) and `abair` (to say).

- The prefix `do-` combined with `beir` to form `tabhair` (to give, literally "to-carry"). This is visible in older forms like `do-bheireann`. The prefix was often omitted, but the verb remained lenited: `beireann sé` (he brings) vs. `bheireann sé` (he gives).
- The prefix `as-` combined with `beir` to form `abair` (to say, literally "to bring out").

## Dialectal Variations

### Ulster

- **Present:** In the south, `beireann` is used alongside `beiridh`. In the north, the endingless form `beir` is common. `Breitheann` is sometimes used for animals giving birth. The autonomous is `rugtar` or `rugthar` in the south, and `beirtear` in the north.
- **Future:** `Béarfaidh` is used alongside `beirfidh`. The autonomous is `rugfar` (pronounced `rucar`).
- **Preterite:** Both `bheir` and `rug` are used. The autonomous form `rugadh` means "was born", while `rugas` or `beireadh` are used for other meanings, e.g., *beireadh air* (he was caught).
- **Verbal Noun:** `Breith` is standard, but `rugan` is found in the north.

### Connacht

- **Present:** Both `beireann` and `béarann` are used.
- **Imperfect:** Both `bheireadh` and `bhéaradh` are found. The autonomous is `beirtí` or `béartaí` (unlenited).
- **Future/Conditional:** Only `béarfaidh` and `béarfadh` are used. The autonomous conditional is `béarfaí` (unlenited).
- **Preterite:** `Rug`, `bhéar`, and `bhreith` all occur. The autonomous `rugadh` means "was born", while `rugas` or `rugús` are used for other meanings, e.g., *Rugús air* (He was caught).

### Munster

- **Future/Conditional:** The `éa` sound often changes to `ia`, so `béarfaidh` is pronounced [b′iarhig′] and `béarfadh` is pronounced [b′iarhax].
- **Preterite:** `Bheir` or `riug` (with a slender r) are common. The form `rug`/`riug` is less common in the Dingle peninsula.
- **Verbal Adjective:** The verbal adjective is pronounced with a broad r, as in `bertha`.
